## 1. Introit

**Reference:** Psalm 17:19–20, with psalm verses 17:2–3 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 18 in most modern Bibles).

Factus est Dóminus protéctor meus, et edúxit me in latitúdinem: salvum me fecit, quóniam vóluit me. Ps. ibid., 2–3 Díligam te, Dómine, virtus mea: Dóminus firmaméntum meum, et refúgium meum, et liberátor meus. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Rubric:** the *Glória in excélsis* is said on every Sunday after Pentecost but not on weekdays when the preceding Sunday's Mass is used.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 17 is David's thanksgiving after deliverance from his enemies and Saul. The appointed antiphon recalls danger, rescue, breadth, and God's delight; its psalm verse first names the Lord as strength, refuge, and liberator. The Introit therefore makes a mature royal thanksgiving the assembly's entrance voice.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's stopping point after the doxology is retained, with the secondary's explicit repeat incipit documented above.

## 2. Collect

Sancti nóminis tui, Dómine, timórem páriter et amórem fac nos habére perpétuum: quia numquam tua gubernatióne destítuis, quos in soliditáte tuæ dilectiónis instítuis. Per Dóminum nostrum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical oration asks that fear and love of God's holy Name remain together and endure. Its reason is God's continuing governance of those established in the solidity of divine love. As the Collect it gathers the assembly's prayer before the readings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's short conclusion is retained. See the witness difference under `Unresolved discrepancies`.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** 1 John 3:13–18.

Caríssimi: Nolíte mirári, si odit vos mundus. Nos scimus, quóniam transláti sumus de morte ad vitam, quóniam dilígimus fratres. Qui non díligit, manet in morte: omnis qui odit fratrem suum, homicída est. Et scitis quóniam omnis homicída non habet vitam ætérnam in semetípso manéntem. In hoc cognóvimus caritátem Dei, quóniam ille ánimam suam pro nobis pósuit: et nos debémus pro frátribus ánimas pónere. Qui habúerit substántiam huius mundi, et víderit fratrem suum necessitátem habére, et cláuserit víscera sua ab eo: quómodo cáritas Dei manet in eo? Filíoli mei, non diligámus verbo, neque lingua, sed ópere, et veritáte.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** 1 John 3 was read in full, with the preceding community crisis in chapter 2. Verses 11–12 recall the command to love and Cain's murder of Abel. The appointed passage contrasts the world's hatred with passage from death to life, identifies Christ's self-gift as love's form, and tests profession by material mercy. Verses 19–24 continue from deed and truth to confidence before God, faith in Christ, mutual love, and the indwelling Spirit. The Epistle makes concrete charity the truthful response to divine invitation.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 4. Gradual

**Reference:** Psalm 119:1–2 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 120 in most modern Bibles).

Ad Dóminum cum tribulárer clamávi, et exaudívit me. ℣. Dómine, líbera ánimam meam a lábiis iníquis, et a lingua dolósa.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 119 opens the Songs of Ascents. The speaker remembers an answered cry yet still asks rescue from lying speech, then laments dwelling among peoples hostile to peace. The Gradual places truthful prayer and deliverance from deceit between the Epistle's demand for love in deed and truth and the Gospel's courteous but false excuses.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Psalm 7:2 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering).

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Dómine Deus meus, in te sperávi: salvum me fac ex ómnibus persequéntibus me, et líbera me. Allelúia.

**Rubric:** this Alleluia with its verse follows the Gradual on all Sundays after Pentecost, including weekdays when the Sunday Mass is used.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 7 is an individual lament in which an accused speaker seeks the just God's protection, takes an oath of innocence, and expects violence to recoil upon its maker before ending in praise. The Alleluia gives persecuted Christians the psalmist's trust immediately before Christ exposes refusal of the kingdom's summons.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Luke 14:16–24.

In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus pharisǽis parábolam hanc: Homo quidam fecit cœnam magnam, et vocávit multos. Et misit servum suum hora cœnæ dícere invitátis ut venírent, quia iam paráta sunt ómnia. Et cœpérunt simul omnes excusáre. Primus dixit ei: Villam emi, et necésse hábeo exíre, et vidére illam: rogo te, habe me excusátum. Et alter dixit: Iuga boum emi quinque, et eo probáre illa: rogo te, habe me excusátum. Et álius dixit: Uxórem duxi: et ídeo non possum veníre. Et revérsus servus nuntiávit hæc dómino suo. Tunc irátus paterfamílias, dixit servo suo: Exi cito in platéas et vicos civitátis: et páuperes, ac débiles, et cæcos, et claudos introdúc huc. Et ait servus: Dómine, factum est ut imperásti, et adhuc locus est. Et ait dóminus servo: Exi in vias, et sepes: et compélle intráre, ut impleátur domus mea. Dico autem vobis, quod nemo virórum illórum, qui vocáti sunt, gustábit cœnam meam.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Luke 13–15 and the complete table scene in 14:1–24 were read. During the long journey toward Jerusalem Jesus dines on a Sabbath at the home of a leading Pharisee, heals, teaches humility and hospitality toward the poor, and answers a guest's blessing of kingdom bread with this parable. Prepared gift, refusal, widening invitation, and a filled house therefore test the hearer's present response. The following sayings address the cost of discipleship, while chapter 15 answers complaints about Jesus welcoming sinners.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Psalm 6:5 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering).

Dómine, convértere, et éripe ánimam meam: salvum me fac propter misericórdiam tuam.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 6 is a personal lament appealing to mercy amid sickness, sin's consequences, weeping, enemies, and fear of death. The prayer turns at its end toward confidence that God has heard. At the Offertory the congregation ceases making excuses and asks the Lord to turn, rescue, and save for mercy's sake.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 8. Secret

Oblátio nos, Dómine, tuo nómini dicánda puríficet: et de die in diem ad cæléstis vitæ tránsferat actiónem. Per Dóminum.

**Rubric:** Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** the Secret asks that the oblation dedicated to God's Name purify the offerers and transfer them day by day toward the action or conduct of heavenly life. It is the priest's proper prayer over the gifts and gives the Gospel's banquet and the Epistle's concrete love a progressive sacramental grammar.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Psalm 12:6 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 13 in most modern Bibles).

Cantábo Dómino, qui bona tríbuit mihi: et psallam nómini Dómini altíssimi.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 12 moves from four cries of “how long,” through petition for illumined eyes, to trust in mercy and promised thanksgiving. The Communion antiphon gives the last verse to recipients of the sacred gifts: song answers benefits that God first bestows.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Sumptis munéribus sacris, quǽsumus, Dómine: ut, cum frequentatióne mystérii, crescat nostræ salútis efféctus. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** after receiving the sacred gifts, the Church asks that the effect of salvation increase with repeated participation in the mystery. The Postcommunion treats frequency as ordered toward growth rather than as bare repetition or automatic completion.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline comma after `ut` is retained, with the secondary punctuation documented above.
