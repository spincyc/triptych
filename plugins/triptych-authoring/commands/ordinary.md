# /ordinary

Create or revise a whole-order exposition of the Ordinary or Order of Mass, or a bounded exposition of one of its units, from an explicit edition manifest and sequence inventory.

## What to provide

The user's complete invocation context is:

$ARGUMENTS

Treat all of it as controlling input, whether it follows the suggestions below or is entirely free-form.

- The rite, form, exact liturgical books and editions, language or languages, territory, and comparison object if any.
- Whether the target is the complete Order, the stable Ordinary, a defined unit, adjacent prayers, or an existing document path.
- The desired unit divisions or editorial numbering and the intended biblical, patristic, historical, symbolic, ritual, or comparative purpose.
- Text witnesses, source links, disputed developments, reader needs, exclusions, and all free-form context, guides, limits, staging, or commit instructions.

## Preflight

1. Apply `commands/_conventions.md` and read `guidance/liturgy/ordinary-expositions.md` completely.
2. Establish the edition manifest, sequence inventory, stable-versus-variable boundary, included adjacent prayers, language and territory, and comparison rules before drafting.
3. Inspect the target’s research scope and source audit, existing unit architecture, catalog placement, and any shared text consumers.
4. Treat editorial unit numbers as navigational aids, not received liturgical divisions; never expose an internal catalog ordering key such as `O00` in the title or prose.

## Plan

Define the liturgical object, complete ordered sequence, text and translation policy, three analytical layers, unit template, whole-order thesis, source records, publication artifacts, and authorized commit stage.

## Commands

1. Create or revise the correct Ordinary-exposition leaf and its `research/edition-manifest.md`, `research/sequence-inventory.md`, `research/scope.md`, and `research/source-audit.md` records.
2. Give the bounded received text or precise locus for every unit, with translation status and edition identity. Do not silently fold propers, ceremonial accretions, adjacent devotions, or another edition into the Ordinary.
3. For each unit, establish its immediate scriptural language and biblical context; distinguish direct quotation, verbal echo, typology, thematic correspondence, patristic reception, and original editorial synthesis.
4. Document the prayer’s textual and ritual history with calibrated dates, witnesses, regions, reforms, and attributions. Do not assign a text to a pope or author without adequate evidence.
5. Relate each unit to what precedes and follows and to the action of the whole Mass. Keep the profile’s textual-liturgical, historical-developmental, and theological-symbolic layers distinct but integrated.
6. Treat adjacent devotions, comparative forms, and later appended prayers—such as the Leonine Prayers—within explicit boundaries rather than implying that they are units of the stable Ordinary.
7. Refresh structured generation metadata, build and inspect every page, install the reviewed PDF, and preserve its first-place internal ordering in the temporal-propers table without giving that ordering a separate public treatment.

## Verification

Check the edition manifest against the sequence inventory; every text boundary, translation, biblical locus and relationship class; every historical attribution and date; whole-order transitions; adjacent-prayer labels; research records; metadata; PDF pages; installed artifact; and catalog links.

## Summary

Report the exact Ordinary or unit, editions and boundaries, analytical layers and historical additions treated, records and artifacts changed, verification, authorized commit, and unresolved textual or historical questions.
