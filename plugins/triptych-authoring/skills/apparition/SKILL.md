# Apparition Studies

Create or revise a Marian-apparition study that identifies exactly what was claimed, what the competent Church authority judged, and what remains historically or theologically uncertain.

## What to provide

Treat the user's complete request and thread context as controlling input, whether it follows the suggestions below or is entirely free-form.

- The apparition, received tradition, group of events, corpus, table, or existing target to treat.
- Place, country, diocese or eparchy, Church *sui iuris*, dates, recipients, witnesses, and reported messages as far as known.
- Known local, national, dicasterial, or papal acts; source links; original languages; and the date through which status should be checked.
- Desired narrative depth, historical setting, thematic comparison, chronology, map, table, or appendix.
- Inclusion and exclusion rules, evidentiary concerns, devotional or pastoral questions, and all free-form context, guides, limits, staging, or commit instructions.

## Preflight

1. Read `../../references/conventions.md` completely, apply it, and read `guidance/theology/mariology.md` completely.
2. Inspect the existing apparition corpus, its `research/corpus-status.md`, source audit, scope record, and any inventory that controls additions.
3. Establish the competent authority, exact object of every act, operative formula, date, later intervention, and current status under the applicable historical or current judgment norms.
4. Check current Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith materials and official diocesan or eparchial sources. Treat indexes, news reports, devotional sites, and translations as leads until verified.

## Plan

Define whether the work is an event monograph, comparative corpus, table revision, or received-tradition study; then set its evidentiary boundary, status vocabulary, source hierarchy, narrative architecture, checked-through date, research records, publication artifacts, and authorized commit stage.

## Actions

1. Distinguish the historical event, testimony, reported message, devotional reception, fruits, doctrinal assessment, and juridical or prudential judgment. Do not make one category stand in for another.
2. Name the competent authority and quote or closely paraphrase the dispositive language from the controlling act. Keep legacy formulas and the six categories of the 2024 norms distinct and explain transitions without silently translating one into the other.
3. Present reported messages as reported private revelation, never as additions to the deposit of faith or as facts established merely by ecclesiastical permission for devotion.
4. Treat ancient apparition traditions according to the provenance actually available: received liturgical or local tradition, historical attestation, later ecclesiastical reception, and modern judgment are separate claims.
5. Update `research/corpus-status.md`, the event or corpus inventory, `research/scope.md`, `research/source-audit.md`, and structured generation metadata. State the checked-through date and any official archive or decree not obtained.
6. Avoid claims to an exhaustive worldwide registry or to “all approved apparitions” unless an identified competent authority actually supplies that bounded registry. In tables, give every row the same explicit authority, act, status, date, source, and evidentiary treatment.
7. Build, inspect, install, and catalog the completed work under the Mariology profile when the request authorizes publication artifacts.

## Verification

Check every date, place, name, reported quotation, decree, competent-authority attribution, judgment formula, later intervention, translation, private-revelation boundary, corpus row, source-record mapping, metadata field, PDF page, installed artifact, and catalog link.

## Summary

Report the event or corpus treated, checked-through date, controlling judgments and authorities, records and artifacts updated, verification and commit if authorized, and every material status or provenance limitation.

## Next Steps

Name the one missing decree, original-language witness, archival record, or specialist review most important to the study.
