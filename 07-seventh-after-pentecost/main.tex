\weektitle{Seventh Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Septima Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: true teaching, sanctifying fruit, and sacramental medicine}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Omnes gentes, plaudite manibus} (Ps. 46:2)}{Universal kingship and praise}{The universal King gathers praise before the Gospel warns that religious appearance can counterfeit truth.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Deus, cuius providentia}: remove harms; grant what profits}{Providence and purification}{Discernment begins by asking God's unfailing providence to remove and supply what the soul cannot master alone.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:19--23}{Slavery, sanctification, eternal life}{Freedom from sin becomes service to God whose present fruit is holiness and whose end is life.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Venite, filii, audite me}; \latin{Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini} (Ps. 33:12, 6)}{True teaching and illumination}{The assembly approaches the Lord to receive his teaching and light, the Mass's positive answer to false prophecy.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{Omnes gentes, plaudite manibus} (Ps. 46:2)}{Royal acclamation}{Before the warning, the assembly acclaims the universal King's authority over every teacher and hearer.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 7:15--21}{False prophets, trees, fruits, obedience}{Moral fruit exposes a life masked by piety; doctrinal truth still requires its proper apostolic and ecclesial norm.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Sicut in holocaustis arietum} (Dan. 3:40)}{Humble sacrifice accepted}{The assembly judges no one from above: it first asks that its own offering please God.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Inclina aurem tuam, accelera, ut eripias me} (Ps. 30:3)}{Urgent deliverance}{Communicants remain petitioners who need deliverance, not spectators who have passed the test.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Tua nos, Domine, medicinalis operatio}}{Healing operation}{Sacramental medicine frees from perversity and leads the will toward what is right.}
\end{properstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Romans 6 contrasts slavery to sin, shame, and death with service to God, sanctification, and eternal life; Matthew 7 commands vigilance toward false prophets and identifies obedience to the Father's will as decisive fruit.\\
Allegorical & Christ is the true teacher and obedient Son; the Church bears holy fruit only by his gift. The Gradual's teacher and the Secret's one perfect sacrifice disclose positively what false prophecy and corrupt sacrifice counterfeit.\\
Moral & Test a life by works rather than costume or slogans, submit one's own conduct to the same judgment, and remember that moral fruit does not by itself establish the truth of doctrine.\\
Anagogical & The tree's fire and Paul's opposed ends, death and eternal life, reveal final judgment; medicinal grace is offered now so that the sinner may be changed and bear fruit for life.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{The Discerner Is Also the Patient}

The Gospel's warning does not place the hearer above everyone else. Before and after testing visible fruit, the assembly acclaims a universal King, asks providence for help, receives divine teaching, offers itself through Christ's sacrifice, seeks deliverance, and asks sacramental medicine to heal its own perversity.

\begin{massbox}{The observer's reversal}
The Mass begins with Christ's authority, not private suspicion, and ends with the observer asking to be healed. Discernment is necessary, but it becomes Christian only when the one examining another's fruit also stands under the Father's will and receives grace as a patient.
\end{massbox}

\section*{Two Tests That Must Not Be Confused}

Matthew supplies a test of moral credibility; it does not make favorable outcomes an independent rule of doctrine. The axes below must therefore be considered separately.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.18\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
& \textbf{Holy moral fruit} & \textbf{Corrupt moral fruit}\\
\midrule
Apostolic doctrine & A credible witness to truth. Yet the holiness remains grace and does not make truth depend upon the teacher's personal achievement. & Hypocrisy or scandal contradicts the teaching in practice. The corrupt life does not, by itself, make apostolic doctrine false.\\
False doctrine & Admirable conduct or apparently beneficial outcomes cannot establish a claim as revealed truth. Sheep's clothing can be convincing. & Both the teaching's departure from the received faith and the life's fruit give reason for rejection.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{studybox}{What each axis can establish}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Doctrine} is tested by revealed faith received through Scripture and apostolic tradition in the Church.
\item \textbf{Moral credibility} is tested by sustained obedience and fruit, not costume, slogans, popularity, or striking effects.
\item \textbf{The Gospel's final criterion} is doing the Father's will. The Gradual supplies the prior posture: approach the Lord, receive his teaching, and be enlightened.
\item \textbf{The same judgment reaches the hearer.} The Offertory, Secret, Communion, and Postcommunion prevent discernment from becoming self-exemption.
\end{itemize}
\end{studybox}

\section*{From Master to Fruit to End}

Paul's slavery metaphor and Christ's tree metaphor describe a causal order. Clothing can disguise a tree; it cannot change its root. Grace does not merely improve the disguise: sacramental medicine frees the will from perversity so that a different fruit becomes possible.

\begin{studybox}{What ``cannot'' means}
``A bad tree cannot bear good fruit'' describes a person while remaining evil. It must not be weakened into ``cannot reliably,'' but neither does it deny conversion. Grace can make the tree good; the Postcommunion asks precisely for an operation that changes the diseased root and directs the will toward what is right.
\end{studybox}

\section*{One Sacrifice Across Salvation History}

The Offertory and Secret create a compact typology of sacrifice rather than a generic lesson about sincerity.

\begin{massbox}{Why this matters for discernment}
Daniel's humbled worshipper does not approach God as a successful evaluator of others. The Secret then locates acceptance in Christ's one sacrifice and asks that both gifts and servants be sanctified. The Church's answer to counterfeit religion is participation in Christ, not confidence in religious performance.
\end{massbox}

\section*{Three Patristic Guardrails}

\begin{threestable}{Witness}{Checked text and interpretive claim}{Error prevented}
St. Augustine & \emph{On the Sermon on the Mount} II.24--25 identifies the person as the tree, works as fruit, and impressive religious practices as possible clothing. ``But those are the fruits, to do the will of the Father who is in heaven, in the doing of which He has condescended to exhibit Himself as an example.'' & Religious display, dramatic works, or favorable appearances are not substituted for obedient holiness.\\
St. John Chrysostom on Matthew & \emph{Homily 23 on Matthew}, 8--9, reads the warning as corrupt life masked by virtue while preserving the possibility of conversion. ``For he may indeed change to virtue, being evil; but while continuing in wickedness, he will not bear good fruit.'' & Christ's ``cannot'' is neither weakened nor made fatalistic: the person cannot bear good fruit while remaining evil, but can be changed.\\
St. John Chrysostom on Romans & \emph{Homily 12 on Romans}, on Romans 6:23, distinguishes sin's wages from God's gracious end. ``For he does not say, the wages of good deeds, \textquoteleft{}but the gift of God;\textquoteright{} to show, that it was not of themselves that they were freed.'' & Sanctification is real present fruit, while eternal life remains gift rather than payment earned through moral success.\\
\end{threestable}

\begin{massbox}{A discernment sequence for the Mass}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.45em,itemsep=0.18em]
\item Whose authority do the Introit and Alleluia acclaim before any human teacher is examined?
\item What does the Collect say providence must remove and grant?
\item What positive school of teaching and illumination does the Gradual open?
\item What can moral fruit disclose, and what can it not prove about doctrine?
\item How do Daniel's Offertory and the Secret place the discerner under judgment and within Christ's sacrifice?
\item Why does the Communion leave the communicant urgently asking deliverance?
\item What must the Postcommunion's medicine change before holy fruit can grow?
\end{enumerate}
\end{massbox}
