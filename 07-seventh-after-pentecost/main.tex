\weektitle{Seventh Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Septima Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: true teaching, sanctifying fruit, and sacramental medicine}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Omnes gentes, plaudite manibus} (Ps. 46:2)}{Universal kingship and praise}{The universal King gathers praise before the Gospel warns that religious appearance can counterfeit truth.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Deus, cuius providentia}: remove harms; grant what profits}{Providence and purification}{Discernment begins by asking God's unfailing providence to remove and supply what the soul cannot master alone.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:19--23}{Slavery, sanctification, eternal life}{Freedom from sin becomes service to God whose present fruit is holiness and whose end is life.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Venite, filii, audite me}; \latin{Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini} (Ps. 33:12, 6)}{True teaching and illumination}{The assembly approaches the Lord to receive his teaching and light, the Mass's positive answer to false prophecy.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{Omnes gentes, plaudite manibus} (Ps. 46:2)}{Royal acclamation}{Before the warning, the assembly acclaims the universal King's authority over every teacher and hearer.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 7:15--21}{False prophets, trees, fruits, obedience}{Moral fruit exposes a life masked by piety; doctrinal truth still requires its proper apostolic and ecclesial norm.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Sicut in holocaustis arietum} (Dan. 3:40)}{Humble sacrifice accepted}{The assembly judges no one from above: it first asks that its own offering please God.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Inclina aurem tuam, accelera, ut eripias me} (Ps. 30:3)}{Urgent deliverance}{Communicants remain petitioners who need deliverance, not spectators who have passed the test.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Tua nos, Domine, medicinalis operatio}}{Healing operation}{Sacramental medicine frees from perversity and leads the will toward what is right.}
\end{properstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Romans 6 contrasts slavery to sin, shame, and death with service to God, sanctification, and eternal life; Matthew 7 commands vigilance toward false prophets and identifies obedience to the Father's will as decisive fruit.\\
Allegorical & Christ is the true teacher and obedient Son; the Church bears holy fruit only by his gift. The Gradual's teacher and the Secret's one perfect sacrifice disclose positively what false prophecy and corrupt sacrifice counterfeit.\\
Moral & Test a life by works rather than costume or slogans, submit one's own conduct to the same judgment, and remember that moral fruit does not by itself establish the truth of doctrine.\\
Anagogical & The tree's fire and Paul's opposed ends, death and eternal life, reveal final judgment; medicinal grace is offered now so that the sinner may be changed and bear fruit for life.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Core Scriptural Symbols}
\begin{connectionstable}
Tree & Augustine identifies the tree with the person and its fruit with works. An evil person cannot bear good fruit while remaining evil, yet the tree can be made good through conversion; the image is a diagnosis, not a denial that grace can change the sinner.\\
Fruit & Romans names fruit unto sanctification, while Matthew points to doing the Father's will. Augustine distinguishes the fruit of the Spirit from impressive practices that can serve as sheep's clothing. This is primarily a moral test; doctrinal truth is judged by revealed faith, not outcomes alone.\\
Wolf and Clothing & Chrysostom's \emph{Homily 23 on Matthew} reads the warning as directed especially toward corrupt lives masked by virtue. Clothing can be imitated; sustained action discloses the master actually being served.\\
Slavery & Paul's stark metaphor removes the fiction of neutral freedom. Chrysostom observes that sin yields shame and death, while God's service already bears holiness even before its promised end is fully seen.\\
Fire and Medicine & The Gospel's fire names the final seriousness of corrupt fruit; the Postcommunion's medicine names present mercy. Editorially, the Mass places judgment and cure together so that vigilance becomes conversion rather than self-righteous suspicion.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Patristic Exegesis of the Grouped Propers}
This Mass orders discernment under Christ's kingship and grace: God calls the nations, teaches his children, exposes false appearance, receives sacrifice through Christ's one perfect offering, and heals the communicant so that obedience can become visible fruit.

\begin{connectionstable}
Introit --- entrance & \latin{Omnes gentes} (Ps. 46:2--3) summons every nation before the great King. Editorially, the universal horizon prevents the later warning from shrinking Christian life into suspicious factionalism.\\
Collect --- petition & The Collect asks unfailing providence to remove all harmful things and grant what profits. Discernment begins as dependence upon God, not confidence in one's private power of detection.\\
Epistle --- apostolic diagnosis & Romans 6:19--23 contrasts masters, fruits, and ends. Chrysostom, \emph{Homily 12 on Romans}, emphasizes that holiness is already given as fruit, while eternal life is not wages owed for good works but God's gift in Christ.\\
Gradual --- true teaching & \latin{Venite, filii, audite me: timorem Domini docebo vos} and \latin{Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini} (Ps. 33:12, 6) answer false prophecy positively: come to the Lord, receive his teaching, and be enlightened. This chant is the Mass's central norm for teachability.\\
Alleluia --- royal acclamation & The Alleluia repeats \latin{Omnes gentes} (Ps. 46:2). Before the warning is proclaimed, the assembly acclaims the universal King's authority over every teacher and hearer.\\
Gospel --- disclosure and command & Matthew 7:15--21 moves from hidden wolves to visible fruit and finally to the Father's will. Augustine, \emph{Sermon on the Mount} II.24--25, distinguishes true fruit from show; Chrysostom, \emph{Homily 23}, targets corrupt life masked as virtue and allows that the sinner can change. Neither makes favorable outcomes alone a sufficient doctrinal test.\\
Offertory --- self-offering & \latin{Sicut in holocaustis} (Dan. 3:40) asks that a humbled people's offering be accepted. The one discerning others first stands under judgment and offers himself without display.\\
Secret --- Christ's one sacrifice & The Secret says that God perfected the diversity of legal victims in one sacrifice, then asks him to receive and sanctify the servants' offering as he blessed Abel's gifts. This is the altar's answer to counterfeit religion: not performance, but participation in Christ's perfect sacrifice for the salvation of all.\\
Communion --- urgent reception & \latin{Inclina aurem tuam, accelera, ut eripias me} (Ps. 30:3) keeps the communicant in the posture of need. Discernment has not made the soul its own healer; it receives deliverance.\\
Postcommunion --- continuing effect & \latin{Tua nos, Domine, medicinalis operatio} asks sacramental action to free the faithful from perversity and lead them to what is right. Grace changes the tree so that obedience and sanctification can become its fruit.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\textbf{Grouped instruction (editorial synthesis).} Praise the universal King, learn from the Lord before judging rival voices, distinguish moral fruit from the separate question of doctrinal truth, submit one's own offering to Christ's one sacrifice, and receive the medicine that alone can heal the root before final judgment.

\sectionguard
\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item ``A bad tree cannot bear good fruit'' describes the person while remaining in evil; Augustine and Chrysostom both preserve the possibility of conversion rather than weakening ``cannot'' to ``cannot reliably.''
\item Matthew's sequence ends with doing the Father's will. Religious speech, apparent success, and even striking works are not substitutes for obedient holiness.
\item Moral fruit can expose hypocrisy, but it is not an independent rule for deciding doctrine. The Gradual supplies the positive posture: approach the Lord, receive his teaching, and be enlightened.
\item The Secret makes Christ's one perfect sacrifice, rather than the observer's correctness, the center of the Mass's discernment.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Homiletic Synthesis}
\begin{connectionstable}
Proposition & Christian discernment is truthful only when the universal King teaches it, Christ's sacrifice humbles it, and sacramental medicine changes the judge into a tree that bears obedient fruit.\\
Movement I --- gift & God teaches his children, frees them from sin, gives holiness as present fruit, and promises eternal life as gift rather than wages.\\
Movement II --- resistance & Sheep's clothing, pious speech, and judgment of others can conceal a corrupt life; supposed neutrality hides the master actually being served.\\
Movement III --- sacramental conversion & The Offertory submits the discerner to judgment, the Secret joins the offering to Christ's one perfect sacrifice, and Communion receives deliverance and medicine for the root.\\
Final horizon & Test one influence and one's own response by obedience and holy fruit, verify doctrine by the Church's received faith, and accept present healing before every tree is finally disclosed.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Apply the Gospel first to the preacher and parish: where does religious appearance outrun obedience to the Father's will?
\item Test moral fruit patiently, but test teaching by Scripture read in the Church's apostolic faith rather than by popularity or apparent success.
\item Bring one entrenched habit to confession and Communion as a sick root needing medicine, not merely a bad appearance needing concealment.
\end{itemize}
