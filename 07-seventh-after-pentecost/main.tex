\weektitle{Seventh Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Septima Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: fruits, false prophets, and sanctification}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Omnes gentes, plaudite manibus} (Ps. 46)}{Universal kingship}{St. Augustine on Psalm 46; St. Leo the Great on the nations called to Christ}{The nations rejoice because Christ's rule exposes falsehood and gathers true worship.}
\properrow{Collect}{Remove harmful things; grant what profits}{Providence and discernment}{St. Ignatius of Loyola on discernment; St. Thomas Aquinas on prudence}{The prayer asks God to sort what the soul cannot safely sort alone.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:19-23}{From slavery to sin into sanctification}{St. John Chrysostom on Romans; St. Maximus on freedom in virtue}{Freedom is not neutrality; it is service to God bearing fruit unto eternal life.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 7:15-21}{False prophets known by fruits}{St. Augustine, Sermon on the Mount; St. Vincent of Lerins on catholic discernment}{Doctrine and life are tested by fruit. Words of piety without obedience do not enter the kingdom.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Sicut in holocaustis arietum...} (Dan. 3)}{Humble sacrifice accepted}{St. Cyprian on sacrifice and obedience}{True offering is not display but a contrite people asking to be received.}
\properrow{Communion}{Commonly linked with Psalmic trust in the Lord's goodness}{Fruit received as gift}{St. Teresa of Avila on deeds over consolations}{Communion strengthens the tree from within so that fruit is not merely performed but borne.}
\end{properstable}

\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Matthew 7 warns against false prophets and teaches that trees are known by their fruits; Romans 6 contrasts the wages of sin with the gift of eternal life.\\
Allegorical & The good tree is Christ and, by grace, the Church grafted into him; false prophets are wolves under religious appearance, threatening the flock.\\
Moral & The hearer must test teachers, habits, and his own life by fruit: obedience, humility, charity, chastity, truth, and perseverance.\\
Anagogical & The bad tree is cut down and cast into fire, while sanctification bears fruit unto eternal life. Discernment now prepares for judgment then.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Homily Sketch}
\textbf{Proposition.} The fruit reveals the tree, and the tree reveals its master. Christian freedom is not self-ownership but fruitful service unto sanctification.

\textbf{Opening movement.} The Introit summons all nations to clap and rejoice. Yet the Gospel immediately warns that not every religious voice belongs to the King. Universal praise requires real discernment.

\textbf{Doctrinal center.} Romans 6 explains the Gospel's fruit. Sin pays wages; God gives life. The Christian is not choosing between slavery and autonomy, but between slavery to sin and service to God.

\textbf{Moral turn.} ``Lord, Lord'' is not enough. Speech, charisma, and religious appearance must be tested by obedience to the Father's will.

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Matthew's image moves from wolves in sheep's clothing to trees and fruit. Hidden inward reality eventually becomes visible.
\item The Epistle's \latin{fructum vestrum in sanctificationem} gives the positive side: the goal is not suspicion but holiness.
\item The Offertory from Daniel places the true offering in humility, not performance.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. Augustine}{The fruits of the Spirit reveal the good tree.}{Use Augustine to distinguish impressive religious clothing from charity, joy, peace, and continence.}
\witnessrow{St. John Chrysostom}{A corrupt life can hide under virtuous appearance.}{This supports sober discernment without cynicism.}
\witnessrow{St. Vincent of Lerins}{Hold what has been believed everywhere, always, by all.}{Connect fruit with Catholic continuity in doctrine and life.}
\witnessrow{St. Ignatius of Loyola}{Discernment attends to where a spirit leads.}{The practical question: what fruit does this voice produce over time?}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Fruit and freedom & Romans explains Matthew: the fruit reveals the master. Sin pays wages in death; God gives life that becomes sanctification.\\
Discernment and obedience & False prophecy is unmasked not only by doctrinal slogans but by fruits measured against the Father's will. The collect asks for this purifying providence.\\
Universal praise and local testing & The Introit opens to all nations; the Gospel insists that catholic breadth does not mean naive acceptance of every voice.\\
Offering and humility & Daniel's Offertory turns discernment inward: the preacher and hearer must ask whether their own fruit is humble obedience.\\
Prayer focus & Test one habit, teacher, or influence by its fruit: does it make obedience, humility, charity, and truth easier or harder?
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Teach discernment without paranoia: the test is patient fruit, not instant suspicion.
\item Ask what repeated habits are producing: peace, chastity, truth, humility, and charity, or their opposites.
\item Apply the warning first to the preacher and parish, then to outside influences.
\end{itemize}
