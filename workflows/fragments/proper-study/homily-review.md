# Review the homily as speech and exegesis

Read the spoken body continuously as a listener would hear it, then check its
evidence and relation to the reviewed study. Require a clear exegetical claim,
meaningful use of appointed Scripture and prayer, an intelligible movement,
pertinent pedagogy, concrete pastoral fruit and hope. A fluent generic sermon
that could fit any Sunday fails. So does a lecture assembled from quotations.

Check adult-parish language, sentence length, oral transitions, recoverable
images and precise theological claims. The body must be speakable verbatim
with no editorial instructions or dependence on visible footnotes. Examine
the actual spoken word count and estimated pace for the default 10–12-minute
scope; never certify a timed human performance from a written estimate.
Check the source-and-delivery note for exact loci and truthful review facts.

Refuse invented anecdotes, personal experience, clerical identity, unsupported
attribution, hidden disagreement, or a newly composed liturgical prayer.
Use `HOM-` findings with `research`, `study`, `synthesis`, or `homily` as the repair owner.
