## Concise argument and comparison

The first recoverable interpretation concerns witness through affliction. Chrysostom begins with the disciples' discouragement after the sower's losses and the enemy's weeds. Christ makes their apparently inadequate presence fruitful through contact with those around them. Aquinas explains the power of preaching through both extraordinary gifts and virtuous conduct, Spirit-prompted speech, and adequate teaching. The Thessalonians receive the word, endure, and become an example. The prayers seek cleansing, right action, protection, and continued desire for a community whose conduct affects others. Resurrection and deliverance with Christ remain the final end.

The second concerns the whole person's conversion. Jerome contrasts apparently unimpressive preaching about a crucified God with philosophical eloquence; faith nourishes the seed into understanding. His accounts of the leaven bring spirit, soul, and body, or reason, anger, and desire, into agreement. Augustine's leaven as love explains the affection that rouses someone to pursue the good understood. Penitence, forgiveness, the Secret's governance, and the Postcommunion's desire concern conversion that continues after reception. Augustine's explanation of the risen Head and his members gives this interpretation its bodily and final horizon.

These answers are intelligible from the concise prose alone. They meet within the developed commentary's questions rather than appearing as consecutive abbreviated essays.
