## Consequential-difficulty tests

1. **Refusal of oil:** at `sections/30-mercy.tex:14–20`, the prose poses the real objection to oil as mercy: the supposedly generous virgins refuse an urgent, limited request. Chrysostom, Matthew 78.1–2, explicitly distinguishes unwillingness from inability at the final encounter and invokes the rich man/Lazarus separation, then identifies the poor as sellers to be approached during life. The study teaches that inference and its temporal premise instead of merely announcing that preparation matters. Outcome: supported and intelligible. Its subsequent warning against refusing help to a living dying person is consistent with the premise.
2. **All sleep:** at `sections/40-charity-within.tex:38` and `sections/10-treatment.tex:39`, the prose explains Augustine's objection to interpreting sleep as cooled charity: the wise also sleep and nevertheless enter. Sermon 93 §§6–9 instead identifies the common sleep with death. Outcome: the exact inference is supported and taught.
3. **Visible good works without inward charity:** at `sections/40-charity-within.tex:3–22`, Augustine §§2–5, 9–14 and Gregory 12.1–3 support the distinction between good deeds, their motive, external approval and conscience. The study explains why deeds must continue, why praise cannot supply charity, and why inward testimony does not authorize self-acquittal. The differing sellers in Chrysostom and Augustine/Gregory remain distinct. Outcome: supported, substantive and not a repetition of the mercy interpretation.
