Verdict

There is a defensible quality complaint, most clearly in the matched
Seventeenth Sunday pair: GPT's interpretation choices are narrower, its
condensation more repetitive, and its homily less textually surprising than
Claude's. The strongest explanation supported by the files is selection and
development of evidence, followed by derivative prose preserving that selection.
The evidence does NOT establish a general model regression or that everything
GPT recently wrote is weak. GPT's Nineteenth Sunday study provides important
counterexamples: it reconstructs arguments rather than merely naming them.
One current mechanical homily defect and one repaired historical discovery
defect are real; neither should become a universal explanation of style.

Ranked findings

1. HIGH — The clearest loss is a narrower theological question, before prose
   compression begins. [Demonstrated artifact difference; causal inference
   limited to the recorded evidence map and its derivative documents.]

G57 sections/30-healed-charity.tex, 40-one-people.tex and
50-faithful-offering.tex ask how the heart is healed, how a community bears
its members, and how worship becomes fidelity. These are valid complementary
readings, but they repeatedly converge on grace-enabled patient conduct.
G57 sections/synthesis/10-study.tex:8,14,16,24,28,44,56,66,74–76 repeatedly
recasts the same material under the three labels. The Gospel's difficult
second half receives its divine-lordship conclusion, then is quickly applied
to those preselected concerns (especially :14–16).

C57 sections/30-son-and-lord-of-david.tex:23–91 instead works through the
actual question: how David's descendant can be his Lord. Augustine's form of
God/form of servant, Jerome's eternal generation, Chrysostom's indirect
pedagogy, and Aquinas's three dignities are distinguished, then connected to
Eph 4:6's different expositions (:93–153) and the Word/Spirit of Ps 32:6
(:155ff). This is more than extra material: it gives the second half of the
Gospel intellectual consequence and makes this reading genuinely different
from the moral/ecclesial one.

The narrower inputs are visible in G57 research/scope.md:34–40,47–63,65–71.
Thomas on Ephesians is checked, but direct Thomas Gospel exposition and
additional ancient Gospel witnesses are not adopted; Theodoret is an
unverified lead; Basil is a secondary locator; the psalm sweep principally
uses Augustine's abridged historical English and Bellarmine. C57 develops
additional directly attributed argument in the passages above. This does
not prove every extra author was necessary or that G57 dishonestly ignored
evidence. It does explain why the long study gives the later writer a less
varied theological repertoire. Even within G57's admitted evidence, Thomas's
Trinitarian appropriation is acknowledged in scope.md:59 but barely developed
in the synthesis; source selection alone is therefore not the whole cause.

Action: have research identify the actual exegetical questions and strongest
apparent difficulty of the appointed units before choosing interpretations.
At review, test whether each reading produces a different inference or
consequence and whether the hardest material has been reduced to a quick
conclusion. Do not require a doctrinal lane, extra authors, or length by quota.

2. HIGH — The homilies differ in discovery and listener movement, not merely
   polish. [Textual comparison, with an explicit editorial preference.]

G57 sections/homily/10-homily.tex:1–17 opens with a hurtful reply and treats
love, patience and correction. It is understandable, concrete, and pastoral.
Only at :23–25 does it arrive at David's son/Lord question, supplies the
answer, and returns to grace for moral action. Its conclusion (:31–37)
successfully returns to the next sentence. The issue is that the scriptural
difficulty supplies support for a familiar practical problem more than it
changes the listener's initial understanding.

C57 sections/homily/10-homily.tex:12–24 opens with the three questions and
the silence left by the last. At :50–67 the apparent change from love to an
old enthronement psalm becomes the doctrinal turn: the one whom the command
requires us to love is standing there asking the question. Later the Creed
and altar answer the initial silence. A hearer discovers why both halves
belong together before receiving the practical response. The concrete parish
examples (:83–89) grow from that movement.

C58 sections/homily/10-homily.tex:15–22 similarly begins with the man who
never speaks; :51–69 preserves the witnesses' disagreement over whose faith;
:73–96 develops the visible sign of invisible forgiveness and ministerial
power; the final command reverses being carried into carrying. It shows that
adult oral clarity can retain a genuine exegetical difficulty. However its
recurring silence/"keep him in view" device is itself a recognizable template;
Claude is not free of formula. Its greater density and quotational register
are also a reader preference, not an automatic merit for every audience.

G59's opening is better anchored in its own parable, and its eye/foot image
explains Chrysostom lucidly. Nevertheless its latter movement reproduces much
of G57's pattern: demanding charity, prayer for enabling grace, Eucharistic
healing, one concrete act, eternal hope (G59 sections/homily/10-body.tex:
81–134; compare G57 :17–37). These are appropriate themes for both formularies;
the recurrence is evidence of a limited rhetorical range, not plagiarism or
proof that the sermons could literally fit any Sunday.

Action: in the homily audit identify the textual question/image, the
exegetical turn that changes its meaning, and the response that follows.
Review how the listener is carried through these steps. Prefer a fresh
argument from the Sunday over merely inserting its proper nouns into a
familiar moral arc. Do not require every literary technique or prohibit an
ordinary-life opening when it genuinely serves the text.

3. MEDIUM — Compression sometimes removes the mechanism while expansion
   restores repeated applications instead. [Demonstrated passages; motive
   or token-saving intent is not established.]

Compare G57 sections/synthesis/10-study.tex:10 with C57
sections/concise/10-commentary.tex:11–55. GPT gives sound conclusions about
love and obedience. Claude explains four distinct relations: Augustine's
single current and ordered love, Gregory's measure/measurelessness,
Chrysostom's reciprocal dependence, Aquinas's image of God. The last paragraph
then compares the actual answers. Names and extra quotations alone would not
do this; the value is the explicit connecting reasoning.

G57's current concise commentary also repeats: :12 and :18 revisit the
questioner's motives; :42 and :48 repeat created/spiritual heavens; :38 and
:50 revisit Spirit-given union versus Thomas's city; :62–68 repeatedly
restate common vows and humility. Some restatement deepens the point, but
the proportion spent reassigning a paragraph to all three readings reduces
space for new reasoning. C57's longer treatment is not automatically preferable,
but its comparison more often juxtaposes different source arguments directly.

The history gives a bounded explanation: G57 began under v1 with a five-page,
1,686-word concise work, then reached a ten-page, 3,975-word concise argument
after pagination recovery. G57 research/production-review.md:1114–1118 and
1169–1194 explicitly distinguish these states. The current source is not a
five-page abstract, and there is no evidence of an actual generation token
cap. Historical compression plus later expansion is demonstrated; that this
caused every repetition is an inference, not an established operation log.

Action: condensation should preserve premise -> interpretive move ->
consequence, pruning repeated triadic labels. If an explanation needs more
room, add what the reader needs to understand why the source concludes it,
not another restatement of its application.

5. MEDIUM — Psalm dossiers often answer the composition-bound question while
   leaving the reader's historical-event question thin. [Visible limitation,
   not a claim that a false numerical date was printed.]

G59 sections/concise/03-chronology.tex:4–24 supplies narrative summaries for
Ps 77 and 104, but the generated date cells are solely the broad Psalter
composition bound: research/chronology-annotations.tex:29,33 and the other
psalm groups. "Covenant, Joseph, Exodus and land" (:9–12) names remembered
history without locating those events. G57's Psalm 32/75/101/118 dossiers
likewise repeatedly state a general bound and absence of an individual date
(sections/concise/03-date-location.tex:14–49). Those caveats are truthful but
do little to orient a reader historically.

This is not simply a GPT failure: C58's Psalm 101 and 121 dossiers also end
with the broad critical Psalter boundary (sections/concise/03-date-location.tex:
41–58), even while they supply Sion/exile context. C58's Psalm 95 dossier
(:28–39), by contrast, separately labels traditional setting, composition,
and prophetic referent; that is the useful model of distinct relations.

Action: use shared chronology records to separate a traditional attributed
frame, an actually identified narrated/retrospective event, composition or
formation, and a prophetic referent. Carry event claims only at their supported
locus reach. An entire psalm's historical recital must not turn every opening
verse into a dated Exodus event, and a Davidic title must not falsely date every
poem to David's reign. When only a contextual historical anchor is supportable,
label it as such rather than replacing the truthful composition bound.

The reviews nonetheless accepted the narrower exposition: G57

not a problem solved by counting sources, pages, rhetorical figures, or lanes.

Counterevidence that must survive the diagnosis

* G59 is not an empty or merely moral study. sections/30-clothed-charity.tex:
  14–39 reconstructs Augustine's elimination of goods shared by good and bad;
  :63–90 reconstructs Gregory's Bridegroom/Incarnation and weaving argument;
  :160–177 distinguishes imperfect charity from total absence. Its concise
  10-commentary.tex:6–34 and :66–75 explicitly compare different inferential
  routes. This is the very practice the upcoming study should retain.
* G57 preserves meaningful distinctions: charitable correction versus
  negligent agreement (homily:13), sacramental preservation versus automatic
  perseverance (:29), different accounts of Daniel's representative confession
  (synthesis/10-study.tex:46), and public versus inward judgment (:64–68).
* Claude's stronger finished arguments are products of correction, not proof
  of effortless initial superiority. C58 CYCLES.md:32–41 lists invented
  conflicts, misattributions, and a grace error found during the run. C57's
  generation-metadata.tex author-study entry records eight study iterations
  and corrections to Chrysostom's reciprocal love argument and its Psalm
  citation. The latest artifacts should be judged as finished artifacts while
  those differing revision histories remain causal confounders.
* The long/short studies' recorded extents differ substantially (G57 roughly
  7,443 study words and 22 pages; C57 13,918 and historically 38 pages), but the
  better examples above depend on reasoning, not on that ratio. Homilies are
  relatively similar in spoken extent (G57 1,283; C57 1,429; G59 1,301), so
  sheer available time does not account for the craft difference.

Upcoming-packet recommendations, in priority order

1. Repair the contradictory homily coverage gate before pinning the new run.
2. In the research interpretation record make each reading answer an actual
   exegetical question, show its decisive source inference, and face its
   strongest textual difficulty. Use existing containment/formulary discovery
   for material gaps; do not inflate a bibliography to meet a count.
3. Have the study reviewer inspect what was selected and what that selection
   leaves thin, with particular attention to the Gospel's hard or apparently
   disconnected part. Require reasoning where a doctrinal conclusion is now
   merely stated; preserve complementary readings without inventing disputes.
4. Derive the concise study by comparing arguments, retaining each mechanism
   and consequential qualification. Remove repeated three-way applications
   when they add no new conclusion.
5. Derive the homily around a specific textual movement. The listener should
   arrive somewhere that the opening has not already resolved. Make pastoral
   response follow that discovery and its liturgical fulfillment.
6. Let the corrected chronology corpus supply honest historical relations;
   do not make publication-local dates or substitute a general composition
   bound for a sourced narrated setting.

These are bounded changes to craft and review discrimination, not a request
to copy Claude's prose, force every study longer, or replace source discipline
with rhetorical confidence. No claim is made about hidden model settings,
token pressure, worker effort beyond recorded declarations, or unobserved
author intentions.
