{p1 mattins-heading} MATTINS, OR MORNING PRAYER.
{p1 mattins-note-1} The proper hour for Mattins is midnight, at which time it is said in many Convents.
{p1 mattins-note-2} In others it is said at 2 or 5 A.M.
{p1 mattins-note-3} In the Cathedral of Rome (St. John Lateran’s) and other Churches of the same country, the hour is about 7 A.M.
{p1 mattins-note-4} It is allowable to say it at any hour after the sun has begun to decline, and an ordinary practice is to do so late in the afternoon.
{p22 lauds-heading} LAUDS, OR THE MORNING PRAISES OF GOD.
{p22 lauds-note-1} The proper hour for Lauds is the dawn of day.
{p22 lauds-note-2} This is reckoned to be about 3 A.M., at which time this Office is said in many Convents.
{p22 lauds-note-3} For this purpose it is, in choirs, invariably (except where it forms part of the same service with the Midnight Mass at Christmas) said immediately after and as one service with Mattins.
{p22 lauds-note-4} Hence it follows 1st, that it is said late in the afternoon, when Mattins are said at that time, and 2ndly, that the Lord’s Prayer and Angelic Salutation are not said at the beginning.
{p22 lauds-note-5} This service is constructed on the same general principle as Vespers, and answers to that Office as Prime does to Compline.
{p35 prime-heading} PRIME, OR THE FIRST HOUR.
{p35 prime-note-1} Prime is the first service of the Church for the day-time, Mattins and Lauds being for the middle and close of night.
{p35 prime-note-2} Its proper hour is when the sun has fairly risen, and day begun, which is reckoned to be about 6 A.M., about which time it is generally said in choirs.
{p35 prime-note-3} Sometimes Mattins, Lauds, and Prime are said together early in the morning, forming the complete morning service of the Church.
{p35 prime-note-4} It is from this aggregation that the “Morning Prayer” of the Anglican Prayer Book is derived.
{p66 none-heading} NONE, OR THE NINTH HOUR.
{p66 none-note-1} The proper hour for None is 3 P.M., but in Choirs it varies.
{p176 vespers-heading} VESPERS, OR EVENSONG.
{p176 vespers-note-1} The proper hour for Vespers is sunset, reckoned to be about 6 P.M.
{p176 vespers-note-2} In private recitation, they ought not usually to be begun before noon.
{p205 compline-heading} COMPLINE.
{p205 compline-note-1} Compline (Completorium) is the last office of the Church, and is proper to the end of the evening before going to bed, reckoned to be about 9 p.m., but it may be said any time before midnight.
{p205 compline-note-2} It is very frequently recited along with Vespers, thus forming the complete Evening Service of the Church, and it is from this aggregation that the “Evening Prayer” of the Anglican Prayer Book is derived.
