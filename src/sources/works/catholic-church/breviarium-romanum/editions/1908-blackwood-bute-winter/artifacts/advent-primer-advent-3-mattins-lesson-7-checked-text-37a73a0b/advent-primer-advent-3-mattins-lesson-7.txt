{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} Seventh Lesson.
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} The Lesson is taken from the Holy Gospel according to John (i. 19.)
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} AT that time: The Jews sent Priests and Levites from Jerusalem to John to ask him: Who art thou?
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} And so on.
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} Homily by Pope St Gregory [the Great,] (7th on the Gospels.)
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} Dearly beloved brethren, the first thing which striketh us in to-day’s Gospel is the lowly-mindedness of John.
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} He was so great that it was thought he might be the Christ; yet he soberly chose rather to seem only what he really was, than to let the belief of men invest him with a dignity which did not belong to him; for “he confessed, and denied not, but confessed, I am not the Christ,” at the same time he would not deny what he was in reality; and thus his very truth-speaking made him a member of Him Whose title he would not by falsehood take.
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} In that he arrogated not to himself the name of Christ, he became a member of Christ.
{p241 adv3-mattins-l7} While he humbly strove to confess his own weakness, he earned by his simplicity a part in the grandeur of his Master.
