During the procession, the hymn Pange lingua, gloriosi Corporis mysterium is sung as far as the words Tantum ergo; if needed, the same hymn is repeated.

Tongue, proclaim the mystery
of the glorious Body
and of the precious Blood
which, as the ransom of the world,
the King of the nations,
the fruit of a noble womb, poured out.

Given to us, born for us
from an undefiled Virgin,
and having lived in the world,
after sowing the seed of the word,
he brought his time of dwelling here
to a close in a wondrous order.

During the night of the final supper,
reclining among his brothers,
having fully observed the law
in the foods that the law required,
he gives himself with his own hands
as food for the company of twelve.

The incarnate Word makes real bread
into flesh by his word;
the pure wine becomes Christ's Blood.
If the senses fall short,
faith alone is sufficient
to make the sincere heart firm.

Therefore, let us bow in reverence
before so great a Sacrament.
Let the ancient observance
yield to the new rite;
may faith supply
what our senses lack.

To the Father and to the Son
be praise and joyful acclaim,
salvation, honor, power,
and blessing as well;
to the One who proceeds from both
let equal praise be given.
Amen.
