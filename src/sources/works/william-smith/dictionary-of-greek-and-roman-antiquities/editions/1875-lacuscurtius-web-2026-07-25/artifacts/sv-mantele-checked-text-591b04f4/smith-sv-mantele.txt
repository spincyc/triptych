{Mantele} The circumstance, that forks were not invented in ancient times, gave occasion to the use of napkins at meals to wipe the fingers; also when the meal was finished, and even before it commenced, an apparatus was carried around for washing the hands.
{Mantele} The boy or slave who poured out the water, also held the napkin or towel for wiping the hands dry.
{Mantele} The word mappa, said to be of Carthaginian origin, denoted a smaller kind of napkin, or a handkerchief, which the guests carried with them to the table. The mantele, as it was larger than the mappa, was sometimes used as a table-cloth.
