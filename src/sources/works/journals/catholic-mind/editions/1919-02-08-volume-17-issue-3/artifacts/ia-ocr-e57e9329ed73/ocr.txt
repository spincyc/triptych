A Pastoral Letter to the Clergy and Faithful 
of the Savannah Diocese. 


HE approach of the holy season of Advent with all 
its beautiful lessons reminding us of the necessity 


of making our souls a fit dwelling place for the Prince 
of Peace, whose temporal advent is so soon to be cele- 
brated, suggests to me the propriety and necessity of 
correcting the many misstatements which were so per- 
sistently made during the course of the war about the 
attitude and actions of the one whom Catholics recognize 
and revere as the personal representative on earth of 
the Prince of Peace, our Holy Father, the Pope. 

Every day at Mass the priests of the diocese beg the 
Good Shepherd that He would look down in mercy on 
His servant, Benedict XV, whom He has appointed to 
rule over His Church, and grant that both by word and 
example he may edify all those who are under his charge, 
so that with the flock committed to his keeping he may 
at last arrive at everlasting life. To him in the person 
of Blessed Peter did the Lord give the charge and guid- 
ance of all who belong to the Flock of Christ on earth, 
and his honor is our honor and what reflects on him 
deeply concerns each one of us. These incorrect and 
misleading statements have not only prejudiced the minds 
of many of our non-Catholic citizens, but have deeply 
wounded us; and hence I have concluded to notice them 
in this pastoral letter. 

Although the-signing of the recent armistice is by no 
means the last scene in the awful tragedy of this world 
war, yet we may be well assured that peace has come. 
The war, its conduct, its successes and its failures will 
for many years be the subject of dispute and discussion, 
and while there may be honest differences of opinion 
regarding the compelling motives and actual causes of 
the struggle, yet I am sure that with us in the United

I freely confess that I have not always been able to

court representing all the nations must be created with 
power to compel the submission of disputed questions and 
enforce compliance with its decisions, and that Germany 
must be a member of it. 

One of the happy results which I fondly anticipated

the necessity for harmony and united effort which was so 
imperatively demanded by the war would have had the 
effect of causing writers and speakers to exercise a little 
care to avoid wounding the feelings of Catholics in this 
country. I sincerely regret to say that my hopes in this 
matter have not been realized.

It is not my purpose in this letter to take up and 
refute all the absurd and malicious charges which have 
been made against the Church and her creed. I pro- 
pose to confine it to a defense of the conduct of the 
Pope during the war. The charges to which I refer

against the horrible conduct of the Germans in Bel-

Before entering upon a denial of these charges, for 
all of them are false, it may not be without service to 
say a word about the position of the Pope. It is one 
of the doctrines of the Catholic Church that the Pope 
is the successor of St. Peter and the Vicar on earth of 
Our Lord and Saviour, Jesus Christ; that he is the 
visible head on earth of the Catholic Church, and as 
that Church numbers among her children more Chris-

the Pope is the Supreme Spiritual Head cf the oldest 
and largest Christian Church. This fact might have sug- 
gested to the critics the propriety of abstaining from 
wounding the feelings of Catholics by these public at- 
tacks on their religious belief. 

I might here add that among the subjects of the Cen- 
tral Empires (Germany and Austria) the Pope counts 
57,000,000 Catholics, and among the Allied Powers 
there are found 111,000,000 Catholics; which fact might 
suggest the possibility that the sympathies of the Pope 
might, with considerable reason, be expected to incline 
to the Allies rather than the Germans. 

The peoples of the Allied countries and Central Em- 
dires have their national aspirations, views and ideas, 
which are sure sooner or later to come into conflict. 
Could the Pope reasonably be expected to take a de- 
cided stand when such a clash comes, and thus involve 
himself in the turmoil and troubled sea of political ac- 
tivity? The only lamp for his feet and the only guide 
for his conduct are the everlasting Law of God and the 
Divine teaching of the Gospel of Christ: His only aim, 
to safeguard and defend the purity of faith, and to pro- 
vide for the salvation of the flock of Christ, committed 
by Him to his care and guidance.

was recognized as the final arbiter, and men looked to 
him to defend the weak and restrain the strong. At

THE Pope’s PEACE EFForTs. 


His very first message to the world after his election 

was a pleading letter to all the warring nations begging 
them in the name of God and for the sake of humanity 
to put an end to the war and bring peace to the world. 
te strove to induce them to proclaim a truce during 
the days when the whole Christian world was celebrat- 
ing the birth of the Prince of Peace. In January, 1916, 
he again sent a letter imploring men to cease warring and 
restore peace. In February he ordered a day of prayer 
to be observed throughout the entire world on which 
God’s mercy was implored and He was entreated to 
bring peace. In May he ordered a fast of three days 
for the same purpose, and there has scarcely passed a 
month since the war commenced that the Pope has not 
made every endeavor to put an end to it. 

But the Pope did not confine his activities to these 
means. His heart was touched by all the horrors and 
miseries of this dreadful struggle, and in Decembe:, 
1914, he communicated with all the belligerents and 
begged them to enter into an agreement to exchange all 
prisoners who were unfit for further military duty, and 
as the suggestion of the Pope was favorably received, 
thousands of permanently disabled soldiers were. thus 
exchanged. Then the Pope proposed that soldiers who 
were not permanently disabled, but were unfit for service, 
might be interned in Switzerland and Holland. I have 
read official statements to the effect that about 10,000 
French soldiers were thus interned. He also made an

GERMANY’s War ON CATHOLICISM. 









Nearly fifty years ago the newly established German 
Empire entered upon a struggle with the Catholic 
Church. The State found the Lutheran clergy ready to 
accept the supreme authority of the Kaiser, and in 1871 
Bismarck entered on a campaign against the Catholic 
Church in Prussia, which was afterwards waged in 
Baden, Hesse and Bavaria. The German Empire had 
just been established and Bismarck was engaged in ef- 
forts to consolidate it and enlarge its powers. As a 
Conservative he had fought the Liberal party, but now 
he found a strong support in the Reichstag among men 
of different parties, and the only opposition to his cen- 
tralizing and despotic rule came from Windthorst and 
the Centrist party. I do not think there can be any’ doubt 
that Bismarck was possessed with the idea of establish- 
ing a great Protestant empire in the very home and

center of the Reformation, and I believe that he was 
convinced that the so-called “old Catholic party” would 
greatly aid him by forming a National German Catholic 
Church, which he could make use of against the Center 
party and the Pope as well. 

One of his first acts in the contest was to abolish 
the Catholic Section of the Department of Worship and 
turn over its functions to a board, the majority of whose 
members were Protestants. He then expelled the Jesuits, 
Redemptorists, Lazarists, Fathers of the Hoiy Ghost and 
Sisters of the Sacred Heart from Germany. He tried 
by legislative enactments to make the Bishops of Prussia 
independent of the Pope, and the priests independent of 
the Bishops, and all of them entirely subservient in all 
things to the State. When any member of the Catholic 
clergy was appointed to a position it was required: 
(1) He must be a German; (2) He must have received 
his education in a German college and must submit to a 
State examination in philosophy, history, classics and 
German literature; (3) He must have his name presented 
to the President of the Province in which he is to offi- 
ciate; (4) He must await the approval of the civil au- 
thorities before entering upon the duties of his office. 
The seminaries in which such of the clergy were edu- 
cated must be under the control of the State, and the 
colleges must be closed. As the higher schools of learn- 
ing in Germany were almost every one under the control

yet it will hardly be credited that the subserviency to

lation would have been to compel the Catholic Church to 
accept as the teachers of her clergy men who were ra- 
tionalists or agnostics. The Government then impris- 
oned Bishops and priests for not obeying this iniquitous 
legislation, and tried to depose Bishops and decreed that 
only those who were in favor of the law could be their

direct influence and defeatist policy of the Vatican. The 
insinuation was believed by none save those who had a 
direct object to achieve in spreading this malicious false- 
hood. Nevertheless, it was spread and spread assidu-

Benedict XV and the Peace Conference 


AN ENcYCLICAL LETTER. 


ENERABL E Brothers, Health and Apostolic Bene- 
diction: That which the entire world has so long 
sighed for, what Christianity implored with so much 
fervent prayer, and what We, the interpreter of the sor- 


row of all, with the heart of a father, continually kept 
asking for, has come in a moment. The clang of arms 
has ceased at last. It is true that peace has not yet for- 
mally put an end to the war. However, with the armis- 
tice, which has meantime suspended carnage and devas- 
tation by land, sea and air, the road to peace is for- 
tunately clear. 

In order to explain-such a sudden event several causes 
might be adduced. But if we want to seek the principal 
cause, we absolutely must look to Him who governs all 
occurrences, who led to mercy by the continual prayers 
of the good, granted humanity to withdraw from so 
many struggles and so many causes of anguish. There- 
fore, while giving thanks to the goodness of the Lord, 
we rejoice at the many imposing demonstrations of piety 
that have been held throughout the Catholic world for 
the purpose. 

Now it remains to us to beg of Divine Clemency that 
the great favor granted to us may have. its crowning 
success. Within a short time the delegates of the several 
nations will unite in solemn congress for the purpose 
of giving to the world a just and lasting peace. They 
will accordingly have to come to decisions of such grave

importance and of so complex a nature as were never 
before taken by any human assembly. 

It is then not necessary to point out how imperative 
it is that they receive light from above in order to exe-

tion of decisions that concern the weal of all humanity 
to the highest degree, without any doubt all Catholics,
