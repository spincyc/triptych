{notice} By a Novena we mean the nine days that precede a feast; the first day of the novena of Christmas is, therefore, the 16th of December.
{notice} These discourses may serve either for meditation or for spiritual reading. After the discourses will be found novenas of meditations and of prayers. There is also added a list of the indulgences attached to this exercise.—Ed.

{advent} Meditations for Every Day of Advent.
{advent.1} MEDITATION I.
{advent.1} First Sunday.
{advent.1} Goodness of God in the Work of the Redemption.
{advent.1} Consider that God, having created the first man, in order that he might serve him and love him in this life, and be conducted afterwards to reign with him forever in Paradise, enriched him for this end with knowledge and grace.
{advent.1} But ungrateful man rebelled against God, refusing him the obedience which he owed him in justice and gratitude; and thus, miserable sinner, was he left with all his posterity as a rebel, deprived of divine grace, and forever excluded from paradise.
{advent.2} MEDITATION II.
{advent.2} First Monday.
{advent.2} Grandeur of the Mystery of Incarnation.
{advent.2} Our Lord sent St. Augustine to write upon the heart of St. Mary Magdalene of Pazzi the words, And the Word was made flesh.
{advent.3} MEDITATION III.
{advent.3} First Tuesday.
{advent.3} The Love of God for Men.
{advent.3} Consider that the eternal Father, in giving us his Son for a Redeemer, for victim and price of our ransom, could not have given us stronger motives for hope and love, to inspire us with confidence, and to oblige us to love him.
{advent.4} MEDITATION IV.
{advent.4} First Wednesday.
{advent.4} The Word was made Man in the Fulness of Time.
{advent.4} Consider that God allowed four thousand years to pass, after the transgression of Adam, before he sent his Son upon earth to redeem the world.
{advent.5} MEDITATION V.
{advent.5} First Thursday.
{advent.5} The Abasement of Jesus.
{advent.5} The eternal Word descends on earth to save man; and whence does he descend? His going out is from the end of heaven.
{advent.6} MEDITATION VI.
{advent.6} First Friday.
{advent.6} Jesus enlightens the World and glorifies God.
{advent.6} Before the coming of the Messias the world was buried in a dark night of ignorance and sins.
{advent.7} MEDITATION VII.
{advent.7} First Saturday.
{advent.7} The Son of God was laden with all our Iniquities.
{advent.7} Consider the humble state to which the Son of God chose to abase himself; he not only vouchsafed to take upon him the form of a servant, but that of a sinful servant: In the likeness of sinful flesh.
{advent.8} MEDITATION VIII.
{advent.8} Second Sunday.
{advent.8} God Sends His Son to die in order to restore us to Life.
{advent.8} Consider that sin is the death of the soul; because this enemy of God deprives us of divine grace, which is the life of the soul.
{advent.9} MEDITATION IX.
{advent.9} Second Monday.
{advent.9} The Love that the Son of God has shown us in the Redemption.
{advent.9} Consider that the eternal Word is that God who is so infinitely happy in himself that his happiness cannot be greater than it is, nor could the salvation of all mankind have added anything to it or have diminished it;
{advent.10} MEDITATION X.
{advent.10} Second Tuesday.
{advent.10} Jesus, the Man of Sorrows, from the Womb of His Mother.
{advent.10} Thus does the prophet Isaias designate our Lord Jesus Christ "the man of sorrows;" yes, because this man was created on purpose to suffer, and from his infancy began to endure the greatest sorrows that any man ever suffered.
{advent.11} MEDITATION XI.
{advent.11} Second Wednesday.
{advent.11} Jesus charged with the Sins of the Whole World.
{advent.11} Consider that the divine Word, in becoming man, chose not only to take the form of a sinner, but also to bear all the sins of men, and to satisfy for them as if they were his own: He bore their iniquities.
{advent.12} MEDITATION XII.
{advent.12} Second Thursday.
{advent.12} Jesus suffers during His Whole Life.
{advent.12} Consider that all the sufferings and ignominy that Jesus endured in his life and death, all were present to him from the first moment of his life: My sorrow is continually before me; and even from his childhood he began to offer them in satisfaction for our sins, beginning even then to fulfil his office of Redeemer.
{advent.13} MEDITATION XIII.
{advent.13} Second Friday.
{advent.13} Jesus wished to suffer so much in order to gain our Hearts.
{advent.13} Consider that Jesus suffered, even from the first moment of his life, and all for the love of us.
{advent.14} MEDITATION XIV.*
{advent.14} Second Saturday.
{advent.14} The Greatest Sorrow of Jesus.
{advent.14} Jesus Christ revealed to the Venerable Agatha of the Cross that whilst he was in his Mother's womb, that which afflicted him more than any other sorrow was the hardness of the hearts of men, who should, after his Redemption, despise the graces which he came into the world to diffuse.
{advent.14.footnote} * On December 16 we begin the Novena, page 214.
{advent.15} MEDITATION XV.
{advent.15} Second Sunday.
{advent.15} The Poverty of the Infant Jesus.
{advent.15} The Holy Church, in contemplating this great mystery and prodigy of a God being born in a stable, exclaims, full of admiration, "O great mystery! O wonderful sacrament! for animals to behold the Lord lying in a manger."
{advent.16} MEDITATION XVI.
{advent.16} Third Monday.
{advent.16} Jesus is the Fountain of Grace.
{advent.16} Consider the four fountains of grace that we have in Jesus Christ, as contemplated by St. Bernard.
{advent.17} MEDITATION XVII.
{advent.17} Third Tuesday.
{advent.17} Jesus the Charitable Physician of our Souls.
{advent.17} Your physician will come, says the prophet, to cure the infirm; and he will come swiftly like the bird that flies, and like the sun, which on rising from the horizon, instantly sends its light to the other pole.
{advent.18} MEDITATION XVIII.
{advent.18} Third Wednesday.
{advent.18} We should hope all Things from the Merits of Jesus Christ.
{advent.18} Consider that, since the Eternal Father has given us his own Son to be our mediator and advocate with him, and the victim in satisfaction for our sins, we cannot despair of obtaining from God whatever favor we ask of him, if we avail ourselves of the help of such a Redeemer.
{novena} Meditations for the Novena for Christmas.*
{novena.footnote} * Further on, page 300, we shall find another Novena of meditations with the chaplet to be recited before every meditation.
{novena.1} MEDITATION I.
{novena.1} December 16.
{novena.1} God has given Us his only Son to save Us.
{novena.1} Consider that the Eternal Father addressed these words to the Infant Jesus at the instant of his conception: I have given Thee to be the light of the Gentiles, that Thou mayest be My salvation.
{novena.2} MEDITATION II.
{novena.2} December 17.
{novena.2} Bitterness of the Heart of Jesus in the Womb of his Mother.
{novena.2} Consider the great bitterness with which the heart of the Infant Jesus must have felt itself afflicted and oppressed in the womb of Mary at the first moment when his Father proposed to his consideration all the series of contempt, sorrow, and agonies which he was to suffer during his life, to deliver men from their miseries: In the morning He wakeneth my ear, and I do not resist; I have given my body to the strikers.
{novena.3} MEDITATION III.
{novena.3} December 18.
{novena.3} Jesus made Himself a Child to gain our Confidence and our Love.
{novena.3} Consider that after so many centuries, after so many prayers and sighs, the Messias, whom the holy patriarchs and prophets were not worthy to see, whom the nations sighed for, "the Desire of the eternal hills," our Saviour, is come;
{novena.4} MEDITATION IV.
{novena.4} December 19.
{novena.4} The Passion of Jesus lasted during His Whole Life.
{novena.4} Consider that in the first moment that the soul of Jesus Christ was created and united to his little body in the womb of Mary, the Eternal Father intimated to his Son his will that he should die for the redemption of the world;
{novena.5} MEDITATION V.
{novena.5} December 20.
{novena.5} Jesus Offered Himself for our Salvation from the Beginning.
{novena.5} The divine Word, from the first instant that he was made man and an infant in Mary's womb, offered himself of his own accord to suffer and to die for the ransom of the world: He was offered because it was His own will.
{novena.6} MEDITATION VI.
{novena.6} December 21.
{novena.6} Jesus a Prisoner in the Womb of Mary.
{novena.6} Consider the painful life that Jesus Christ led in the womb of his Mother, and the long-confined and dark imprisonment that he suffered there for nine months.
{novena.7} MEDITATION VII.
{novena.7} December 22.
{novena.7} The Sorrow that the Ingratitude of Men has caused Jesus.
{novena.7} In these days of the holy Nativity St. Francis of Assisi went about the highways and woods with sighs and tears and inconsolable lamentations.
{novena.8} MEDITATION VIII.
{novena.8} December 23.
{novena.8} The Love of God manifested to Men by the Birth of Jesus.
{novena.8} Consider that by the grace that is said here to have appeared is meant the tender love of Jesus Christ towards men,—a love that we have not merited, which therefore is called "grace."
{novena.9} MEDITATION IX.
{novena.9} December 24.
{novena.9} Saint Joseph goes to Bethlehem with His Holy Spouse.
{novena.9} God had decreed that his Son should be born not in the house of Joseph, but in a cavern and stable of beasts, in the poorest and most painful way that a child can be born; and therefore he caused Cæsar to publish an edict, by which people were commanded to go and enroll themselves, every one in his own city whence he drew his origin.
{other-novena} Other Meditations for the Novena of Christmas.
{other-novena.chaplet} Chaplet to be recited before every Meditation.
{other-novena.chaplet.1} 1. My most sweet Jesus, who wert born in a cave and wert afterwards laid in a manger upon straw, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.2} 2. My most sweet Jesus, who wert presented and offered by Mary in the temple, to be afterwards one day sacrificed for us upon the cross, have mercy upon us. R. Have Mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.3} 3. My most sweet Jesus, who wert persecuted by Herod and constrained to fly into Egypt, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.4} 4. My most sweet Jesus, who didst dwell in Egypt for seven years, poor, unknown, and despised by that barbarous nation, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.5} 5. My most sweet Jesus, who didst return to Thy country to be one day crucified there in the midst of two thieves, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.6} 6. My most sweet Jesus, who at the age of twelve years didst remain in the temple to dispute with the Doctors, and after three days wert found by Mary, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.7} 7. My most sweet Jesus, who didst live concealed from the world for so many years in the shop at Nazareth, serving Mary and Joseph, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.8} 8. My most sweet Jesus, who for three years before Thy Passion didst go about preaching and teaching the way of salvation, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
{other-novena.chaplet.9} 9. My most sweet Jesus, who for the love of us didst terminate Thy life by dying on the cross, have mercy upon us. R. Have mercy, O Lord, have mercy upon us. Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory be to the Father, etc.
