# Historical papal-source coverage

Audit date: 26 October 2026.

The catalog and reader manifests describe measured digital source sets. They
do not constitute a census of every surviving papal document. Completing the
Vatican's selected HTML indexes, obtaining a complete volume download, and
recovering every constituent act are separate results.

## Chronological and documentary extent

| Period or source family | Present boundary | Remaining coverage question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before 440 | Outside the selected Turin *Bullarium*'s starting date. | Early letters and decretals require their own identified editions and attribution records. |
| 440–1740 | Twenty-four selected *Bullarium* main-volume witnesses; the acquired appendix contains only its first installment, through Silverius. | These collections select and attribute documents; their chronological span does not establish exhaustive survival, inclusion or authenticity. Reconstruction holds remain in the separate catalog. |
| Benedict XIV, 1740–1758 | Selected Vatican HTML entries, supplemented by a separately acquired historical *Bullarium* set. | Constituent reconstruction must include the final-year supplement and distinguish complete tomes from numbered physical parts. |
| Clement XIII through early Gregory XVI | The Rome *Bullarii Romani continuatio* is a separate source series from the Turin collection. | Newly acquired volume inputs require their own series identities, exact act boundaries and reading-order review. |
| Gregory XVI, 1831–1846 | Selected Vatican entries and separately identified *Acta Gregorii Papae XVI*. | The four-volume 1901–1904 edition has three canonical volumes and a separate civil volume; each needs its own language and constituent treatment. |
| Pius IX before 1865 | Selected Vatican entries and the bounded *Quanta cura*–*Syllabus* publication witness. | The wider *Pii IX Pontificis Maximi Acta* series requires part/volume reconciliation and constituent recovery. |
| 1865–1908 | The forty-one *Acta Sanctae Sedis* volumes. | Papal acts must remain distinct from curial acts, quoted older documents and other journal contents. Unresolved boundaries remain explicit. |
| 1909–1930 | Historical *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* witnesses with edition-specific distribution decisions. | A nominal volume year does not date every issue; the 1930/1931 boundary requires fascicle and contrary release evidence. |
| 1931–2026 | Later journal and Vatican-index identities, including linked language editions. | Catalog availability supplies no affirmative redistribution basis for protected bodies or later translations. The latest annual archive can be incomplete. |

The separate [archive inventory](archives.json), [Vatican catalog](catalog.json),
[*Bullarium* catalog](bullarium-catalog.json) and [Acta catalog](acta-catalog.json)
own their source snapshots and measured counts. Their records may overlap as
different witnesses of the same act; their totals must not be added as a count
of unique papal works.

## Historical collections between 1740 and 1865

The [Pontifical Gregorian University's source guide](https://www.iuscangreg.it/diritto_anteriore.php?lang=EN)
distinguishes Benedict XIV's four-tome *Bullarium*, the Rome *Continuatio* and
the later Turin *Bullarium*. The [Columbia Law catalog](https://pegasus.law.columbia.edu/record/123971)
describes the Rome continuation as nineteen volumes, published in 1835–1857.
The Gregorian guide labels its linked continuation sequence 1–20. The
[additional Google Books witness](https://books.google.com/books?id=WV9GAAAAcAAJ)
is a genuine printed tome XX: its first body page bears the footer
*Bull. Rom. Tom. XX* and begins act CCCXII, dated 5 February 1835, under
Gregory XVI's fifth pontifical year. Page 2 continues that act and begins
CCCXIII. Its offered page list contains eighty-eight numbered body pages.
This separately observed source must remain visible beside the nineteen
acquired volume input sets; its whole-file access and complete extent require
their own disposition.

The continuation's openly offered Internet Archive inputs were pursued under
both *Bullarii Romani continuatio* and its numbered item family. The direct
`BRC-Tomo-01` through `BRC-Tomo-12` items supply whole OCR, layout XML, scan
metadata and page-number files. The next seven names in that pattern return
empty metadata responses; later Rome volumes are available under separately
identified `bub_gb_` items. Those empty responses establish a failed identifier
route, not the absence of the volumes. Prato re-editions and duplicate copies
remain separate from the Rome volume sequence.

Title-page images control the acquired printing identity. In particular, the
selected direct-family witnesses print volume VI in 1843, VII in 1845 and X in
1845, while their archive date fields respectively say 1842, 1843 and 1844.
The item labeled `BRC-Tomo-05` has the erroneous metadata title “TOMO 03”; its
printed title identifies tome V. Neither the raw OCR nor the source metadata
is silently corrected by these bibliographic findings.

The Benedict XIV Hanicq items numbered 1–4 are physical parts of a thirteen-part
1826–1827 edition, rather than four complete original tomes. A separately
identified four-tome Mainardi/Occhi set offers complete OCR and layout inputs;
its final-year appendix must be included when stating its temporal coverage.
The [Berkeley Law catalog](https://lawcat.berkeley.edu/record/339866) distinguishes
the Hanicq parts and volumes. A source set must follow the acquired printing's
own structure rather than inherit a count from a differently arranged edition.

The four original *Acta Gregorii Papae XVI* witnesses are available as
[volume I](https://archive.org/details/actagregoriipapa0001vinc),
[volume II](https://archive.org/details/actagregoriipapa0002vinc),
[volume III](https://archive.org/details/actagregoriipapa0003vinc) and
[volume IV](https://archive.org/details/actagregoriipapa0004vinc). Their printed
titles identify I–III as *Pars prima canonica* and IV as *Pars secunda civilis*.
The civil volume contains Italian material; the set cannot be described as
four volumes of original Latin canonical acts.

The [first Pius IX *Acta* volume](https://archive.org/details/1846-1878-ss-pius-ix-pontificis-maximi-acta-pars-i-vol-01-lt),
Rome 1854, states a selection limit in its preface, printed p. IV: acts and
letters about private persons which the pope did not intend publicly issued
are omitted. Pages V–VI explain the division between universal-Church and
Pontifical-State civil material. Thus acquisition of the complete printed
series would still preserve that edition's own selection boundary.

The Gregory XVI civil volume likewise contains documents by other issuers.
Its first item, printed p. 1, is Treasurer Mario Mattei's circular of
14 January 1831, before Gregory's election. A constituent's actual issuer,
date and form therefore require their own identification; the collection
title does not establish papal authorship.

## Earlier and supplementary document families

For the period before 1198, [Regesta Pontificum Romanorum Online](https://www.papsturkunden.de/EditMOM/database-information.do)
identifies papal contacts, dates, transmission and authenticity findings across
several scholarly regesta traditions. Its [project news](https://www.papsturkunden.de/EditMOM/showNews.do)
also describes the gradual addition of records and restrictions on access to
certain texts and images. Regesta are descriptions and source locators;
they cannot be substituted for the complete wording of an act.

The [digital MGH letter editions](https://www.dmgh.de/epp.htm) include Gregory
the Great's *Registrum epistolarum* in two parts and further medieval letter
collections. The [Colby medieval canon-law library](https://web.colby.edu/canonlaw/registers/)
links Jaffé, Potthast, Gregory IX's registers and chancery-rule editions.
These are additional edition families rather than proof that the selected
*Bullarium* contains the same complete texts. Exact editions and modern
editorial contributions need their own acquisition and rights decisions.

Some register-image collections require physical consultation. [Saint Louis
University](https://www.slu.edu/library/vatican-film-library/collections/papal-letter-registers.php)
describes reading-room CD-ROMs of Vatican letter and supplication registers
and prohibits transferring their image files. The [MGH archive](https://www.mgh-bibliothek.de/archiv/d/)
likewise describes consultation at a designated computer and forbids copying
or lending the register media. These routes are access-limited sources, not
missing historical documents and not open download corpora.

The [Catholic University library's bullaria guide](https://guides.lib.cua.edu/c.php?g=711409&p=5283968)
also distinguishes recipient- or subject-specific collections, including
Franciscan, Polish and American materials. Such collections can identify
documents beyond the selected general bullarium; overlap and attribution
must be resolved act by act.

## Languages and unresolved states

The historical reconstruction is principally Latin. The retained English
collection is one identified 1903 translation edition with thirty-one bounded
entries, including an abridged entry. It supplies no general English coverage
of the reconstructed Latin collections. Modern linked translations and older
English editions remain separate witnesses with separate rights and textual
histories. No missing translation is supplied by a model.

The Vatican discovery scope follows the named formal-document indexes.
Ordinary correspondence is an optional expansion; audiences, homilies,
messages and other document families require their own stated boundary.
An act appearing in more than one category is not necessarily another work.

Unresolved records distinguish an available source awaiting cataloging or
reconstruction, an extraction failure such as inseparable columns, an
identified historical fragment, unavailable or consultation-limited access,
and text withheld for an unresolved distribution basis. A failed route or
empty literal search does not establish that no document survives.
