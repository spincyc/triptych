# Leo XIII historical Latin publication review

Reviewed: 2026-10-26.

This review accounts for 103 Latin-delivery candidates from the initial Vatican catalog and five additional acts discovered through the Latin interface. All 108 have identified historical publication witnesses: 107 additional approvals and the previously approved *Rerum novarum*. The publication-approval inventory owns the exact delivery, transformed-text and historical-scan hashes.

All 26 complete ASS volumes XI–XXXVI were acquired mechanically (80,745,718 bytes). Opening and closing Latin wording were matched against the complete scan OCR, with the historical heading, dating or signature context and bounded note apparatus inspected. The three bilingual deliveries were also checked in their Latin columns. This establishes historical publication identity and an artifact-specific expired-term basis in the United States. It does not establish full verbal collation, a corrected text, or a critical edition.

Publication years recorded as upper bounds identify the end of the historical volume interval. The official archive labels early volumes by their first year only; that label is preserved separately. The original printing and modern scan/reprint wrapper have distinct rights.

## Expression and transcription limits

- *Vi è ben noto*, *Il divisamento* and *Ea animi sollicitudo* are identified historical Latin versions printed beside Italian. They are explicitly recorded as historical Latin translations/versions, with uncredited translators; their catalog incipits do not determine the text language.
- Modern source bibliographies, the 2012 Denzinger references, their separator rules and connecting title asterisks are excluded by exact pinned transformations. Historical body wording, spelling and note numbering remain unchanged.
- The Vatican delivery mislabels many ASS references as AAS. *Grande munus* and *Sancta Dei civitas* identify volume XXIII where the actual witnesses are in XIII. *Misericors Dei Filius* supplies a spurious 1898 source year and drops a word from its dating clause. The text retains its transcription errors; source citations identify the historical printing.
- *Vi è ben noto* continues through ASS XX, p. 215, beyond the web bibliography’s p. 213. Its web transcription omits the printed dating line. *Dilectus Domini Nostri* ends at p. 653, before a different Pius X letter begins; the web bibliography extends through p. 655. The Vatican versions of that act and *Hoc mandatum* omit additional printed subscriptions after the papal signature.
- The additional *Eximiam praeclarorum* witness itself abbreviates standard legal closing formulae with “etc.” Those abbreviations remain; a full expanded act is not claimed. An extraneous standalone period before its web heading is excluded.
- The additional *Quemadmodum Christiana religio* edition transcribes the papal brief and omits separate liturgical formularies inserted within the ASS container. It supplies no reconstructed prayer text.

## Date discrepancies

Index-interface disagreements remain visible in the catalog. In particular, its Latin index gives *Quod auctoritate* as 1855 rather than the printing’s 1885, *Pergrata* as 4 rather than 14 September 1886, and *Ad extremas* as 1983 rather than 1893. These index defects do not change the identified historical publication. Four further discrepancies are retained in source limitations:

| Act | Index date | Web/printed dating clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Pastoralis officii* | 12 September 1891 in the English/Italian indexes | XXII Septembris 1891 in the Latin delivery and ASS XXIV, p. 207 |
| *Hoc mandatum* | 27 June 1897 | sexto Kalendas Iunias 1897 (27 May) |
| *Cum plura Nobis* | 10 July 1898 | X Iulii MDCCCLXXXVIII (10 July 1888), ASS XXI, p. 6 |
| *Aeterni Pastoris* | 24 October 1899 | Calend. Novembris 1899 (1 November) |
