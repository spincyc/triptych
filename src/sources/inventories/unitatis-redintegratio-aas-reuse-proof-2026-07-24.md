# *Unitatis Redintegratio* and AAS 57 Reuse Review

Reviewed on 2026-07-24.

This record documents the bounded normalization of *Unitatis redintegratio*
within `family.vatican-ii.acts` and its reuse of the volume 57 component of
`family.acta.aas-ass`. It makes complete official witnesses centrally
identifiable for later expansive examination, binds current publications only
at the loci they actually use, and proves cross-publication propagation by
mutating run-local copies of the real source graph. The canonical manifests
and publication-local bindings remain the machine-enforced records.

## Identity boundaries

The graph distinguishes the Decree, its expressions, and its official-gazette
container:

- *Unitatis redintegratio* is one intellectual work, solemnly promulgated on
  21 November 1964 and comprising numbered articles 1--24;
- the dated English and Latin Vatican web states are separate editions and
  exact HTML artifacts;
- the Latin constituent printed in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* 57 (1965) is a
  separate edition represented as one segment of the existing exact volume
  artifact; and
- *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* volume 57 remains an official-gazette container
  containing many distinct acts, not another identity for the Decree.

The Latin Vatican delivery appends the separately titled *Lumen gentium*
*Notificationes*, the embedded Doctrinal Commission declaration of 6 March
1964, and the complete *Nota explicativa praevia* between the Decree's
subscriptions and notes. Those are distinct works already represented in the
source graph. They are explicitly excluded from this Decree's edition and
passage boundaries.

The English delivery contains known local defects, including “thou has” in

names, and several references that differ from the Latin and *Acta* witnesses.
The artifact record identifies the checked examples without claiming an
exhaustive diplomatic collation. The Latin delivery's subscription block is
abridged relative to the *Acta* and is followed by the unrelated appended
material just described. Those defects and boundaries are recorded rather
than silently normalized.

In the exact *Acta* artifact, the Decree begins on printed p. 90. Its numbered
body occupies pp. 90--107; the promulgation formula, subscriptions, and
notarial attestations continue through p. 112. A new issue and the *Acta Pauli
PP. VI* begin on p. 113. The OCR is a locating aid rather than an independently
authoritative transcription.

The Vatican portal's legal notice did not establish repository redistribution
permission. The HTML and PDF artifacts are therefore `restricted`,
nonretained, and nonindexable. The repository stores exact hashes, byte or
page extent, provenance, boundaries, and dependency records, but not their
payloads.

## Expansive availability and bounded use

The complete dated English and Latin web states and complete Latin *Acta*
constituent are centrally identifiable for later work-wide examination. That
availability does not imply that every consumer inspected or relied on the
whole Decree.

The reusable English passage records cover articles 1--24, 3, 3--4, 11, and
22. The narrower records govern the current claims about imperfect communion,
ecumenical practice, doctrinal integrity and the hierarchy of truths, and
Baptism's sacramental bond. The complete English and *Acta* body records
remain available without a verified publication consumer. The Latin web state
is likewise cataloged as a complete witness without manufacturing a
publication-level claim of direct use.

## Source-of-source non-inheritance

The Decree's notes expose Scripture, earlier councils, Augustine, and other
sources as leads. A publication using a Decree article does not thereby become
a direct consumer of those cited works. Likewise:

- article 3 does not make a consumer a direct user of Florence, Lateran IV,
  Lyon II, or Augustine's second exposition of Psalm 32;
- article 22 does not itself establish every rule for determining baptismal
  validity, indelibility, or non-repetition;
- CCC 1271 is a proximate synthesis of articles 3 and 22 for the sacramental
  reference, but neither direction of citation creates undeclared inherited
  dependencies; and
- the appended *Lumen gentium* apparatus in the Latin HTML is not inherited
  merely because a publication cites *Unitatis redintegratio*.

This preserves first-class source discovery while preventing the graph from
claiming inspection or evidentiary dependence that did not occur.

## Reviewed consumers and exclusions

Nine binding rows across four publications now share the normalized
identities. Eight rows across all four publications are fingerprinted verified
uses:

- the council article uses English articles 3 and 3--4 for imperfect
  communion, ecclesial elements, and the Council's bounded ecumenical program;
- the heresies reference uses articles 3, 3--4, and 11 for non-imputation,
  truthful dialogue, doctrinal integrity, false irenicism, and the hierarchy
  of truths;
- the sacramental treatise uses articles 3 and 22 for Baptism's imperfect
  communion and sacramental-unity consequences; and
- the at-a-glance companion uses article 22 for the compact Baptism summary it
  imports from the treatise.

The ninth row is the council article's work-level catalog binding for title,
genre, promulgation date, and subject in its sixteen-document inventory. It
does not claim inspection of the complete body.

No consumer inherits the complete English or Latin bodies, the Latin web
artifact, the appended *Lumen gentium* apparatus, or the Decree's
source-of-source notes merely because those witnesses are now reusable.

## Textual corrections and acceptance state

The proof produced four reader-facing source calibrations:

- the council article now distinguishes article 3's doctrinal account of
  imperfect communion and ecclesial elements from article 4's practical
  ecumenical directions, and names all three acts promulgated on 21 November
  1964;
- the heresies reference corrects the Decree's chronology from 1965 to 1964
  and grounds its ecumenical boundary in articles 3--4 and 11;
- the sacramental treatise distinguishes articles 3 and 22 from CCC 1272 and
  CIC 845, which separately control indelibility and non-repetition; and
- the shared Baptism summary now attributes its compact unity claim directly
  to article 22 without creating a spill page in either sacramental
  publication.
