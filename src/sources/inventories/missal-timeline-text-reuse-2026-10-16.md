# Existing missal text reuse audit — 2026-10-16

## Conclusion
The current tree supplies substantial partial 1962 Proper content, a structured 1861 lay-missal Ordinary, and historical OCR acquisition inputs. It cannot currently reconstruct any complete, verified, fully structured historical Missal witness. Importing the modern calendar projection into earlier act commits would create unsupported edition attribution. Counts below are derived snapshots, not new canonical census claims.

## Ordinary
The pre-1955 and 1962 inventories resolve to Eugene Cummiskey, Philadelphia 1861, The Roman Missal translated into the English language for the use of the laity, first revised edition. Twelve TSV artifacts under that edition carry six divisions in English/facing Latin: praeparatio, oblatio, praefationes, canon-missae, communio, conclusio. Recorded transcription is visual at 640 dpi, doubtful characters at 1600 dpi. This is a lay book, not an altar Missal. Its Canon lacks the later St Joseph clause; its eleven Prefaces and seasonal second orations are its own.
The pre-1955 frame holds 195 English and 112 Latin elements; the 1962 frame 189 English and 112 Latin after six exclusions. All are antecedent and target-uncollated. The postconciliar frame models 59 elements, holds no English and 20 Latin: five collated antecedents, fifteen uncollated. ALL THREE report ZERO held target-edition Ordinary elements by language. These artifacts can support an explicitly named partial 1861 witness, not verbatim 1920/1962/2008 text.

All paths/hashes in [the holdings check](missal-timeline-holdings-v1.toml); none already constitutes complete liturgical-unit segmentation.
- Roman Venice 1570, Varisco/Faletti consortium: whole-volume public-domain OCR; NOT Rome editio princeps. Degraded Latin, requiring checked passages.

- Roman Pustet Ratisbon 1862: whole public-domain OCR, relatively better Latin; commercial hand-format reproduction of Salviucci Rome 1856 with local appendices/errata. Not a typical edition and not an unchanged 1634 book. Registered page-image routes available; OCR corrupts accents/tables.
- Roman Tournai Desclee 1889, Leonis XIII auctoritate recognitum: whole public-domain OCR of a conformed printing, not 1884 typical impression. Edition record says the 1884-instrument reading remains undone.

- Lasance/Walsh Benziger revised 1945: insert-excluded OCR; scan is approximately 1955–56, not 1937. Repository non-renewal basis admits main work but excludes pp. 1298a–1298n and 1302a–1302d. Deliberately not complete unmodified scan text.
- Ambrosian Milan Pachel 1499: public-domain OCR, very degraded; copy lacks leaves [*]1 and m8. Not the 1475 princeps; incomplete witness.
- Ambrosian Milan Sirturi 1640: whole public-domain OCR. Edition notes identify an archiepiscopal revision letter while older act inventory treats it merely as printed; preserve unresolved classification.
- Ambrosian Milan de Sirturis 1712: public-domain OCR also contains separately printed 1733 bound-in Masses. Separate strata before claiming a 1712 state.
- Mozarabic Rome Monaldini/Lesley 1755: TWO public-domain OCR scans of ONE edition. NYPL 408-leaf copy stops mid-sanctoral; Florence 761-leaf copy reaches explicit but has worse OCR.
- Mozarabic Paris Migne 1862, PL85 tomus prior: whole public-domain OCR of Lesley with apparatus, not a new rite recension. PL86 is a breviary.
- Sarum Dickinson Burntisland 1861–1883 and Legg Oxford 1916: public-domain OCR of critical editions. Dickinson is a fascicle range; registered copy is described as whole but this audit did not independently prove gathering completeness. Legg edits three manuscripts, not one historical witness. Separate editorial apparatus.

## Registered does not mean reusable full text
The vatican-typica-1920 OCR is remote/unresolved: scan body dated 1951–54, later appendix from 1970 or later; withdrawn from Git. Do not resurrect as 1920. Tours Mame 1922 editio quarta iuxta typicam has a remote public-domain 1,162-page PDF and verified passages, NOT Vatican 1920. Benziger ia-1947 actually means editio IV, copyright 1942/1944, exact printing year unestablished; remote/unresolved. CMAA Vatican 1962 and Benziger 1962 artifacts are remote/unresolved: the 1,068 permitted old-text projections do not clear the whole modern book. Braga 1924 scan includes additions through 1961; its rights inventory refuses even presumed 1924 portions until a proven leaf map or clean witness isolates them. No tracked bytes. Older acquisition prose calling it wholly printable is superseded.
ICEL website permission does not cover public-Git/download payloads. Modern English ICEL/USCCB/CCD text remains quarantined for this surface. Modern Latin editions cannot be filled from older wording on similarity alone. These are existing repository dispositions, not newly researched legal findings.

## Whole-book scope still missing
No structured family defines an exhaustive expected-book inventory joining edition-specific front matter, kalendarium/computus, general rubrics and Ritus servandus/De defectibus, Ordinary and all Prefaces, temporale, sanctorale, Commons, votive/ritual/dead formularies, oration collections, local appendices, ceremonies/blessings and variants. Some matter exists in raw scans or lay Ordinary, but no validated single-book coverage proof. Rome 1570, 1634, exact 1884/1900/1920 impressions, distinct 1956–60 state and intervening revisions are not all represented as exact whole witnesses. Non-Roman rites have bounded OCR/acquisition records; Eastern Catholic Eucharistic books remain explicitly unattempted by the Roman-shaped audit.

## Narrow adapter recommendation
1. Separate act station from bibliographic witness. Export known 1962 bodies with current stable target identity, source hash, projection identity/transformations, target verification, rights basis and public-git surface. No backward fill by inheritance.
2. Use _calendars.load_document/resolve_propers and mass-propers.carry_translations; retain recension stamps, typed gaps, cycle/form/occurrence identity and language roles. Selected Bible words remain a separate Bible witness, not literal Missal wording.
3. Directly adapt the twelve 1861 Ordinary TSVs as a PARTIAL 1861 lay-witness snapshot. Do not relabel the 1962 assembly as 1861 either.
4. Put historical OCR into a witness-specific segmentation/collation queue. Isolate supplements and account for missing leaves. OCR may be archived as OCR; it cannot satisfy complete structured text. 1604 offers the cleanest exact typical identity; 1862 more legible input and collation routes, but is commercial printing.
5. Preserve unknown denominators. No adapter supplies missing book domains, restricted modern text, or target collation absent from the records.
