A work is the intellectual thing — a treatise, a homily, a decree. An edition is one materially identified form of it whose wording or authority can differ from another's, so a Greek original, Migne's Latin and a public-domain English translation are three editions and never one work with three texts. An artifact is the exact bytes that were acquired, with their hash and their rights. A passage is an addressable place in an edition, controlled by exactly one artifact. Nothing here flattens those four into one.

Rights are read from the records, never from the disk. A passage is set here only where the artifact controlling it records an affirmative distribution basis and is stored in the repository. Most of this corpus fails that test — it is under copyright, or its rights have never been settled — and those passages are listed with the reason rather than quietly dropped. A record you cannot read here is still a record you can find, cite and follow to its source.
