About this page

Catena Omnia sets a chapter of Scripture beside every commentary fragment this project holds on it, oldest voice first — each excerpt carrying its author, work, date, extent, edition and rights, and nothing shown whose rights forbid showing it.

The chain is assembled in your browser, from the same tracked records the project's checks replay; no chapter is pre-rendered. This edition of the page therefore needs JavaScript: if scripts do not run here, the chapter and its commentary cannot be shown, and nothing in this section will change.

The editions the fragments are cut from are catalogued in the Source Library, and the rest of the site — Scripture, liturgy, history, law — is reachable from the links in the footer below and from the site's own entrance.

A catena is a chain of excerpted comment running beside the text. This page shows only commentary this project actually holds: the works its acquisition record merely lists are printed separately, under their own heading, and never beside an excerpt. Fragments are stored at the extent their author addressed and the chapter view is derived from that, so a fragment reaching across a chapter boundary appears under both chapters at its full extent rather than being cut at the seam.

Once the page renders, the chapter and the first commentator stand open; every other fragment is closed until you open it, and opening is what it costs to read. A chapter fetches a list of who comments on it — never their words — so a chapter with nothing held fetches nothing at all; a fragment's text arrives when you open that fragment, which is why its length is printed beside its name. The consequence is worth knowing: your browser's own find-in-page reaches only what you have opened.
