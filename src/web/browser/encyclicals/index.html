How summaries were written

English texts may be local or external. Source PDFs reproduce identified historical printings. English online links open external texts, whose availability does not grant permission to copy them here. Fragments, damaged texts and unreviewed extents do not count as complete English.

A reading edition is a two-column setting of one identified historical printing — the Latin as printed by 1930, or an English translation printed by 1930 — whose every page was reviewed before it was installed. Each source PDF link names the printing it was set from. Fragment marks an edition of a printing that holds only part of the act; damaged printing marks one whose source is defective in places. The edition itself says what is missing. A complete label describes the text’s extent, not word-for-word collation or an endorsement of the translation.

The papal wording in these editions is historical text, in the public domain in the United States on its identified printing by 1930; its status elsewhere may differ, and it is excluded from this project’s licence. See Third-party material. A Vatican link opens the Holy See’s own site. English online links may also open identified third-party sites. This project does not control those sites or republish their modern translations.
