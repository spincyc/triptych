Pick a date and a missal, and read that day's Mass: the text of each proper, with the rubric that put it there in the margin beside it — which days fell on the date, which one took it, and which of the rest survive as the second and third collects.

Turn the Ordinary on and the page becomes a missal to follow the Mass in: the frame runs from the foot of the altar to the last Gospel, and the day's Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Communion and Postcommunion are set into it where they are said. Where each one belongs is the missal's own declaration, carried in its Ordinary and cited to the rubric that states it; a proper the missal seats nowhere is shown in the missal's own order rather than given a place this page invented for it.

Choose the language the Ordinary is read in, and choose whether the priest's actions are shown. Where a missal has nothing in the language asked for, each element of the frame still holds its place and names the recorded reason it is silent — the reason itself is stated once, above the Mass, with how many elements it covers. That does not stop an element holding a place: the postconciliar Collect is withheld under licence and the day's Collect is still set down after it.
