℣How this day's Mass is assembled

Pick a date and a missal. The page works through the five decisions in order — which days fall on the date, which of them takes it, what becomes of the ones that lose, which Mass may be said, and what is then read out of the book — and shows the numbered rubric behind every step. The second and third collects are the point of the exercise: they are what is left of the days that lost.

This is a finding aid, not an Ordo

Nothing here says what was celebrated anywhere. The dates are computed arithmetic and the rules are transcribed from this repository's own collated study of the rubrics. The competent annual Ordo of the place controls, together with its particular calendar — diocesan, religious, parish and church — and any indult. Where the rules run out this page says so rather than choosing; where a territorial decision is in play it shows both forms and takes neither.

Nothing here is pre-computed. There is one rules file per missal and one calendar file per year; the assembly for a date is derived in the browser from the two of them. Pre-building an assembly for every day of every year would be tens of thousands of stored objects, and every one of them would go stale the moment a rule was corrected.

The propers themselves are read on the propers page, which is where a Mass is read rather than argued about.
