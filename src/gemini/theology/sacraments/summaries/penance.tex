\sacramentSummaryStart{Penance}{First Sacrament of Healing --- Reconciliation with God and the Church}{The second plank after shipwreck, restoring post-baptismal grace and ecclesial peace}

\begin{summaryidentity}
Definition & Sacrament instituted by Christ reconciling contrite baptized sinners with God and the Church through priestly absolution (\emph{ST}~III, q.~84; Trent~XIV).\\
Institution & Keys promised (Matt~16:19; 18:18) and authority to forgive or retain sins conferred by the risen Christ on Easter evening (John~20:21--23).\\
Sacramental structure & \latin{Sacramentum tantum}: penitent's acts and absolution. \latin{Res et sacramentum}: interior penance and \latin{pax cum Ecclesia}. \latin{Res tantum}: forgiveness, restored grace.\\
Order of ends & \textbf{Primary:} reconciliation with God after mortal sin. \textbf{Secondary:} ecclesial peace, healing of venial sin. \textbf{Contingent:} serenity, counsel. \textbf{Ultimate:} eternal communion.\\
\end{summaryidentity}

\begin{summaryconstitution}
Matter & Remote: sins. \latin{Quasi materia}: contrition, confession, satisfaction (Trent~XIV, ch.~3). & Integral confession of mortal sins by species and number; sorrow and amendment. & Forgotten mortal sins remitted indirectly, confessed later; venial sins commended.\\
Form & Judicial absolution perfecting penitent's acts (\emph{ST}~III, q.~84, a.~3; Trent~XIV, can.~9). & Latin: ``I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit'' (CCC~1449). & Eastern Catholic Churches validly employ ancient deprecative prayers.\\
Minister & Valid bishop or priest acting \latin{tanquam Christi minister} as judge and physician. & Requires Holy Orders and canonical \textbf{faculty} (CIC~c.~966; CCEO~c.~722). & Danger of death: \emph{any} priest validly and licitly absolves all sins and censures (c.~976).\\
Subject & Living baptized person guilty of post-baptismal sin, contrite or attrite. & Individual integral confession is sole ordinary norm (c.~960; CCEO~c.~720~\S1). & General absolution strictly limited to imminent death or grave necessity; confess later.\\
Change effected & Accidental formal restoration of grace; judicial and relational restoration. & \textbf{No character} (\emph{ST}~III, q.~63, a.~6); repeatable without limit (q.~84, a.~10). & Unbaptized incapable; sacramental seal is absolute (c.~983; CCEO~c.~733).\\
\end{summaryconstitution}

\begin{summaryeconomy}
Salvation history & Old Law atonement (Lev~5; Ps~50; Joel~2:13). & Keys (Matt~16:19); Easter breath (John~20:21--23). & Reopens worthy access to the Eucharist (\emph{ST}~III, q.~65, a.~3). & Perseverance unto beatific communion.\\
Ecclesial order & Ministry of reconciliation (2~Cor~5:18--20). & Applies Christ's Cross to post-baptismal falls. & Restores \latin{pax cum Ecclesia} (\emph{LG}~11); individual confession norm. & Purifies temporal debt via satisfaction and Purgatory.\\
\end{summaryeconomy}

\begin{summarywitnesses}
Scripture & Matt~16:19; 18:18; Luke~15; John~20:21--23; 2~Cor~5:18--20; 1~John~1:8--9 & Christ grants power to forgive or retain sins and entrusts reconciliation.\\
Fathers & Cyprian (\emph{De lapsis}); Ambrose (\emph{De paen.}); Augustine (Tr.~49, 121; \emph{Serm.}~71) & Refute Novatianism: binding and loosing belong to true priests.\\
Scholastics & Aquinas (\emph{ST}~III, qq.~84--90); Lombard (IV, d.~17); Gratian & Distinguishes virtue and sacrament, \latin{quasi materia}, guilt, temporal debt.\\
Magisterium & Lateran~IV; Florence~(\S13); Trent~XIV; \emph{Rom.\ Cat.}; JPII (\emph{RP}, \emph{MD}) & Defines \latin{quasi materia}, contrition/attrition, judicial act, satisfaction.\\
Canon law & CIC~1983 (cc.~959--997); CCEO~1990 (cc.~718--736) & Governs faculties, danger-of-death absolution, three forms, and seal.\\
\end{summarywitnesses}

\begin{studybox}{Distinctions to preserve}
\textbf{Judicial act:} absolution is a judicial sentence in Christ's name, not bare declaration (Trent~XIV, can.~9), requiring Orders and faculties. \textbf{Contrition vs.\ attrition:} perfect contrition reconciles immediately with \latin{votum} to confess; attrition suffices with absolution. \textbf{Guilt vs.\ temporal debt:} absolution removes guilt and eternal punishment; temporal debt heals via satisfaction, Purgatory, and indulgences.
\end{studybox}
