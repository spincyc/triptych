\subsection{Purpose, Corpus, and Architectural Ownership}
\label{subsec:scope-purpose-corpus}

This reference treatise presents a unified Catholic account of the seven sacraments across Sacred Scripture, the Fathers of the Church, St.~Thomas Aquinas, the ecumenical councils, the modern Magisterium, and the canonical discipline of both the Latin Church and the twenty-three Eastern Catholic Churches \latin{sui iuris}. Its body unfolds the continuous theology of Christian Initiation (Baptism, Confirmation/Chrismation, Eucharist), Healing (Penance, Anointing of the Sick), and the Service of Communion (Holy Orders, Matrimony), closing with a Christological synthesis and the complete bibliography. The terminal appendices hold this scope notice and metaphysical lexicon, the seven-sacrament comparative matrix, the twenty-four-Church initiation table, and the seven structurally uniform one-page sacrament summaries.

All shared fragments---the comparative matrix, the metaphysical lexicon, the twenty-four-Church initiation table, and the seven one-page summaries---have their single authoritative home here. The companion booklet \emph{The Seven Sacraments at a Glance} imports those exact fragments mechanically without independent theological prose.

\subsection{Source Hierarchy and Method}
\label{subsec:scope-method}

Claims follow the Catholic theological hierarchy of witnesses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sacred Scripture} as received in the canon of the Church, quoted verbatim in English from the public-domain Douay-Rheims (Challoner) text with the Clementine Vulgate as Latin control; where Vulgate and modern Greek or Hebrew verse numbering diverge (as at John~6:54 vs.~6:53 and Acts~14:22 vs.~14:23), both numbers are noted.
  \item \textbf{Ecumenical Councils and Solemn Magisterium}, chiefly Fourth Lateran (1215), Florence (1439), Trent (Sessions~V, VI, VII, XIII, XIV, XXI, XXII, XXIII, XXIV), and Second Vatican (\emph{Sacrosanctum Concilium}, \emph{Lumen Gentium}, \emph{Orientalium Ecclesiarum}, \emph{Unitatis Redintegratio}, \emph{Presbyterorum Ordinis}, \emph{Gaudium et Spes}), read alongside universal catechisms (\emph{Roman Catechism} of 1566; \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}) and papal doctrinal and liturgical constitutions.
  \item \textbf{Fathers and Doctors of the Church}, headed systematically by St.~Thomas Aquinas (\emph{Summa theologiae}~III, qq.~60--90 and \emph{Supplementum}) and St.~Augustine, alongside St.~Ignatius of Antioch, St.~Justin Martyr, St.~Irenaeus, St.~Cyril of Jerusalem, St.~Ambrose, St.~John Chrysostom, St.~Leo the Great, and St.~John of Damascus.
  \item \textbf{Universal and Particular Canon Law}, distinguishing the \emph{Code of Canon Law} (CIC~1983, governing the Latin Church, c.~1) from the \emph{Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches} (CCEO~1990, governing the twenty-three Eastern Catholic Churches, c.~1), together with subsequent papal amendments (\emph{Omnium in Mentem}, 2009; \emph{Multum fructum}, 2015; \emph{De concordia inter Codices}, 2016; \emph{Spiritus Domini}, 2021; \emph{Pascite gregem Dei}, 2021) and the liturgical books and synodal particular laws of each Church \latin{sui iuris}.
\end{enumerate}
Direct block quotations are drawn exclusively from verified public-domain printings and critical editions; restricted modern Holy See documents (Vatican~II, CCC, CIC~1983, CCEO~1990, and modern papal acts after Leo~XIII) are paraphrased accurately rather than reproduced in bulk, except where short essential sacramental formulas themselves are cited for doctrinal accuracy. Unrevised nineteenth-century printings of the Fathers (ANF/NPNF CCEL, Dods 1871) are used for direct quotations rather than modernized web revisions.

\subsection{Authority, Jurisdiction, Currentness, and Global Limits}
\label{subsec:scope-limits}

Doctrinal definitions of ecumenical councils bind universal Catholic faith permanently; disciplinary canons, liturgical sequences, age thresholds for Confirmation or First Eucharist, clerical celibacy norms, and matrimonial form rules are mutable ecclesiastical law governed by competent authority for each Church \latin{sui iuris} and territory. Canonical and census descriptions reflect universal law and the twenty-four-Church Catholic communion as verified through November~2026 (including the 2015 erection of the Eritrean Metropolitan Church \latin{sui iuris} and the 2015 elevation of the Hungarian Greek Catholic Metropolitan Church \latin{sui iuris}). Because particular synodal, eparchial, and diocesan initiation policies vary locally and are amended periodically by competent hierarchies, local liturgical books and diocesan or eparchial norms always control actual celebration.

This work is an AI-assisted theological study aid. It carries no ecclesiastical \emph{imprimatur}, \emph{nihil obstat}, or independent human theological review; it is not an official catechism, liturgical book, or canonical opinion, and never substitutes for the Church's official books or competent pastoral and canonical counsel in concrete cases of sacramental validity, liceity, or marriage nullity.
