# Terminology Audit

Canonical record of preferred English theological terms, Hebrew/Greek/Latin source terms, translation choices, and lexical collisions in `src/gemini/theology/angelology/`, synchronized 1-to-1 with `appendices/09-terminology-and-witnesses.tex`.

| Preferred English Term | Source Terms (Hebrew / Greek / Latin) | Meaning in This Treatise | Collisions & Translation Choices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Angel** | *mal'ak* / *angelos* / *angelus* | Strictly a name of *office* ("messenger"), not of nature (which is *spiritus*, Gregory *Hom.* 34.8; Augustine *Enarr. in Ps.* 103); also the proper name of the ninth and lowest angelic order (*CH* 5, 9). | Avoid conflating generic "angel" (any created pure spirit) with the lowest order "Angels" (Triad III.3) or with the pre-incarnate Logos ("Angel of Great Counsel", Is 9:5 LXX). |
| **Demon / Devil** | *satan* / *diabolos*, *daimonion* / *daemon* | Exclusively a fallen, wicked spiritual creature. | Decisive collision with Greek philosophy (Hesiod, Plato, Apuleius), where *daimon* denoted an intermediate or benevolent spirit. Following LXX Ps 95:5 and Augustine (*Civ. Dei* IX.19), "demon" is never used for a good angel. |
| **Dominations** | *Kyriotetes* / *Dominationes* | Fourth order (first of Middle Triad): governing lordship directing ministries. | John Parker (1899) renders *Kyriotetes* as **Lordships**; older English translations also use **Dominions**. Standardized as **Dominations** in exposition, noting Parker's "Lordships" in *CH* quotations. |
| **Virtues** | *Dynameis* / *Virtutes* | Fifth order in Dionysius (Middle Triad.2); seventh in Gregory *Hom.* 34.7: virile strength executing mighty works. | **Critical collision:** Parker (1899) translates Greek *Dynameis* as **Powers**, which collides with Latin *Potestates* (*Exousiai*). Standardized to Latin-traditional **Virtues**, flagging Parker's "Powers" explicitly wherever *CH* 8 is quoted. |
| **Powers** | *Exousiai* / *Potestates* | Sixth order (Middle Triad.3 in Dionysius and Gregory *Hom.* 34.7): harmonious ordering and restraint of hostile forces. | **Critical collision:** Parker (1899) renders *Exousiai* as **Authorities**. Standardized to **Powers**, flagging Parker's "Authorities" explicitly wherever *CH* 8 is quoted. |
| **Principalities** | *Archai* / *Principatus* | Seventh order in Dionysius (Lowest Triad.1); fifth in Gregory *Hom.* 34.7: leadership over nations and public ministry. | Rendered "Princedoms" in some 19th-century patristic translations (Nazianzen *Or.* 28, *Moralia* XXXII). Standardized as **Principalities**. |
| **Subsisting form / Separate substance** | *substantia separata* / *forma subsistens* | An immaterial creature whose essence is pure form without matter, yet composed of essence (potency) and received *esse* (act) (*ST* I q. 50 a. 2; *De ente* chs. 4–5). | Distinguished from Bonaventure's universal hylomorphism (which posits non-corporeal "spiritual matter" to account for creaturely potency). |
| **Aevum** | *aevum* | The proper measure of angelic duration: having a beginning in creation without intrinsic substantial change, intermediate between God's eternity and corporeal time (*ST* I q. 10 a. 5). | Distinct from discrete angelic time (*tempus discretum*), which measures the succession of distinct intellectual acts and local movements (*ST* I q. 53 a. 3). |
| **Virtual contact** | *contactus virtutis* | An angel's presence in a place by applying active spiritual power rather than occupying dimensional space (*ST* I q. 52 a. 1). | Contrasted with dimensive bodily presence (*contactus dimensivus*) and with Scotus's definitive presence independent of operation. |
| **Innate intelligible species** | *species intelligibiles inditae / concreatae* | Intellectual similitudes impressed in the angelic mind by God at creation, enabling knowledge of creatures without abstraction from senses (*ST* I q. 55 a. 2). | Distinct from human abstracted species and from the uncreated Divine Essence seen in the beatific vision (*De ver.* q. 8 a. 5). |
| **Morning and evening knowledge** | *cognitio matutina / vespertina* | St. Augustine's distinction (*Gen. ad litt.* IV; *Civ. Dei* XI.7, 29; *ST* I q. 58 aa. 6–7): knowing creatures in the Word (morning) vs. in their own created nature (evening). | Distinguished from demonic "night" knowledge (which refuses to refer evening knowledge back to praise of the Creator). |
| **Illumination vs. Speech** | *illuminatio* vs. *locutio* | *Illuminatio* strengthens a lower angel's intellect and manifests truths rooted in the First Truth (strictly downward, *ST* I q. 106); *locutio* voluntarily directs an interior concept rooted in created will to another angel (bidirectional and private, *ST* I q. 107). | Avoid collapsing angelic communication into illumination; lower angels speak to higher angels without illuminating them. |
| **Latria vs. Dulia** | *latreia* vs. *douleia* | *Latria* is the supreme adoration and sacrifice due to God alone (Col 2:18; Apoc 19:10); *dulia* is the lawful honor, friendship, and invocation offered to holy angels and saints (*hyperdulia* to Mary). | Crucial guard against both pagan demonolatry/theurgy (Augustine *Civ. Dei* X) and idolatrous angel-worship condemned at Laodicea c. 35. |
| **Apocryphal** | *apokrypha* | Extra-canonical Jewish writings (*1 Enoch*, *Jubilees*, *Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs*) excluded from the biblical canon (Augustine *Civ. Dei* XV.23). | Not to be confused with the deuterocanonical books (such as Tobit), which belong to the Catholic canon of Sacred Scripture. |
| **Pious tradition** | *pia traditio* | Devotional practices, saintly visions, and traditional narratives received in the Church's spiritual life without magisterial definition (e.g., Monte Gargano, saints' angelic visions). | Presented affirmatively as the Church receives it, without debunking and without elevating it to defined dogma. |
| **Potentia absoluta vs. ordinata** | *potentia absoluta / ordinata* | What God could do simply by omnipotence vs. what aligns with His established wisdom and order (used by Aquinas in *De malo* q. 16 a. 5 ad 13 regarding demonic obstinacy). | Prevents confusing the moral/ontological irreversibility of demonic choice with an external limit on divine omnipotence. |
