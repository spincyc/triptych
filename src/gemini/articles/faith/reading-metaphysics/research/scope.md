## 1. Governing Question and Target Competence

Given a difficult passage of classical metaphysics, how can an intelligent adult reader without formal scholastic or symbolic-logic training identify:
1. what is being claimed (in the author's wording and in plain language without flattening distinctions),
2. what the load-bearing words mean in context,
3. what kind of warrant supports each premise (separating source/provenance from warrant and keeping revealed premises labelled),
4. which sentence carries the substantive philosophical burden (separating definition from discovery),
5. what actually follows at licensed strength and what is *not yet established*, and
6. what rejecting the burden costs (distinguishing contradiction, conflict with experience, explanatory cost, rival framework at full strength, and non-assent)?

## 4. Faith/Reason & Authority Discipline

- Revealed premises (e.g. the temporal beginning of the world in *ST* I, q. 46, a. 2) remain explicitly labelled as revelation—neither laundered into natural reason nor faulted for lacking demonstrations they never claimed.
- Natural-reason claims are judged on warrant alone; Aquinas's or Aristotle's authority is never offered as a philosophical warrant.
- Textual correction established from primary witnesses: in *ST* I, q. 2, a. 1, St. John of Damascus (*De Fide Orthodoxa* I, 1, 3) is cited in **Objection 1** (and qualified in Reply 1), whereas the ***sed contra*** cites Aristotle (*Metaph.* IV) and Psalm 52:1; Lesson 2, Worked Passage 2 reports the verified primary text accurately rather than repeating the spec's slip at §13.11.
