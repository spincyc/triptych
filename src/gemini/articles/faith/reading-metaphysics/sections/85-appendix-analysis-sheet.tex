\section{The One-Page Analysis Sheet}

This sheet is the sole structured layout in this guide, printed on a single page so you may copy it or keep it beside an open text. It is a reading habit, not a form to be graded: when you finish writing under the six headings below, you should be able to delete every heading and read your notes as continuous prose.

\begin{tabular}{>{\bfseries\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.20\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.75\linewidth}}
\toprule
Heading & What to Write in Continuous Prose \\
\midrule
PASSAGE & Author, work, exact division (book/chapter or part/question/article), edition or translation; the passage as written. \\
CLAIM & The claim as the author states it, with every qualification intact. \emph{Plain-language reading:} the same claim in ordinary diction, simplifying no distinction. \\
MEANING & Terms carrying a technical sense here; distinctions the argument relies on; any term whose sense shifts between occurrences. Fixed before evaluation begins. \\
WARRANT & Premise by premise, what is offered in support: \emph{definition}, \emph{logical consequence}, \emph{experience}, \emph{prior result}, \emph{metaphysical principle}, \emph{hypothesis}, \emph{revelation}, or \emph{provenance only} (a diagnosis, never a warrant). \\
BURDEN & The first proposition whose truth is not supplied merely by vocabulary or deferred to a prior result. Test: could an intelligent opponent grant all definitions and still deny this? \\
CONSEQUENCE & What follows if the burden is granted, stated at its weakest licensed strength. \emph{Not yet established:} specific propositions commonly claimed here that this passage has not reached. \\
DENIAL & What rejecting the burden costs, classified accurately: \emph{contradiction}, \emph{conflict with experience}, \emph{explanatory cost}, \emph{rival framework} (stated at its strongest), or \emph{non-assent}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\subsection*{Worked Specimen: \emph{Nemo Dat Quod Non Habet}}

\textbf{Passage.} A causal principle invoked across the scholastic tradition: \emph{nemo dat quod non habet}---nothing gives what it does not have.

\textbf{Claim.} Whatever perfection a cause confers on its effect, the cause must itself possess. \emph{Plain-language reading:} you cannot pass on what you have not got; a cause cannot be the source of a feature unless that feature is somehow already in the cause.

\textbf{Meaning.} Everything turns on \emph{have}. Classical expositions distinguish possessing a feature in the same way the effect has it (fire heating water), possessing it in a higher mode, and possessing active power to produce it (an architect building a house). Unless \emph{have} is fixed upfront, the maxim is either plainly false or unfalsifiable. \emph{Give} is likewise technical: being the source of, not transferring a physical quantity.

\textbf{Warrant.} Offered as a \emph{metaphysical principle}, often illustrated by \emph{experience} (heat from fire), yet frequently received as a \emph{definition}. If \emph{have} stretches to mean merely ``is able to produce,'' the principle collapses into ``whatever causes a feature is able to cause it''---true by vocabulary alone and empty of discovery.

\textbf{Burden.} The burden sits inside one word: that \emph{have} carries substantive content restricting how a cause must possess what it confers, rather than collapsing into bare power to produce. Nothing in the vocabulary supplies that restriction.

\textbf{Consequence.} Granting a substantive reading of \emph{have}, a feature cannot arise from a cause wholly lacking any foundation for it. \emph{Not yet established:} that there is a first cause of all perfections, that modes of possession form the hierarchy classical arguments need, or that possessing a perfection in a higher mode is intelligible rather than merely stipulated.

\textbf{Denial.} A thinker holding that genuinely novel properties emerge from causes lacking them incurs neither \emph{contradiction} nor bare \emph{conflict with experience}. He adopts a \textbf{rival framework} (emergentism) carrying a real \textbf{explanatory cost}: he must say what distinguishes cases where features are inherited from cases where novelty appears. Claiming contradiction here overreaches. No formal notation is needed here, because no quantifier or modal scope is obscured---the entire question lives inside the sense of \emph{have}.
