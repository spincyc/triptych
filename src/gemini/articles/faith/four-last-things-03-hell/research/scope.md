# Research Scope — Four Last Things, III: Hell (`Depart from Me`)

## Governing Identity

- **Provider and leaf**: `gemini/articles/faith/four-last-things-03-hell`
- **Document type**: `four-last-things-v1` (kind: `article-faith`; owning profile: `guidance/articles.md` and `guidance/editorial.md`)
- **Series placement**: Part III of the Gemini Four Last Things series (Death, Judgment, Hell, Heaven), written as a standalone discursive Catholic faith essay set in the close of the liturgical year (Last Sunday after Pentecost, All Souls' Day / Requiem, threshold of Advent).
- **Intended reader**: A serious Catholic believer seeking a sober, tradition-inhabiting, source-verified exposition of the Church's doctrine on Hell that moves naturally from Scripture, councils, Fathers, Doctors, and saints into liturgical supplication, examination of conscience, and received vocal prayer.

## Governing Question and Boundaries

1. **In scope**:
   - Christ's own scriptural teaching on Gehenna, unquenchable fire, the undying worm, exterior darkness, the shut door, the fixed chaos, the everlasting sin, and the final sentence (Matt 25:41, 46), together with the apostolic testimony (2 Thess 1:8--9; Heb 10:26--31; 2 Pet 2:4; Jude 1:6, 13; Apoc 14:11; 20:9--10, 14--15; 21:8) and the Roman Catechism's lexical distinction between the limbo of the Fathers, purgatory, and Gehenna proper.
   - The unbroken conciliar and magisterial confession: *Quicumque* (DS 76), Synod of Constantinople (543, can. 9, DS 411), Lateran IV (*Firmiter credimus*, 1215, DS 801), Lyons II (1274, DS 858), Benedict XII (*Benedictus Deus*, 1336, DS 1002), Florence (*Laetentur caeli*, 1439, DS 1306; *Cantate Domino*, 1442, DS 1333, 1351, identifying its exact addressees), Trent (Sess. XIV ch. 4 and can. 5, 1551, DS 1678, 1705), Paul VI (*Solemni hac liturgia*, 1968), *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (1033--1037, 1861), John Paul II (General Audience, 28 July 1999), and Benedict XVI (*Spe salvi* 45--47).
   - The theology of the *poena damni* (pain of loss) as the chief torment and the *poena sensus* (pain of sense), carrying faithfully the patristic and scholastic divergence on the physical nature of hell's fire (Augustine *City of God* XXI.10 and *Supplement* q.97 aa.5--6 vs. John of Damascus *De fide orthodoxa* IV.27) and the open question on the undying worm (Augustine *City of God* XXI.9 vs. *Supplement* q.97 a.2).
   - The eternity of punishment, the obstinacy of the damned will, and the bodily resurrection of the wicked (Augustine, Cyril of Jerusalem, John of Damascus, Gregory the Great, Aquinas, *Supplement* q.98).
   - The narrow gate, the sober patristic/Thomistic teaching on the many and the few (Chrysostom, Aquinas *ST* I q.23 a.7 ad 3) alongside the Church's refusal to fix the ratio or define the damnation of any named individual (noting accurately Scripture and the 1962 Holy Thursday/Good Friday Collect on Judas).
   - The martyrs' watchfulness and the saints' compassion (Polycarp, Ignatius of Antioch, Cyprian, Chrysostom, Ignatius of Loyola, Francis de Sales, Teresa of Avila, Alphonsus Liguori, Fatima).
   - Holy fear as a motion of grace (Augustine *Hom. on 1 John* 9.4--6, Aquinas *ST* II-II q.19, Trent DS 1678, 1705) paired with God's universal salvific will.
   - Roman liturgical supplication (*Hanc igitur*, Litany of the Saints, Requiem Offertory, *Libera me*, *Dies irae*), concrete examination of conscience, and verbatim received vocal prayers.

2. **Material exclusions**:
   - Extended treatments of physical death and ars moriendi (Part I), Particular and General Judgment procedures and signs of the Parousia (Part II), and Purgatory or the Beatific Vision (Part IV), except where directly intersecting the doctrine of hell.
   - Speculative debates on the unbaptized infants' state (limbo of infants) beyond reporting the exact wording of Lyons II (DS 858) and Florence (DS 1306) where they distinguish actual mortal sin from original sin alone.
   - Secular literary afterlives (e.g., Dante's *Inferno* or Milton) and polemical responses to modern non-Catholic universalist systems staged merely for refutation.
   - Modern copyrighted Scripture translations or project-composed vocal prayers.
