## Governing Question
What happens when a creature reflexively apprehends that its own act of being is continuously received from an uncreated source that receives nothing in return, why need that arrest not harden into recoil, and how does consent reach the natural-theological judgment that God loves all existing things?

## Epistemic Order and Structural Boundaries
1. **Strictly philosophical opening (Sections 1–3):**
   - Section 1 establishes the difference between causes of becoming (*causae fiendi*) and a cause of present being (*causa essendi*), engaging existential inertia, brute-fact skepticism, self-causation, and observational underdetermination at full strength without smuggling theological premises.
   - Section 2 analyzes ancient Greek antecedents (Plato's participation and Form of the Good; Aristotle's potency/act, ordered causal series, and Unmoved Mover) in prose without unverified verbatim quotations, anchoring their exact boundary—non-causation of primary matter and absence of a universal cause of existence—in Aquinas, *ST* I, q. 44, a. 2.
   - Section 3 develops Aquinas's real distinction of essence and *esse* (*De Ente et Essentia* ch. 4–5 in Aquinas's own epistemic sequence, withholding divine naming until Step 3 concludes, followed by the ch. 5 *esse commune* clarification; *ST* I, q. 3, a. 4; q. 44, a. 1), explicitly marking it as a classical Thomistic thesis debated by Henry of Ghent, Duns Scotus, and Suárez, and expounds the First Way (*ST* I, q. 2, a. 3) with explicit limits (*per se* vs. *per accidens* causal series in *ST* I, q. 46, a. 2, ad 7; non-demonstrability of a temporal beginning by natural reason in *ST* I, q. 46, a. 2; exclusion of Kalam and Big Bang cosmology proofs).
2. **Conservation, immanence, and the explicit transition to Christian testimony (Section 4):**
   - Extends the metaphysical argument to continuous conservation (*ST* I, q. 104, a. 1), creation as real relation in the creature (*ST* I, q. 45, aa. 2–3), and God innermost in all things (*ST* I, q. 8, a. 1).
   - Marks the transition explicitly from philosophical ascent to Sacred Scripture (Exod. 3:14—paired with *ST* I, q. 13, a. 11 and St. John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* I.9; Ps. 99:3, 103:29–30; Wis. 11:26; Acts 17:27–28; Col. 1:17; Heb. 1:3), patristic testimony (Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* II.34.3–4; Athanasius *Contra Gentes* 41–42; Augustine *De Gen. ad litt.* IV.12.22–23, *Confessions* III.6.11, *City of God* XI.10, XII.2, XII.25), solemn conciliar dogma (Fourth Lateran *Firmiter credimus* DS 800; Vatican I *Dei Filius* ch. 1 and can. 5 *De Deo rerum omnium creatore*), and doctrinal paraphrase of CCC 300–301.
3. **First-person arrest, asymmetry, recoil, and noncompetitive agency (Sections 5–6):**
   - Turns universal metaphysics into reflexive first-person recognition anchored in Augustine's *Confessions* VII.10.16, VII.11.17, VII.17.23 and Gregory Nazianzen's *Or.* 28.21.
   - Expounds the mixed/one-sided real relation (*ST* I, q. 13, a. 7) and refutes four misreadings (unreal divine knowledge, fictional conservation/lordship, cold indifference, inert column literalism).
   - States recoil at full existential weight and resolves it via noncompetitive primary/secondary causality (*ST* I, q. 105, a. 5), concurrence without divine authorship of sin's defect (*ST* I-II, q. 79, a. 2; Augustine *City of God* XII.7), the harmony of humility (*ST* II-II, q. 161, aa. 1–3) and magnanimity (*ST* II-II, q. 129, aa. 1, 3, ad 4), and Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* IV.39.2.
4. **Consent, divine freedom, analogical love, apophatic restraint, and the natural-reason halt (Sections 7–8):**
   - Works divine freedom in creating (*ST* I, q. 19, a. 3; *Dei Filius* ch. 1, can. 5; Augustine *City of God* XI.24), wisdom/justice/mercy at the root of creation (*ST* I, q. 21, aa. 1, 4), and the analogical judgment that God loves all existing things (*ST* I, q. 20, a. 2; q. 13, a. 5; Wis. 11:25–27).
   - Pairs affirmative natural theology with apophatic restraint (Aquinas *ST* I, q. 3, a. 4, ad 2; q. 12, aa. 12–13; q. 13, a. 5; Gregory Nazianzen *Or.* 28.4–6, 17 and *Or.* 38.7–8; Pseudo-Dionysius *Celestial Hierarchy* II.3, IV.1, historically identified as a late fifth- or early sixth-century Christian author).
   - Grounds both the positive reach and exact ceiling of natural reason in *ST* I, q. 32, a. 1 and Vatican I *Dei Filius* ch. 2, can. 1 *De revelatione*, and ch. 4 without attempting to demonstrate the Trinity or other revealed mysteries, and names adoration (*ST* II-II, q. 84, a. 1; Matt. 4:10; Rom. 11:33–36) as the lived threshold toward Part II (*The Due Return*).

## Explicit Exclusions
- Kalam cosmological arguments and empirical Big Bang temporal-origin proofs.
- Purported natural-reason demonstrations of the Trinity, Incarnation, or supernatural grace.
- Detailed liturgical, sacrificial, or covenantal theology of worship (reserved for Part II) and supernatural revelation/grace (reserved for Part III).
- Unverified quotations from editions not tracked in the repository (Plato, Aristotle outside Aquinas's tracked quotations, Henry of Ghent, Duns Scotus, Suárez, Hume, Russell, and copyrighted CCC text).

## Editorial Synthesis Disclosure
The organizing terms *ontological vertigo*, *arrest*, *recoil*, and *consent* are original source-grounded editorial synthesis used to articulate the existential reception of received being; this status is disclosed directly in Appendix B (`sections/90-scope-method-qualifications.tex`).
