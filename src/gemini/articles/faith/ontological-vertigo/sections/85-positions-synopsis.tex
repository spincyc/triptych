\section{Synopsis of Metaphysical Distinctions and Doctrinal Status}

The two tables below consolidate the governing conceptual distinctions worked through the argument and classify the epistemic and doctrinal standing of its major claims.

\subsection*{Core Philosophical and Theological Distinctions}

\begin{threestable}{Distinction}{Primary Anchors}{Analytical Function}
Cause of becoming (\emph{causa fiendi}) vs.\ cause of being (\emph{causa essendi}) & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~104, a.~1; Augustine, \emph{De Gen.\ ad litt.} IV.12.22--23 & Separates finite agents that rearrange pre-existing matter (builder and house) from the uncreated cause whose continuous act sustains existence itself (sun and lit air).\\
\emph{Per se} vs.\ \emph{per accidens} causal series & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~2, a.~3; q.~46, a.~2, ad~7 & Distinguishes simultaneous, hierarchical dependence (staff moved by hand), where infinite regress is impossible, from successive temporal series (father begetting son), where natural reason cannot exclude an infinite past.\\
Essence (\emph{essentia}) vs.\ act of existence (\emph{esse}) & Aquinas, \emph{De Ente et Essentia}, ch.~4--5; \emph{ST} I, q.~3, a.~4; q.~44, a.~1 & Grounds the difference between unreceived subsistent being (\emph{ipsum esse subsistens}) and finite beings by participation; distinguishes divine simplicity from indeterminate universal being (\emph{esse commune}).\\
Real relation vs.\ relation of reason (mixed relation) & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~13, a.~7; q.~45, a.~3 & Secures radical ontological asymmetry: creatures are really ordered to God, whereas no real accidental relation of need or passive change accrues to God.\\
Competitive vs.\ noncompetitive causality & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~105, a.~5; I-II, q.~79, a.~2; Augustine, \emph{De Civ.\ Dei} XII.7 & Refutes zero-sum rivalry and occasionalism: God as Primary Cause constitutes rather than erases secondary creaturely agency, while moral defect arises solely from the deficient created will.\\
Humility vs.\ magnanimity & Aquinas, \emph{ST} II-II, q.~161, aa.~1--3; q.~129, aa.~1, 3, ad~4 & Reconciles truthful non-aseity (humility grounded in subjection to God and recognition of one's own defect) with truthful nobility of aim (magnanimity honoring real gifts held from God).\\
Responsive human love vs.\ creative divine love & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~19, a.~3; q.~20, a.~2; q.~21, aa.~1, 4; Wis.~11:25--27 & Contrasts creaturely love, elicited by pre-existing goodness or need, with analogical divine love, which freely infuses and creates goodness without seeking return.\\
\end{threestable}

\subsection*{Epistemic Order and Doctrinal Classification}

\begin{threestable}{Claim Family}{Epistemic / Doctrinal Standing}{Principal Sources and Limits}
Existence of one uncreated, purely actual First Cause of being & Demonstrable by natural reason; confirmed by Catholic faith (Vatican~I, \emph{Dei Filius}, ch.~2, can.~1 \emph{De revelatione}) & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~2, a.~3; q.~3, a.~4; q.~12, a.~12; Rom.~1:20. Establishes \emph{that} God is and what pertains to him as First Cause, not \emph{what} he is in essence.\\
Real distinction of essence and \emph{esse} in creatures & Adopted Thomistic metaphysical thesis (school theology; not binding dogma) & Aquinas, \emph{De Ente}, ch.~4--5; \emph{ST} I, q.~3, a.~4. Catholic schools (Henry of Ghent, Scotus, Su\'{a}rez) dispute the mode of distinction while agreeing on total creaturely dependence.\\
Creation \emph{ex nihilo}, radical distinction of God from the world, absolute divine freedom, and continuous providence & Solemn dogmatic teaching of ecumenical councils & Fourth Lateran Council, \emph{Firmiter credimus} (DS~800); First Vatican Council, \emph{Dei Filius}, ch.~1 and can.~5 \emph{De Deo rerum omnium creatore}; patristic testimony in Irenaeus, Athanasius, and Augustine; summarized doctrinally in CCC~300--301.\\
Temporal beginning of the world & Held by divine faith; not demonstrably proved by natural reason (on Aquinas's judgment) & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~46, a.~2; Lateran~IV, \emph{Firmiter credimus} (\emph{ab initio temporis}). Kalam and physical-cosmology proofs are excluded.\\
\emph{Ontological vertigo}, \emph{arrest}, \emph{recoil}, and \emph{consent} & Source-grounded editorial synthesis & Phenomenological framing anchored in Augustine's \emph{Confessions} VII.10--17, Gregory Nazianzen's \emph{Or.}~28.21 and 38.7--8, Irenaeus's \emph{Adv.\ Haer.}~IV.39.2, and Aquinas's metaphysics of relation and virtue.\\
God loves all existing things by willing and causing their good & Natural-theological analogical judgment harmonized with Scripture & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~13, a.~5; q.~20, a.~2; Wis.~11:25--27. Predicated analogically without attributing passion, need, or creature-caused change to God.\\
Trinity of Persons, Incarnation, and supernatural elevation by grace & Strictly supernatural mysteries known only through divine revelation & Aquinas, \emph{ST} I, q.~32, a.~1; Vatican~I, \emph{Dei Filius}, ch.~4. Natural reason halts at the unity of divine essence and the threshold of adoration (\emph{ST} II-II, q.~84, a.~1).\\
\end{threestable}
