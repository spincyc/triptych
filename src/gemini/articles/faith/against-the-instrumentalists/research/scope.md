## Governing Question
Does the extraordinary success of physical instruments and empirical testing warrant the claim that only instrument-detectable realities exist or that only empirically verifiable propositions qualify as knowledge — and once that exclusivism fails under scrutiny, what rational standing remains for metaphysical reasoning from sensible effects toward God?

## Inclusions
1. Genuine appreciation of physical instrumentation and sharp delimitation of the coined target (*instrumental exclusivism*), clearly separated from classical philosophy-of-science instrumentalism (Mach, Duhem) and constructive empiricism (van Fraassen), ordinary empiricism, methodological naturalism, and working scientists as such.
2. Metrological and epistemological anatomy of physical measurement: indication versus measurement result, measurand definition, calibration chains, background physical models, logic and mathematics, memory, testimony, moral norms, rational judgment, and the cause-versus-ground distinction corroborated via Chesterton (*Orthodoxy*, chs. 2–3) and Newman (*Parochial and Plain Sermons*, Vol. 1, Sermon XV).
3. Accurate intellectual history of the exclusive criterion from Hume's Fork and Comte's positivism (including Lesson 19 on stellar chemistry) through the Vienna Circle, A. J. Ayer (1936, 1946), Karl Popper's demarcation correction, Alonzo Church's 1949 formal triviality proof, Carl Hempel's 1950 survey, and W. V. O. Quine's 1951 holism.
4. Rigorous self-application testing under three serious formulations (substantive truth claim, stipulative convention, metalinguistic ascent / inductive track record), analysis of "measurable in principle," and explicit boundary stating that collapsing exclusivism clears space for reason without automatically validating unmeasured claims or proving God's existence.
5. Classical Catholic philosophical epistemology: Aquinas (*ST* I, q. 84, aa. 6–7; q. 79, a. 3; q. 85, a. 1; q. 2, aa. 2–3; q. 3, a. 1; q. 12, aa. 12–13; q. 1, a. 8), Augustine (*De Trinitate* III.3; *Confessions* X.6), and Newman (*Parochial and Plain Sermons*, Vol. 1, Sermon XVII).
6. Scriptural and Catholic magisterial teaching across a clearly marked epistemic seam: Wisdom 11:21, 13:1–9; Romans 1:18–25; Vatican I (*Dei Filius*, chs. 1, 2 & 4, Canons I.2 & II.1); Leo XIII (*Aeterni Patris*); St. Pius X (*Pascendi Dominici Gregis*); Vatican II (*Gaudium et spes* 36, 57); *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (31–39); and St. John Paul II (*Fides et ratio* 55, 67, 83, 88).
7. Diagnosis of contemporary scientistic residue (Hawking/Mlodinow, Krauss, fact/value scientism) and restoration of physical instruments within an integrated rational order rejecting both scientism and fideism.
8. Terminal Positions Synopsis appendix (`sections/85-positions-synopsis.tex`), Scope, Method, and Qualifications appendix, and complete used-only References.
