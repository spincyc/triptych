\section{Synopsis of Objections and Replies}

The table below gathers the principal objections against Christian particularity across the metaphysical, historical, cosmological, pluralistic, sacramental, and moral fronts, isolating the hidden premise each carries, the Catholic reply and its primary witnesses, and the epistemic boundary that remains open to mystery or faith.

\textbf{Metaphysical disdain for singulars} &
Perfection scales with generality; matter and individuation are falls from intelligibility. &
Only singulars subsist in reality; abstraction is a finite human cognitive economy (\textit{ST} I, qq.\,85--86; \textit{De ente}, c.\,3). God is not \latin{esse commune} or a genus, but the universal cause of every singular's being, knowing and governing each individual directly (\textit{De ente}, c.\,5; \textit{ST} I, q.\,3, a.\,5; q.\,8, a.\,1; q.\,14, a.\,11; q.\,22, aa.\,2--4; q.\,29, aa.\,1, 3). &
Metaphysics shows particular divine action is coherent; school theories of individuation (Aquinas vs.\ Scotus) remain open; divine naming stays analogical. \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Historical ditch of contingent dates} &
Eternal saving truth cannot depend on contingent events in one corner of history (Celsus, Lessing, Kant). &
Revelation unfolds through concrete deeds and words in history (\textit{Dei Verbum} 2--4; Luke 2--3); because the subject of Christ's finite acts is the eternal Word, dated events possess universal efficacy (Leo, \textit{Letter 28}; \textit{Fides et ratio} 11--12; \textit{Dominus Iesus} 6, 13--15). &
Historical probability does not force faith; unconditional assent crosses a real gap enabled by interior grace, not pure deduction. \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Biblical election as tribal favoritism} &
Choosing one people implies divine partiality or mere natural political fortune (Spinoza). &
Abram is called so all kindreds of the earth may be blessed (Gen 12:1--3); Deuteronomy rejects antecedent greatness and binds election to love of the stranger (Deut 7:6--8; 10:14--19); prophets and Paul affirm universal mission and Israel's irrevocable call (Amos 9:7; Isa 49:6; Rom 11:29; \textit{Nostra aetate} 4). &
Why God ordered covenants through Israel's specific timetable rather than another remains an unsearchable counsel of providence (Rom 11:33). \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Why one singular human body rather than universal humanity or cosmic display?} &
A universal savior should assume humanity in general or manifest through grander celestial bodies. &
Human nature in the abstract exists only in thought; assuming it would be a fictitious Incarnation, while assuming all individuals would erase human persons and redemptive suffering for others (\textit{ST} III, q.\,4, aa.\,4--5). Christ is universal Savior by causality, and came as physician to heal fallen man, not to dazzle (\textit{On the Incarnation} \S\S\,41--44; Irenaeus, \textit{AH} III.18, 22). &
The Incarnation is shown fitting (\latin{conveniens}) in retrospect, never logically necessitated or deduced by natural reason alone (\textit{ST} III, q.\,1, a.\,1). \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Cosmic scale and smallness of earth} &
Ontological significance scales with spatial volume, mass, or galactic centrality (Volusianus, Paine). &
``God is great not in mass, but in might'' (Augustine, \textit{Ep.}\ 137 in \textit{ST} III, q.\,1, a.\,1, ad 4). One rational person capable of truth and love outranks lifeless cosmic immensity (Ps 8:4--6; Gregory Nazianzen, \textit{Oration 38}, \S\,11; Wis 11:21, 25, 27). &
Scripture neither affirms nor excludes rational life elsewhere in the cosmos; the scale of creation displays divine prodigality without resolving every cosmological question. \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Religious pluralism and geography of birth} &
Unique historical mediation makes salvation hostage to birthplace or denies grace outside Christianity (Hick). &
Christ's coming reaches all ages (Irenaeus, \textit{AH} IV.22.2; Augustine, \textit{City of God} X.32); the Spirit offers association with the Paschal mystery in ways known to God (\textit{Gaudium et spes} 22; \textit{Lumen gentium} 16; \textit{Nostra aetate} 2), always sourced in Christ's one mediation without indifferentism (\textit{Redemptoris missio} 5--6, 28; \textit{Dominus Iesus} 13--15). &
The uneven geographic and temporal spread of explicit Gospel preaching remains a genuine pastoral and providential mystery (\textit{City of God} X.32). \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Material sacraments vs.\ spiritual worship} &
Water, oil, and bread are crude material crutches unworthy of worship in spirit and truth. &
Visible signs fit embodied knowers, humble pride, and extend the Incarnation after the Ascension (Leo, \textit{Sermon 74}, \S\,2; Augustine, \textit{City of God} X.5--6; \textit{Tractate 80}, \S\,3; \textit{ST} III, qq.\,60--61). In the Eucharist, substantial presence makes the whole Christ universally available on every altar without local confinement (\textit{ST} III, q.\,75, a.\,1; q.\,76, a.\,3). &
God has not bound his power to the sacraments (\textit{ST} III, q.\,64, a.\,7); real Eucharistic presence rests on divine authority received in faith alone (\textit{ST} III, q.\,75, a.\,1). \\[0.4em]

\textbf{Abstract love of humanity vs.\ concrete neighbor} &
Universal benevolence suffices without tangled obligations to inconvenient individuals. &
Claiming to love the unseen God while hating the seen brother is a lie (1 John 4:20); the same Christ who consecrates the Eucharist identifies himself with the hungry, naked, and imprisoned neighbor (Matt 25:35--40; Chrysostom, \textit{Homily 50 on Matthew}, \S\S\,4--5). &
Charity demands concrete discernment in particular circumstances; liturgical beauty and material mercy belong together without false rivalry. \\
