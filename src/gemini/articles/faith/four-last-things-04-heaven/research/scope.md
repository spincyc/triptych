# Research Scope — Four Last Things, IV: Heaven (`Come, Ye Blessed`)

## Governing Identity

- **Provider and leaf**: `gemini/articles/faith/four-last-things-04-heaven`
- **Document type**: `four-last-things-v1` (kind: `article-faith`; owning profile: `guidance/articles.md` and `guidance/editorial.md`)
- **Series placement**: Part IV of the Gemini Four Last Things series (Death, Judgment, Hell, Heaven), completing the quartet as the radiant counterpart to Part III (`Depart from Me`), written as a standalone discursive Catholic faith study grounded in the Church's liturgical threshold of heaven (Holy Mass, Roman Canon, Feast of All Saints, *Te Deum*).
- **Intended reader**: A serious Catholic believer seeking a luminous, source-grounded exposition of the Church's doctrine on Heaven, the Beatific Vision, the resurrection and endowments of the glorified body, the communion of Mary, angels, and saints, the renewal of creation, and the theological virtue of hope, culminating in liturgical contemplation, examination of conscience, and received vocal prayer.

## Governing Question and Boundaries

1. **In scope**:
   - The restless human heart and Christ's promise of the kingdom prepared from the foundation of the world (Matt 25:34; John 14:2–3; John 17:3; 1 Cor 2:9; 1 Cor 13:12; 1 John 3:2; St. Augustine *Confessions* I.1 and *Hom. on 1 John* IV.6; St. Thomas Aquinas *ST* I-II qq. 1–5 on why no created good suffices as man's beatitude).
   - The Church's dogmatic confession of immediate entry into heavenly glory and clear, intuitive, face-to-face vision of the Divine Essence: Fourth Lateran Council (*Firmiter credimus*, 1215, DS 800–801), Pope Benedict XII (*Benedictus Deus*, 1336, DS 1000–1001), Council of Florence (*Laetentur caeli*, 1439, DS 1305), *Roman Catechism* (Creed Art. XII), and *Catechism of the Catholic Church* (§§1023–1029).
   - The theology of the *lumen gloriae*, divine incomprehensibility, and the vision of the Trinity: Council of Vienne (*Ad nostrum*, 1312, DS 895), Fourth Lateran Council (*Firmiter credimus*, DS 800), First Vatican Council (*Dei Filius*, 1870, ch. 1, DS 3001), St. Augustine (*De Trinitate* III.11.21–22; *Tractates on John* 105.3), St. Gregory of Nazianzus (*Orations* 16.9, 28.3), St. Thomas Aquinas (*ST* I q. 12 aa. 2, 4–7) and *Supplement* q. 92 a. 1 (non-mediated union of God's essence as intelligible form, seeing God wholly without exhaustive comprehension), and St. Bernard of Clairvaux (*De diligendo Deo* ch. 10, the fourth degree of love).
   - Inequality of glory without envy ("many mansions", John 14:2; 1 Cor 15:41; Florence DS 1305; Trent Sess. VI can. 32 DS 1582; St. Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* V.36.2; Aquinas *ST* I q. 12 a. 6; *Supplement* q. 93 aa. 2–3 and q. 96 on the three *aureoles*; St. Augustine *Tractates on John* 67.2 and *City of God* XXII.30).
   - The resurrection of the body of the just (Job 19:25–27; Ezech 37; Dan 12:2–3; 1 Cor 15; Lateran IV DS 801; St. Cyril of Jerusalem *Cat.* 18.3; St. John of Damascus *De fide orth.* IV.27; St. Augustine *City of God* XXII.19 on the radiant scars of the martyrs; Aquinas *ST* III q. 54 a. 4 and I-II q. 4 aa. 5–6 alongside the open question in *Supplement* q. 93 a. 1 vs. Aquinas's mature retraction; St. Bernard *De diligendo Deo* ch. 11).
   - The four endowments (*dotes*) of the glorified body — impassibility, subtlety, agility, and clarity — rooted in directly inspected tracked witnesses: 1 Cor 15:42–44, Matt 13:43, Phil 3:21, the *Roman Catechism* (Creed Art. XI), Aquinas (*ST* III q. 54 aa. 1–2), St. Cyril of Jerusalem (*Cat.* 18.18), St. Augustine (*City of God* XXII.21, 29–30), and *Supplement* q. 91 a. 1.
   - The Communion of Saints, the Nine Choirs of Angels (Heb 12:22–24; Apoc 7:9–17; St. Gregory the Great *Homilies on the Gospels* 34.6–10; Aquinas *ST* I q. 108 aa. 1, 5), the Assumption and Queenship of the Blessed Virgin Mary (Apoc 12:1; Pope Pius XII *Munificentissimus Deus* §44 [DS 3903] and *Ad Caeli Reginam*), and accidental beatitude in the fellowship of the blessed (*ST* I-II q. 4 a. 8; *Roman Catechism* I.12; *Supplement* q. 94 a. 1; St. Francis de Sales *Introduction to a Devout Life* I.16; St. Alphonsus *Preparation for Death* XXIX).
   - The renewal of creation — new heavens and a new earth (Isa 65:17, 66:22; Rom 8:18–22; 2 Pet 3:13; Apoc 21–22; St. Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* V.36.1; St. Augustine *City of God* XX.16; Venerable Bede *Explanation of the Apocalypse* chs. 21–22; *Supplement* q. 91 aa. 1, 3; CCC 1042, 1048).
   - The theological virtue of hope transforming earthly life (Aquinas *ST* II-II q. 17 aa. 1–2, 5; q. 18 a. 2; q. 20 a. 1; q. 21 a. 1; St. Augustine *Confessions* IX.10.25 Ostia, *City of God* XXII.30 *vacabimus et videbimus*, *Hom. on 1 John* IV.7; St. Teresa of Avila *Life* chs. 38, 40; St. Francis de Sales *Introduction to a Devout Life* I.16–17; St. Alphonsus *Preparation for Death* XXIX).
   - Liturgical foretaste of heaven in the Holy Mass (*Sursum corda*, Preface, *Sanctus*, Roman Canon *Communicantes*, *Supplices te rogamus*, *Nobis quoque peccatoribus*), Feast of All Saints propers, the classical pre-1955 Roman Breviary Bede homily for the Second Day within the Octave of All Saints (2 November, Bute 1879), examination of conscience, and received vocal prayers (*Te Deum laudamus* Latin & English, Collect of All Saints Latin & English, and St. Alphonsus's prayer on Heaven).

2. **Material exclusions**:
   - Extended treatments of physical death, *ars moriendi*, and last rites (Part I), Particular and General Judgment procedures and full Purgatory doctrine (Part II), and Hell (Part III), except where directly intersecting the doctrine of Heaven.
   - Secular literary visions (e.g., Dante's *Paradiso* or Milton) and modern non-Catholic reconstructions of heaven staged merely for polemical refutation.
   - Modern copyrighted Scripture translations or project-composed vocal prayers.
