## Governing Question
Once Part I has established received being and the Creator's sustaining presence and Part II has named what justice owes to God and neighbor, what stands at the end of every human *why*, how does infused charity elevate obedient justice into filial friendship with God amid hope, prayer, dryness, Eucharistic desire, suffering, and neighbor-love, and what consummation completes creaturehood without dissolving it?

## Intended Reader
Intelligent adult readers—practicing Catholics and serious inquirers alike—engaging classical Catholic theological, patristic, and spiritual sources without requiring prior graduate formation in scholastic terminology.

## Doctrinal and Epistemic Scope
1. **The Last End and the Examination of Created Goods:** *ST* I-II, q. 1, aa. 4, 6–8; q. 2, aa. 1–8; St. Augustine, *Confessions* I.1.1; *CCC* 1718 (paraphrase).
2. **Beatific Vision Beyond Nature and the Epistemic Seam to Revelation:** *ST* I-II, q. 3, a. 8; q. 5, a. 5 (replies 1–2); *ST* I, q. 12, aa. 1–2, 4–5, 7 (*lumen gloriae*, deiformity, divine incomprehensibility); explicit marking of the seam between philosophical ascent and divine revelation (First Vatican Council, *Dei Filius*, ch. 2 & Canon II.3; Ps 26:8 DR / 27:8 Heb.; 1 Cor 2:9; 1 Cor 13:12; 1 John 3:1–2; Pope Benedict XII, *Benedictus Deus*, DS 1000–1002 in paraphrase; *CCC* 1023–1029 in paraphrase). Natural desire is explicitly denied to create any exigency binding God.
3. **Commanded Love, the New Law, and Charity as Friendship:** *ST* I-II, qq. 106–108 (New Law chiefly the grace of the Holy Spirit, secondarily written Gospel, moral precepts, and sacraments—never antinomian); *ST* II-II, q. 23, aa. 1, 6, 8 and q. 24, a. 2 (charity as infused *amicitia* founded on shared beatitude, form and mother of the virtues); transformation of servile fear into filial reverence (*ST* II-II, q. 19, aa. 2, 4, 6, 10–11; St. Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* IV.13.1–4, 14.1).
4. **Hope, Petitionary Prayer, Dryness, and the Meantime:** *ST* II-II, q. 17, aa. 1–2; q. 20, a. 1 (despair); q. 21, a. 1 (presumption); *Spe salvi* (cataloged paraphrase); *ST* II-II, q. 83, a. 2 (petitionary prayer as secondary causality under immutable providence, cross-referencing *ST* I, q. 19, aa. 7–8; q. 22, aa. 2, 4); four modes of divine presence (*ST* I, q. 8, a. 3; q. 43, a. 3; Eucharistic presence; sensible consolation); verified spiritual doctors on dryness: St. Teresa of Ávila (*Life* chs. 8, 11), St. Francis de Sales (*Introduction to a Devout Life* Part IV, chs. 13–14), and St. Thérèse of Lisieux (*Story of a Soul*, Taylor ed., chs. 8–10).
5. **Eucharistic Desire, Suffering Within Grace, and Love of Neighbor:** *ST* III, q. 73, a. 3; q. 79, aa. 1, 8; q. 80, aa. 1, 4 (*Ecclesia de Eucharistia*, Trent Sess. XIII, *CCC* 1324, 1385, 1415 in paraphrase), preserving worthy sacramental reception and Reconciliation for known grave sin; suffering as an evil, resisting removable suffering (St. Francis de Sales, *Introduction to a Devout Life* Part III, ch. 3, collated with 1832 French text), free union of unavoidable suffering with Christ's complete Passion (Col 1:24), strict theology of merit (*ST* I-II, q. 114, aa. 1, 4–5; Trent Sess. VI; *CCC* 2007–2011 in paraphrase—pain itself has zero merit, first grace cannot be merited, self-harm and passivity before injustice excluded); love of neighbor as the inseparable test and fruit (1 John 4:20–21; *ST* II-II, q. 25, a. 1; St. Thérèse ch. 11).
6. **Bernard's Degrees of Love and the Everlasting Sabbath:** St. Bernard of Clairvaux, *On Loving God* (Patmore 1884, chs. 1–2, 7–11); created participation (*deiform*, 2 Pet 1:4) strictly bounded against absorption or essence-identity (*Dei Filius* ch. 1, Canons I.3–4; "God is charity" never reversed); St. Augustine, *City of God* XXII.30 (Dods 1871 & CSEL 40.2); deliberate trilogy close.
