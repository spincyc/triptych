# Scope: The Four Last Things, Part II — The Books Were Opened (Judgment and Purgatory)

## Purpose and Intended Reader

Part II of Gemini's *Four Last Things* quartet (`four-last-things-02-judgment`) presents a discursive, source-grounded Catholic study of Particular Judgment, General Judgment, and Purgatory for serious Catholic readers. Moving from Sacred Scripture and solemn conciliar definitions through the Fathers, Scholastic and post-Tridentine Doctors, the Requiem liturgy, and the saints, it concludes with a concrete five-point examination of conscience and received prayers verbatim from public-domain witnesses.

## Thematic Boundaries and Zero-Overlap Coordination

- **Owned by Part II (*The Books Were Opened*)**:
  1. Christ's universal judiciary power as God and Man, merited by His Passion, and His glorious wounds (John 5:22–29; Acts 10:42; 17:31; 2 Cor 5:10; *ST* III q. 59; *Supplement* q. 90; *Roman Catechism* Art. VII).
  2. Particular Judgment immediately after death and solemn definitions of immediate retribution (Heb 9:27; Luke 16:19–31; 23:43; Profession of Faith of Pope Clement IV sworn at Lyons II DS 856–858; *Benedictus Deus* DS 1000–1002; *Laetentur caeli* DS 1304–1306; CCC 1021–1022; St. Francis de Sales I.14; St. Alphonsus Cons. XXIV).
  3. Fittingness of both Particular and General Judgment (*ST* III q. 59 a. 5; *Supplement* q. 88 a. 1; *Roman Catechism* Art. VII four reasons; St. Augustine *City of God* XX.1–2; CCC 1039–1040).
  4. Christ's return, the Sign of the Cross, the opened books of conscience, the saints as judicial assessors, and the separation of Matt 25:31–46 (Lateran IV DS 801; St. Augustine *City of God* XX.5, 14–15; St. Cyril of Jerusalem *Cat.* 15; *Supplement* qq. 87–89; St. Gregory *Moralia* XXIV.8.16; St. Alphonsus Cons. XXV).
  5. Purgatory in Scripture and magisterial confession/definitions (2 Mach 12:43–46; 1 Cor 3:11–15; Matt 12:32; Matt 5:25–26; Profession of Faith at Lyons II DS 856; Florence DS 1304; Trent Sess. VI can. 30 DS 1580, Sess. XXII ch. 2 & can. 3 DS 1743, 1753, Sess. XXV DS 1820; CCC 1030–1032; *Spe salvi* 46–48).
  6. Distinction between eternal guilt (*reatus culpae*) and temporal punishment (*reatus poenae temporalis*), mortal vs. venial sin, and lingering wounds of sin (2 Kings 12:13–14; *ST* I-II q. 87 aa. 1, 3–8; III q. 86 aa. 1, 4–5; *Roman Catechism* Satisfaction; Augustine *City of God* XXI.13, 24, 26; Bellarmine on Ps 37).
  7. Nature of purgatorial purification: confirmation in grace, certainty of salvation, love, Florence's deliberate restraint on material fire (Archbishop Bessarion of Nicaea, Mark of Ephesus, and the Latin doctors), St. Robert Bellarmine on Ps 65:12, St. Catherine of Genoa *Treatise on Purgatory* II, and *Spe salvi* §47.
  8. Communion of Saints, four classical suffrages (especially Mass), Indulgences from the Treasury of the Church applied *per modum suffragii*, and current post-1967 Latin-Church indulgence discipline (St. Cyril *Cat.* XXIII.9–10; St. John Chrysostom *1 Cor.* 41.8 & *Phil.* 3; St. Augustine *Confessions* IX.11–13; Trent Sess. XXV DS 1835; Pope St. Paul VI *Indulgentiarum doctrina* §5, Norms 1, 3–5, 7, 20; 1983 *Code of Canon Law* cann. 992–997; *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum* 4th ed. Norm 20; CCC 1471–1479; *Baltimore Catechism No. 3* Qq. 839–845, 853–854, 861, 863, 1384–1385).
  9. Harmony of divine justice and mercy in Christ the Judge (*ST* I q. 21 aa. 1–4; Bellarmine on Ps 84:10–12; St. Gregory *Moralia* XIV.58.79; steering between presumption and despair; 1 Cor 11:31–32).
  10. Requiem liturgy (*Memento etiam*, Introit, Gradual, Tract *Absolve, Domine*, *Dies irae* strophes 1–8 Latin & W. J. Irons stanzas 1–4 English, *De profundis* & Bellarmine on Ps 129, *Fidelium, Deus* Latin & 1915 received English, and St. Alphonsus Cons. XXV prayer on Judgment).
- **Deferred to sibling parts**:
  - Physical death, *ars moriendi*, and Last Rites belong to Part I (*Into Thy Hands*).
  - Full treatise on Hell, *Dies irae* strophes 9–17, *Hanc igitur*, *Domine Jesu Christe*, and *Libera me* belong to Part III (*Depart from Me*).
  - Full treatise on the Beatific Vision, the four endowments of glorified bodies, and the New Heavens and New Earth belongs to Part IV (*Come, Ye Blessed*).

## Editorial and Theological Guardrails

- Distinguish clearly in prose and Appendix A between *de fide* definitions, ordinary magisterial doctrine, *sententia communis*, and open theological questions (especially the physical nature of purgatorial fire, duration/mode of purgation, and vocal vs. mental conduct and exact place of the General Judgment).
- Reproduce all vocal prayers verbatim from verified public-domain witnesses; never paraphrase or compose liturgical prayers.
- Attribute the *Supplementum Tertiae Partis* transparently as posthumously compiled from St. Thomas Aquinas's *Scriptum super Sententiis* (Book IV).
- Keep all citations exact to Challoner Douay-Rheims and Clementine Vulgate numbering.
