## Governing Question Under Test

Does Genesis 15's narrated sequence require—or textually establish—that God led Abram outside to look up and number the stars (Gen 15:5) under a sunlit daytime sky before the sunset reported in Gen 15:12 and 15:17, as a widespread modern preaching and devotional motif claims?

## Passage and Canonical Boundaries

- **Primary passage**: Genesis 15:1–21, focusing on the chronological joints at 15:1 (*ba-maḥazeh* / *per visionem*), 15:5 (*wayyōṣēʾ ʾōtō ha-ḥūṣāh... habbēṭ-nāʾ ha-shāmaymāh ūsfōr ha-kōkāvīm*), 15:7 (covenant transition), 15:9–11 (sacrificial preparation and birds), 15:12 (*wayehī ha-shemesh lāvōʾ*), 15:17 (*wayehī ha-shemesh bāʾāh wa-ʿalāṭāh hāyāh*), and 15:18 (*ba-yyōm ha-hūʾ*).
- **Old Testament star-promise recurrences**: Gen 13:16 (contrast: dust of the earth), 22:17, 26:4; Exod 32:13; Deut 1:10, 10:22, 28:62; 1 Par 27:23; 2 Esdr (Neh) 9:23.
- **New Testament echoes**: Rom 4:3, 18–22; Gal 3:6–9; Heb 11:1, 11–12; plus Phil 2:15 and 1 Cor 15:41 where cited by St. Bede.

## Witness Families Examined

1. **Biblical text and ancient versions**: Masoretic Hebrew (Kittel 1909), Septuagint (Swete 1909, Alexandrinus across Vaticanus's opening lacuna Gen 1:1–46:28a; Brenton 1844 Sixtine English), Clementine Vulgate and Challoner Douay-Rheims, Syriac Peshitta, Targum Onkelos, Targum Pseudo-Jonathan (Etheridge 1862).
2. **Second Temple Jewish retellings**: *Book of Jubilees* 14:1–20 (Charles 1913), Philo of Alexandria (*Quis rerum divinarum heres sit* §§75–89, Yonge 1854), Flavius Josephus (*Antiquitates Judaicae* I.10.3, Whiston 1737).
3. **Rabbinic and medieval/early-modern Jewish exegesis**: Babylonian Talmud (*Shabbat* 156a, *Pesachim* 6b), *Bereshit Rabbah* 44:12, Rashi, Abraham Ibn Ezra, Moses Maimonides (*Guide* II.46), David Kimhi (Radak), Nachmanides (Ramban), Hezekiah ben Manoah (Chizkuni), Bahya ben Asher, Jacob ben Asher (*Tur HaArokh*), Isaac Abarbanel (Questions 7 and 16 on Gen 15, Warsaw 1862), Malbim, Naftali Zvi Yehuda Berlin (*Ha'amek Davar*).
4. **Patristic, medieval, and Catholic Latin tradition**: St. Clement of Rome (*1 Clement* 10), St. Ambrose (*De excessu fratris Satyri* II.95), St. Jerome (*Quaestiones Hebraicae in Genesim* ch. 15), St. Augustine (*De Civitate Dei* X.8, XVI.23–24; *De Genesi ad litteram* XII.6), St. John Chrysostom (*Homilies on Romans* 9; *Commentary on Galatians* on 3:6), St. Bede (*In principium Genesis* on Gen 15), Alcuin of York (*Interrogationes* 165–170), Angelomus of Luxeuil (*Commentarius in Genesim* ch. 15), St. Thomas Aquinas (*Summa Theologiae* II-II q. 173 a. 3, q. 174 a. 6, III q. 30 a. 3 ad 3; *In Gal.* 3:16), Cornelius a Lapide (*Commentaria in Pentateuchum* on Gen 15, Antwerp 1700), George Leo Haydock (1811–1814).

## Explicit Exclusions and Uncollated Holdings

- **Uncollated Greek/Syriac/Latin Genesis commentaries**: St. John Chrysostom's *Homilies on Genesis* 36–37, St. Ephrem's *Commentary on Genesis*, Theodoret of Cyrus's *Questions on Genesis*, St. Ambrose's *De Abraham*, and the Latin text of St. Augustine's *Retractationes* II.43 are not held in tracked primary text in `src/sources/works/`; no claim of verified silence is extended to them beyond directly inspected works of those authors.
- **Uncollated modern noncommercial translations**: Licensed CC BY-NC modern translations (such as the William Davidson Talmud or Strickman–Silver Ibn Ezra) are never quoted verbatim.
- **Spiritual reflection separation**: Section 7 offers Catholic spiritual reflection drawn from the verified theological tradition of Genesis 15 and Romans 4, explicitly demarcated from the philological and historical test in Sections 1–6.
