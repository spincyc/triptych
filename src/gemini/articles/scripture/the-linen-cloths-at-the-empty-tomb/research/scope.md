# Research Scope — The Linen Cloths at the Empty Tomb

## Governing Questions

This single-passage Catholic scriptural study (`article-scripture`, `guidance/articles.md`) examines **John 20:3--10** alongside **Luke 24:12** (with immediate context John 20:1--2 and lexical/burial controls across Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts, and Romans) to test two specific claims directly against primary witnesses:

1. **Textual and exegetical claim:** What the Greek and Latin sentences of John 20:3--10 and Luke 24:12 actually say about Simon Peter, the other disciple, the *othonia* (linen cloths), the *soudarion* (head-cloth), *entetyligmenon* (rolled/wrapped up), the verbs of seeing, the textual status of Luke 24:12, and the disagreement among Fathers and Doctors over the object of *episteusen* ("he believed") in John 20:8.
2. **Historical/cultural claim:** Whether the widely circulated modern story that first-century Jewish households used a folded vs. wadded table napkin to signal "I am coming back" vs. "I am done" to a waiting servant has any basis in Greek lexicography, rabbinic table law, Roman dining practice, Hebraist commentary, or patristic and scholastic exegesis — and where the modern story can actually be traced in dated archives.

## Included Corpus and Boundaries

- **Primary Scripture:** Douay--Rheims (Challoner revision) verbatim for controlling English quotations; Clementine Vulgate for controlling Latin; King James Version (1611), Revised Version (1895), and World English Bible (Catholic Edition) for English lexical translation comparison; Robinson--Pierpont RP2018 Byzantine Textform (with inline Nestle--Aland divergences), SBL Greek New Testament v1.2 (Luke), and MorphGNT SBLGNT v6.12 lemma counts; Westcott and Hort (1882 Appendix p. 71) for Luke 24:12 textual criticism.
- **Lexicography:** Joseph Henry Thayer, *Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament* (entries *entylisso*, *othonion*, *soudarion*).
- **Patristic, Medieval, and Catholic Commentators:** St. John Chrysostom (*Homilies on John* 85.4, 86.1, 87), St. Ambrose (*Expositio Evangelii secundum Lucam* X.179), St. Augustine (*Tractates on John* 49.24, 120.7--9, 121.1; *De Consensu Evangelistarum* III.23--25), St. Cyril of Alexandria (*Commentary on John* Book XII on John 20:1--9), St. Gregory the Great (*Homiliae in Evangelia* 22.3--5), St. Bede the Venerable (*In Lucae Evangelium Expositio* VI on Luke 24:12), Blessed Theophylact of Ohrid (*Enarratio in Evangelium Ioannis* 20, PG 124 Latin column), St. Thomas Aquinas (*Super Evangelium S. Ioannis Lectura* 20, lect. 1; *Catena Aurea in Ioannem* caps. 11 and 20), and Cornelius a Lapide (*Commentaria in Quatuor Evangelia* on John 11:44 and John 20:1--11).
- **Magisterial and Liturgical Witnesses:** Catechism of the Council of Trent (Part I, Articles III and V); 1962 *Missale Romanum* (*Victimae paschali laudes*; Easter Saturday Gospel John 20:1--9); 1981 *Ordo Lectionum Missae* no. 42 (Easter Sunday Gospel John 20:1--9); Edward Caswall's 1849 literary translation of *Victimae paschali laudes*.
- **Rabbinic, Classical, and Hebraist Controls:** *Mishnah Berakhot* 8:3 (Hebrew), *Babylonian Talmud Berakhot* 42a and 52b (Cohen 1921), *Mishnah Sanhedrin* 6:1 (English + Hebrew), William Smith (*Dictionary of Greek and Roman Antiquities*, 1875, s.v. *Mantele*), Suetonius (*Nero* 22.2), and John Lightfoot (*Horae Hebraicae et Talmudicae*, Gandell 1859, vol. 3).
- **Modern Exegesis and Circulation Witnesses:** Henry Latham (*The Risen Master*, 1901 / 1905 reprint, pp. 34, 43), Brooke Foss Westcott (*The Gospel according to St. John*, ed. Arthur Westcott, 1908, vol. 2, p. 339), David Bivin (*Jerusalem Perspective*, 7 Oct 2006 / Wayback 7 Mar 2013), *RecipesAndMore* list archive (21 May 2007), *TruthOrFiction* (28 Jan 2008 / 17 Mar 2015), and *GotQuestions* (consulted 25 July 2026).

## Explicit Exclusions

- Forensic, radiocarbon, textile, pollen, and provenance debates surrounding the Shroud of Turin and the Sudarium of Oviedo are excluded as belonging to distinct material-relic disciplines outside the textual exegesis of John 20:3--10 and Luke 24:12.
- No copyrighted English Bible translation is quoted.
- No prose, framing, or citations were copied from any other provider branch (`src/claude/` or `src/gpt/`); every primary witness was inspected directly in `src/sources/`.
