## Reader and governing question

Written for a serious general reader, student, or historian seeking a single,
evidence-controlled portrait of Gregory I. Governing questions:

1. How did a reluctant Roman contemplative—grieving the loss of monastic quiet
   throughout his pontificate—become the practical steward who held a
   depopulated, flood- and plague-stricken, Lombard-besieged Rome together
   while launching the evangelization of the English?
2. What survives when Gregory's own firsthand testimony (letters, homilies,
   *Regula Pastoralis*, *Moralia in Iob*) and contemporary external witness
   (Gregory of Tours, *Historia Francorum* X.1) are systematically distinguished
   from eighth- and ninth-century hagiography (Whitby *Vita*, Bede, Paul the
   Deacon, John the Deacon) and Carolingian liturgical retrojection?
3. How do Gregory's real pastoral virtues (agrarian justice, captive ransom,
   refusal of forced Jewish baptism, refusal of the title "Universal Pope",
   the theology of *Regula Pastoralis*) coexist honestly with his historical
   limits and misjudgments (sixth-century penal coercion toward Donatists and
   rural pagans, and the 603 congratulatory letters to the usurper Phocas)?

## Thesis and boundary

Gregory the Great is neither the solitary medieval inventor of Western liturgy
and chant nor a secularized administrator who forgot his monastic cell. He was
a Roman senator turned monk who governed a collapsing imperial city out of
obedience rather than ambition, translating contemplative discernment into
pastoral law, agrarian justice, diplomacy, and missionary strategy while
insisting that pastoral office is a dangerous servitude (*servus servorum Dei*)
accountable before an imminent Judge.

This publication is a source-grounded historical synthesis. It makes no
miracle certifications, relic authentications, doctrinal adjudications beyond
reporting what Gregory taught and what the Church received, or original
archival, manuscript, or archaeological claims.

## Included

- Late-sixth-century Rome after the Gothic Wars and Justinianic reconquest;
  family background (Gordianus, Silvia, virgin aunts Tharsilla, Gordiana, and
  Aemiliana, Pope Felix III as *atavus*, and the evidentiary ceiling of the
  *Gens Anicia* tradition); classical and legal formation; tenure as *Praefectus
  Urbi* (by 573).
- Monastic conversion (c. 574–575) as narrated in the *Moralia* Epistle
  Dedicatory; endowment of six Sicilian monasteries and St. Andrew's on the
  Caelian Hill; ordination as seventh regional deacon (Benedict I vs. Pelagius
  II tension noted) and mission as *apocrisiarius* at Constantinople (c.
  579–585); friendship with Leander of Seville, genesis of *Moralia in Iob*,
  and the corporeal-resurrection dispute with Patriarch Eutychius (*Moralia*
  XIV.56).
- The 589–590 crisis from the eyewitness report in Gregory of Tours, *HF* X.1:
  Tiber flood, *lues inguinaria* plague, death of Pelagius II, Gregory's
  election and intercepted appeal to Emperor Maurice, *Oratio de mortalitate*,
  the *litania septiformis* to St. Mary Major (and its total silence regarding
  the Castel Sant'Angelo apparition), attempted flight, and consecration on
  3 September 590.
- *Regula Pastoralis* (590–591): its theology of pastoral reluctance,
  contemplation-and-action balance, the thirty-six pairs of contrasting souls
  in Part III, and self-examination in Part IV, plus rapid reception (Licinianus
  of Cartagena; Alfred the Great at reported level).
- Governance, *Registrum Epistolarum*, and imperial/Lombard politics: Sicilian
  patrimonial justice (*Ep.* I.44), captive ransom (*Ep.* VII.38), Ariulf and
  Agilulf sieges (*Ep.* II.46, V.40), Exarch Romanus, Maurice's monastic law
  (*Ep.* III.65), Queen Theodelinda and Lombard truces, protection of Jewish
  synagogues and ban on forced baptism (*Ep.* I.10, I.35, I.47) contrasted with
  coercion against Donatists and rural pagans, Three Chapters impasse, the
  "Universal Bishop" / *servus servorum Dei* controversy (*Ep.* V.18–21, 43;
  VII.33, 40; VIII.30), and the 603 Phocas letters (reported via Barmby).
- Mission to the English (595–601): Gallic purchase of English boys (*Ep.* VI.7),
  dispatch of Augustine of Canterbury in July 596 (*Ep.* VI.51–59), Christmas
  597 baptisms (*Ep.* VIII.30), and 601 consolidation (*Libellus Responsionum*
  and temple-adaptation policy, reported via Barmby from *Ep.* XI and Bede).
- Exegete, preacher, and hagiographer: *Moralia in Iob*, *Homiliae XL in
  Evangelia* (including Hom. 1.1, 1.5, 8.1, 37.7–9, 38.15–16, 40.7, 40.11–12),
  *Homiliae in Hiezechihelem* (including Book II Hom. 6 and 10 during Agilulf's
  siege), and *Dialogi* (reported; authorship debate noted honestly).
- Liturgy and chant: documented Roman reforms (*Kyrie*/*Christe eleison*,
  *Hanc igitur*, *Pater noster*, stational Masses, *Oratio* and litany) vs.
  the Carolingian *Hadrianum* (Wilson 1915, *Hucusque* preface, Gregory's own
  feast on p. 25) and John the Deacon's late-ninth-century *Antiphonarius* and
  *schola cantorum* narrative (Migne PL 78).
- Death (12 March 604), burial in Old St. Peter's portico, posthumous Roman
  famine reaction (reported), epitaph (*Consul Dei*, reported), cult (12 March
  and 3 September), Boniface VIII's 1298 Doctors decretal (reported), and
  Byzantine veneration (*Dialogos*).
- Dedicated six-tradition audit: *Non Angli sed Angeli*, Archangel Michael at
  Hadrian's Mausoleum, the whispering dove, Gregorian Masses (trental),
  Emperor Trajan's deliverance, and Gregorian Sacramentary/chant authorship.

## Excluded or bounded

- No exhaustive catalogue of all ~854 epistles or technical diplomatic edition
  of *Registrum* manuscripts (Maurist vs. MGH numbering divergence is noted
  where relevant).
- No independent text-critical edition of the *Dialogi* or resolution of the
  modern Francis Clark authenticity debate (summarized at critical-consensus
  level).
- No musicological transcription or modal analysis of Old Roman vs. Frankish
  Gregorian neumes.
- No architectural/archaeological authentication of Gregory's tomb or Roman
  relics.
- No direct inspection of untracked texts (*Dialogi*, *Epistles* IX–XIV, Bede
  *HE*, Whitby/Paul/John *Vitae*, Boniface VIII's 1298 decretal, or *Legenda
  Aurea*); every use is held explicitly at its tracked reporting ceiling.

## Evidence classes

A (Gregory's own writings), N (contemporary external witnesses: Gregory of
Tours *HF* X.1, Licinianus *Ep.* II.54), E (eighth-century English/Lombard
tradition: Whitby *Vita*, Bede, Paul the Deacon), L (later hagiography,
Carolingian attributions, medieval legends and iconography), M (official
ecclesial reception: *Hadrianum* feast, 1902 *Martyrologium Romanum*, reported
1298 decretal, 1969 General Roman Calendar), K (named modern scholarship:
Barmby 1895, Wilson 1915, Fortescue 1922, modern consensus on *Dialogi* and
chant), S (project historical synthesis). Used identically in the publication's
terminal appendix and all research records.

## Material uncertainties

- Exact birth year (c. 540) and family membership in the *Gens Anicia*
  (tradition unconfirmed in Gregory's own writings).
- Exact year of monastic conversion (c. 574–575) and whether Benedict I or
  Pelagius II ordained Gregory seventh regional deacon.
- Exact wording of *Registrum* Books IX–XIV, *Dialogi*, Bede *HE*, and the
  early *Vitae* beyond what is quoted or summarized in tracked local editions
  (Barmby, Wilson, Fortescue, Migne PL 76/78).
- How completely Gregory knew the details of Phocas's murder of Maurice and his
  sons when dispatching *Ep.* XIII.31, 38–39 in spring/summer 603.
- Exact liturgical extent of Gregory's lost *Sacramentarium* core beneath the
  late-eighth-century *Hadrianum*.
