## Chronological framework and methodological notes

Gregory's pontificate (3 September 590 – 12 March 604) is one of the most
tightly dated careers in early medieval Europe because his surviving
*Registrum Epistolarum* is organized by Roman fiscal indiction years beginning
every September (Indiction IX in Sept. 590–Aug. 591 through Indiction VII in
Sept. 603–March 604). By contrast, Gregory's first fifty years (c. 540–590)
lack dated letters of his own and depend on relative sequence in the *Moralia*
Epistle Dedicatory, contemporary notes in Gregory of Tours (*HF* X.1), imperial
regnal anchors (Justin II, Tiberius II, Maurice), and later lives (reported via
Barmby). Two recurring chronological tensions are preserved openly rather than
smoothed over:

1. **Ordination as seventh regional deacon (c. 578–579):** Gregory of Tours
   (*HF* X.1) links Gregory's appointment directly with Pelagius II, whereas
   other traditional witnesses (discussed by Barmby, Prolegomena) credit
   Benedict I (575–579) with the diaconate and Pelagius II (579–590) with the
   embassy to Constantinople. The publication records both views cleanly.
2. **Benedictine/Maurist vs. MGH epistle numbering:** Barmby's NPNF2-12 follows
   the Benedictine/Maurist (PL 77) order; Ewald–Hartmann (MGH) reorders several
   letters by indiction-month, shifting numbers by 1–2 digits (e.g., Maurist
   *Ep.* I.35 = MGH I.34; I.43 = I.41; I.47 = I.45). All citations in the
   biography follow the tracked Maurist numbering with explicit footnote
   disclosure.

## Dated and anchored sequence

1. **c. 540 — Birth at Rome.** Exact year unrecorded in any surviving primary
   witness. Anchored backward from his tenure as *Praefectus Urbi* (by 573) and
   from his family connection to Pope Felix III (483–492), whom Gregory calls
   *atavus meus* (*Hom. in Evang.* 38.15, A). Status: **approximate** (consensus
   K, Barmby).
2. **535–554 (context: 546, 549–550) — Gothic Wars and Justinianic reconquest.**
   Totila's sieges and the depopulation of Rome occurred during Gregory's
   childhood; reflected retrospectively in *Hom. in Hiez.* II.6.22 (A) and
   Barmby's historical background (K). Status: **secure historical frame**.
3. **568 — Lombard invasion of Italy under Alboin.** Establishes the permanent
   military crisis of Gregory's adult life; Barmby's Prolegomena (line 21034, K,
   following Latin *viginti septem* in *Ep.* V.21 and citing reported *Ep.*
   XIII.38) records twenty-seven years of Lombard devastation by 594–595 and
   thirty-five years by 602–603 (note that tracked NPNF2-12 *Ep.* V.21 line
   38537 prints "seven years"). Status: **secure**.
4. **By 573 — Urban Prefect of Rome (*Praefectus Urbi*).** Attested by
   contemporary external allusion to his silk and jeweled magisterial attire
   (*HF* X.1, N) and confirmed by Barmby's synthesis of the *Registrum*
   allusion (*Ep.* IV.2, K). Status: **secure as office; exact start/end
   approximate**.
5. **c. 574–575 — Monastic conversion and Caelian/Sicilian foundations.**
   Gregory narrates his long inner delay and decisive turning in *Moralia*,
   Ep. Ded. 1 (A), and mentions "my monastery beside the church of Saints John
   and Paul" in *Hom. in Evang.* 38.16 (A). Gregory of Tours (*HF* X.1, N)
   independently confirms six Sicilian monasteries plus a seventh inside Rome
   funded after his father's death. Status: **episode secure; exact year
   approximate**.
6. **c. 578–579 — Ordination as regional deacon and appointment as papal
   *apocrisiarius* to Constantinople.** Gregory (*Moralia*, Ep. Ded. 1, A)
   writes that he was dragged from monastic peace by the burden of ecclesiastical
   order and sent to the imperial palace. *HF* X.1 (N) notes his appointment
   under Pelagius II; Barmby (K) notes the tradition of Benedict I's ordination.
   Status: **event secure; ordaining pope debated**.
7. **c. 579–585 — Residence in Constantinople.** Meets Leander of Seville and
   begins oral conferences of *Moralia in Iob* (*Moralia*, Ep. Ded. 1, A).
   Disputes with Patriarch Eutychius (who died April 582) before Emperor
   Tiberius II (reigned 578–582) on the palpable resurrection body (*Moralia*
   XIV.56, A). Stands sponsor/godfather for the eldest son of Emperor Maurice
   (acceded Aug. 582; Barmby, K). Status: **secure** (bounded tightly by
   Eutychius's death in 582 and Tiberius II's reign).
8. **c. 585–586 — Recall to Rome.** Returns to St. Andrew's monastery and
   serves as advisor/secretary to Pelagius II (Barmby, K; Paul the Deacon, E,
   reported). Status: **approximate**.
9. **November 589 – February 590 — Tiber flood, plague, and death of Pelagius II.**
   *HF* X.1 (N, eyewitness Tours deacon): catastrophic Tiber flood ruins church
   granaries in November 589; *lues inguinaria* plague strikes in January 590;
   Pope Pelagius II dies first (7/8 Feb. 590, Barmby K). Gregory is unanimously
   elected pope and writes to Emperor Maurice begging him not to confirm the
   election; Urban Prefect Germanus intercepts the letter and substitutes the
   Roman decree of election (*HF* X.1, N). Status: **secure**.
10. **Spring–August 590 — *Oratio de mortalitate* and *litania septiformis*.**
    As pope-elect during the vacancy, Gregory delivers his penitential plague
    sermon (*HF* X.1, N; Migne PL 76 col. 1311, A) and organizes the sevenfold
    procession converging on St. Mary Major; eighty participants drop dead in
    one hour (*HF* X.1, N). Note: no apparition of Archangel Michael appears
    in this contemporary witness. Status: **secure** (exact month in spring vs.
    August debated between *HF* X.1 and later redactions; placed cleanly in
    the 590 vacancy).
11. **3 September 590 — Consecration as bishop of Rome.** After Maurice's
    confirmation arrives and Gregory's attempted flight is thwarted (*HF* X.1,
    N), he is consecrated in Old St. Peter's on Sunday, 3 September 590 (day
    preserved in *Martyrologium Romanum* 1902 p. 132, M, and Barmby, K).
    Status: **secure**.
12. **Late 590 – 591 (Indiction IX) — *Regula Pastoralis* and opening reforms.**
    Composes *Regula Pastoralis* in answer to John of Ravenna's rebuke of his
    reluctance (A); sends grief-stricken letter to Theoctista (*Ep.* I.5, Oct.
    590), Terracina synagogue letters (*Ep.* I.10, Nov. 590; I.35, Mar. 591),
    synodical confession of the Four/Five Councils (*Ep.* I.25, Feb. 591),
    Leander letter (*Ep.* I.43, May 591), Sicilian agrarian charter (*Ep.* I.44,
    May 591), and ban on forced baptism of Jews (*Ep.* I.47, June 591). Status:
    **secure**.
13. **590–592 — *Homiliae XL in Evangelia* and Ariulf's march.** Preaches forty
    Gospel homilies in Rome's stational churches (A, Preface to Secundinus;
    Hom. 1, 8, 20, 37, 38, 40). Negotiates truce with Duke Ariulf of Spoleto
    amid Exarch Romanus's paralysis (*Ep.* II.46, 592, A). Licinianus praises
    *Regula Pastoralis* (*Ep.* II.54, c. 592, N). Status: **secure**.
14. **593 (Indiction XI–XII) — Agilulf's siege, *Homiliae in Hiezechihelem*,
    and Maurice's monastic law.** Protests Maurice's law barring soldiers and
    officials from monasteries (*Ep.* III.65, Aug. 593, A). Preaches Book II of
    the Ezekiel homilies during King Agilulf's siege of Rome and breaks off
    exposition in grief (*Hom. in Hiez.* II.6.22, II.10.24, A; corroborated
    retrospectively in *Ep.* V.40, June 595, A). Status: **secure**.
15. **593–594 — *Dialogi* (4 books).** Composed during the aftermath of the
    Lombard siege (Barmby, K; Migne PL 76/77 cross-references; text not
    inspected verbatim). Status: **secure as span (reported)**.
16. **June 594 — *Ep.* IV.30 to Empress Constantina.** Refuses apostolic head
    relic; articulates Roman *brandea* tradition (A). Status: **secure**.
17. **January–September 595 (Indiction XIII–XIV) — *Moralia* completion,
    "Universal Bishop" crisis, and English slave boys.** Dispatches five
    coordinated letters opposing John the Faster's *Oikoumenikos* title (*Ep.*
    V.18–21, 43 — dated *Kalends of January, Indiction 13* [= Jan. 595] in
    Barmby's Benedictine text of *Ep.* V.18, lines 22112 & 38232, and assigned
    to June 595 in MGH chronology, A), defends Rome's siege conduct and exposes
    island tax abuses (*Ep.* V.40–42, June 595, A), sends completed *Moralia*
    and *Regula Pastoralis* to Leander (*Ep.* V.49, July 595, A), and instructs
    Candidus to purchase English youths in Gaul (*Ep.* VI.7, Sept. 595, A).
    Status: **secure**.
18. **23 July 596 – July 598 — English mission launched and confirmed.**
    Dispatches Augustine of Canterbury and forty monks (*Ep.* VI.51–59, July
    596, A). Writes to Maurice calling "Universal Priest" the precursor of
    Antichrist (*Ep.* VII.33, 597, A) and to Eulogius of Alexandria on the
    threefold Petrine chair (*Ep.* VII.40, 597, A). Announces 10,000 English
    baptized at Christmas 597 and refuses "Universal Pope" (*Ep.* VIII.30, July
    598, A). Status: **secure**.
19. **598–599 — First general Lombard truce and *Ep.* IX.12.** Truce concluded
    via Queen Theodelinda (Barmby, K, citing *Ep.* IX.42–43). Explains Roman
    *Kyrie*/*Christe eleison* usage in *Ep.* IX.12 (Fortescue 1922 pp. 234–235,
    K/A reported; cf. p. 172 on *Hanc igitur* and *Pater noster* before
    Communion). Status: **secure (reported)**.
20. **601 — Consolidation of the English Church.** Sends Mellitus, Justus,
    Paulinus, pallium for Augustine, *Libellus Responsionum*, and Mellitus's
    temple-adaptation letter (reported via Barmby, K, from *Ep.* XI.28–29,
    64–66, 76 and Bede *HE* I.27–32). Status: **secure (reported)**.
21. **November 602 – September 603 — Fall of Maurice, Phocas letters, renewed
    truce.** Phocas overthrows and executes Maurice and his five sons (Nov.
    602). Gregory sends congratulatory letters to Phocas and Leontia (spring–
    summer 603, *Ep.* XIII.31, 38–39, reported via Barmby). Renews Lombard
    truce through Theodelinda (Sept. 603, *Ep.* XIV.12, reported via Barmby).
    Status: **secure (reported)**.
22. **12 March 604 — Death and burial at Rome.** Dies after years of severe
    gout and gastric pain; buried in the portico of Old St. Peter's Basilica
    (feast day attested in *Hadrianum* p. 25, M, and *Mart. Rom.* 1902 p. 36,
    M; burial details and *Consul Dei* epitaph reported via Barmby, K). Status:
    **secure**.

## Posthumous reception milestones (dates of later witnesses, not lifetime events)

- **c. 704–714 (early 8th c.) — Anonymous Whitby *Vita Gregorii*.** Earliest
  known narrative Life; first carrier of *Non Angli* and Emperor Trajan
  legends (E, reported via Barmby).
- **731 — Bede, *Historia Ecclesiastica Gentis Anglorum*.** Preserves English
  mission documents (Book I.23–32) and biographical notice (II.1) with *Non
  Angli* and epitaph (E, reported via Barmby).
- **Late 8th c. (784–791) — Paul the Deacon & the *Hadrianum* Sacramentary.**
  Paul writes a Roman/Lombard *Vita* and *Historia Langobardorum* (E, reported);
  Pope Hadrian I sends the *Hadrianum* to Charlemagne, followed by the
  Carolingian *Hucusque* Supplement (L/K, inspected directly in Wilson 1915 and
  Migne PL 78).
- **c. 873–875 — John the Deacon, *Vita S. Gregorii* (4 books).** First witness
  to attribute the complete *Antiphonarius* and *schola cantorum* to Gregory
  (L, II.6–9 inspected in Migne PL 78 cols. 650–651; remainder reported via
  Barmby).
- **13th century — *Legenda Aurea* and Castel Sant'Angelo synthesis.**
  Popularizes the Archangel Michael sword-sheathing vision atop Hadrian's
  Mausoleum (L, reported).
- **1298 — Pope Boniface VIII.** Orders double feasts for the four original
  Latin Doctors of the Church: Ambrose, Augustine, Jerome, and Gregory (M,
  reported).
- **1969 — General Roman Calendar revision.** Transfers Gregory's obligatory
  memorial from 12 March (always falling in Lent) to 3 September, the
  anniversary of his 590 episcopal consecration (M).
