## Reader and governing questions

Written for a serious general reader, student, or researcher seeking one coherent, evidence-controlled life of Saint Ambrose of Milan and a clear separation between fourth-century primary witnesses, fifth-century hagiography, medieval legends, and official ecclesial reception.

Governing questions:
1. How did a Roman aristocratic magistrate, unbaptized at his popular election in 374, become one of the four original Latin Doctors of the Church?
2. What portrait emerges when Ambrose's own contemporary letters, homilies, and treatises—together with contemporary eyewitnesses Basil of Caesarea, Augustine of Hippo, and Quintus Aurelius Symmachus—are separated from Paulinus of Milan's fifth-century *Vita* and Eastern ecclesiastical historians?
3. How do the major public confrontations of Ambrose's episcopate—the Altar of Victory (384), the Milanese basilicas (385–386), Callinicum (388), and Thessalonica (390)—stand when evaluated with historical candor and Catholic theological discipline?
4. How did the pastor of fourth-century Milan become the Patron of the Ambrosian Church, associated by later tradition with the entire Ambrosian Rite, a large hymnal, and the *Te Deum*?

## Organizing synthesis and boundary

A career magistrate drawn from the governor's tribunal into the Milanese see forged an enduring Latin synthesis of Nicene orthodoxy, biblical exegesis, sacramental mystagogy, clerical ethics, congregational hymnody, and episcopal accountability over imperial power. Catholic reception honors his sanctity and doctrinal witness without canonizing every polemical excess or political intervention, notably his refusal to permit imperial restitution after the synagogue burning at Callinicum (388).

This synthesis is historical and analytical. No doctrinal adjudication, forensic relic authentication, miracle certification, independent specialist review, canonical opinion, rights opinion, or ecclesiastical approval is claimed.

## Inclusions

- Roman senatorial lineage, father's praetorian prefecture of the Gauls, birth range (c. 339–340 vs. c. 333), Roman upbringing, Marcellina's Christmas veiling by Pope Liberius (*De Virginibus* III.1), legal advocacy under Probus, and consular governorship at Milan;
- Death of Homoian bishop Auxentius of Cappadocia, popular acclamation of the unbaptized magistrate, 374 vs. 373 debate, baptism and consecration within eight days, divestment of wealth, formation under Simplicianus, *De Officiis* I.1.4, and Basil the Great's *Letter* CXCVII confirming the return of St. Dionysius's relics;
- Death of brother Satyrus and *De Excessu Fratris Satyri* I–II (shipwreck Eucharist, refusal of Luciferian schism, intense fraternal grief, Resurrection hope);
- *De Virginibus* and *De Viduis* and domestic resistance from Milanese mothers; breaking up sacred altar vessels to ransom captives after Adrianople (*De Officiis* II.28);
- Nicene defense for Emperor Gratian (*De Fide* I–V, *De Spiritu Sancto* I–III, Jerome's critique via NPNF II.8, Sirmium, and Council of Aquileia 381);
- Exegesis (*Hexameron* I, V.24.88–25.89, VI.2; *Expositio Evangelii secundum Lucam* II.19, 26, 41–42), mystagogy (*De Mysteriis* I–IX; *De Sacramentis* IV.5–6 in PL 16), clerical ethics (*De Officiis*), anti-Novatianist mercy (*De Paenitentia*), and authenticated congregational hymnody (four core hymns; *Deus creator omnium* in *Confessions* IX.12.32);
- Altar of Victory debate (384): fair presentation of Symmachus's *Relatio* III alongside Ambrose's *Epistles* XVII and XVIII;
- Basilica crisis (385–386): *Epistle* XX (*missam facere*, rescue of Castulus, refusal to surrender basilicas), *Letter* XXI, *Sermon against Auxentius* ("The Emperor is within the Church, not above it"), and June 386 discovery of Sts. Gervasius and Protasius (*Letter* XXII; Augustine *Confessions* IX.7.16 and *City of God* XXII.8);
- Ambrose and Augustine in Milan (384–387) strictly from Ambrose's evidentiary horizon (*Confessions* V–IX, *Letters* 36 and 54 on Saturday fasting, *De Doctrina Christiana* IV.21, *On the Grace of Christ* I.43–44 preserving Pelagius's tribute);
- Emperor Theodosius I: Callinicum (388, *Letters* XL–XLI) analyzed candidly without apologetic flattening, and Thessalonica (390, confidential handwritten *Letter* LI) distinguished sharply from Theodoret's fifth-century doorway dramatization;
- Final years (Valentinian II's death, Eugenius and *Letter* LVII, Frigidus and clemency pleas in *Letters* LXI–LXII, Theodosius's death, Vercellae *Epistle* LXIII, death on Holy Saturday 4 April 397, burial beside Gervasius and Protasius);
- Official liturgical and ecclesial reception (*Manuale Ambrosianum*, 1640 *Missale Ambrosianum* preface, 1902 *Martyrologium Romanum*, 1962 *Missale Romanum* and 1945 *New Roman Missal* Collect witnesses, postconciliar calendar rank, 1298 Doctorate at reported level);
- Dedicated six-point tradition audit (bees on infant's lips, child's cry, *Te Deum* legend, cathedral-door clash with Theodosius, Ambrosian Rite/hymnal attribution, 835/1864 tomb tradition with correction of de Romestin's Cosmas-and-Damian slip).

## Material uncertainties

- Exact birth year (c. 339–340 vs. c. 333) and birthplace (probable Trier);
- Episcopal consecration year (7 December 374 vs. 7 December 373);
- Exact date of Satyrus's death (378 vs. 379; 17 September vs. 17 October);
- Authentic boundaries of the Ambrosian hymnal beyond the four externally authenticated hymns;
- Stenographic authorship status of *De Sacramentis*;
- Degree of hagiographic shaping in Paulinus's *Vita* (bees, child's cry, judicial torture stratagem, deathbed dialogue);
- Unverified pre-1864 relic chain and absence of a locally tracked Basilica Ambrosiana archaeological report.
