## 3. Complete Appointed Formulary in Liturgical Order

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — Ps 24:1–3, 4b (Heb. Ps 25:1–3a, 4)
- **1962 locus:** Printed p. 1 (opening unnumbered folio), PDF p. 82 left column, marginal unit 1; printed heading `Ant. ad Introitum`, printed citations `Ps. 24, 1-3` and `Ps. ibid., 4`.
- **Collation status:** **300/400 dpi CMAA 1962 page-image collated (2026-11-25)** (`temporal-advent-1-scriptural-propers-and-rubrics-collation-2026-11-25`); corroborated word-for-word in public-domain **Tournai Desclée 1889** (`ia-djvu-ocr-715f57fd`, lines 10752–10767, attesting `Deus meus in te confido` and `exspectant`) and **Pustet Ratisbon 1862** (printed p. 1, lines 13196–13217; note that Pustet 1862's optical layer drops `Deus meus, in` after the drop-capital and spells `expectant`), cross-checked against Clementine Vulgate Ps 24:1–4. Exact 1962 printed spellings confirmed on page image: `exspéctant`, `sǽcula sæculórum` (accented `ǽ` on `sǽcula` and unaccented `æ` on `sæculórum`, uniform across Introit unit 1, Collect unit 2, and Secret unit 8), `Glória`, `Fílio`, `Spirítui`, followed by printed repetition rubric (`Quo finito, repetitur Ad te levávi usque ad psalmum. ¶ Hic modus repetendi antiphonam ad Introitum servatur per totum annum.`).
- **Latin (1962 visually collated form, ligatures expanded and stress marks omitted; 1862 `expectant` / OCR dropout disclosed above):**
  > Ad te levavi animam meam: Deus meus, in te confido, non erubescam: neque irrideant me inimici mei: etenim universi, qui te exspectant, non confundentur.
  > *Ps.* Vias tuas, Domine, demonstra mihi: et semitas tuas edoce me.
  > *V.* Gloria Patri, et Filio, et Spiritui Sancto. Sicut erat in principio, et nunc, et semper, et in saecula saeculorum. Amen. — *Repetition of antiphon Ad te levavi... non confundentur.*
- **Clementine Vulgate comparison (Ps 24:1–4):** `In finem. Psalmus David. Ad te, Domine, levavi animam meam: Deus meus, in te confido; non erubescam. Neque irrideant me inimici mei: etenim universi qui sustinent te, non confundentur. Confundantur omnes iniqua agentes supervacue. Vias tuas, Domine, demonstra mihi, et semitas tuas edoce me.`
- **Liturgical adaptations:** Omits psalm superscription (*In finem. Psalmus David.*) and vocative *Domine* in v. 1; retains Old Latin / Roman Psalter *qui te exspectant* against Gallican *qui sustinent te* in v. 3b (Heb. 25:3a); omits the imprecatory half-verse v. 4a (*Confundantur omnes iniqua agentes supervacue.*) and takes v. 4b (Heb. 25:4) in Gallican form (*demonstra mihi*) as the psalm verse. *Gloria Patri* is retained throughout Advent (`RGMR 427–429`).
- **English biblical witness (Douay–Rheims Challoner, `american-1899-ebible` = `challoner-gutenberg-1581`, Ps 24:1–3, 4b; omitted portions noted above):**
  > To thee, O Lord, have I lifted up my soul. In thee, O my God, I put my trust; let me not be ashamed. Neither let my enemies laugh at me: for none of them that wait on thee shall be confounded.
  > *V.* Shew, O Lord, thy ways to me, and teach me thy paths.
  *(Note: Douay–Rheims translates the Vulgate's `Domine` in v. 1 and `non` / `none of them` idiomatically for `universi ... non confundentur`; the Missal drops `Domine` in v. 1 and sings `exspectant` rather than `sustinent`.)*
