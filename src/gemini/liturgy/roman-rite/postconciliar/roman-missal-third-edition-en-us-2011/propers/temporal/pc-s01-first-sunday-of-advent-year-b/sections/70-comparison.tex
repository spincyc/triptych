\runninghead{Comparing the Three Interpretations}
\section{Comparing the Three Interpretations}

The three interpretations weigh the same twelve appointed parts of the First Sunday of Advent in Year~B from different exegetical starting points, part ways where ancient and medieval commentators disagree, and leave a parish assembly with different pastoral counsels.

Their sharpest exegetical divergence falls on Isaiah~63:17 and 64:3b~Vg.\ (Heb.~64:2b). The first interpretation follows St.~Jerome --- attributing the experience of wandering and hardness to God's \emph{nimia patientia} (``excessive forbearance'') and reading \emph{Descendisti} (``thou didst come down'') directly of the Incarnation --- whereas the second follows St.~Thomas Aquinas, who treats hardening as permissive withholding of grace and prefers a historical literal sense for \emph{Descendisti} (deliverance from Egypt in Exodus~4 and the cliffs of Arnon in Numbers~21). The third interpretation reads Isaiah~63:17 and 64:5--7~Vg.\ (Heb.~64:4--6) as a portrait of spiritual drowsiness set against God meeting those who do justice.

Because each interpretation puts a different passage in the foreground --- divine hiddenness across Isaiah, 1~Corinthians~1:7, and Mark~13:32--34; human frailty and divine fidelity across Isaiah~64:6--8~Vg.\ (Heb.~64:5--7), Psalm~80(79), and 1~Corinthians~1:4--9; or household vigilance across Mark~13:33--37 and patristic two-Advent preaching --- each also checks the blind spot of the others. The table below sets their starting questions, textual cruxes, principal teachers, pastoral emphases, and characteristic risks side by side.
