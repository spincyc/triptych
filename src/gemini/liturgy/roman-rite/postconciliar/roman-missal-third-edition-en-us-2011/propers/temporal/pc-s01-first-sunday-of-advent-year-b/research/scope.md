## 4. Passage-by-Passage Reception Matrix and Material Disagreements

Every appointed element of the Mass is mapped below with its exact checked witnesses, witness roles, key exegetical findings, material disagreements, and unresolved leads.

### 4.1 First Reading — `Isaiah 63:16b–17, 19b; 64:2b–7` [Vg `63:16b–17; 64:1, 3b–8`]
- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Jerome**, *Commentarii in Isaiam* Lib. XVII, cap. 63–64 (`artifact.jacques-paul-migne.patrologia-latina-volume-24.paris-1865.ia-djvu-ocr-7be28ebd`, PL 24 cols. 642–651, OCR lines 44340–44985; `inspected` raw interleaved two-column DjVu OCR locating witness; role: `direct-witness`):
    - On **63:17** (*Quare errare nos fecisti... indurasti cor nostrum*): explains the bold prayer through God's **excessive forbearance/patience** (*nimia patientia*): by not striking sinners immediately, God's delay appears to let hearts harden and wander, even as He allows corrective discipline (citing Hos 4:14, 2:14; Heb 12).
    - On **64:1 → 64:3b** (*Utinam dirumperes caelos, et descenderes... descendisti*): highlights the abrupt grammatical shift from optative longing (*Utinam dirumperes*) immediately to accomplished past action (*Descendisti!*) as **answered prayer in the Incarnation** (*Verbum caro factum est*), melting the proud "mountains."
    - On **64:4** (*A saeculo non audierunt... oculus non vidit*): defends Paul's quotation in `1 Cor 2:9` as a free sense-for-sense paraphrase drawn from Isaiah (*non verbum ex verbo reddens... sed sensuum exprimens veritatem*, lines 44615–44622), explicitly rejecting apocryphal books (*Ascensio Isaiae* and *Apocalypsis Eliae*, lines 44641–44649) and Gnostic fabrications (naming Basilides and citing St. Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* I, lines 44654–44657, 44704–44711). *(Attribution boundary: the suggestion that Jerome here targets Origen belongs solely to Vallarsi's editorial footnote `a` interleaved by the raw OCR at lines 44660–44684, never to Jerome's own text.)*
    - On **64:5** (*Ecce tu iratus es, et peccavimus*): resolves the startling causal order by identifying *ordo praeposterus* ("not because you were angry did we sin, but because we sinned, you were angry").
    - On **64:6** (*quasi pannus menstruatae universae justitiae nostrae*): contrasts legal/Old-Covenant righteousness with evangelical righteousness in Christ (Phil 3:8).
    - On **64:7** (*Abscondisti faciem tuam a nobis*): **explicitly cross-references Psalm 79(80):4** (*Ostende faciem tuam, et salvi erimus*, OCR lines 44871–44872) — supplying documented reception linking the First Reading and the Year B Responsorial Psalm refrain.
    - On **64:8** (*nos vero lutum; et fictor noster tu*): grounds hope wholly in the Creator-Potter's clemency rather than human merit.
- **Medieval / Doctoral exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Thomas Aquinas**, *Expositio super Isaiam ad litteram* ch. 63–64 (`artifact.thomas-aquinas.opera-omnia-volume-14.1863-parma.ia-complete-ocr-02c6dc25`, Parma Vol. 14 pp. 370–371, OCR lines 90930–91220; `inspected` raw DjVu OCR locating witness; role: `direct-witness`):
    - On **63:17**: explains divine hardening through **permissive withholding / non-infusion of grace** (*permittendo, non immittens gratiam, et non corrigens*, citing Rom 9).
    - On **64:1**: reads *dirumperes caelos* of divine condescension and *kenosis* (*exinanires temetipsum, deponens majestatem, et carnem assumeres*).
    - On **64:3a → 64:3b** (*Cum feceris mirabilia, non sustinebimus; descendisti, et a facie tua montes defluxerunt*, lines 91073–91106): on the preceding omitted clause **`64:3a`**, Aquinas cites Israel's terror at **Sinai (`Exod. 20`, line 91079)**; on **`64:3b` (*Descendisti*)**, he notes Final Judgment (*ad judicandum nos*) and the Incarnation (*in uterum virginis*) as alternative/mystical readings, but **materially disagrees with Jerome on the preferred literal sense** (*vel melius, secundum litteralem sensum*, lines 91089–91106), reading *Descendisti* literally as an argument from past covenant proof — God's descent to **deliver Israel from Egypt (`Exod. 4`, with `Ps 17:10`)** and the melting of **the cliffs of Arnon (`scopuli Arnon, Num. 21`)**.
    - On **64:4**: enumerates four goods prepared for those who wait (eternal rest, the kingdom, the divine table of Ps 22, eternal light of Ps 131), quoting `1 Cor 2:9`.
    - On **64:5**: applies *Occurristi laetanti* mystically to Simeon (Luke 2).
    - On **64:6**: distinguishes **four ways human works of justice become defiled** (mortal sin, negligence, perverse intention, and pride) without denying graced righteousness.
- **Doctrinal illumination checked:**
  - **St. Augustine**, *De gratia et libero arbitrio* ch. 45 [XXIII] (`artifact.nicene-and-post-nicene-fathers.series-1-volume-5.ccel-web-2026-09-17.ccel-text-bf3dd167`, lines 46111–46195; role: `context` / doctrinal illumination): frames God's two advents (mercy first, judgment second, with hidden judgment already operating) and holds together God's just judgment in hardening with human free will ("both God hardened him by His just judgment, and Pharaoh by his own free will").
- **Material disagreements recorded:** (1) **Jerome** (*nimia patientia*) vs. **Aquinas** (permissive non-infusion of grace) vs. **Augustine** (just judgment + free will) on Isa 63:17; (2) **Jerome** (Incarnation directly in *Descendisti*, 64:3b) vs. **Aquinas** (deliverance from Egypt [`Exod. 4`] and the cliffs of Arnon [`Num. 21`] literally on 64:3b, after Sinai terror [`Exod. 20`] on 64:3a, with Incarnation/Judgment mystically); (3) **Jerome** (Old-Law vs. evangelical justice in 64:6) vs. **Aquinas** (fourfold moral/spiritual defilement of righteous deeds).
- **Unresolved leads:** Cyril of Alexandria (*In Isaiam*) and Theodoret of Cyrus (*In Isaiam*) are not held as tracked texts in `src/sources/` for ch. 63–64 and remain uninspected Eastern leads.

### 4.2 Responsorial Psalm — `Psalm 80(79):2ac, 3b, 15–16, 18–19`, R. `v. 4`
- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Augustine**, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* on Ps 79 (NPNF1-8 Ps LXXX, `artifact.augustine.enarrationes-in-psalmos.english-npnf8-ccel-web-2026-09-05.ccel-npnf108-text-d6841950`, lines 39602–39986; admitted L3 passages `psalm-79-coxe-{1,2,3,9,11}`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - Opens explicitly: *"The song here is of the Advent of the Lord and of our Saviour Jesus Christ, and of His vineyard... Christ and the Church."*
    - Explains *Cherubim* (v. 2) as *"fulness of knowledge"* = **charity** (Rom 13:10); reads *Stir up thy power* (v. 3b) of the Crucified appearing weak in flesh yet rising in power (likened to Jacob's wrestling).
    - Reads *illumine Thy face* (v. 4) of lifting the veil of lowly flesh so Christ is recognized in heavenly majesty.
    - Reads *perfect this vineyard... upon the Son of man* (vv. 15–16) of building and perfecting the Church upon **Christ the sole foundation** (1 Cor 3:11).
    - Reads *Thou wilt quicken us, and we will call upon thy name* (v. 19) as loving God *gratis* for Himself alone rather than for earthly goods (resonating directly with Isa 64:7's lament that no one calls on God's name).
- **Early modern Doctoral exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Robert Bellarmine**, *Explanatio in Psalmos* (`passage.robert-bellarmine.commentary-on-the-book-of-psalms.2026-09-05-osullivan-ecatholic2000.psalm-79-verses-2-4` and `psalm-79-verses-15-19`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - Grounds Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh geographically/historically around the ark and Israel's restoration, then insists on vv. 15–19 that *"All agree that this passage refers literally to Christ"* because post-exilic Israel never regained its former glory, linking *visit this vineyard* to the *Benedictus* (Luke 1:68) and Jerusalem's missed visitation (Luke 19:44), and identifying the *man of thy right hand* / *Son of man* with Christ formed by the Holy Spirit (Luke 1:35).
- **Material nuances:** Augustine emphasizes the spiritual-ecclesiological *totus Christus* (Cherubim = charity; vine perfected on Christ the foundation), while Bellarmine integrates geographical/historical Israel with an explicit literal-messianic proof for vv. 15–19.
- **Unresolved leads:** Cassiodorus (*Expositio Psalmorum* in PL 70) on Ps 79 was not extracted/read in this pass and remains a `lead`.

### 4.3 Second Reading — `1 Corinthians 1:3–9`
- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:**
  - **St. John Chrysostom**, *Homilies on First Corinthians*, Homily II (`artifact.nicene-and-post-nicene-fathers.series-1-volume-12.ccel-web-2026-09-28.ccel-text-10a3454d`, lines 654–920; admitted L3 passages `2.1cor-1.{4-sec-1,4-sec-2,4-6,7,8,9}`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - Confronts the sharp exegetical puzzle of **1:4–7**: how can Paul call the carnal, quarreling Corinthians (cf. 1:10–12; 3:1–3) *"enriched in all utterance and all knowledge"* and *"behind in no gift"*? Chrysostom proposes three complementary answers: (i) initial fervor followed by later remissness; (ii) praising the faithful remnant (like Stephanas) while sparing open shaming; (iii) pastoral economy (*oikonomikos*), attributing every endowment strictly to **grace** (*in gratia Dei*) to cut down pride before administering rebuke.
    - Observes Paul's **incessant repetition of the Name of Jesus Christ** in vv. 1–10 as targeted medicine for party-spirit ("I am of Paul, I am of Apollos").
    - Unpacks the precise theological force of **`apokalupsis` ("revelation / manifestation", 1:7)**: *"implying that although He be not seen, yet He is, and is present even now, and then shall appear."*
    - Notes that *confirm* and *unreprovable* (1:8) covertly signal that the Corinthians are still wavering and in danger.
    - On **1:9 (*Fidelis Deus*)**: insists God's faithfulness does not nullify human freedom or guarantee salvation against an unwilling heart (*"if we be not quite impatient of His rein"*), and quotes **Paul's own words in `1 Corinthians 2:9`** (*"and the good things in store, which eye hath not seen, nor ear heard"*, lines 847–848 — eye-before-ear order, echoing `Isa 64:4` without naming Isaiah) in exposition of the blessings to which God calls.
- **Early modern reception checked:**
  - **Cornelius a Lapide**, *Commentaria in omnes divi Pauli epistolas* Vol. 7 (`artifact.cornelius-a-lapide.commentaria-in-omnes-divi-pauli-epistolas.1908-grant-cobb.volume-7-ocr-c9d11cbf`, lines 418–510, `inspected` raw Google Books OCR locating witness; plus facsimile-checked admitted passages `1cor-1-5`, `1cor-1-7`; role: `reception`, standing `ecclesiastical-writer`):
    - Reads vv. 4–5 of the genuine spiritual gifts granted to the Corinthian church at large, and interprets **1:8–9** (*confirmabit... Fidelis Deus*) **conditionally** (*so far as His part is*), citing the Council of Trent (Sess. VI cc. 11 and 13, following Augustine): God does not forsake unless first forsaken, forbidding presumption of infallible perseverance without special revelation.
- **Material nuance, negative search & unresolved leads:** Chrysostom's pastoral-economy diagnosis of vv. 4–7 sharpens the tension with 1:10ff. more acutely than Lapide's general-church reading, while both converge against unconditional perseverance without cooperation. **Bound negative inspection:** Direct inspection (lines 1–120) and literal search (`"SUPER PRIMAM EPISTOLAM"`, 0 matches) of tracked Aquinas Dessain 1857 Vol. 2 OCR (`artifact.thomas-aquinas.in-omnes-pauli-epistolas-commentaria.dessain-volume-2-1857.book-ocr-2b7b11dc`, Binding 102) confirms it begins at *Expositio super Secundam Epistolam ad Corinthios* and contains no commentary on I Corinthians; Corpus Thomisticum's restricted *Super I ad Corinthios* (`artifact.thomas-aquinas.super-i-ad-corinthios.latin-corpusthomisticum-web-2026-07-26.c1c-html-8be5b71c`, Binding 103) is untracked and unavailable offline, so Aquinas on 1 Cor 1 and Ambrosiaster on 1 Cor 1 remain uninspected `lead`s.

### 4.4 Gospel — `Mark 13:33–37` (with context of omitted `Mark 13:32`)
- **Direct ancient & medieval exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Hilary of Poitiers**, *De Trinitate* IX.58–68 (`artifact.nicene-and-post-nicene-fathers.series-2-volume-9.1898-edinburgh-ccel.ccel-complete-859ba34c`, lines 21361–21620; role: `direct-witness`):
    - Directly addresses the omitted preceding verse (`13:32`) and its link to `13:33`: Christ's profession of "not knowing" is not infirmity of divine nature, but an economy of silence (*dispensatio* / hiding what it is not yet time to declare). In **IX.67** (lines 21594–21611), Hilary explains *why* the day is concealed: *"withholds from us the security of certain knowledge, that our minds may be kept on the stretch by the uncertainty of suspense... for the security of knowledge might breed negligence of the faith."*
  - **St. Bede the Venerable**, *In Marci Evangelium Expositio* Lib. IV, cap. 13 (`artifact.bede.in-marci-evangelium-expositio.2026-10-13-latin-wikisource-migne.latin-text-b8197766`, lines 402–405; role: `reception`):
    - Refutes Arian/Eunomian misuse of `13:32` (citing Acts 1:7; Col 2:3) and notes Christ says *nescitis* (not *nescimus*) so disciples live in *"pendulae exspectationis incerto"* (the uncertainty of poised expectation).
    - Identifies the departing man (*homo peregre proficiscens*, 13:34) with Christ ascending bodily to heaven (*reliquens corporaliter*, borrowing Gregory Hom. 9: earth is the proper place of flesh, carried into a far country when exalted to heaven) while **never withdrawing His divine presence and safeguard** from the Church.
    - Identifies *authority over every work* (*potestatem cujusque operis*) as the **grace of the Holy Spirit** enabling good deeds.
    - Identifies the *janitor* ("porter", 13:34) with pastors and spiritual rulers guarding the flock, while v. 37 (*Quod autem vobis dico, omnibus dico: Vigilate*) extends doorkeeping to **every believer guarding the door of the heart**, quoting Gregory's crisp threefold definition of vigilance: *"Vigilat qui ad aspectum veri luminis mentis oculos apertos tenet; vigilat qui servat operando quod credit; vigilat qui a se torporis et negligentiae tenebras repellit."*
  - **St. Gregory the Great**, *Homiliae in Evangelia* Hom. 1, Hom. 9, Hom. 13 (`artifact.gregory-the-great.homiliae-in-evangelia.wikisource-web-2026-09-17.complete-extracted-text-dc25dd69`, lines 14–19, 90–97, 139–145; plus checked Latin PL 76 passages `1.1`, `1.5`; role: `direct-witness` / analogue):
    - **Hom. 1 (`Luke 21:25–33`):** Contrasts the terror of judgment for those attached to a world growing old with lifting up heads in Advent hope.
    - **Hom. 9 (`Matt 25:14–30`):** Source of Bede's *homo peregre proficiscens* (human flesh carried into the "far country" of heaven at the Ascension).
    - **Hom. 13 (`Luke 12:35–40`):** Interprets the watches of the night as the **ages of human life** (boyhood, youth, old age), portraying God as a *patiens redditor* (patient Judge) whose hidden hour (*ut semper possit esse suspecta*) keeps every season of life alert.
  - **St. Thomas Aquinas**, *Catena Aurea in Marcum* 13:32–37 (`passage.thomas-aquinas.catena-aurea-in-marcum.1842-english-oxford-ccel.13-32-37`; role: `reception`):
    - Mediates **Theophylact of Ohrid** (reading the four watches as four stages of life: evening = old age, midnight = mid-youth, cockcrow = maturity of reason, morning = childhood; and warning against wicked wakefulness), **Pseudo-Jerome** (sleep as chasing phantoms of worldly love), **Hilary**, **Bede**, **Gregory**, and **St. Augustine, *Epistula 199* §3** — solving the generational puzzle of *"I say to all: Watch"* when only one generation will live at the Parousia: *"that day comes to each man when his day comes for departing from this life such as he is to be, when judged in that day."* (Note: Augustine *Ep. 199* full HTML is restricted/untracked, so this witness is cited honestly as mediated through Aquinas's *Catena Aurea*.)
- **Material disagreements / distinct emphases:** Ecclesial-pastoral doorkeeping + ages-of-life vigilance (Bede / Gregory / Theophylact) vs. personal-death eschatology solving *"to all"* (Augustine *Ep. 199* via *Catena Aurea*) vs. Trinitarian economy of silence (*sacramentum silentii*, Hilary *De Trin.* IX).

### 4.5 Entrance Antiphon (`Ps 25[24]:1–3`), Gospel Acclamation (`Ps 85[84]:8`), Communion Antiphon (`Ps 85[84]:13`), and Advent Common Psalms (`Ps 25[24]`, `Ps 85[84]`)
- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Augustine**, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* on **Ps 24(25)** (`ccel-npnf108-text-d6841950`, lines 6850–6925; admitted passages `24.1-2`, `24.3`, `24.10`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - Christ speaks in the person of the Church (*Ad te levavi*): lifting up the soul trodden down by carnal desires; pride brought to shame when trusting in self; on **v. 10** (*All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth* — directly relevant to Advent Common Psalm `Ps 25[24]`), Augustine explicitly identifies "all the ways of the Lord" with **the two Advents of the Son of God — one in mercy, the other in judgment**.
  - **St. Augustine**, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* on **Ps 84(85)** (`ccel-npnf108-text-d6841950`, lines 41518–41952; admitted passages `psalm-84-coxe-{6,10,11,12}`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - On **v. 8** (*Ostende nobis, Domine, misericordiam tuam, et salutare tuum da nobis*): reads *"grant us Thy salvation"* directly as **"grant us Thy Christ"**, humbling pride because every good is mercy, and purifying the heart by faith to behold Christ.
    - On **vv. 12–13** (*Veritas de terra orta est... Dominus dabit benignitatem, et terra nostra dabit fructum suum*): reads *truth springing from earth* both **Christologically** (Christ born of the Virgin Mary to take mortal flesh as healing victim) and **morally** (confession of sin springing from sinful human earth), and explains *benignitas* as **sweetness / delight in righteousness** replacing delight in sin — God's rain making the heart furrowed by confession yield genuine fruit.
- **Early modern Doctoral exegesis checked:**
  - **St. Robert Bellarmine**, *Explanatio in Psalmos* (`psalm-24-verses-1-3`, `psalm-84-verse-8`, `psalm-84-verse-13`; role: `direct-witness`):
    - On **Ps 24(25):1–3**: tearing the soul away from earthly briers/thorns; "waiting on the Lord" as steadfast hope backed by ancestral experience and divine promises.
    - On **Ps 84(85):8**: explicit prayer for the Incarnation / coming of the Savior (citing Titus 2:11; 3:4).
    - On **Ps 84(85):13**: stresses the **Marian-Incarnational sense** — *Dominus dabit benignitatem* = the Holy Spirit overshadowing the unploughed Virgin Mary so "our earth" yields Christ her fruit (Cant 2:1).
- **Material nuance:** On Ps 84(85):13, Augustine pairs the Incarnation with moral confession and infused delight in righteousness (*sweetness*), whereas Bellarmine foregrounds the virginal fruitfulness of Mary overshadowed by the Spirit.

### 4.6 Missal Orations (`collect`, `prayer-over-the-offerings`, `prayer-after-communion`), Seasonal Preface (`preface`), and Concluding Blessing (`concluding-blessing`)

## 5. Chronological and Geographical Research Note

The leaf's canonical chronology is generated directly from `src/sources/chronology/` via `tools/tpt proper-chronology` (`research/chronology.toml`, `research/chronology-annotations.tex`, `research/chronology-table.tex`, presentation `orientation-v3`) with one explicit Catholic critical comparison (`research/chronology-profile-comparisons.toml` for `gospel` = Mark 13:33–37 under `catholic-critical-v1`).

- **Drafting cross-walk note (Vulgate orientation display vs. U.S. Lectionary Hebrew/NABRE numbering):** Because the restricted U.S. Lectionary day page (`112926.cfm`) and 2010 ICEL Antiphonary were unavailable offline, `research/chronology-inputs.toml` declares `system = "vulgate"` matching the inspected Latin OLM 1981 and MR 2002 witnesses (and `kind = "identified-basis"` for uncollated `Ps 24:1–3`). Consequently, the generated `orientation-v3` table (`research/chronology-table.tex`) prints Vulgate Isaiah verse boundaries (`Is. 63:16b–17; 64:1, 3b–8`) and leads with Vulgate Psalm numbers (`Ps. 24, 79, 84`, with `Heb. 25/80/85`), whereas the U.S. English Lectionary prints Hebrew/NABRE verse boundaries (`Isa 63:16b–17, 19b; 64:2b–7`; `Ps 25, 80, 85`). Downstream drafting in the expansive study and concise opening must explain this numbering shift plainly for U.S. parish readers without modifying any generated chronology row.
- **Historical settings and geography distinct from composition dates:**
  - **Isaiah 63–64:** Traditional attribution situates Isaiah's ministry in **Judah and Jerusalem** (`740–701 B.C.`, Souvay's traditional account); modern Catholic critical scholarship situates the communal lament of `Isaiah 56–66` in **postexilic Judah/Jerusalem** after the Babylonian return (reflecting a devastated sanctuary, `63:18`, and longing for a new divine descent recalling Exodus deliverance, `64:3b`).
  - **Psalm 80(79):** Names **Israel / Joseph** and the northern/central tribes **Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh** (`vv. 2–3`, omitted in the brief liturgical selection `2ac, 3b` yet foundational to the psalm's geography), imaging the people as a vine transplanted from Egypt into the land (`vv. 9–16`). Its occasion date remains unresolved in the corpus; composition falls before `c. 165 B.C.`
  - **Psalm 25(24) & Psalm 85(84):** Both have unresolved historical occasion dates; `Ps 25(24)` carries traditional Davidic attribution (Hebron, then Jerusalem, `c. 1055–1015 B.C.`, alongside later proposals 30–50 years lower) and pre-Maccabean composition limit (`before c. 165 B.C.`); `Ps 85(84)` belongs to the **Sons of Korah** collection (`c. 740–536 B.C.` collection estimate), whose prayer for restoration of "our land" (*terra nostra*, `v. 13`) bridges prophetic expectation and return from exile.
  - **1 Corinthians 1:3–9:** Addressed to the nascent urban Christian assembly in **Corinth** (Roman provincial capital of Achaia, famous for rhetoric and factionalism, illuminating Paul's mention of *utterance and knowledge* in `1:5`), written from **Ephesus** (cf. 1 Cor 16:8), with corpus composition proposals spanning `A.D. 56–57`.
  - **Mark 13:33–37:** Spoken by Jesus on the **Mount of Olives opposite the Herodian Temple** (`Mark 13:3`), closing the Olivet Discourse during Holy Week (`c. A.D. 29`, derived Maas chronology, Passion dates disputed). Composition is kept strictly distinct from the narrated event: traditional proposals span `A.D. 38–70` (Rome traditionally), while the explicit Catholic critical comparison (`critical.gospel-of-mark`, NABRE Mark introduction) records a tentative proposal of *"perhaps shortly after A.D. 70"* with Syria as a possible provenance.
- **Query-envelope precision note:** Fractional verses (`Isaiah 63:16b–17; 64:1, 3b–8` and `Psalm 79:2ac, 3b, 15–16, 18–19`) are queried at whole-verse canonical granularity; no subverse chronology or appointed modern wording is asserted from those envelopes.
