## Exact Distinction Between Formal USCCB Adaptations and Universal Roman Norms

Direct collation against the 2011 United States GIRM (under GIRM 390) establishes the following normative split:

- **Formal USCCB territorial adaptations in Chapters IV–VI:**
  - **GIRM 283:** Broad extension of Communion under Both Kinds governed by USCCB *Norms*.
  - **GIRM 301:** Dignified, solid, well-crafted **wood** permitted for a fixed altar mensa if structurally immobile.
  - **GIRM 304:** Additional altar cloths beneath or alongside the required white cloth may be of other colors with Christian honorific or festive significance.
  - **GIRM 329:** Sacred vessels (specifically ciboria, patens, pyxes, and similar vessels) may be crafted from other solid, noble, non-breaking, non-deteriorating, non-absorbent materials such as ebony or hard woods (while chalices remain bound to nonabsorbent bowls under GIRM 330 and glass/earthenware/clay remain reprobated under *Redemptionis Sacramentum* 117).
  - **GIRM 339:** Acolytes, altar servers, readers, and other lay ministers may wear the alb or other appropriate and dignified clothing.
  - **GIRM 346(e), 346(h):** White, violet, or black vestments permitted for funeral services and Masses for the Dead; gold or silver vestments permitted on more solemn occasions.
- **Universal Roman norms (no U.S.-specific textual insertion in GIRM):**
  - **GIRM 307** (candlesticks on or around the altar), **GIRM 309** (stationary ambo), **GIRM 315** (tabernacle preferably off the altar of celebration, with location judged by the Diocesan Bishop), **GIRM 316** (continuous oil or wax sanctuary lamp), and **GIRM 336** (alb as common vestment; cincture unless fitted; amice if neck clothing is exposed; surplice never substituted for alb when chasuble, dalmatic, or stole alone is worn).
