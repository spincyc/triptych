# Current-Law Matrix — The Latin Sunday Obligation

This matrix records the current universal Latin-law example used in *The Due Return*. It is current through 20 July 2026. It does not determine any named person's obligation, excuse, culpability, canonical status, or sacramental access.

## Governing proposition

Natural and revealed theological reasons establish the good of worship and the Paschal Lord's Day. Current juridical obligation must still be reproduced from the competent promulgated law. For the example in this article, the governing body is the 1983 *Codex Iuris Canonici* as amended, for the Latin Church, together with applicable particular law that a real case would require.

## Authority, translation, and effect cautions

1. The official promulgated Latin and applicable later legislation control. Holy See English pages are reference renderings.
2. The *Catechism*, *Sacrosanctum Concilium*, and *Dies Domini* authoritatively explain the theological and moral good of Sunday but are not substituted for the Code when stating present juridical effect.
3. Natural law can ground the generic duty of worship without supplying the current legal details of time, subjects, satisfaction, dispensation, or particular calendar.
4. The Decalogue, a canon, an episcopal-conference norm, a diocesan prescription, a dispensation, and a confessor's moral judgment have different authors, objects, and effects.
5. Legal obligation, moral gravity, personal culpability, canonical delict, penalty, and sacramental access are not interchangeable conclusions.
6. A particular case may implicate privilege, custom, proper law, singular administrative act, physical or moral impossibility, travel, domicile, age, capacity, or facts not represented here.

## Abstract case sequence

Before reaching a conclusion in a real case:

1. Establish Church membership, governing code, age, and sufficient use of reason.
2. Establish territory, domicile or quasi-domicile, travel status, and applicable universal, particular, and proper law.
3. Identify the feast and the exact obligation alleged.
4. Establish the available Masses, timing, rite, and what participation occurred.
5. Establish impossibility, serious reason, family need, social service, or other facts relevant under the governing norm.
6. Establish whether competent authority granted a dispensation or commutation and its scope.
7. Keep objective obligation separate from knowledge, consent, and moral culpability.
8. Keep moral culpability separate from any canonical penal, sacramental, or public-status effect.
9. Refer personal uncertainty to a pastor, confessor, competent authority, or qualified canonist with complete facts.

## Negative-result statement

This matrix does not conclude that online or broadcast viewing satisfies c. 1248 §1; does not define every serious reason, grave cause, or just cause; does not list territorial holy days; does not apply Latin law to Eastern Catholics; does not judge a named person's sin; and does not infer a canonical penalty or sacramental incapacity from the Sunday omission alone.
