\section{What Can Be Given Back?}

``How can I repay the Lord?'' (Ps 116:12). The question becomes harder the longer one stays with it. What could be given to the One from whom every power of giving has already come? The breath of thanks, the body that kneels, the hour kept for worship, the bread and wine carried to the altar---all have first been received.

The psalmist does not answer with a possession he can hand over. He will receive the cup, call upon the name of the Lord, fulfill his vows, and give thanks in the presence of the people (Ps 116:13--19). The verbs do not sound like repayment: receive, call, fulfill, give thanks. His answer to giving back begins by receiving again.

The question waits inside every ``I have to go to Mass.'' Those words can make worship sound like another will pressing against our own. Yet worship is enacted with a life, a body, and an hour that the worshipper did not first supply. The ordinary sentence now contains the whole difficulty: everything has been received, yet something may still be owed.

There is no place outside that difficulty from which to answer it. \emph{I am, and I am not from myself}; that dependence is true before I welcome or resist it. What am I to do with a life I did not give myself?

St.~Paul sharpens the difficulty. ``Who has given [God] anything that he may be repaid?'' The answer is no one. ``From him and through him and for him are all things'' (Rom 11:35--36). No creature reaches behind the first gift, supplies a divine lack, or makes God its debtor. Paul has denied repayment; the psalmist's desire to return something remains.
