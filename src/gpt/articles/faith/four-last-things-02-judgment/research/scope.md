# Judgment — Research Scope

- **Publication:** `articles/faith/four-last-things-02-judgment`, GPT provider.
- **Profile:** discursive faith/theology article for serious general Catholic readers.
- **Question:** Why is there a final judgment after the particular judgment, and how can judgment according to works remain entirely dependent on grace?
- **Governing claim:** the same person receives a definitive destiny at death and its bodily and public consummation at the resurrection; judgment reveals grace-enabled freedom within completed history.
- **Included:** Christ as Judge; the continuing conscious self; embodied reward and punishment; the continued effects of acts without new postmortem merit or demerit; faith, works and merit; unequal histories and culpability; conscience; mercy as the root of justice; purification within salvation and real help through suffrages.
- **Excluded:** any person's eternal state, a complete system of predestination, psychiatric diagnosis or treatment, canonical tribunal procedure, an imagined perceptual mechanism for disclosure, or a timetable for purification.
- **Authority:** Scripture and authoritative Catholic teaching govern. The CDF's 1979 letter clarifies personal continuity. Aquinas III.59.5 and I.21.4 provide precise theological explanations. Spe salvi 47's purifying-encounter account is identified as a theological proposal, with paragraph 48 explaining intercession. Pastoral applications are synthesis.

- **Rights:** official Vatican source bodies remain outside tracked content; registered Dominican electronic texts retain their United States public-domain and transcription qualifications. No territorial liturgical vernacular is reproduced.
