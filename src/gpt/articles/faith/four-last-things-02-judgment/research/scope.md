# Judgment — Research Scope

- **Publication:** `articles/faith/four-last-things-02-judgment`
- **Profile:** discursive faith/theology article (`guidance/articles.md`).
- **Series:** second of four independent articles in *The Four Last Things*.
- **Question:** How can judgment be according to works while salvation remains wholly initiated and enabled by grace, and why are particular and final judgment both necessary?
- **Governing claim:** judgment is Christ's truthful manifestation of a life and of history; it neither reduces salvation to moral bookkeeping nor makes free, grace-enabled love irrelevant.
- **Included:** particular judgment, final judgment, faith and works, grace and merit, conscience, mercy and justice, disclosure and vindication.
- **Excluded:** identification of the destiny of any person, comprehensive predestination controversies, scrupulosity treatment, and canonical tribunal procedure.
- **Authority:** Scripture, councils, and the Catechism govern. Aquinas provides a theological articulation of mercy, justice, and merit. Applications are project synthesis.
- **As of:** Catechism 1021–1022 and 1038–1041 checked at the Holy See site on 2026-07-24.
- **Review:** no independent dogmatic, Thomistic, pastoral, psychological, or ecclesiastical review.
