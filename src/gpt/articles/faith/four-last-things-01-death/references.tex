\section{References}
\begingroup\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.25em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item Sacred Scripture: Genesis 2:17; 3:19; Luke 13:1--5; John 6:54; 9:1--3; 11:33--44; Romans 5:12--21; 6:3--11, 23; 14:8; 1 Corinthians 15:12--49; Hebrews 2:14--15.
\item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, official English text: 624--626 (Christ's death); 988--1014 (resurrection and dying in Christ); 1021--1032 (judgment, heaven, purification); 1514--1525 (anointing and Viaticum); 1684--1690 (funerals); 2276--2279 (care of the dying). The citation of Benedict XII's \emph{Benedictus Deus} follows the sources reproduced at CCC 1022--1023.
\item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Gaudium et spes} (1965), no.~18: death, the desire for enduring life, and Christ's victory. Official English text, Holy See.
\item Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, \href{https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19790517_escatologia_en.html}{\emph{Letter on Certain Questions Concerning Eschatology}} (17 May 1979), nos.~2--5: the whole person, the surviving human self, prayer for the dead, and the future manifestation of Christ. Official English text.
\item St.~Augustine, \emph{The City of God}, XIII.4--5, trans. Marcus Dods, vol.~I (Edinburgh: T.\ \& T.\ Clark, 1871), in the registered Project Gutenberg transcription: why the baptized still die and how the good use death well. This historical translation is not a critical edition.
\item St.~Thomas Aquinas, \emph{Summa theologiae} III, q.~50, aa.~2--3, trans. Fathers of the English Dominican Province, Project Gutenberg eBook 19950 (2006 electronic edition): the continuing personal union of the Word with Christ's body and soul in death.
\end{itemize}\endgroup
