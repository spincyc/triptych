# Death — Research Scope

- **Publication:** `articles/faith/four-last-things-01-death`, GPT provider.
- **Profile:** discursive faith/theology article; serious general Catholic readers, without assumed scholastic training.
- **Series:** first of four; introduces death and judgment in relation to the opposed definitive outcomes of hell and heaven. Purgatory is located within salvation.
- **Question:** How does Christ transform death without making the rupture of embodied life good in itself, and how does that hope shape Christian preparation and care?
- **Governing claim:** the same human self survives death, awaits bodily resurrection, and is accompanied by Christ and his Church through the Paschal and sacramental life.
- **Included:** the All Saints/All Souls and Advent horizon; grief; sin and bodily corruptibility; Augustine's baptized-yet-dying objection; the continuing Incarnation in Christ's death; conscious personal continuity and bodily incompleteness; Baptism, anointing, Viaticum, funerals and suffrages; general moral boundaries for care.
- **Excluded:** predictions, a prescribed annual or local lectionary, a medical decision manual, determination of death, organ donation, a full theology of martyrdom, funeral rubrics, private revelation, and judgments about particular deceased persons.
- **Authority:** Scripture and authoritative Catholic teaching govern. The CDF letter is authoritative Curial teaching approved for publication, not a new papal definition. Augustine and Aquinas are identified theological witnesses. Practical applications and rhetorical movement are synthesis.
