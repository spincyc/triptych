# Demographic and Survey Inventory — Council, Missal, and Crisis

Checked 2026-07-13. This record supports the published numerical tables. It preserves source year, geography, denominator, question wording, and calculation boundaries so that “collapse” is never used as an unqualified global statistic.

## Survey instruments

### Pew 2017: Reformation-era contrasts

- Population: U.S. adults, with reported Catholic subsample.
- Constructs used: faith and good deeds versus faith alone; Scripture plus Church teaching/tradition versus Scripture alone; and belief in purgatory.
- Catholic results reported: 81% faith and good deeds; 75% Scripture plus teaching/tradition; 70% purgatory; 67% gave both of the first two “Catholic” answers.
- Boundary: forced binaries compress the Catholic doctrines of grace, justification, and the relation of Scripture and Tradition. The results establish durable confessional instincts, not complete theological mastery.

### Pew 2019: Eucharistic knowledge and forced choice

- Population: U.S. self-identified Catholics.
- Instrumental contrast: bread and wine “actually become” Christ’s body and blood versus “are symbols.”
- Results used: 31% selected actual change; 69% selected symbols; 50% knew the Church teaches actual change; 22% knew the teaching but selected symbolism; 43% selected symbolism and thought that was the Church’s teaching; 63% of weekly attenders selected actual change.
- Boundary: Catholic sacramentality includes true symbolism; the question intended a contrast with actual change but did not say “merely” or “only” symbol. “Actually become” can be misconstrued as visible or chemical change. The article reports the result as grave but not as a perfect transubstantiation examination.

### CARA/NORC 2022 fieldwork, 2023 report

- Field dates: 11 July–2 August 2022.
- Sample: 1,031 self-identified Catholics in a probability-based AmeriSpeak panel.
- Reported margin of sampling error: approximately ±4.45 percentage points at the 95% confidence level.
- Instruments retained: open-ended description; closed contrast between true presence under appearances and “only symbolically present”; direct follow-up concerning Jesus Christ’s presence; knowledge of the Church’s teaching; and composite coding across answers.
- Results used: 44% on the principal closed true-presence choice; 57% on direct follow-up; 64% under CARA’s composite coding; 38% both knew and believed; 9% knew but did not believe; 48% neither knew nor expressed belief; 5% expressed Real Presence without knowing it was the Church’s teaching.
- Boundary: the composite preserves inconsistent respondents but relies on an explicit coding rule. The three percentages are not competing trend points; they are different measures in one instrument family.

### Pew 2024: United States and six Latin American countries

- Construct used: whether the Church should permit selected practices, including birth control and women’s ordination.
- Result used: majorities favored birth-control permission in every surveyed country, with a reported range from 63% in Brazil to 86% in Argentina; most national Catholic samples favored women priests.
- Boundary: policy preference is not a direct test of doctrinal status, knowledge, or culpability; national comparisons require each survey’s weights and margins.

### Pew 2025: U.S. Catholic identity

- Construct: whether selected commitments are “essential” to personal Catholic identity.
- Results used: 70% personal relationship with Jesus; 50% Marian devotion; 46% receiving the Eucharist; approximately one-third papal leadership and apostolic continuity; 83% of weekly attenders called receiving the Eucharist essential; 15% of Catholics who never attend did so.
- Boundary: “essential to my identity” is not the same as “I assent to the objective doctrine.” Respondents could regard something as important but not essential.

### Pew 2025: U.S. policy attitudes

- Constructs used: whether the Church should allow birth control, IVF, women priests, and married priests; and whether to become more inclusive even if some teaching changes.
- Results used: 84% birth control; 83% IVF; 59% women priests; 63% married priests; 60% preferred greater inclusion even if some teaching changes; 41% of weekly attenders supported women priests.
- Boundary: married priesthood in the Latin Church is principally discipline; reservation of priestly ordination to men is definitive teaching; “birth control” can be heard more broadly than artificial contraception; “IVF” omits several morally material steps; Pew explicitly notes the loss of theological nuance in accessible wording.

## Comparability decisions

- The 2019 Pew Eucharist figure and the 2022/2023 CARA figures are **not** plotted as a change over time.
- U.S. administrative Catholic population is not equated with the denominator of every Pew self-identification survey.
- Raw counts are paired with per-Catholic rates when population growth materially changes interpretation.
- The article does not call global Catholic population, parishes, schools, or diocesan priests a postconciliar “collapse.”
- Association between weekly attendance and doctrinal alignment is reported without assigning causal direction.
- No pre-1960 national survey using the same Eucharistic instrument was located; the article therefore does not invent a preconciliar baseline.

## Rejected numerical claims

- “Only one-third of all Catholics believe in the Real Presence” as a universal, wording-independent fact: rejected.
- “Eucharistic belief doubled from 31% to 64% between 2019 and 2023”: rejected because instruments, coding, samples, and field dates differ.
- “The worldwide Church collapsed after Vatican II”: rejected by global population, parish, deacon, and school series.
- “Global raw priest count proves there was no crisis”: rejected because priests per million Catholics fell from about 642 to 289.
- “The council caused an 81.5% fall in U.S. women religious”: rejected as a causal inference from two administrative points; the decline is established, its causal distribution is argued separately.
