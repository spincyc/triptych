# Conciliar Authority Matrix — Vatican I and Vatican II

Checked 2026-07-13; the *Unitatis redintegratio* and *Nostra aetate* loci and authority calibrations were checked 2026-07-24. This record prevents three recurrent conflations: ceremonial solemnity with a new extraordinary definition, document title with one undifferentiated theological note, and later reception with the promulgated act.

## Governing rules

| Rule | Primary source | Editorial consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vatican I’s promulgated teaching consists of two dogmatic constitutions; unfinished schemas are not conciliar acts. | Official Vatican I index; *Dei Filius*; *Pastor aeternus*; suspension by *Postquam Dei munere*. | The article does not attribute an unpromulgated ecclesiology, discipline, or episcopal schema to the council. |
| An ex cathedra act requires the office, universal teaching role, supreme apostolic authority, faith-or-morals object, definitive act, and universal obligation specified in *Pastor aeternus*. | *Pastor aeternus*, ch. 4; Gasser’s *relatio*. | The article analyzes each clause and never applies “infallible” from document genre or papal authorship alone. |
| “Irreformable of itself, not from the consent of the Church” excludes a subsequent ratification condition; it does not create doctrine outside Scripture, Tradition, episcopal witness, or the Church’s faith. | Gasser; German bishops’ 1875 declaration; *Lumen gentium* 25; CDF 1998 primacy reflections. | The article calls the clause pastorally combustible in isolation while affirming its dogmatic purpose and truth. |
| Vatican II binds definitively only when it openly declares that intention; its other teaching is received according to subject and manner as teaching of the supreme magisterium. | General Secretary’s *Notificationes* of 16 November 1964, printed with *Lumen gentium* and transcribing the Theological Commission’s declaration of 6 March 1964. | No sentence is classified by title alone; the notification and the declaration it reproduces retain their distinct dates and identities. |
| Vatican II deliberately avoided new extraordinary solemn definitions but taught with the authority of the supreme ordinary magisterium. | Paul VI, general audience, 12 January 1966. | “No new dogma” is not rendered as “no doctrine” or “nonbinding.” |
| A conciliar paragraph can combine prior dogma, authentic doctrine, discipline, exhortation, historical diagnosis, and prudential proposal. | The texts’ internal form; *Lumen gentium* 25; CDF *Mysterium Ecclesiae* and 1998 *Professio fidei* commentary. | Classification is proposition-level where the distinction is material. |

## Vatican I acts

| Act / locus | Object | Authority used in article | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Dei Filius*, ch. 1 and canons | One Creator distinct from creation; free creation; universal Providence. | Defined dogma. | Historical explanations of rationalism and pantheism are contextual, not themselves the defined clauses. |
| *Dei Filius*, ch. 2 and canons | Natural knowability of God; supernatural Revelation. | Defined dogma. | Does not claim that every person easily produces complete natural theology. |
| *Dei Filius*, ch. 3 and canons | Faith as supernatural assent because of God revealing; grace and motives of credibility. | Defined dogma. | Does not reduce faith to an empirical conclusion or deny psychological trial. |
| *Dei Filius*, ch. 4 and canons | Harmony and distinction of faith and reason; legitimate science; development in the same meaning and judgment. | Defined dogma. | The council’s nineteenth-century examples and polemical setting remain historical. |
| *Pastor aeternus*, chs. 1–2 | Petrine institution and perpetuity in Roman successors. | Defined dogma. | Does not require every century to display identical administrative form. |
| *Pastor aeternus*, ch. 3 | Full, supreme, ordinary, immediate, episcopal universal primacy; preservation of bishops’ ordinary and immediate jurisdiction. | Defined dogma. | Does not make bishops agents whose office derives from papal delegation. |
| *Pastor aeternus*, ch. 4, defining clause | Conditioned ex cathedra infallibility and irreformability without later consent as a ratifying cause. | Defined dogma. | Excludes interviews, prudential governance, empirical statements, discipline, inspiration, impeccability, and omniscience. |
| Gasser’s *relatio* | Deputation’s meaning of the proposal; rejection of “personal,” “separate,” and “absolute”; fitting inquiry. | Privileged official conciliar explanation. | Not a third dogmatic constitution and not an exhaustive later theology. |
| Unfinished Church schemas | Episcopate, whole Church, discipline, missions, clergy, religious, and other planned matters. | Historical evidence of the interrupted architecture. | No proposed sentence is cited as Vatican I teaching. |

## Vatican II authority map

| Class | Test | Representative use in article | Required response / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reaffirmed revealed dogma | The council clearly repeats a truth already definitively proposed as revealed. | Trinity, Incarnation, Eucharistic sacrifice and Real Presence, papal primacy and infallibility, Marian dogmas. | Theological faith because of the doctrine’s prior or universal definitive status, not because Vatican II newly defined it. |
| Definitive doctrine already taught by the ordinary and universal magisterium | The proposition is to be held irrevocably, whether revealed or necessarily connected with Revelation. | Core sacramental constitution, apostolic succession, fundamental moral prohibitions. | Firm and definitive assent; the article avoids pretending Vatican II issued a new formula for every example. |
| Authentic non-definitive doctrine | The council teaches as council without manifest final defining intent. | Much of the synthesis on collegiality, including *Unitatis redintegratio* 3 on imperfect communion and ecclesial elements, religious liberty’s development, and Church–world relations. | Religious submission of mind and will, calibrated by subject, wording, and repetition; not “optional.” |
| Discipline and authorized pastoral reform | The council orders, exhorts, or permits institutional action. | Liturgical revision, restored catechumenate, formation and governance reforms, and the practical ecumenical directions of *Unitatis redintegratio* 4. | Response follows the proposition's form: obedience to binding directives and faithful implementation of pastoral direction; later competent authority may reform the discipline or practical program. |
| Prudential or contingent judgment | The council diagnoses a historical condition or recommends a time-bound application. | Some economic, political, cultural, technological, and strategic passages in *Gaudium et spes* and other texts. | Serious respect and consideration; later evidence or authority can correct application without reversing dogma. |

## Disputed loci and controlling interpretation

| Locus | Misreading rejected | Controlling evidence | Article’s qualified result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Lumen gentium* 8, *subsistit in*; *Unitatis redintegratio* 3, imperfect communion and ecclesial elements | Either “the Catholic Church is only one branch” or “nothing ecclesial exists beyond visible Catholic boundaries.” | The two exact conciliar loci; CDF 2007 Responses. | The Church of Christ endures concretely in the Catholic Church; those who believe in Christ and have been truly baptized are in real though imperfect communion with her, and real ecclesial elements exist outside her visible structure. |
| *Lumen gentium* 22 and Preliminary Note | Either democratic delegation to the pope or a college with no real subjectivity. | Text and note; Vatican I, ch. 3. | The college is subject of supreme power only with and never without its head; bishops receive office through consecration for hierarchical exercise. |
| *Dignitatis humanae* 1–3 | Religious indifferentism or denial of objective duties toward truth. | The declaration’s express saving clause and coercion analysis. | Civil immunity from coercion is distinct from moral approval of error; development and tension with older applications remain proper historical questions. |
| *Lumen gentium* 16 / *Nostra aetate* 2 | Parallel saving religions or unnecessary mission. | *Nostra aetate* 2's Christological qualification; *Ad gentes*; CDF *Dominus Iesus*. | Grace can save without culpable explicit knowledge, and other religions can contain true and holy elements; Christ and his Church remain uniquely and universally mediating. |
| *Gaudium et spes* 19–21 | Approval of Communism because the proper noun is absent. | Text’s analysis of systematic atheism, state coercion, and suppression; footnoted prior condemnations. | Strategy changed from named condemnation to diagnosis and dialogue with persons; prior doctrinal judgments were not repealed. |
| Entire council labeled “pastoral” | Private advice with no binding force. | 1964 notification; Paul VI 1966. | Pastoral purpose identifies mode and end, not absence of authority. |

## Negative findings

- No Vatican II document contains a new extraordinary dogmatic definition openly declared as such.
- No Vatican II act repeals *Pastor aeternus*, *Divini Redemptoris*, the Catholic judgment on Masonic associations, transubstantiation, missionary necessity, or the Creator–creature distinction.
- No document title mechanically makes every sentence definitive.
- Solemn promulgation in public session is a real juridical and ecclesial fact but is not synonymous with extraordinary infallible definition.
- The article’s five-level table is editorial synthesis governed by the cited sources, not a quotation from one Roman act.
