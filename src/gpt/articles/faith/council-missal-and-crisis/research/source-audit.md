# Source Audit — Council, Missal, and Crisis

Access and canonical-currentness checks were completed through 2026-07-16 unless a row states otherwise. Official texts govern doctrine, law, promulgation, and current discipline. Professional histories reconstruct event and context; participant accounts are identified as such. Data and survey reports establish their own measures, not causes.

## Consequential negative results and rejected claims

- Vatican I did not define papal inspiration, impeccability, omniscience, best governance, or the irreformability of every papal statement.
- The phrase *ex sese, non autem ex consensu Ecclesiae* does not mean a pope may manufacture doctrine against Scripture, Tradition, bishops, or the Church’s faith.
- Vatican II made no new extraordinary dogmatic definition; it was not therefore a non-teaching or optional council.
- A bounded inspection of the complete official English body of *Dei Verbum* (nos. 1–26) found no explicit mandate for the breadth, speed, rhetoric, or institutional effects described in the article's postconciliar-reception section, and those effects do not follow necessarily from the constitution's doctrinal propositions. This is an interpretive non-entailment finding about that bounded witness, not a claim that the constitution prohibited later reform, caused no historical effect, or by itself supplies a complete counterfactual history of reception.
- No official source supports “Vatican II approved Communism or Freemasonry.” A bounded inspection of the complete official English normative text of *Sacrosanctum Concilium* (nos. 1–130 and the calendar appendix) found no explicit mandate for total abolition of Latin or total vernacularization, versus-populum orientation, Communion in the hand, multiple or new anaphoras or Eucharistic Prayers, or rewriting or replacing the Offertory. This is not a finding that the constitution prohibited those later measures or excluded their authorization under its general reform mandate.
- The council did not draft or vote on the 1970 Missal.
- Lawfulness and validity do not prove every reform choice prudent; postconciliar decline does not prove a promulgated rite invalid.
- U.S. collapse is not universalized to global Catholic population, parishes, schools, deacons, or every region.
- The 31%, 44%, 57%, and 64% Eucharistic figures are not interchangeable and do not form a trend.
- The SSPX was never fully regularized; the July 2026 DDF act is the current endpoint.
- Attachment to authorized older Roman books is not itself schism.
