\section{The Occult Inversion: Love Placed under Will}

The proximity of two slogans can conceal an opposition of first principles. Aleister Crowley's \emph{Liber AL vel Legis} gives the Thelemic formulas ``Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law'' (I.40) and ``Love is the law, love under will'' (I.57). The wording recalls Augustine strongly enough that historical dependence must be asked; the order differs strongly enough that theological judgment cannot stop at words.

\subsection{What history does and does not establish}

Crowley was an English occultist who developed the religious-magical system called Thelema and styled himself the Great Beast 666. In an unfinished 1926 essay, ``The Antecedents of Thelema,'' he explicitly named Augustine's ``Love, and do what you will'' before turning to François Rabelais. He also said Augustine's meaning was not the meaning of \emph{The Book of the Law} and treated Rabelais as the more important antecedent. Graham John Wheeler's source history confirms this multiple genealogy and the transgressive setting in which Crowley adopted the precept (\emph{Religio} 27.1 [2019]: 17--41).

It would therefore be inaccurate to claim that Crowley merely copied Augustine, still less that a direct demonic dictation is a demonstrable historical fact. It would be equally inaccurate to dismiss the resemblance as accidental. Crowley acknowledged Augustine retrospectively and marked the difference himself. The responsible description is \emph{documented retrospective occult appropriation and theological inversion within a wider genealogy}.

Fairness requires one further point. Crowley often denied that ``will'' meant passing appetite and described ``True Will'' as an austere vocation or cosmic course requiring self-discipline. The critique need not reduce Thelema to ``do whatever feels pleasant.'' Precisely at its strongest, the contrast with Catholic doctrine remains: Who gives the will? Who identifies its truth? What good measures it? What is love, and under what does it stand?

The Catholic judgment concerns this hierarchy, not merely Crowley's scandalous biography. Augustine begins from God who is Love and who gives charity. Because charity participates in divine life, it can rule willing without destroying it. Crowley's ordering takes a Christian-sounding term, ``love,'' and subordinates it to an esoterically discerned True Will. Even when that will is disciplined and cosmic rather than impulsive, it lacks the Christian grammar of creation, revealed law, sin, prevenient grace, Cross, and beatitude.

\subsection{In what sense ``satanic''?}

Thelema should not be treated as historically identical with every religion or movement that scholars classify as Satanism. The term here is a Catholic theological judgment and must be stated with that limit. Crowley deliberately cultivated anti-Christian and Beast imagery; more fundamentally, the formula performs the spiritual reversal Catholic tradition associates with temptation: the received creature treats its own will as final measure and subjects love to it.

Calling that reversal \emph{satanic} does not claim knowledge of every adherent's culpability, every motive in Crowley's composition, or a visible demon as the proximate author of a sentence. It names the formal corruption: gift is detached from Giver, freedom from truth, love from charity, and will from obedient participation. The lie is powerful because it retains partial goods---vocation, integrity, discipline, a protest against whim---and relocates their center.

The Christian answer is not a weaker will. It is a will made stronger by surrender to reality's first Truth and Good. Christ does not dissolve personality when he says, ``not my will but thine''; his human freedom reaches its supreme act. Augustine does not put love beneath an esoterically discerned will; he lets divine charity become the root from which the self at last acts freely.

\begin{studybox}{The decisive word order}
The occult inversion says: \emph{love under will}. Augustine's Christian order is the reverse: \emph{willing under charity, charity from God}. The first makes an esoterically discerned will the proximate law outside the Christian order of Creator, revelation, grace, and objective good; the second lets grace conform the whole person to the Love who created freedom and brings it home.
\end{studybox}
