\section{The Word and Love}

Christian revelation does not place three beings behind the one first Cause. It confesses one simple divine essence in three really distinct Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. The distinction is not made of parts, degrees, separate wills, or temporal stages. It is relation of origin within the undivided divine life. Everything that belongs to the divine essence---being, wisdom, goodness, power, will, and essential love---is wholly possessed by each Person, because each Person is the one God (Lateran IV, \emph{Firmiter credimus}; CCC 202, 252--255).

The Son is not a first creature added to God. He is the eternal Word, begotten and not made. St.~Athanasius built his defense of Nicene faith around this truth: if the Word through whom all things are made belonged among the things made, creation would still lack the uncreated Word by whom it comes to be. The Father is never without his proper Word and Wisdom (\emph{Orations Against the Arians} I.14, 19; \emph{On the Incarnation} 3).

Aquinas receives the patristic confession and gives it a metaphysical grammar. In an intellectual nature, an inward word proceeds as what is conceived in understanding. In the infinite simplicity of God, this procession is not a motion, an acquisition of knowledge, or the production of an accident. The divine Word is subsistent and eternal, one in essence with the Father from whom he is begotten (ST I, q.~27, aa.~1--2; q.~34, aa.~1--2).

Love gives the second analogy. What is known as good awakens love; in God, the procession according to will is no created affection added to the lover. Aquinas calls the Holy Spirit ``Love'' personally, as the Word is named personally of the Son. Essential love remains common to Father, Son, and Spirit; proceeding Love names the Spirit according to relation of origin (ST I, q.~27, aa.~3--4; q.~37, a.~1).

These analogies do not let the mind inspect the Trinity from above. ``Word'' and ``Love'' are analogical names schooled by revelation. They exclude change, ignorance, bodily generation, emotional need, and division; they help confess an order of origin without a before and after. The Father does not become Father. The Son does not begin to be. The Spirit is not a later bond introduced between previously separate subjects. Eternity has no childhood.

\subsection{Love without need}

Created love often begins in lack. The lover discovers a goodness not possessed, is moved by it, and reaches outward. Bodily emotion may accompany the movement; time may deepen or diminish it. None of this creaturely imperfection can simply be projected into God.

In God, love is pure act. God does not discover goodness outside himself, become attracted, or acquire communion. The Father, Son, and Spirit are not three incomplete selves who need relationship to become whole. Each is the one infinite divine essence; the Persons are really distinct by subsistent relations of origin within the one simple divine life.

This is why creation can be genuinely free. If God first became loving by creating, the creature would complete the Creator and the world would be necessary to divine fulfillment. But the Father loves the Son in the communion of the Holy Spirit without dependence upon anything made. Jesus' words in John 17:24 open this abyss: the Father's love for the Son is not one event within the history of the world. It is already eternal when there is no world to supply an audience.

``God is love'' therefore says infinitely more than ``God loves.'' It does not merely assign one admirable activity to a subject who could be otherwise. In the one simple essence, love is not an accident. The living God is the unreceived plenitude signified by the name---essentially the one charity of Father, Son, and Spirit, and personally, in Aquinas's careful usage, the Holy Spirit as proceeding Love.

Here the metaphysical and revealed movements meet without collapsing. Pure act means there is no needy potential waiting to be fulfilled. Trinity means the uncreated fullness subsists personally as Father, Son, and Spirit. Simplicity means Love is not one part of God. Creation means finite being is not divine substance. Revelation means these truths are not a portrait drawn from philosophical desire, but a confession governed by the Word made flesh and the Spirit given.

The first Cause is not behind Love, as though love required a deeper explanation. Nor is Love behind being, as though two principles competed for ultimacy. In the simple God, the One who is and the One who is Love are not two.
