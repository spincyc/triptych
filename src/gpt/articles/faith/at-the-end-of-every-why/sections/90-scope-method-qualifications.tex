\section{Scope, Method, and Qualifications}

\subsection{Reader, question, and method}

This is a discursive metaphysical and spiritual-theological article for a serious general reader. It asks what kind of ultimate source is required by changing, caused, and participated being, what existential posture follows from that dependence, and how Christian revelation transforms the ascent without being reduced to its final philosophical step.

The argument is deliberately Thomistic: it distinguishes a temporal series from present essentially ordered dependence; moves through act and potency, essence and existence, divine simplicity and unity, creation and conservation, participation and analogical naming; and states exactly where natural reason ends. The patristic and mystical witnesses do not function as a vote or as substitutes for demonstration. They show Christian reception, apophatic discipline, and the conversion of metaphysical judgment into wonder, desire, and charity.

\subsection{Included and excluded scope}

Included are the relevant contours of Aquinas's first two ways; the essence--existence and participation arguments; divine simplicity, unity, presence, creation, and conservation; the analogical attribution of love to God; the natural unknowability of the Trinity; the revealed processions of Word and Love; and a bounded patristic and mystical reception in Athanasius, Gregory Nazianzen, Augustine, Pseudo-Dionysius, Bernard, and John of the Cross.

Excluded are a comprehensive defense of every premise in classical theism; modern analytic reconstructions and objections; scientific cosmology; the Kalam argument; the complete doctrine of divine ideas, providence, evil, grace, deification, or the beatific vision; East--West disputes over Trinitarian procession; and a history of apophatic or mystical theology. The article neither proves a first temporal instant nor claims that a philosophical demonstration compels the mystery of the Trinity.

The phrase ``God is love'' is correctly attributed to 1 John 4:8 and 4:16, not to the Gospel of John. The Gospel's Prologue and John 17 provide the Johannine account of the eternal Word and the Father's love for the Son; the First Letter supplies the exact confession within the missions of Son and Spirit and the command to love one another.

\subsection{Material qualifications}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.45em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item ``First'' is metaphysical, not necessarily chronological. Aquinas holds the temporal beginning of creation by faith while denying that reason demonstrates it (ST I, q.~46, a.~2).
\item The article does not argue that ``everything has a cause.'' It argues from actualized potency, derivative causal power, and participated existence; pure act and subsistent being are not members of the received class.
\item \latin{Ipsum esse subsistens} is Thomistic metaphysical language. The article notes a traditional consonance with Exodus 3:14 but does not turn the Hebrew verse into a scholastic proof.
\item Creation \latin{ex nihilo} does not make ``nothing'' a material and does not mean an emanation of divine substance. Creatures are really distinct from God and continuously dependent upon him.
\item Natural reason can attribute love to God analogically. It cannot demonstrate the three divine Persons or the full Trinitarian and Paschal content of 1 John 4.
\item ``God is love'' neither reverses into ``every love is God'' nor identifies human emotion with divine essence. In the simple God, love is without passion, need, acquisition, or change.
\item Augustine's lover--beloved--love triad is used as a contemplative starting point under revelation, not as a natural proof or literal diagram of the Trinity.
\item Pseudo-Dionysian darkness means excess beyond finite comprehension, not evil, divine absence, irrationalism, or a technique guaranteed to produce experience.
\item Participation by grace never means absorption into the divine essence. The creature remains creature; union perfects rather than abolishes the created distinction.
\item The article's universal claim that created being is freely willed does not claim knowledge of God's particular providential reasons for every event, still less call moral evil good.
\end{enumerate}
