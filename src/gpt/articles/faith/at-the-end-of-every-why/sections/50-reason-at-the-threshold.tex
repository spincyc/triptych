\section{Reason at the Threshold}

The boundary must be stated without haze. Natural reason can ascend from creatures to one Creator and can predicate of him, analogically, intellect, will, goodness, and love. Aquinas argues that love is in God because love is the first movement of will toward good, while the divine will is identical in reality with the simple divine essence (ST I, q.~20, a.~1). But this does not disclose the distinction of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

Aquinas is emphatic: the Trinity of divine Persons cannot be demonstrated by natural reason. Creatures lead the mind to what belongs to God as their one creative principle; creative power is common to the whole Trinity and does not of itself reveal the personal relations (ST I, q.~32, a.~1). Arguments that make companionship necessary to divine goodness fail twice. They weaken faith by presenting a noncompelling inference as proof, and they imagine that infinite goodness needs another in order to be complete.

\begin{studybox}{The hinge of the article}
Metaphysics reaches the one uncreated source of all being. It does not derive the Trinity, and it does not by itself reach the full Johannine confession ``God is love.'' At this threshold the mode of knowing changes: reason does not surrender its integrity, but listens to what God freely reveals.
\end{studybox}

St.~Augustine's famous triad of lover, beloved, and love must remain on the right side of this boundary. In \emph{De Trinitate} VIII, Augustine moves through created goods toward Good itself and through true love of the brother toward the God whom the First Letter of John names Love. He then notices three terms in love: one who loves, what is loved, and love. Yet he immediately treats this as a place from which to continue seeking, not as a psychological container for God or a proof that compels the doctrine of the Trinity (VIII.3.4--5; VIII.7.10--10.14).

That restraint makes the analogy more fruitful. Revelation can teach the mind to recognize vestiges it could not correctly interpret in advance. Once God has named himself Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, created word and love can become distant likenesses of eternal processions. Without revelation, they cannot tell us that the one essence subsists in three Persons. They can prepare wonder; they cannot produce the mystery.

\subsection{The Johannine threshold}

The confession toward which this ascent is drawn is Johannine, though its two halves lie across the Gospel and the First Letter. ``God is love'' occurs twice in 1 John, at 4:8 and 4:16. The Gospel supplies the prologue to the confession: the Word is with God and is God; all things come to be through him; the Word becomes flesh and makes the unseen Father known (John 1:1--3, 14, 18). In Jesus' final prayer, the Father loved the Son before the foundation of the world (John 17:24).

The letter then names the mystery within a history of self-gift. Love is from God. The Father sends the Son so that the world may have life through him. The Son is sent as expiation and Savior. The Spirit is given so that believers abide in God, confess the Son, and love one another. Only within that revealed, Trinitarian, and Paschal movement does John repeat the words: ``God is love'' (1 John 4:7--16).

The sentence is therefore not an abstract equation between God and whatever a culture happens to call love. It does not mean that love is a genus above God, that intense feeling is divine, or that truth and judgment dissolve into affirmation. The letter gives the words their grammar: initiative from the Father, manifestation in the Son's mission and self-offering, indwelling through the Spirit, and a commanded participation made visible in love of the brother.

Reason arrives at the threshold with real knowledge and empty hands. The one source is not anonymous, but reason cannot compel him to speak. Revelation is not one more rung in the causal ladder. It is the free self-disclosure of the One whom the ladder could only know from effects.

And what he discloses is more astonishing than the mind's highest unaided conclusion. Apart from every creature and without any increase from creation, God is eternally Father, Word, and Love.
