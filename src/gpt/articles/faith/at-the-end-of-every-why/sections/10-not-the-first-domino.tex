\section{Not the First Domino}

\subsection{Backward in time is not yet metaphysically deep}

The imagination hears ``first cause'' and supplies a picture: an unimaginably remote beginning, a divine hand touching the first domino, the universe then traveling away under its own momentum. That picture can gesture toward a temporal beginning, which Christian faith confesses. It does not yet reach what Aquinas means by a cause that is first in the order of being.

For Aquinas, reason alone does not demonstrate that the world had a first instant. He argues that even a world with no temporal beginning---considered only as a philosophical hypothesis---would still be created, because ``created'' means receiving being from another, not merely beginning after a stretch of empty time (ST I, q.~46, a.~2). A beginning answers \emph{when}. Creation reaches the more radical question: \emph{from what does the whole actuality of the creature come?}

Earlier events may explain later ones through powers they already possess. A dead ancestor need not remain present for a descendant to act. This is an accidentally ordered series: once generated, the later cause has a real created power of its own. Metaphysical regress becomes decisive when the power exercised by an intermediate is derivative in the very order under consideration. A mirror truly reflects, but it does not illuminate by being merely a mirror. A procession of mirrors may carry light from one to another; multiplying reflections, even without a last countable member, does not make reflected light underived.

The analogy is limited, but the point is exact. Created causes are not unreal. Fire genuinely heats, parents genuinely beget, intellects genuinely understand, and wills genuinely choose. Yet in the act by which any of them is actual, each displays a dependence more intimate than succession. The question is not whether a creature acts, but why there is an acting creature with an actual power to exercise.

\subsection{From potency to act}

Change makes the structure visible. What is only potentially warm cannot, precisely insofar as it remains potential, make itself actually warm. When it becomes warm, potency has been reduced to act. That reduction requires what is already actual in the relevant respect (ST I, q.~2, a.~3).

The point is wider than motion through space. Growth, learning, chemical change, choice, and the exercise of causal power each require actuality under some aspect. If every cause in the relevant order had only borrowed power, none would account for the power being exercised. An endless deferral does not become an explanation merely by lacking a visible edge.

This is the force of an essentially ordered regress. Aquinas's first two ``ways'' begin not from the careless premise ``everything has a cause'' but from actualization and caused causal power. What is actualized cannot actualize itself in the same respect; what receives its causal power cannot be the ultimate source of that power. Explanation terminates only in a source that does not receive the actuality being explained.

\begin{studybox}{What ``first'' means}
``First'' here does not mean earliest in a timeline, nearest to the beginning of the universe, or first among otherwise comparable objects. It means non-derivative in the order of actuality and being. The first Cause is not the first domino. If every domino vanished, God would not be left as a lonely domino. He is not a member of the series at all.
\end{studybox}

\subsection{Why the regress cannot end in another creature}

Suppose the mind names one more finite cause. However powerful, if it receives its actuality, the question remains. If its nature includes unrealized possibilities, acquired perfections, components that must be united, or an existence that it can fail to have, it is not the underived source the regress requires. Calling it ``first'' changes a label, not its metaphysical condition.

Taken together with Aquinas's ensuing arguments from immutability and simplicity, the underived first source is without passive potency altogether: not something waiting to become, acquire, assemble, or continue (ST I, q.~3, aa.~1--2). Aquinas calls this pure act. The phrase does not mean frantic activity or maximal energy. It means that in the first source there is no unrealized capacity and no received perfection. Nothing stands behind it to confer the actuality by which it causes.

Here the vertigo sharpens. The mind is accustomed to understanding one thing through another. But at the root, ``another'' must cease. Not because inquiry becomes lazy, and not because an arbitrary wall has been erected, but because an ultimate explanation cannot possess by dependence the very actuality it is invoked to explain. There must be One who does not become real, who is not kept real, and whose causal power is not on loan.
