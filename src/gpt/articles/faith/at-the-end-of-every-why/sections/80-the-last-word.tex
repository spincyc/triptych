\section{The Last Word}

Return once more to the ordinary room. The table, the wall, the moving hand have not become less real because they are dependent. They have become more astonishing. Their solidity is not self-explanation. Their contingency is not emptiness. Each stands in its own nature because the uncreated source gives it an act of being that is really its own and never self-originated.

Trace the ascent once more. Change points beyond potency to act, borrowed causal power to an underived source, received existence to subsistent being itself. Composition yields to simplicity and possible first principles to the unity of pure act. Creation from nothing is seen as present conservation; transcendence makes possible an intimacy deeper than place; knowledge rises until light is dark from excess.

Then reason stops honestly. It has not failed. It has reached its natural shore. It knows that the source is one, uncreated, intelligent, free, good, and loving; it does not know by its own power that the One is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. It cannot convert the structure of an argument into a divine self-revelation.

Across that boundary comes not a deduction but a Word. The Word is with God and is God; all things are made through him. The Word becomes flesh. The Father sends the Son. The Spirit is given. The eternal communion hidden from the unaided ascent enters history, touches wounds, bears death, and opens created persons to a participation no metaphysical nature could demand.

The first Cause is thus not less personal than the minds he causes. The simplicity of God is not a frozen emptiness. The unity of God is not solitary arithmetic. The immutability of God is not indifference. The darkness is not vacancy. At the root of every created act is unreceived Act; within the life of the unreceived One, revelation discloses eternal Word and proceeding Love.

There is no further cause behind God, no deeper principle that explains why he is. His essence is his existence. There is also no need behind creation, as though a defect compelled him to make. The particular counsels of Providence remain hidden, and ``love'' must never be used to pretend we can decode every suffering. Yet the most universal truth about the creature's existence is gift: it is freely willed by the One whose love does not take goodness from things but gives goodness to them.

This is the existential reversal. At first the regress seemed to strip the world of support. In truth it strips away imaginary independence. The abyss beneath creatures is not another layer of matter, an impersonal law, or an infinite postponement. It is the uncreated plenitude by whose will there are creatures at all. The mind looking for a final explanation finds that it is itself already known, already caused, already held.

And faith hears the name of that plenitude not in a theorem but in the apostolic witness to Father, Son, Spirit, and Cross. The phrase is small enough to memorize and inexhaustible enough to contain the rest of one's life. It does not abolish the metaphysical ascent. It reveals why the ascent ends in adoration.
