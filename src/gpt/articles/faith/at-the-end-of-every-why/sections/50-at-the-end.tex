\section{At the End of Every Why}

The command is not figurative. God is to be loved with the whole heart. Yet an external precept can name the good and expose refusal; it cannot of itself make the good beloved. The New Law does not lower the command. It gives, in the Holy Spirit, the capacity for a free answer.

Charity is that answer: friendship with God, not religious warmth or preference. The friend remains a creature. Christ remains Lord. What changes is not the asymmetry but the creature's relation to the good. Justice is not left behind; its due becomes the act of one who has begun to love the One whom justice acknowledges.

The asymmetry has not narrowed. It has reached even the answer. God gives not only being and the good to be loved but the power to love him. The act is received and free, unequal in source yet capable by grace of being whole.

Ontological vertigo is not resolved by discovering an independent ground beneath creaturehood. Its resolution begins when the will ceases to demand one. Stability is not self-possession over against God but adherence to the One upon whom the creature depends.

What charity removes is not reverence but alienation. Recoil gives way to adherence; service is taken into friendship; the infinite disproportion between Creator and creature becomes no obstacle to communion.

I give God nothing he lacks. The One whom nothing completes gives himself.

At the end of every creaturely why there is no higher law imposed upon God, no need behind his gift, no cause behind his being.
