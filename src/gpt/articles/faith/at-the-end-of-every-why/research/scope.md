# At the End of Every Why — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/articles/faith/at-the-end-of-every-why/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; faith and theology articles.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive metaphysical and spiritual-theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. Online source witnesses were checked through 2026-07-20.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader capable of following basic philosophical distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** What existential and theological vision opens when changing, caused, and participated being is traced to one uncreated source, and how does Christian revelation transform that ascent by disclosing that the one God is Love?
- **Thesis:** in the Thomistic synthesis, present derivative actuality and participated existence require one uncaused source who is pure act and subsistent being itself. This source is simple, unique, freely creative, continuously sustaining, transcendent, and intimately present as cause. Natural reason cannot demonstrate the Trinity. Revelation names the one Creator as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and locates “God is love” within the missions of Son and Spirit and the Paschal command of charity.
- **Intended literary effect:** disciplined metaphysical vertigo, wonder, and contemplative desire arising from the argument rather than substituted for it. No feeling is treated as proof, grace-state test, or promised experience.

## Included scope

The article includes:

1. a compact distinction between temporal and essentially ordered causal regress;
2. act and potency, pure act, essence and existence, divine simplicity and unity;
3. creation *ex nihilo*, continuous conservation, causal intimacy, and participation without pantheism;
4. apophatic knowledge and mystical reception in Gregory Nazianzen, Augustine, and Pseudo-Dionysius;
5. the explicit boundary between natural theology and revealed Trinity;
6. the Johannine relation between the Gospel's Word theology and 1 John 4:7–16;
7. Thomistic processions of Word and Love and divine love as cause of created goodness;
8. bounded monastic and mystical reception in Bernard and John of the Cross; and
9. the ethical test of mystical participation in love of the neighbor.

## Excluded scope

Excluded are a comprehensive proof-defense against every modern objection; physical cosmology and a first temporal event; the Kalam cosmological argument; a general survey of classical theism; a complete treatment of divine ideas, providence, evil, grace, deification, or beatific vision; Trinitarian procession controversies; and a history or manual of mystical prayer.

The article does not compose or offer a prayer for vocal recitation. Its contemplative prose remains exposition and editorial synthesis.

## Authority classes and boundaries

- **Sacred Scripture:** governs creation, the divine name, Word, missions, Cross, Spirit, and the exact confession “God is love.” The philosophical synthesis is not attributed to the human author of an isolated proof-text.
- **Dogma and authoritative synthesis:** Lateran IV, Vatican I, and the *Catechism* govern one Creator, creation from nothing and in freedom, the Creator–creature distinction, simplicity, Trinity, and the truth that God's very being is Love.
- **Patristic witness:** Athanasius, Gregory Nazianzen, Augustine, and Pseudo-Dionysius show reception, doctrinal development, apophatic discipline, and mystical grammar. Breadth is not treated as an authority count.
- **Thomistic theology:** Aquinas supplies the adopted causal, participatory, analogical, and Trinitarian articulation. The article labels its exact philosophical integration as Thomistic rather than attributing every step to a definition of faith.
- **Mystical and monastic witness:** Bernard and John of the Cross show love returning by received grace. Their images are not definitions, automatic methods, or guarantees of experience.
- **Project synthesis:** the ordinary-room opening, metaphysical vertigo, mirrors, shore, and final recapitulation are source-grounded editorial constructions attributed only to this article.

## Material qualifications

1. “God is love” is in the First Letter of John at 4:8 and 4:16, not in the Gospel of John. The document retains the user's Johannine instinct by using John 1 and 17 as the Gospel preparation and 1 John 4:7–16 as the controlling pericope.
2. “First” is metaphysical and hierarchical, not necessarily temporal. ST I, q. 46, a. 2 expressly denies that natural reason demonstrates the world's temporal beginning.
3. The argument is not “everything has a cause.” It concerns what is actualized, derivative, composite, or existent by participation. Pure act and subsistent being are not arbitrary exceptions to a universal causal premise.
4. Essence–existence composition and *ipsum esse subsistens* are Thomistic metaphysical claims. Exodus 3:14 is received as consonant revelation, not treated as a Hebrew-language scholastic proof.
5. Creation from nothing is not change in pre-existing material, and conservation is not temporal repetition in God. God's one eternal act causes temporal, continuously dependent effects.
6. Causal presence does not make creatures parts of God. Natural participation in being and supernatural participation by grace remain distinct; neither is identity of essence.
7. Divine love is not passion, need, permissiveness, or one separable property. Divine simplicity requires love, goodness, being, intellect, and will to be one in reality, while the personal name Love is used of the Holy Spirit under revealed relation of origin.
8. Natural reason cannot demonstrate the Trinity. Augustine's lover–beloved–love triad is a revealed contemplative starting point, not a proof.
9. Pseudo-Dionysian darkness signifies excess beyond finite knowing. It does not authorize irrationalism, doctrinal indifference, or a manufactured private experience.
10. The Cross manifests eternal divine love in the human suffering of the incarnate Son; it does not introduce change or passibility into the divine essence.
11. The universal claim that creatures are willed in their positive actuality does not disclose God's particular providential reasons for every event or make moral evil good.
