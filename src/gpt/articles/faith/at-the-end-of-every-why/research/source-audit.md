## Scripture and authoritative Catholic witnesses

The article uses Gen 1:1; Exod 3:1–15; Deut 6:4; Wis 11:24–26 and 13:1–9; 2 Macc 7:28; John 1:1–3, 14, 18 and 17:20–26; Acts 17:24–28; Rom 1:19–20, 5:5, and 11:33–36; Eph 3:14–21; Heb 1:1–3; 2 Pet 1:4; 1 John 1:5 and 4:7–21.

The exact phrase “God is love” is located at 1 John 4:8 and 4:16. The official Holy See English Catechism repeats that wording at nos. 214 and 221 and identifies it as the teaching of the apostle John. The controlling article page is <https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_1_i_believe_in_god.index.html>.

Authoritative doctrinal loci:

- Fourth Lateran Council, *Firmiter credimus* (1215), DS 800–802: one entirely simple essence in three Persons; creator of all visible and invisible things from nothing.
- First Vatican Council, *Dei Filius* (1870), chs. 1–2 and canons 1.1–5 and 2.1, DS 3001–3005, 3021–3026: one true, living, simple God; creatures distinct from the divine substance; free creation from nothing; natural knowledge through created things. Official Latin: <https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/i-vatican-council/documents/vat-i_const_18700424_dei-filius_la.html>.
- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 198–231, especially 202, 206–213, 221, and 230; 232–267; 279–301: one God, the name “I AM,” creatures receiving being, divine incomprehensibility, Trinity, God as Love, and creation from nothing through Word and Spirit.
- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 464–469, especially 468–469: the one divine Person of the Son is the subject of the acts and sufferings of his assumed humanity, while the two natures remain without confusion or change. Official English: <https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_two/artcile_3.html>.

## Thomistic source corpus

The governing Latin is the Leonine-text corpus exposed through Corpus Thomisticum: <https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html>. The New Advent *Summa* index (<https://www.newadvent.org/summa/>) served as a working public English aid. No long translation is incorporated, and citations by work and locus govern over web wording.

Particularly checked Latin phrases and conclusions:

- ST I, q. 3, a. 4: God is his existence; what has existence but is not existence is being by participation.
- ST I, q. 4, a. 2: God is *ipsum esse per se subsistens* and contains the perfection of being without creaturely division.
- ST I, q. 8, a. 1: created being is God's proper effect, caused for as long as the creature exists, so God is present intimately as cause.
- ST I, q. 11, a. 3: simplicity and infinite perfection exclude several first gods.
- ST I, q. 20, a. 1: love must be affirmed of God without sensory passion.
- ST I, q. 20, a. 2: *amor Dei est infundens et creans bonitatem in rebus*; the article gives a close, brief analytical rendering.
- ST I, q. 29, a. 4: in God, Person signifies a subsistent relation; real personal distinction does not divide the divine essence.
- ST I, q. 32, a. 1: natural reason reaches what belongs to God as common creative principle, not personal distinction.
- ST I, q. 37, a. 1: love is said essentially and, as proceeding Love, personally of the Holy Spirit.
- ST I, q. 43, aa. 1–2: a divine mission includes eternal procession together with a temporal effect and is itself temporal.
- ST I, q. 44, a. 1: all beings other than God participate in being and are caused by the one subsistent first being.
- ST I, q. 104, a. 1: conservation is the continuation of the giving of being without a new action or temporal change in God.

## Consequential negative results and rejected formulations

- The exact phrase “God is love” was not found in the Gospel of John. It occurs twice in the First Letter of John, at 4:8 and 4:16. The article corrects the attribution while retaining the Johannine Gospel–epistle arc.
- The article rejects “everything has a cause.” Aquinas's arguments concern moved, caused, necessary-by-another, or participated realities; God is not an exception to a premise that includes him.
- An infinite temporal past is not treated as the target of Aquinas's regress. ST I, q. 46, a. 2 blocks that attribution.
- “Created” is not defined as “began after a time when it was absent.” It is dependence for being, compatible in Aquinas's philosophical analysis with the hypothetical absence of a first instant.
- Exodus 3:14 is not presented as a direct lexical proof of essence–existence identity. Its relation to *ipsum esse* is identified as traditional Christian reception.
- No source supports turning God into common being, a cosmic field, the sum of the universe, or a substance divided into creatures. ST I, q. 3, aa. 5, 8 and q. 44, a. 1 exclude those readings.
- “Concepts create idols; only wonder comprehends anything” is not used; it is a modern adaptation often misattributed to Gregory of Nyssa.
- Pseudo-Dionysian divine darkness is not treated as literal darkness, ignorance without prior knowledge, or a warrant for anti-intellectualism.
- Augustine's lover–beloved–love triad does not demonstrate Trinity. Augustine himself presents it as a point from which inquiry continues, and Aquinas expressly denies natural demonstration.
- God's eternal love is not made dependent upon creation, companionship, lack, emotional change, or suffering in the divine nature.
- “God is love” is not reversed into “love is God,” and no created feeling is canonized as divine merely by intensity.
- The article does not claim a private mystical experience, promise one to the reader, or make an existential feeling evidence of grace.
- No composed prayer, modernized received prayer, or AI-generated text for vocal recitation is included.
