\section{References and Source Boundary}

\noindent\emph{These sources materially govern the article. Masonic self-descriptions establish what the named body publicly claims and regulates; they do not independently establish historical effects or Catholic compatibility. Holy See texts are cited by exact act, date, and locus. Online legal and institutional evidence was checked through 13 July 2026.}

\subsection*{Sacred Scripture and Catholic theological sources}

{\footnotesize
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em,topsep=0.15em]
\item Ex 20:7; Deut 6:13; Pss 115 and 127; Matt 5:33--37, 7:15--20, 22:34--40; John 1:1--18, 2:13--22, 8:12, 14:6, 17:17--23; Acts 4:12; Rom 1:18--25, 12:1--21; 1 Cor 8--10, especially 10:14--22; 2 Cor 6:14--18; Eph 2:11--22, 4:1--6; Heb 1:1--3, 3:1--6, 11:10; Jas 5:12; 1 Pet 2:4--10. Brief scriptural wording is a working rendering; no modern translation is reproduced at length.
\item Second Vatican Council, dogmatic constitution \emph{Dei Verbum} (1965), nos.~2--6, on God's self-Revelation and the obedience of faith; declaration \emph{Dignitatis humanae} (1965), nos.~1--3, on civil immunity, the duty to seek truth, and the unchanged doctrine of the true religion; \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651207_dignitatis-humanae_en.html}{official English text}.
\item First Vatican Council, dogmatic constitution \emph{Dei Filius} (1870), chs.~2--3, on natural knowledge of God, supernatural Revelation, and faith; \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/i-vatican-council/documents/vat-i_const_18700424_dei-filius_la.html}{official Latin text}.
\item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, nos.~144--165 (faith), 813--822 (unity), 1262--1274 (Baptism), 1735 and 1857--1861 (imputability and mortal sin), 2104--2109 (religious truth and civil freedom), 2110--2117 (superstition and occult practice), 2150--2155 (oaths), 2477--2479 (rash judgment, detraction, and calumny).
\item St.~Augustine, \emph{City of God} XIV.28, on the two loves and cities; the encyclical's use is governed by its own paragraph 11 and by Augustine's eschatological account of intermingled societies.
\item St.~Thomas Aquinas, \emph{Summa theologiae} I--II, qq.~18--21 (object, end, circumstances, and moral specification); II--II, qq.~1--5 (faith), q.~23 (charity), q.~81 (religion), q.~89 (oaths), qq.~92--96 (superstition and illicit religious practices), q.~104 (obedience). \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html}{Corpus Thomisticum}; \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/summa/}{working English index}.
\end{itemize}}

These sources establish self-description, not Catholic theological approval.
