# Freemasonry and the Catholic Church — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Document identity and governing question

This record supports `src/gpt/articles/faith/freemasonry-and-the-catholic-church/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; faith and theology articles.
- **Primary genre and profile:** discursive historical and systematic faith/theology article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Mixed modules:** the history modules follow documentary historical method; the canon-law module follows the mixed-article and Latin-canon-law requirements within the same profile.
- **Question:** What is Freemasonry as a historically diverse initiatic family, why has the Catholic Church continuously judged Masonic membership incompatible with the faith, how has the judgment’s theological and juridical expression developed, what effects can be responsibly attributed to Masonic bodies and networks around the world, and what is the present Catholic discipline?
- **Intended reader:** a serious Catholic lay reader, pastor preparing catechesis, Catholic considering or leaving a lodge, relative of a Mason, or researcher seeking a source-disciplined account.
- **Governing thesis:** the prohibition does not depend on proving a single world conspiracy or practical hostility in every lodge. It rests on the structural conflict between Catholic faith and an initiatic moral-ritual order that makes the revealed identity of God non-governing, places contradictory sacred authorities in analogous ritual roles or makes theism optional, and creates a transconfessional fraternity through graded initiation and obligations. Historical hostility is real in specified settings but is not the whole rationale.
- **Online verification and legal as-of date:** 2026-07-13.

## Legal scope

- **Code and edition:** the 1983 *Codex Iuris Canonici* for the Latin Church, official Latin and Holy See English web texts, including Book VI as revised by *Pascite gregem Dei* and effective 8 December 2021.
- **Jurisdiction and persons:** Latin Catholics universally insofar as the Code applies. The article expressly does not apply the Latin Code automatically to Eastern Catholics; a CCEO, particular-law, and competent-authority analysis would be required.
- **Universal non-codal acts:** CDF declaration of 17 February 1981; CDF declaration of 26 November 1983 approved and ordered published by John Paul II; CDF explanatory reflection of 23 February 1985; DDF response approved by Francis on 13 November 2023.
- **Historical law:** 1917 CIC canon 2335 and Pius IX’s 1869 censure inventory are explained as superseded law, not present penalties.
- **Mutable-source check:** the current Holy See Code text, *Pascite gregem Dei*, and DDF document index were checked through the as-of date. No later universal Roman act reversing the 1983 judgment was located.
- **Case facts not assumed:** age, Church *sui iuris*, degree and mode of membership, knowledge, coercion, office, clerical or religious status, public manifestation, a body’s actual plotting, particular law, penal precepts, prior censures, and sacramental forum. Any of these can alter a concrete legal or pastoral conclusion.

## Included scope

1. Documentary development of speculative Freemasonry from the Schaw Statutes and seventeenth-century accepted membership through 1717, the 1723 Constitutions, the Antients/Moderns division, the 1813 union, global expansion, and the 1877 regular/Continental divide.
2. Exact working definitions of lodge, Grand Lodge or Grand Orient, Craft degrees, rite, appendant body, recognition, regularity, liberal or adogmatic Masonry, secrecy, and Masonic temple terminology.
3. UGLE as a principal current witness to Anglo-American regular Masonry and the Grand Orient de France as a principal current witness to liberal/adogmatic Masonry. These are representative bodies, not universal world constitutions.
4. A complete papal, codal, and Roman-dicasterial corpus under the inclusion rule recorded below, plus a separately labeled set of broader adjacent texts.
5. A paragraph-structure exposition of Leo XIII’s *Humanum genus*, including its naturalism diagnosis, empirical-historical limits, paragraph 11 qualification about individuals, and positive pastoral program.
6. Catholic theological analysis of Revelation, the doctrine of God, natural and supernatural virtue, grace, Temple and Church, oaths, secrecy, Baptism, fraternity, religious liberty, and cooperation.
7. Development from named automatic excommunication under the 1917 Code to current doctrine, sacramental discipline, and canon 1374.
8. Representative global cases: Britain and empire, France, Italy and P2, the United States and Prince Hall, Latin American independence, the Philippines, Ottoman lands, and Nazi suppression.
9. Myth control concerning Satanism, the Taxil hoax and false Pike quotation, Illuminati and Templar identity claims, one-world-command claims, and the alleged requirement that Masonic buildings be near Catholic tabernacles.
10. A pastoral path for discernment, withdrawal, confession, family relations, catechesis, and legitimate civic cooperation.

## Excluded or bounded scope

- No complete ritual script or universal ritual concordance is published. Publicly acknowledged constitutional structures suffice for the core theological analysis; ritual variants require lodge-, rite-, and jurisdiction-specific primary evidence.
- The article is not an encyclopedia of every Masonic, para-Masonic, fraternal, occult, chivalric, political, or youth body.
- It does not determine whether any named living person is a Mason, any person’s subjective mortal culpability, any actual censure, or admission to Communion in a concrete public case.
- It does not provide a complete national history for every jurisdiction. The global section is representative and explicitly distinguishes corporate action, member networks, symbolism, and retrospective myth.
- It does not equate the Carbonari, Bavarian Illuminati, Knights Templar, Rosicrucians, P2, Scottish or York Rite bodies, Shriners, or Satanism with Freemasonry generically. Overlap must be separately proved.
- It does not claim that every nineteenth-century papal empirical or political assessment is a timeless description of all later lodges. It distinguishes authoritative theological judgment from historically testable application.
- It does not treat Catholic civil-religious-liberty doctrine as approval of religious indifferentism, nor old polemics as license for coercion or persecution.

## Roman-document inclusion rule

The phrase “all papal proclamations” has no stable research boundary if it means every incidental papal occurrence of “Masonry,” “secret societies,” naturalism, liberalism, the Carbonari, or anti-clerical movements. The published appendix and `research/roman-corpus.md` therefore use this rule:

> Include every located universal papal constitution, bull, encyclical, allocution, or letter whose principal object is Freemasonry or whose named adjacent association is expressly incorporated into the developing Masonic/secret-society discipline; every historical codal or papal penal provision directly governing Masonic enrollment; and every Roman-dicasterial act authoritatively interpreting the present prohibition. List broader verified contextual acts separately and do not call that secondary list exhaustive.

This yields a reproducibly complete **controlling and materially direct corpus under a stated rule**, not a claim to a word-level concordance of every papal text, local episcopal norm, or archive.

## Authority classes and editorial boundaries

- **Scripture and dogmatic sources:** the identity and self-Revelation of the triune God, Christ’s unique mediation, faith as response to divine Revelation, Baptismal incorporation, the Church as Temple and Body, and the moral character of religious acts.
- **Authoritative but non-definitive magisterial judgment:** the continuous papal and CDF/DDF judgment that Masonic membership is forbidden and its principles irreconcilable with Catholic doctrine. Each act is cited by exact genre, date, and material locus.
- **Canon law:** the text of historical and current law is distinguished from theological prohibition, sacramental consequence, interpretation, and case application. The article does not call a current membership-only consequence automatic excommunication.
- **Masonic institutional sources:** establish how the named body publicly defines and regulates itself. They do not independently authenticate its entire history, prove effects, or decide Catholic compatibility.
- **Professional historical scholarship:** supports institutional development, networks, and regional effects while preserving debate and causal limits.
- **Thomistic analysis:** Aquinas on faith, religion, oaths, superstition, obedience, and moral specification supplies theological articulation. It is not presented as though Aquinas wrote about modern Freemasonry.
- **Project synthesis:** the family definition, four-level causal test, documentary inclusion rule, comparative diagrams, claim audit, and pastoral decision map are the article’s editorial syntheses.

## Material qualifications

1. **No world sovereign:** autonomous Grand Lodges, recognition systems, and the UGLE/Grand Orient divide disprove a single public world jurisdiction. This does not prove that informal or transnational networks never coordinate.
2. **“Regular” is not Catholic approval:** it is an internal recognition term and may include a Supreme-Being requirement. It does not mean doctrinally orthodox, morally licit, or canonically permitted.
3. **Generic theism is not Trinitarian confession:** the phrase Great Architect can be innocently used by a Christian, but its institutional common-denominator function is the issue.
4. **Masonry and religion:** the article avoids making compatibility depend on the unstable one-word classification “religion.” The practices materially engage acts and symbols of religion even where the organization denies being a religion.
5. **Natural virtue:** the article acknowledges charity, mutual aid, discipline, and civic goods accomplished by lodges and members. A good effect does not make an incompatible initiatic bond licit.
6. **Leo XIII and persons:** *Humanum genus* 11 expressly distinguishes generic institutional judgment from individuals’ awareness and participation. The two-cities frame is not used to label every Mason a conscious Satanist.
7. **Historical causation:** a famous member’s participation is not automatically a lodge decision. The published rule tests institutional decision, use of Masonic organization or resources, and importance among multiple causes.
8. **Vatican II:** *Dignitatis humanae* concerns civil immunity from coercion while retaining the duty to seek and adhere to truth. It does not reverse the Masonic prohibition or establish ritual equivalence.
9. **Grave sin and culpability:** the 1985 CDF reflection explains membership as objectively grave. Full subjective mortal guilt still requires knowledge and deliberate consent; the 2023 note’s formally and knowingly enrolled formulation is retained.
10. **No current membership-only automatic excommunication:** current canon 1374 does not expressly attach a *latae sententiae* excommunication. A body that actually plots against the Church can fall under the canon; other delicts or precepts may arise on additional facts.
11. **Canons 915 and 916:** the personal obligation not to receive is not identical to a minister’s external-forum determination of obstinate perseverance in manifest grave sin. The article does not decide a concrete Communion case.
12. **Tabernacle proximity:** no governing Masonic requirement or reliable historical evidence was located for a rule that Masonic temples must be near a Catholic tabernacle. The universal claim is retained only as a rejected lead. An idiosyncratic local motive cannot be universally disproved.
13. **Taxil:** the Palladian/Luciferian narrative and the circulating Pike “Lucifer” quotation are not used as evidence. The hoax is included as a warning about source discipline.
14. **Civil dignity:** doctrinal opposition never licenses arson, confiscation, racial conspiracy, torture, collective punishment, or false accusation. Nazi anti-Masonic persecution is included as a moral and historical boundary.

## Copyright and quotation review

The article does not reproduce full modern Holy See, Masonic, or scholarly texts. It links official full texts and gives claim-focused exposition. Direct quotations are limited to brief legally or doctrinally necessary phrases, including the 1917 canon’s short Latin text, the current CDF formula, and a few institutional terms. No ritual exposure, copyrighted monograph chapter, bulk archival scan, or full papal translation is vendored into the repository.
