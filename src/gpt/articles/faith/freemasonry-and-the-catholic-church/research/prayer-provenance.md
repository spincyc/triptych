# Exorcism and Masonry-Prayer Provenance Audit

Checked 2026-07-13. This record supports the article’s section “Spiritual Warfare, Exorcism, and the Masonic Name.” It records textual provenance and authority; it is not a prayer manual, a diagnosis, or permission to use a reserved rite.

## Governing distinctions

1. A promulgated liturgical formula has the authority and restrictions of the rite in which it appears.
2. An episcopally approved private prayer book is not thereby part of the Roman Ritual.
3. An imprimatur attaches to the identified text or edition; CIC canon 829 prevents silent extension to altered editions or translations.
4. A Catholic ministry website can establish present circulation and its own audience label. It does not establish ancient authorship, universal promulgation, or the approval of an unidentified version.
5. A prayer’s petitions, renunciations, historical assertions, demon names, genealogical theory, and medical claims are analytically distinct. The prayer does not independently prove the non-devotional propositions it contains.
6. Official demonology establishes the reality of Satan and spiritual combat while preserving divine sovereignty, human responsibility, natural causes, sacramental priority, reserve, and critical prudence.

## Leo XIII: exact historical findings

- The official title is Exorcismus in Satanam et angelos apostaticos iussu Leonis XIII P. M. editus.
- The rescript is dated 18 May 1890.
- The phrase omnis congregatio et secta diabolica is generic grammar, not a Masonic proper name.
- Humanum genus (20 April 1884), the exorcism (18 May 1890), and Dall’alto (15 October 1890) share a pontificate and an intelligible spiritual-political horizon. Chronology permits comparison but not textual substitution.
- The long exorcism and the short St Michael prayer associated with the prayers after Low Mass are different texts.
- No official 1890 statement was located saying that a private vision caused the formula or that Freemasonry was its specific object.
- The 1985 CDF letter controls later lay-use claims.

## Version-control findings

- “The Freemasonic curse prayer” does not identify one stable text.
- The Stevens witness carries repeated revision dates.
- The Catholic adaptation adds, removes, and rearranges material.
- St Michael Center supplies long, short, and ratification texts with different assigned users.
- Online reposts can combine headings, clauses, and instructions from different versions.
- Approval must therefore be stated as: identified title, edition, language, date, approving ordinary if verified, and intended user.

## Embedded-claim audit

| Claim type in the prayer family | What the text establishes | What additional proof is required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renunciation of an unjust oath | The author proposes this as a Christian act and the user may sincerely reject a sinful obligation. | Concrete membership and the actual obligation help specify the moral act; a pastor guides sacramental reconciliation. |
| Degree words, signs, penalties, and interpretations | The prayer attributes material to degrees. | Authenticated ritual editions, jurisdiction, date, and independent historical comparison. |
| A demon assigned to a degree | The prayer or minister makes the attribution. | It cannot be established by the prayer itself or by alleged demonic testimony; no independent official doctrinal list was located. |
| Degree-to-organ or disease mapping | The prayer alleges a causal pattern. | Competent medical evidence and case-specific ecclesiastical discernment; no automatic causal inference from prayer language. |
| Ancestral transmission | The text assumes effects may pass through lineage. | Distinguish personal guilt, natural and social consequences, a specific external curse, and extraordinary affliction. Universal automatic transmission is not established. |
| Reported inability to finish the prayer or symptom change | A person reports an experience. | Not a self-authenticating diagnostic test; pastoral, medical, and psychological assessment remains necessary. |
| Formula reported as efficacious by an exorcist | Pastoral testimony exists. | Does not prove every embedded historical, demonological, genealogical, or medical assertion. |

## Negative results

1. No Masonry-specific formula was located in the official public inventory of the current Roman exorcism rite.
2. Leo XIII’s official 1890 formula does not name Masonry.
3. No official 1890 source was located identifying a Masonic target or private vision as the formula’s promulgated rationale.
4. No evidence was located that the Stevens–Ripperger long form is ancient, composed by Leo XIII, or part of the universal Roman Ritual.
5. No promulgation decree or universal approval was located for the St Michael Center long, short, or anonymous ratification webpage versions.
6. No official Catholic degree-to-demon, degree-to-organ, or Masonic-ancestry diagnostic table was located.
7. No basis was located for treating ancestry as personal guilt or for making completion of a prayer a diagnostic test.

## Editorial consequences

- The article now begins from two initiations and two renunciations: Baptism is controlling.
- Spiritual conflict is affirmed objectively without collapsing body, person, culpability, possession, and ancestry.
- The Leonine formula is treated as official but not Masonic by name.
- The modern prayer family is treated as Protestant in earliest verified provenance, later Catholicized and received in some Catholic ministries.
- An imprimatur is described accurately as edition-specific publication approval.
- Full modern prayers are not reproduced. Titles, short controlling phrases, content anatomy, stable witnesses, and authority status provide the needed analysis without creating an unauthorized devotional manual or copying changing modern texts.
- Pastoral advice places confession, withdrawal, baptismal promises, ordinary care, and diocesan referral before extraordinary diagnosis.

## Review limitations

- No private diocesan exorcist manual or non-public current ritual text was accessed.
- The publicly accessible 2018 front-matter approval record should be checked against a physical copy in any future ecclesiastical-review stage.
- No independent medical review of the prayer family’s symptom claims has been performed; the publication therefore does not repeat them as facts.
- Independent liturgical, sacramental-theological, exorcist, historical, and canonical review remains outstanding.
