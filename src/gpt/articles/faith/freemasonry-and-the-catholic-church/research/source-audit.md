# Source Audit — Freemasonry and the Catholic Church

Access and currentness checks were completed 2026-07-13 unless noted. The publication uses claim-focused paraphrase and short quotations. “Official Masonic” below means authoritative for a named body’s public self-description or constitution, not independent historical validation or Catholic approval.

## Documentary history

| Claim | Source | Evidence use / qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Catholic doctrine and development

The complete verified Roman-act record is in `roman-corpus.md`. The following claims govern the publication.

| Claim | Source and locus | Authority / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catholic prohibition begins universally in 1738 and is confirmed in 1751. | Clement XII, *In eminenti*; Benedict XIV, *Providas*, both reproduced in Leo XII, *Quo graviora*. | Papal disciplinary and doctrinal-historical acts. Old censures are not current law. |
| Pius IX directly renewed the judgment and named censures. | *Multiplices inter* (1865); *Apostolicae Sedis* no. IV (1869). | Direct primary texts; latter is superseded penal discipline. |
| Leo XIII diagnoses naturalism and indifferentism, but expressly limits generic institutional attribution regarding individuals. | *Humanum genus* 9–12, especially 11; doctrinal exposition 12–29; remedy 30–38. | Principal authoritative exposition. Historical-political claims are not universalized without evidence. |
| The 1983 omission of “Masonic” did not reverse the negative judgment; principles remain irreconcilable, membership forbidden, enrollment objectively grave, and enrolled faithful may not receive Communion. | CDF Declaration (1983); CDF *Reflections* (1985), especially its final third explaining objective grave sin. | Controlling current judgment and official explanation. Subjective mortal culpability remains distinct. |
| Practical non-hostility does not remove incompatibility; the deeper issue is a progressive symbolic moral-ritual system with a relativizing effect upon faith and two relations to God. | CDF *Reflections* (1985), paragraphs discussing symbolic system, arcane discipline, relativism, wider unattainable truth, and incompatible relations to God. | Official dicasterial theological explanation; publication does not claim each body professes one philosophical creed. |
| The 2023 response reaffirms the norm for the Philippines and applies the measures to formally and knowingly enrolled persons embracing Masonic principles, including clerics, while calling for catechesis. | DDF, response to Bishop Julito Cortes, 13 Nov. 2023, items (a) and (b). | Latest located specific Roman application through cutoff; Philippine occasion, universal norm cited. |

## Canon-law audit

| Proposition | Controlling source | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The 1917 Code named the Masonic sect and attached reserved automatic excommunication. | 1917 CIC can. 2335. | True historically; exact Latin reproduced briefly. Abrogated by 1983 CIC can. 6 §1, 1°. |
| The current Latin Code does not attach an automatic excommunication to Masonic enrollment as such. | CIC cann. 1314 and 1374; 1983 CDF declaration. | Verified. The doctrinal prohibition and grave-sin judgment remain; they are not a *latae sententiae* excommunication. |
| Current canon 1374 applies to an association that plots against the Church and prescribes a just penalty for joining and interdict for promotion or office. | Revised Book VI, can. 1374, official Holy See text. | Exact current text checked. Factual plotting and penal process/imputability cannot be presumed from the label alone. |
| Penal liability requires an external violation gravely imputable by malice or culpability; current penalties and process have their own rules. | CIC cann. 1321–1325 and 1341–1349. | Governs any actual penalty analysis; article gives no case ruling. |
| A communicant’s can. 916 obligation and a minister’s can. 915 duty are distinct. | CIC cann. 915–916; CDF 1983 and 1985. | The declaration directly tells enrolled faithful not to receive. It does not eliminate the separate external elements of manifest, grave, obstinate perseverance for can. 915. |
| Penance requires repentance and purpose of amendment. | CIC cann. 959, 987–989; CCC 1451–1460. | Supports withdrawal path; an actual old censure or public case needs qualified counsel. |
| Latin law does not automatically govern Eastern Catholics. | CIC can. 1. | Publication expressly limits jurisdiction and does not invent a CCEO penalty. |
| Major exorcism over a possessed person requires a local ordinary’s special and express permission and a suitable priest. | CIC can. 1172; CDF 1985; current Roman Ritual discipline. | No membership, ancestry, private prayer, or website supplies the missing faculty or discernment. |
| Prayer-book approval is edition-specific and does not equal liturgical promulgation. | CIC cann. 826 §3 and 829–830. | Governs the article’s description of the Ripperger edition and online derivatives. |
| Exorcism may not be folded into improvised healing services or inserted into Mass, sacraments, or the Hours. | CDF instruction on prayers for healing (2000), arts. 5–10. | Controls pastoral recommendations and rejects a Masonry-specific counter-liturgy. |

## Myth and negative-result audit

| Lead or claim | Search / evidence checked | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masonic temples must be near a Catholic tabernacle. | UGLE *Book of Constitutions* June 2026 searched for tabernacle, church, temple and siting rules; UGLE public building/governance descriptions; Masons of California and Pennsylvania Grand Lodge building terminology; general historical and web leads. | **Not established.** No governing rule or reliable historical source located. Old civic-center proximity is not evidence of obligation. The result cannot disprove an idiosyncratic local motive but excludes the universal claim. |
| Every Mason or lodge is Satanist. | Current regular and liberal institutional sources; *Humanum genus* 11; CDF rationale. | Unsupported and false as a universal institutional description. Catholic incompatibility is stated without it. |
| Leo XIII’s official exorcism explicitly names Masonry. | ASS 23 (1890–91), 743–746 searched and read in full; compared with *Humanum genus* and *Dall’alto*. | **False as a textual claim.** The prayer is contextually adjacent but contains no Masonic proper name. |
| The long Masonry-specific prayer is ancient Roman liturgy or was composed by Leo XIII. | Stevens publication history; Ripperger 2021 provenance admission; official Roman sources. | **False.** Earliest verified ancestor is Protestant and modern; no Roman promulgation located. |
| An imprimatur makes all prayer claims magisterial facts and all web versions approved. | CIC cann. 826 and 829–830; publisher and webpage comparison. | **False.** Publication approval, doctrine, liturgical promulgation, empirical proof, and derivative-version status are distinct. |
| A Masonic ancestor automatically gives descendants personal guilt or a diagnosable disease/curse pattern. | CCC 405, 1263–1264; ST I–II q. 87 a. 8; Spanish bishops’ 2024 note; official exorcism discernment. | **Rejected as a universal claim.** Real consequences remain possible; guilt is personal and extraordinary claims require competent case discernment. |
| Inability to complete a long prayer or symptom change diagnoses Masonic affliction. | Ripperger interview claim compared with official discernment rules. | **Not established.** Pastoral anecdote is not a self-authenticating diagnostic test; ordinary care and diocesan discernment remain. |
| Taxil’s Palladian order and Pike Lucifer speech are authentic. | Ruben van Luijk, “Unmasking the Synagogue of Satan”; BnF historical account; hoax history. | Rejected as fabrication. Not used as evidence. |
| A high numbered Scottish Rite degree commands Craft Masonry worldwide. | UGLE sovereignty/recognition rules and organizational histories. | False; degree systems and sovereign jurisdictions are distinct. Informal influence requires separate evidence. |
| Bavarian Illuminati, Carbonari, Templars, Rosicrucians, P2, and Freemasonry are interchangeable names. | Professional histories and the named primary acts. | Rejected. Overlap, recruitment, imitation, or claimed genealogy must be proved case by case. |
| Charity or practical non-hostility proves Catholic compatibility. | CDF 1983 and 1985; Masonic charity self-descriptions. | Non sequitur. Real good works are acknowledged; the structural doctrinal judgment remains. |
