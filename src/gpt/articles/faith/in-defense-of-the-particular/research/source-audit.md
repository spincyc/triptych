# Source Audit — In Defense of the Particular

The identified online witnesses were checked through 2026-07-23. Official English is used for conciliar, catechetical, doctrinal, and papal texts. Brief Scriptural phrases are working-English renderings checked at the cited loci; no claim is made that one modern English edition governs every phrase. The credal phrases use the current official U.S.-English Nicene Creed published by the United States Conference of Catholic Bishops. Thomistic claims are governed by work, question, article, and reply, with Latin checked through Corpus Thomisticum and New Advent used as a public working-English aid.

## Primary and authoritative witnesses

## Bounded use of *Dominus Iesus*

The declaration is unusually exact for the article's central Christological move and has therefore been retained after explicit editorial approval. Its public reception in interreligious contexts has been contested. The article responds by:

1. citing only sections 6 and 13–15;
2. using section 6 for the relation between finite human acts and their divine personal subject;
3. using sections 13–15 only for unique and universal mediation;
4. pairing those claims with *Dei Verbum*, *Fides et ratio*, *Gaudium et spes*, and *Nostra aetate*;
5. affirming truth and holiness in other religions and the Spirit's universally offered possibility of association with Christ; and
6. avoiding the declaration's polemical or corrective register as the article's literary voice.

The pairing does not relativize *Dominus Iesus* or place conciliar respect in opposition to Christ's uniqueness. It prevents a precise doctrinal claim from being made to do unrelated comparative work.

## Consequential negative results and rejected formulations

- “A particular God” was rejected as an unqualified metaphysical description. God is not one member of a class called gods.
- The article does not oppose universals, natural theology, analogy, apophatic theology, contemplation, silence, or divine transcendence.
- “Universal nirvana” was rejected. Nirvana is not treated as an impersonal creator deity, and Buddhist traditions are neither defined nor homogenized.
- The target is not classified as formal heresy. “Flight into abstraction” is a project label for an argumentative temptation.
- Election is not superiority, innocence, ethnic favoritism, or license for nationalism.
- Israel is not a disposable ladder or rejected archaeological remainder. The article preserves Romans 11, *Nostra aetate* 4, and CCC 839–840 without attempting a complete covenant theology.
- Christ's historical particularity is not made one manifestation among parallel divine embodiments, and his true humanity is not made infinite or absorbed.
- Unique universal mediation is not universal salvation, a denial of grace beyond explicit Christian knowledge, or a claim that other religions contain nothing true or holy.
- Particular providence is not divine micromanagement, occasionalism, or a warrant to read suffering as personal punishment.
- Sacraments are not magic, automatic subjective fruitfulness, private invention, or divine confinement. Naming transubstantiation and Christ's true, real, and substantial presence does not attempt to supply a complete Eucharistic theology.
- Love of neighbor is not substituted for worship, sacrament, faith, or the Creator–creature distinction.
- Coherence is not substituted for historical evidence or supernatural faith.
