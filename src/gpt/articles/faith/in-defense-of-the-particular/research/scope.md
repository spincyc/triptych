# In Support of the Particular — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/articles/faith/in-support-of-the-particular/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; faith and theology articles.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive philosophical- and dogmatic-theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Series relation:** a standalone companion essay in *The Creature Before God*. It is not Part IV. It addresses an objection at the transition from the natural-theological source reached in Part I to the revealed and sacramental order presupposed by Part II.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, mutable law, or territorial discipline governs the argument. Identified online witnesses were checked through 2026-07-23.
- **Intended reader:** an intellectually serious Christian, or a reader considering the Christian claim, who can follow a basic metaphysical distinction but is not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** Does the universality and transcendence of God make divine choice, historical revelation, Incarnation, particular providence, sacramental signs, and concrete personal address unworthy or incoherent?
- **Thesis:** the recoil often confuses an abstract universal with a universal cause. God is neither a general concept nor one member of a class. The universal cause knows and sustains singular creatures; biblical election is ordered to mission; the eternal Word assumes a determinate human nature and performs historically finite acts whose personal subject is the divine Son; and the one Paschal mystery is communicated through embodied sacramental signs. Particular mediation does not imply a merely provincial destination.
- **Argumentative limit:** the article establishes coherence and the internal Christian logic of historical and sacramental particularity. It does not prove the occurrence of the Incarnation or Resurrection merely from metaphysical fittingness, establish the credibility of every alleged revelation, or supply the reader's act of faith.
- **Intended literary movement:** the name of Pontius Pilate creates the opening pressure; the universal-concept/universal-cause distinction removes the metaphysical veto; Abraham and Israel show election ordered outward; the Incarnation joins finite human action to the divine Person; sacrament and neighbor embody the same logic; and the article ends at Christ's question in Matthew 16:15. The conversational voice is project synthesis, and no affective effect is treated as proof.

## Terms fixed for this study

- **Particular** means concrete, singular, determinate, or historically located: this person, people, act, word, place, time, or sign. It does not mean that God is a member of a genus.
- **Abstract universal** means a concept considered apart from individuating features. It has intentional or logical universality but does not act as a cause merely by being conceived.
- **Universal cause** means a cause whose power extends to every effect within its order. Applied to God, it names universal causality of creaturely being, not “being in general” or the widest concept.
- **Impersonal ultimacy** is a bounded description of the modern tendency under examination. It is not assigned as the definition of Buddhism, nirvana, Hindu traditions, apophatic theology, or every non-Christian account of ultimacy.
- **Election** means gratuitous divine choice giving vocation and mission. It is not a verdict of antecedent superiority, innocence, or exclusive divine concern.
- **Universal salvific scope** means that Christ's unique mediation and the offer of salvation are ordered to all humanity. It does not mean that all persons are in fact saved.
- **Sacramental particularity** means Christ's communication of grace through instituted perceptible words and actions. In the Eucharistic case it includes the Church's confession of transubstantiation and Christ's true, real, and substantial presence. It does not mean magic, mechanical fruitfulness, divine confinement, or that every material event is a sacrament.
- **Flight into abstraction** is the article's editorial name for using conceptual or religious indeterminacy as a refuge from possible divine address. It is not a canonical or ecclesial classification of heresy.

## Included scope

The article includes:

1. the credal location of Christ's Passion under Pontius Pilate;
2. the difference between an abstract universal and universal cause;
3. the Thomistic claims that God is the universal and conserving cause, is not in a genus, and knows singulars;
4. analogical and apophatic limits on speech about God;
5. concrete providence joined to secondary causality and the permission of evil;
6. Abrahamic promise, Israel's priestly and prophetic mission, and election ordered to all nations;
7. the continuing Christian bond with the Jewish people and the irrevocability of God's gifts and call;
8. revelation through deeds and words culminating in the historical Incarnation;
9. the finite human acts of Christ as acts of the divine Person of the Word;
10. Christ's unique and universally offered salvific mediation, without an inference of universal salvation;
11. respect for truth and holiness in other religions;
12. the once-for-all Paschal mystery made sacramentally present through perceptible signs; and
13. the visible neighbor as a test preventing universal love from becoming an abstraction.

## Excluded scope

Excluded are a complete natural theology; proof of the historicity of the Resurrection; a treatise on revelation, inspiration, Christology, Israel and the Church, providence, theology of religions, or sacramental validity; a comparative study of Buddhism; a history of the reception of *Dominus Iesus*; and adjudication of individual salvation.

The article does not defend nominalism, nationalism, religious tribalism, or philosophical hostility to universals. It does not make “nirvana” a synonym for God, define Buddhist liberation, or imply that apophatic theology opposes the Incarnation. It does not treat Israel as a discarded vehicle, the Church as replacing an irrelevant people, sacramental rites as techniques, or every event as a direct divine intervention or punishment. It does not call its target a formal heresy.

## Authority classes and boundaries

- **Sacred Scripture:** governs the actual pattern of promise, election, Incarnation, universal mediation, sacramental sign, neighbor, and Christ's concluding question. Brief phrases are quoted; later metaphysical categories are not silently attributed to the biblical authors.
- **Ecumenical councils:** *Dei Verbum*, *Lumen gentium*, *Nostra aetate*, and *Gaudium et spes* govern revelation through history, the Church and Israel, other religions, the Incarnation, and universal salvific offer.
- **Catechetical synthesis:** the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* governs analogical language, stages of revelation, providence, Jesus's historical identity, Israel and other religions, and sacramental action.
- **Authoritative doctrinal declaration:** *Dominus Iesus* 6 and 13–15 are used for the exact claims that Christ's limited human acts have the divine Person as subject and that his one mediation is universal. The declaration is paired with the conciliar sources and is not made the article's general interreligious rhetoric.
- **Papal philosophical synthesis:** *Fides et ratio* 7 and 11–12 provide the temporal and historical form of revelation and the relation of universal truth to the Incarnation.
- **Thomistic theology:** ST I, q. 3, a. 5; q. 14, a. 11; q. 22, aa. 2–3; q. 44, a. 1; and q. 104, a. 1 provide the principal distinctions among universal causality, non-genus, knowledge of singulars, providence, secondary causes, and present conservation. Their use supports coherence but does not turn a Thomistic articulation into the revealed premise it serves.
- **Project synthesis:** the false contrast between universality and particularity, the “flight into abstraction,” the road-and-destination image, and the movement from Pilate to Matthew 16 are original editorial constructions.

## Material qualifications

1. God is not a particular alongside other particulars. The article predicates particularity of divine effects, historical address, Christ's assumed humanity, and sacramental means.
2. Divine knowledge of singulars follows the adopted classical-Thomistic account; the article does not survey every contemporary philosophy of divine knowledge.
3. Apophatic reserve remains valid: revelation is true without making the divine essence comprehensible.
4. Concrete providence includes genuine secondary causes and permission. No inference is made from suffering to personal guilt or special punishment.
5. Election begins somewhere because historical address must, but this observation alone does not identify which alleged revelation is true.
6. Election is ordered to mission and cannot support boasting. Israel's covenantal history remains integral to Christian identity; its present theological relation to the Church is not exhaustively resolved here.
7. “The Whole in the part” concerns the divine Person acting through an integral human nature; it neither mixes the natures nor makes the human nature infinite.
8. *Dominus Iesus* is authoritative and was ratified and confirmed by John Paul II with sure knowledge and apostolic authority. Its controversial interreligious reception is a reason for exact, bounded use and conciliar pairing, not for suppressing its directly relevant doctrinal claim.
9. Christ's universal mediation does not establish that every person is saved. The Spirit's universally offered possibility of association with the Paschal mystery does not make religious claims interchangeable.
10. Sacramental making-present does not repeat the historical event. Christ is principal actor; proper matter, form, intention, faith, and disposition retain their distinct roles. The Eucharistic paragraph states transubstantiation and the mode of Christ's presence without expanding into a complete Eucharistic treatise.
11. The statement that God is not bound by the sacraments does not make the sacraments optional for the Church or authorize presumption about extraordinary means.
12. Love of the concrete neighbor is a theological and moral test, not an identity of creature and Creator or a reduction of salvation to social action.
13. The concluding question is not a rhetorical demand that a reader claim a conviction not yet possessed.
