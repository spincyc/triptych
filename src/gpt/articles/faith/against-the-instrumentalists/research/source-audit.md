## Consequential distinctions and negative results

- The coined “instrumentalist” is not attributed to the established scientific instrumentalist school.
- Scientism is the nearest received category but is not treated as identical with the narrower device-centered habit analyzed here.
- Empiricism is not equated with laboratory measurement. The adopted Thomistic account itself begins natural knowledge in sense.
- Methodological naturalism is not treated as metaphysical naturalism. Properly delimited scientific method is praised.
- An instrument's access to an unobservable entity through effects is not denied. “Instrumental” is therefore not reduced to naked-eye observation.
- Measurement's dependence upon models does not make it arbitrary, subjective, or incapable of correcting theory.
- The self-application objection is not presented as sufficient against an exclusivist who offers a convention; the argument also examines the convention's reason and domain.
- Failure of the exclusive criterion does not prove God, an immaterial soul, a miracle, moral realism, or every claim lying beyond measurement.
- God is not placed in an empirical gap or proposed as a physical cause competing with secondary causes.
- The moral limit on experimentation is used to distinguish technical capacity from normative judgment, not to exempt moral claims from rational support or empirical facts.
- No appeal is made to quantum mechanics, unexplained consciousness, artificial intelligence, paranormal phenomena, or sensational scientific claims.
- “Heresy” is not used as a doctrinal or canonical classification.
