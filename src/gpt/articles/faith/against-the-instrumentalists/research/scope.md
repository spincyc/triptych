# Against the Instrumentalists — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; faith and theology articles.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive philosophical and theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Series relationship:** independently readable companion essay to *The Creature Before God*, keyed chiefly to the epistemic threshold of Part I. It is not a fourth movement in the trilogy.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. Online witnesses were checked through 2026-07-23.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader who respects empirical science but may consciously or tacitly treat physical detection as the exclusive test of reality or knowledge.
- **Governing question:** Does the success of physical instruments warrant the conclusion that only instrument-detectable realities are real or that only instrument-confirmable propositions are knowledge?
- **Thesis:** it does not. An indication becomes a measurement result only within rational practices of definition, calibration, modelling, inference, and judgment; the success of measurement therefore cannot establish measurement as the exhaustive criterion of truth or being.

## Adopted terms and argument

- **Instrument** means a physical device ordered to detect, compare, register, or measure selected features or interactions. The term is not extended to every symbolic or mental operation.
- **Indication** means the relevant state or output of an instrument; **measurement result** means a warranted claim about the object or quantity, including the model, unit, calibration, and uncertainty required by the case.
- **Instrumentalist** is used in the reader-facing title and prose in a deliberately coined sense. **Instrumental exclusivism** is the more exact project term for the conversion of an appropriate empirical method into an exclusive criterion of knowledge or reality.
- The coined target is not scientific instrumentalism, which concerns whether theories describe unobservables or chiefly systematize and predict observations. It is not ordinary empiricism, methodological naturalism, or metaphysical naturalism. Scientism, verificationism, and operationalism are related but separately identified.
- **Phenomenon to foundation** names the movement in *Fides et ratio* from functional or empirical aspects of a thing toward inquiry into its being and ultimate ground.
- The body moves from praise of instruments, through the rational conditions of measurement and the self-application problem of an exclusive criterion, to the Thomistic account of knowledge from sensible effects and the restoration of instruments to their proper finite work.

## Evidence and authority structure

1. **Authoritative Catholic teaching:** *Gaudium et spes* governs the proper autonomy of scientific methods and the warning against making observable data the rule of all truth. *Fides et ratio* governs the criticism of scientism and the need for metaphysical range. Benedict XVI's Regensburg address supplies a non-definitive papal diagnosis of reduced reason.
2. **Thomistic philosophy and theology:** ST I, q. 84, aa. 6–7 and q. 85, a. 1 govern the adopted sense–intellect account; ST I, q. 2, a. 2 and q. 12, a. 12 govern the bounded movement from sensible effects toward natural knowledge of God.
3. **Technical metrology and philosophy of science:** JCGM 200:2012, NIST, and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy distinguish indications, measurement results, calibration, instrumentalism, operationalism, and verificationism.
4. **Modern apologetic witness:** the Lewis–Anscombe controversy supplies the cause–ground pressure through the identified Foundation and Oxford records. The licensed revised text of *Miracles* was identified but not directly collated; no Lewis wording is reproduced, and the application to measurement is project synthesis.
5. **Project synthesis:** the coined target, application of the cause–ground distinction to measurement, architecture, examples, and final formulations are original synthesis.

## Included scope

Included are:

1. the legitimate strength and proper domain of instrument-mediated science;
2. the distinction between a device output and a warranted measurement claim;
3. the role of calibration, models, units, uncertainty, and inference;
4. the self-application problem of an exclusive empirical criterion;
5. the stronger appeal to scientific success and the ambiguity of “measurable in principle”;
6. plurality of object-appropriate rational warrants without relativism;
7. the Thomistic claim that human knowledge begins in sense without ending in physical registration; and
8. the distinction between empirical explanation and metaphysical inquiry into being and first cause.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded are a comprehensive history of philosophy of science; adjudication between realism and antirealism; a complete argument from reason; philosophy of mind or consciousness; foundations of mathematics; quantum-mechanical apologetics; arguments from gaps in scientific knowledge; historical proof of a miracle; and the actual demonstrations for God's existence.

The article does not infer that every unmeasurable assertion is meaningful, rational, or true. It does not deny that instruments provide evidence for unobservable entities, that empirical facts inform moral and historical judgment, or that natural explanations can be complete at their proper level. The self-application objection is not treated as the sole refutation, since an exclusivist may propose a convention rather than assert an empirically discovered fact. The paper clears space for metaphysical reasoning; it does not establish the conclusions of Part I by default.
