# How the Catholic Church Teaches — Scope

This record supports `src/gpt/articles/faith/catholic-doctrinal-authority/main.tex`.

- **Provider / genre:** GPT; discursive faith and theology article governed by `guidance/articles.md`.
- **Question:** What distinctions does the Catholic Church make among dogma, definitive doctrine, authentic non-definitive teaching, discipline, prudential judgment, and theological opinion; why do they exist; how did they develop; and what response belongs to each?
- **Reader:** serious Catholic lay readers, catechists, clerics, students, and ecumenical readers.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-26.
- **Thesis:** differentiated authority serves the one Revelation. Its natural goods are intelligibility, testimony, memory, ordered common action, conflict location, and protection of conscience; its supernatural ends are faithful transmission, unity in confession, correction of error, holiness, and communion with God.

The work distinguishes the object, mode, competent authority and intention, and required response. It does not call these simply “levels of dogma,” because dogma is one category. It distinguishes the hierarchy of truths from degrees of authority, infallibility from solemnity, and juridical obligation from irreformability.

The modern controlling map is the three-paragraph *Professio fidei*, its 1998 doctrinal commentary, *Ad tuendam fidem*, Vatican II, and the applicable codes. Earlier patristic, conciliar, scholastic, and manualist materials are historical provenance and may not be made to speak in the modern schema.

The article is Latin-Church-centered but identifies CCEO parallels and does not universalize Latin canonical details. It is not an exhaustive dogma catalog, canonical advice, an assessment of a living person's assent, or a promulgated system of theological notes.

Serious oppositions include Protestant Scripture/church-authority arguments, Orthodox primacy/reception objections, historicist accounts of formula, maximalist inflation, minimalist dismissal, and conscience claims. These are treated as families of arguments, not monolithic descriptions of communities.

Independent review in dogmatics, patristics, ecumenism, intellectual history, and canon law remains outstanding. No ecclesiastical approval is claimed.
