# The Supper, the Cross, and the Mass — Scope

## Identity and question

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; faith and theology articles.
- **Profile:** `guidance/articles.md` (faith and theology).
- **Reader:** Catholics and other serious readers evaluating a vivid catechetical claim.
- **Question:** Did the Last Supper mystically transport the apostles forward to Calvary while later Masses transport worshippers backward?
- **Answer:** No literal temporal transport is established. The defensible Catholic account is anticipatory sacramental presence at the Supper, once-for-all historical immolation at Calvary, and sacramental re-presentation of the same sacrifice in later Masses.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and date:** English; cross-edition Roman Catholic doctrinal study rather than a guide to one Missal; no civil or canonical jurisdiction controls the thesis; sources checked through 2026-07-27.

## Boundaries

The article treats Gospel chronology without forcing a solution to the
Synoptic–Johannine calendar question; Passover, Exodus, covenant, sacrifice,
and heavenly-worship controls; four edition-identified patristic witnesses;
Aquinas; Trent; Vatican II; the Catechism; modern papal synthesis; and bounded
Roman Eucharistic-prayer evidence. It distinguishes historical event,
sacramental sign and presence, liturgical memorial, representation,
participation, eschatological expectation, received doctrine, and theological
model.

It excludes a complete history of Eucharistic rites, a complete theology of sacrifice or transubstantiation, Eastern anaphoral comparison, speculative physics, and judgments about an individual teacher's orthodoxy. “Time travel” means literal relocation into another historical time. A poetic use may survive only after its limits are stated.
