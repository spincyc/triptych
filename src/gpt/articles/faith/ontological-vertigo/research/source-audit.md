# Source Audit — Ontological Vertigo

Online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-20. Public English translations are working study witnesses unless otherwise stated. Consequential claims are governed by the identified work and exact locus, not by a claim that an online translation or re-typeset edition is critical.

## Core claim-to-source matrix

| Consequential claim | Exact source locus | Authority and published use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plato supplies antecedent grammars of the Good's ontological priority and becoming's need for a cause, without teaching Christian creation from nothing. | Plato, *Republic* VI, 508e–509b; *Timaeus* 27d–29b. | Historical antecedent with an explicit limit against Christianizing the dialogues. |
| Aristotle analyzes change through potency and act and reaches an eternal unmoved actuality on which heaven and nature depend, without yet reaching Aquinas's universal free Creator. | Aristotle, *Metaphysics* XII.6–7, 1071b3–1072b30; cf. *Physics* VIII.5–6. | Historical antecedent; not used for creation *ex nihilo*. |
| Whatever is moved from potency to act is moved by what is actual; an essentially ordered series of derivative movers requires a first mover not moved by another. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 2, a. 3; cf. SCG I.13. | Governs the paper's explicit presentation of the First Way. It is not made to yield every later divine attribute by itself. |
| A first mover is not necessarily the first event in time; natural reason does not demonstrate a temporal beginning of the world. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 46, a. 2. | Blocks the first-domino and temporal-regress reading. |
| God alone is without beginning; created souls receive life and continue for as long as God wills. | Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* II.34.2–4. | Patristic witness to asymmetric created duration and participated life. |
| God creates from nothing through the Word, and the Word orders and preserves the created whole. | Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–5; *Against the Heathen* 40–42. | Patristic Creator–creature and preservation claims with the soteriological context retained. |
| Augustine reverses the spatial assumption—he exists in God rather than enclosing God—and joins divine inwardness to transcendence; his changeable mind judges mutable things by unchangeable truth. | Augustine, *Confessions* I.2.2; III.6.11; VII.1.1–2, 10.16–11.17, and 17.23. | Patristic introspective reception without pantheism; VII.17.23 governs the explicit mutability judgment. |
| The mind may receive a first flash of Truth, fail to sustain the gaze, and fall back into familiar images. | Augustine, *De Trinitate* VIII.2.3. | Best historical analogue for “ontological arrest”; explicitly not used as proof of conservation. |
| Before eternal Wisdom Augustine both shudders and burns; trembling need not be refusal. | Augustine, *Confessions* XI.9.11: *inhorresco et inardesco*; cf. VII.10.16. | Affective witness used to distinguish initial disproportion from chosen recoil. |
| From created order the mind may know that God is while remaining unable to comprehend the divine nature. | Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration* 28.3–6. | Patristic apophatic boundary: true causal knowledge without comprehension. |
| Beings receive existence from the superessential cause, and ascent passes through affirmation and negation without licensing arbitrary claims. | Pseudo-Dionysius, *Divine Names* V.1–8; *Mystical Theology* I.1–3. | Late-patristic causal and apophatic witness. |
| Love is ecstatic in directing the lover beyond self-enclosure toward the beloved and returning toward the Good. | Pseudo-Dionysius, *Divine Names* IV.13–14; cf. IV.9. | Supports self-forgetfulness as redirection, not annihilation or identity of substance. |
| In creatures, essence does not include unreceived existence; all beings other than God participate in being and are caused by the first being. | Aquinas, *De ente et essentia* 4; ST I, q. 3, a. 4; q. 4, a. 2; q. 44, a. 1. | Adopted Thomistic synthesis, distinguished from the First Way and from dogmatic definition. |
| Creation considered passively is a real relation in the creature to the Creator as principle of being. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 45, a. 3, especially ad 1. | Principal locus for the creature's real ontological dependence. |
| Creatures are really related to God; the corresponding relation predicated of God adds no creature-dependent accident or perfection. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 13, a. 7. | Principal locus for asymmetry; “relation of reason” is not construed as fictional action. |
| The universal cause conserves the act of being creatures do not possess through essence. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 104, a. 1 and ad 4; *De potentia*, q. 5, a. 1. | Governs present dependence and the bounded light-in-air analogy. |
| God grounds rather than eliminates proper secondary operations and moves voluntary causes according to their voluntary mode. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 83, a. 1, ad 3; q. 104, a. 2; q. 105, aa. 4–5; I–II, q. 10, a. 4; *De potentia*, q. 3, a. 7; SCG III.67–70. | Excludes occasionalism and coercion while preserving creaturely agency. |
| Humility restrains false elevation while magnanimity strengthens the appetite against retreat from genuinely great goods; the virtues agree in right reason. | Aquinas, ST II–II, q. 161, a. 1, especially ad 3; q. 129, a. 3, ad 4. | Governs the distinction between proper abasement, self-contempt, and refusal of received goods. |
| Devotion is readiness of will for divine service; adoration is inward reverence expressed in bodily humility, and prostration confesses that the creature is nothing from itself. | Aquinas, ST II–II, q. 82, a. 1; q. 84, aa. 1–2, especially a. 2 ad 2. | Thomistic grounding for consent and adoration. “Nothing from itself” is glossed as non-aseity, not worthlessness. |
| God wills creatures freely rather than from a need to complete divine goodness, and the divine will causes their being. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 19, aa. 1, 3–4; q. 44, a. 4. | Philosophical-theological bridge from non-need to free creation; no necessary emanation is inferred. |
| Love is properly and analogically in God; God loves all existing things, and divine love causes their goodness. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 13, a. 5; q. 20, aa. 1–2. | Governs the natural-theological claim that God wills created good without reducing love to passion. |
| Trinity cannot be demonstrated by natural reason. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 32, a. 1. | Governs the explicit boundary between metaphysical discovery and Johannine revelation. |
| Creation can be apprehended as little, made, loved, and kept; beholding the Maker yields reverent dread, meekness, and charity rather than creature-hatred. | Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love*, long text, chs. 5–6. | Mystical image and response, expressly not an independent proof. |
| God's sustaining substantial presence in every creature differs from transformative union by love and grace, which does not erase the natural distinction between creature and Creator. | John of the Cross, *Ascent of Mount Carmel* II.5.3–7. | Preserves universal ontological dependence, the distinct order of grace, and non-identity in union. |
| The soul answers the Word through voluntary conformity; creaturely love is whole but unequal and continually receives from its source. | Bernard of Clairvaux, *Sermons on the Song of Songs* 83.3–6, especially *etsi non ex aequo*. | Governs free response without reciprocity of ontological need and supplies the fountain contrast. |
| A genuinely humble person is not occupied with performing humility or measuring himself. | C. S. Lewis, *Mere Christianity* III.8, “The Great Sin.” | Narrow modern witness to self-forgetfulness. The popular “thinking of yourself less” sentence is explicitly rejected as a Lewis quotation. |
| Past existence does not by itself explain present existence; in the respect argued, preservation differs from creation only conceptually. | Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, Third Meditation. | Later restatement of present dependence; Descartes's larger method is not identified with Aquinas's. |
| God is really distinct from the world and freely creates without acquiring or increasing divine beatitude; the human person wholly depends on God as Creator and Lord. | Vatican I, *Dei Filius*, chs. 1 and 3 and canons 1.3–5. | Dogmatic Creator–creature boundary without canonizing every Thomistic premise. |
| God sustains creatures and grants them real causality; adoration acknowledges creaturely dependence and frees the worshiper from turning in on himself. | CCC 300–301, 306–308, 2095–2097. | Authoritative synthesis excluding deism, occasionalism, humiliation, and self-absorption. |
| “God is love” is Johannine revelation whose context is the sending of the Son, received love, charity, and fear cast out by perfect love. | 1 John 4:7–21, especially vv. 8–10, 16, 18. | Revealed culmination, not the last premise of a natural demonstration. |
| God's very being is love and his Trinitarian life is freely communicated in creation and salvation. | CCC 218–221, 257, 293–295. | Authoritative Trinitarian synthesis; “God is love” is not inverted into “love is God.” |

## Source witnesses and checking notes

### Ancient sources

- Plato, *Republic* VI, 508e–509b and *Timaeus* 27d–29b. Benjamin Jowett's public-domain working English was checked through the Internet Classics Archive: <https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.7.vi.html> and <https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/timaeus.html>.
- Aristotle, *Metaphysics* XII.6–7, with W. D. Ross's public-domain working English: <https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.12.xii.html>.

These texts establish antecedents, not a direct line of doctrinal identity.

### Patristic and apophatic sources

- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* II.34.2–4, Alexander Roberts and William Rambaut's ANF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103234.htm>.
- Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–5 and *Against the Heathen* 40–42, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm> and <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2801.htm>.
- Augustine, *Confessions* I.2.2, III.6.11, VII.1.1–2, 10.16–11.17, and 17.23, and XI.9.11, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110101.htm>, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110103.htm>, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110107.htm>, and <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110111.htm>. The explicit changeable-mind judgment is checked at VII.17.23; the Latin phrase *inhorresco et inardesco* is checked at XI.9.11.
- Augustine, *De Trinitate* VIII.2.3, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/130108.htm>. The first flash and relapse into familiar images are used analogically for arrest, not attributed to Augustine as a theory of ontological vertigo.
- Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration* 28.3–6, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310228.htm>.
- Pseudo-Dionysius, *Divine Names* IV.9, 13–14 and V.1–8 and *Mystical Theology* I.1–3, John Parker, trans., *The Works of Dionysius the Areopagite*, vol. 1 (1897), public domain: <https://ccel.org/ccel/dionysius/works/works.toc.html>.

Pseudo-Dionysius is identified according to modern convention. Dionysian ecstasy is bounded as redirection toward the beloved, not annihilation or loss of created substance.

### Thomistic corpus

The governing Latin metaphysical and humility loci were checked in the Leonine-text corpus exposed through Corpus Thomisticum: <https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html>. The New Advent *Summa* index (<https://www.newadvent.org/summa/>) served as a public English working aid, including for the short paraphrase of ST II–II, q. 84, a. 2 ad 2. Work, question, article, chapter, and reply govern over either site's wording.

Particularly controlling distinctions:

- ST I, q. 2, a. 3 and q. 46, a. 2: the First Way and the distinction between explanatory priority and a naturally demonstrable temporal beginning.
- ST I, q. 3, a. 4; q. 4, a. 2; q. 44, a. 1: subsistent and participated being.
- ST I, q. 13, a. 7 and q. 45, a. 3: Creator–creature relational asymmetry.
- ST I, q. 104, aa. 1–2; q. 105, aa. 4–5; I–II, q. 10, a. 4: conservation and noncompetitive, noncoercive secondary causality.
- ST II–II, q. 161, a. 1; q. 129, a. 3 ad 4: humility and magnanimity as compatible virtues of right measure.
- ST II–II, q. 82, a. 1; q. 84, aa. 1–2: ready devotion and interior–exterior adoration.
- ST I, q. 19, aa. 3–4; q. 20, aa. 1–2: free willing of creatures and divine love as the cause of created goodness.
- ST I, q. 32, a. 1: natural reason does not demonstrate Trinity.
- ST II–II, q. 19, a. 10: charity removes servility; fear of punishment may remain but diminishes, while filial fear increases.
- ST I–II, q. 79, a. 2: God causes actuality in the act, not the defect constituting sin.
- *De ente et essentia* 4; *De potentia*, q. 3, a. 7 and q. 5, a. 1; SCG II.15–21 and III.65–70: corroborating loci for received existence, creation, conservation, and secondary causality.

## Consequential negative results and rejected formulations

- “Ontological vertigo,” “ontological arrest,” and the response taxonomy were not found as received historical formulas. They are attributed only to this paper.
- The paper does not use “everything has a cause.” Its argument concerns actualized, received, or participated being.
- The First Way is not made to establish the entire doctrine of God in one inference. Later Thomistic arguments and revealed claims retain their distinct roles.
- A temporal regress is not made the ground of present dependence. ST I, q. 46, a. 2 expressly requires the distinction.
- Plato's Good, the *Timaeus* craftsman, and Aristotle's unmoved mover are not equated with the freely creating Triune God.
- Irenaeus, Athanasius, Augustine, and the mystics are not retroactively credited with Aquinas's technical essence–existence analysis or the project's response taxonomy.
- Conservation is not labeled repeated creation, and divine causality is not placed in a gap left by empirical explanation.
- Recoil is not equated with surprise, trembling, or filial fear. No affective stage is promised or treated as proof.
- “Nothing from oneself” is not rendered as “the creature is worthless.” Humility is paired with magnanimity to preserve received goodness and agency.
- Consent to creaturehood is not equated with supernatural faith, quietism, submission to abuse, or passive acceptance of evil.
- The familiar sentence “humility is not thinking less of yourself, but thinking of yourself less” is not quoted or attributed to C. S. Lewis. The concept in Book III, chapter 8 is paraphrased instead.
- “God is love” is identified at 1 John 4:8 and 4:16, not attributed to the Gospel of John. It is not inverted into “love is God.”
- Metaphysics is not said to demonstrate Trinity. The Johannine confession is presented as revelation that perfects rather than supplies a hidden philosophical premise.
- Poetic compression in the conclusion is not treated as evidence. It returns established claims to first-person apprehension.
- Pseudo-Dionysian ecstasy, John of the Cross's union, and Bernard's return of love are not construed as identity of essence or annihilation of created personhood.
- No modern copyrighted patristic or mystical translation, and no Lewis wording, is reproduced or closely paraphrased; the remaining Scriptural and official phrases are brief and source-identified.
