# Source Audit — Ontological Vertigo

Online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-20. Public English translations are working study witnesses unless otherwise stated. Consequential claims are governed by the identified work and exact locus, not by a claim that an online translation or re-typeset edition is critical.

## Core claim-to-source matrix

| Consequential claim | Exact source locus | Authority and published use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plato supplies antecedent grammars of the Good's ontological priority and of becoming's need for a cause, without teaching Christian creation from nothing. | Plato, *Republic* VI, 508e–509b; *Timaeus* 27d–29b. | Historical antecedent with an explicit limit against Christianizing the dialogues. |
| Aristotle analyzes change through potency and act and reaches an eternal unmoved actuality on which heaven and nature depend, without yet reaching Aquinas's universal free Creator. | Aristotle, *Metaphysics* XII.6–7, 1071b3–1072b30; cf. *Physics* VIII.5–6. | Historical antecedent; not used as a source for creation *ex nihilo*. |
| God alone is without beginning; created souls receive life and continue for as long as God wills. | Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* II.34.2–4. | Patristic witness to asymmetry of created duration and participated life. Irenaeus is not made to teach the later essence–existence distinction. |
| God creates from nothing through the Word, and the Word orders and preserves the created whole. | Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–5; *Against the Heathen* 40–42. | Patristic Creator–creature and preservation claims. Athanasius's language of corruption retains its soteriological setting. |
| Augustine asks how he could exist unless God were in him and immediately reverses the direction—rather, unless he were in God—then names divine inwardness and transcendence, rejects spatial divine extension, and apprehends mutable being under immutable Light. | Augustine, *Confessions* I.2.2; III.6.11; VII.1.1–2 and 10.16–11.17. | Patristic introspective reception without pantheism. |
| Before eternal Wisdom Augustine both shudders and burns, in dissimilitude and likeness. | Augustine, *Confessions* XI.9.11: *inhorresco et inardesco*. | Exact affective witness used to describe response, not to prove the metaphysics. |
| From created order the mind may know that God is while remaining unable to comprehend what the divine nature is. | Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration* 28.4–6. | Patristic apophatic boundary: true causal knowledge without comprehension. |
| Beings receive existence from the superessential cause, and theological ascent passes through affirmation and negation without licensing arbitrary claims. | Pseudo-Dionysius, *Divine Names* V.1–8; *Mystical Theology* I.1–3. | Late-patristic apophatic witness. “Darkness” names excess beyond finite comprehension, not a rejection of reason. |
| In creatures, essence does not include unreceived existence; all beings other than God participate in being and are caused by the first being. | Aquinas, *De ente et essentia* 4; ST I, q. 3, a. 4; q. 4, a. 2; q. 44, a. 1. | Adopted Thomistic metaphysical synthesis. It is labeled as such rather than attributed to dogmatic definition. |
| Creation considered passively is a real relation in the creature to the Creator as the principle of its being. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 45, a. 3, especially ad 1. | Principal Thomistic locus for the creature's real ontological dependence. |
| Creatures are really related to God; the corresponding relation predicated of God does not add a creature-dependent accident or perfection in God. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 13, a. 7. | Principal Thomistic locus for asymmetry. “Relation of reason” is not construed as fictional divine action. |
| The universal cause conserves the act of being that creatures do not possess through essence; conservation continues the giving of existence without motion or time. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 104, a. 1 and ad 4; *De potentia*, q. 5, a. 1. | Governs present dependence and the bounded light-in-air analogy. |
| God grounds rather than eliminates the proper operations of secondary causes. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 104, a. 2; q. 105, aa. 4–5; *De potentia*, q. 3, a. 7; SCG III.67–70. | Excludes deism and occasionalism while preserving creaturely agency. |
| God causes the actuality in a sinful act, not the defect by which the act is sinful. | Aquinas, ST I–II, q. 79, a. 2. | Narrow moral-evil guardrail for divine concurrence; the fuller moral analysis is excluded. |
| The temporal beginning of the world is not demonstrated by natural reason; an ontologically dependent creature need not be temporally recent. | Aquinas, ST I, q. 46, a. 2. | Blocks conflation of metaphysical dependence with a temporal regress argument. |
| Creation can be apprehended as little, made, loved, and kept by God. | Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love*, long text, ch. 5. | Mystical image expressly treated as apprehension, not demonstration. |
| God's sustaining substantial presence in every creature differs from transformative union by love and grace. | John of the Cross, *Ascent of Mount Carmel* II.5.3–4. | Mystical precision preserving both universal ontological dependence and the distinct order of grace. |
| Creaturely love answers divine love wholly but not equally and continues to receive from its source. | Bernard of Clairvaux, *Sermons on the Song of Songs* 83.4–6, especially *etsi non ex aequo*. | Monastic and mystical account of response without reciprocity of ontological need. |
| Past existence does not by itself explain present existence; in the respect argued, preservation differs from creation only conceptually. | Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, Third Meditation. | Later restatement of present dependence; Descartes's larger method is not identified with Aquinas's. |
| God is really and essentially distinct from the world and freely creates without acquiring or increasing divine beatitude. | Vatican I, *Dei Filius*, ch. 1 and canons 1.3–5 (DS 3001–3002, 3023–3025). | Dogmatic Creator–creature boundary; not a canonization of every Thomistic premise. |
| The human person wholly depends upon God as Creator and Lord. | Vatican I, *Dei Filius*, ch. 3 (DS 3008). | Conciliar statement of total dependence in the context of faith and obedience. |
| God transcends creation, is present to its inmost being, sustains creatures at every moment, and gives them the dignity of real secondary causality. | *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 300–301, 306–308. | Authoritative contemporary synthesis excluding deism, pantheism, and occasionalism. |

### Thomistic corpus

The New Advent *Summa* index (<https://www.newadvent.org/summa/>) served as a public English working aid. Work, question, article, chapter, and reply govern over either site's wording.

Particularly controlling distinctions:

- ST I, q. 3, a. 4 and q. 4, a. 2: God does not receive existence but is subsistent being itself.
- ST I, q. 13, a. 7: Creator–creature relational asymmetry.
- ST I, q. 44, a. 1: participated being and universal causality.
- ST I, q. 44, a. 1, ad 2: a creature may be necessary through another and still depend upon the cause of its necessity.
- ST I, q. 45, aa. 1–3, 5: creation and its real relation in the creature.
- ST I, q. 46, a. 2: no natural demonstration of a first temporal instant.
- ST I, q. 104, aa. 1–2: conservation and ordered created conserving causes.
- ST I, q. 105, aa. 4–5: divine action in real secondary agents.
- ST I–II, q. 79, a. 2: God causes the act's actuality, not the defect constituting sin.
- *De ente et essentia* 4; *De potentia*, q. 3, a. 7 and q. 5, a. 1; SCG II.15–21 and III.65–70: corroborating loci for received existence, creation, conservation, and secondary causality.

## Consequential negative results and rejected formulations

- “Ontological vertigo is the apprehension of asymmetric dependence” was not found as a received historical formula. It is attributed only to this paper.
- The paper does not use “everything has a cause.” Its argument concerns received or participated actuality, not a universal premise from which God is arbitrarily exempted.
- A temporal regress is not made the ground of present dependence. ST I, q. 46, a. 2 expressly requires the distinction.
- Plato's Good, the *Timaeus* craftsman, and Aristotle's unmoved mover are not equated with the freely creating God of Christian doctrine.
- Irenaeus and Athanasius are not retroactively credited with Aquinas's technical essence–existence analysis.
- “If God stopped thinking about you, you would cease to exist” is not quoted or attributed. The publication states Aquinas's conservation claim in controlled causal language.
- Conservation is not labeled *creatio continua* without qualification. Aquinas distinguishes creation's origin and newness from conservation while relating the conserving action to the continued giving of being.
- A “relation of reason” in God is not said to make God's knowing, willing, creating, preserving, or loving unreal.
- Pseudo-Dionysian darkness is not treated as irrationalism, doctrinal indifference, or permission for private projection.
- Julian's hazelnut image, Augustine's trembling and burning, and other mystical responses are not offered as demonstrations or reproducible psychological stages.
- John's substantial presence is not confused with supernatural union by charity, and mystical union is never called identity of essence.
- No saying attributed only through Raymond of Capua or another later reporter is used as a verbatim word of Catherine of Siena; Catherine is not needed for the final argument.
- No modern copyrighted translation is quoted or closely paraphrased. The previously considered E. Allison Peers Carmelite translation was excluded in favor of the 1906 Lewis–Zimmerman witness.
