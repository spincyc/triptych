# Source Audit — Ontological Vertigo

The core online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-20; the added Augustine, *Confessions* VII.20.26--21.27 and Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration* 28.2 loci were checked on 2026-07-21. The 2026-07-23 revision intensifies the present-tense apprehension of conservation, distinguishes divine presence from a remote mechanism, adds bounded pointers to the two companion essays, and moves exact loci and branch qualifications into twelve concise endnotes. These are project-authored developments of already audited claims and add no new consequential source premise. Public English translations are working study witnesses unless otherwise stated. Consequential claims are governed by the identified work and exact locus, not by a claim that an online translation or re-typeset edition is critical.

Ten endnotes route source loci, working-witness information, and narrow source-role qualifications out of the main rhetorical chain; two route readers to the separately complete companion essays. Every source named in those notes resolves to the matrix and published References below. The note layer does not supply a premise omitted from the body, and the Irenaeus quotation remains verbatim in the argument with its source immediately attached.

## Source witnesses and checking notes

### Ancient sources

- Plato, *Republic* VI, 508e–509b and *Timaeus* 27d–29b. Benjamin Jowett's public-domain working English was checked through the Internet Classics Archive: <https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.7.vi.html> and <https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/timaeus.html>.
- Aristotle, *Metaphysics* XII.6–7, with W. D. Ross's public-domain working English: <https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.12.xii.html>.

These texts establish antecedents, not a direct line of doctrinal identity.

### Patristic and apophatic sources

- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* II.34.2–4, Alexander Roberts and William Rambaut's ANF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103234.htm>.
- Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 3–5 and *Against the Heathen* 40–42, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm> and <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2801.htm>.
- Augustine, *Confessions* I.2.2, III.6.11, VII.1.1–2, 10.16–11.17, 17.23, and 20.26–21.27, and XI.9.11, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110101.htm>, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110103.htm>, <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110107.htm>, and <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110111.htm>. The explicit changeable-mind judgment is checked at VII.17.23; the seeing/way distinction at VII.20.26–21.27; and the Latin phrase *inhorresco et inardesco* at XI.9.11.
- Augustine, *De Trinitate* VIII.2.3, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/130108.htm>. The first flash and relapse into familiar images are used analogically for arrest, not attributed to Augustine as a theory of ontological vertigo.
- Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration* 28.2–6, public-domain NPNF working English: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310228.htm>.
- Pseudo-Dionysius, *Divine Names* V.1–8 and *Mystical Theology* I.1–3, John Parker, trans., *The Works of Dionysius the Areopagite*, vol. 1 (1897), public domain: <https://ccel.org/ccel/dionysius/works/works.toc.html>.

Pseudo-Dionysius is identified according to modern convention. Apophatic negation is bounded by prior causal affirmation and does not license arbitrary claims.

### Thomistic corpus

The governing Latin metaphysical and humility loci were checked in the Leonine-text corpus exposed through Corpus Thomisticum: <https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html>. The New Advent *Summa* index (<https://www.newadvent.org/summa/>) served as a public English working aid. Work, question, article, chapter, and reply take precedence over either site's wording.

Particularly controlling distinctions:

- ST I, q. 2, a. 3 and q. 46, a. 2, with *Summa contra Gentiles* I.13: the First Way and the distinction between explanatory priority and a naturally demonstrable temporal beginning.
- ST I, q. 3, a. 4; q. 4, a. 2; q. 44, a. 1: subsistent and participated being.
- ST I, q. 13, a. 7 and q. 45, a. 3: Creator–creature relational asymmetry.
- ST I, q. 104, aa. 1–2; q. 105, aa. 4–5; I–II, q. 10, a. 4: conservation and noncompetitive, noncoercive secondary causality.
- ST II–II, q. 161, a. 1; q. 129, a. 3 ad 4: humility and magnanimity as compatible virtues of right measure.
- ST I, q. 19, aa. 3–4; q. 20, aa. 1–2; q. 21, a. 1, especially ad 2–3, and a. 4 ad 4: free willing of creatures, divine love as the cause of created goodness, and justice without an antecedent creaturely claim.
- ST I, q. 32, a. 1: natural reason does not demonstrate Trinity.
- ST I–II, q. 79, a. 2: God causes actuality in the act, not the defect constituting sin.
- *De ente et essentia* 4; *De potentia*, q. 3, a. 7 and q. 5, a. 1; SCG II.15–21 and III.65–70: corroborating loci for received existence, creation, conservation, and secondary causality.

## Consequential negative results and rejected formulations

- “Ontological vertigo,” “ontological arrest,” and the response taxonomy were not found as received historical formulas. They are attributed only to this paper.
- The paper does not use “everything has a cause.” Its argument concerns actualized, received, or participated being.
- The First Way is not made to establish the entire doctrine of God in one inference. Later Thomistic arguments and revealed claims retain their distinct roles.
- A temporal regress is not made the ground of present dependence. ST I, q. 46, a. 2 expressly requires the distinction.
- Plato's Good, the *Timaeus* craftsman, and Aristotle's unmoved mover are not equated with the freely creating Triune God.
- Irenaeus, Athanasius, Augustine, and the mystics are not retroactively credited with Aquinas's technical essence–existence analysis or the project's response taxonomy.
- Conservation is not labeled repeated creation, and divine causality is not placed in a gap left by empirical explanation.
- Recoil is not equated with surprise or trembling. No affective stage is promised or treated as proof.
- Resolution is not equated with loss of asymmetry, a guaranteed feeling, or a judgment that continuing disturbance proves refusal. It names the progressive ordering of the creature's response in Parts II and III.
- “Nothing from oneself” is not rendered as “the creature is worthless.” Humility is paired with magnanimity to preserve received goodness and agency.
- Consent to creaturehood is not equated with supernatural faith, quietism, submission to abuse, or passive acceptance of evil.
- The familiar sentence “humility is not thinking less of yourself, but thinking of yourself less” is not quoted or attributed to C. S. Lewis. The concept in Book III, chapter 8 is paraphrased instead.
- The Part I conclusion “God loves” is an analogical judgment about God's free willing and causing of created good. It is not said to disclose the divine inner life or demonstrate Trinity.
- The potency-and-act opening and the section-level images carry argumentative pressure; they are not reports of a universal experience or substitutes for the source-governed distinctions that follow.
- Poetic compression in the conclusion is not treated as evidence. It returns established claims to first-person apprehension.
- No modern copyrighted patristic translation and no Lewis wording is reproduced or closely paraphrased; the remaining official phrases are brief and source-identified.
