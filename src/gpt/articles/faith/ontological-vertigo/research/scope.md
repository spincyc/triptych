- **Genre and profile:** discursive metaphysical and spiritual-theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. Online source witnesses were checked through 2026-07-20.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader capable of following basic metaphysical distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** What does it mean to apprehend one's existence as presently and asymmetrically dependent, how has that structure appeared across ancient, patristic, scholastic, mystical, and later Catholic thought, and what conclusions does it permit?
- **Thesis:** ontological vertigo is the apprehension of asymmetric dependence: the recognition that a creature is wholly real and genuinely active, yet neither originates nor sustains the act by which it exists, while the uncreated source depends upon it in no corresponding ontological way.
- **Intended literary effect:** a sober intellectual and existential recoil arising from exact metaphysical distinctions. No affective response is treated as proof, promised experience, private revelation, or sign of sanctity.

The governing sentence is this article's synthesis. It is not represented as a received technical definition or attributed to any historical witness.

## Terms fixed for this study

- **Ontological** concerns the creature's act of being, not merely biological, social, emotional, or functional need.
- **Dependence** means that the creature receives and is presently conserved in being by a cause that it is not.
- **Asymmetric** means that the creature depends upon the Creator in the order of being while the Creator receives no corresponding actuality, perfection, or constitution from the creature.
- **Apprehension** means an intellectual judgment reflexively grasped as true of oneself; it need not include an unusual sensation.
- **Vertigo** is a phenomenological metaphor for the possible recoil when the self is recognized as real but not self-grounding.
- **Noncompetitive causality** means that primary divine causality grounds rather than replaces the proper powers and acts of secondary causes.

## Included scope

The article includes:

1. selected antecedents in Plato's language of participation and ontological priority and Aristotle's account of potency, act, and unmoved actuality;
2. patristic accounts of created origin, continuance, mutability, preservation through the Word, and divine intimacy in Irenaeus, Athanasius, and Augustine;
3. Gregory Nazianzen and Pseudo-Dionysius on causal knowledge joined to non-comprehension;
4. Aquinas on essence and existence, participation, creation as a real relation in the creature, conservation, and primary and secondary causality;
5. the mystical reception of dependence in Julian of Norwich, John of the Cross, and Bernard of Clairvaux;
6. Descartes's later restatement of the present need for conservation; and
7. doctrinal boundaries supplied by Vatican I and the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*.

## Excluded scope

Excluded are a comprehensive history of metaphysics; a defense of every premise against every contemporary objection; physical cosmology and the Kalam argument; a full treatment of providence, evil, grace, deification, Trinitarian processions, or mystical prayer; and adjudication among Thomist, Scotist, Suárezian, Palamite, and contemporary analytic accounts of dependence.

The paper does not infer a temporal first moment from ontological dependence, reduce dependence to psychology, or treat divine causality as a substitute for empirical explanation. It does not compose a prayer or claim a private mystical experience.

## Authority classes and boundaries

- **Ancient philosophy:** supplies genuine conceptual antecedents. Plato and Aristotle are neither made implicit Christians nor credited with creation from nothing, universal free creation, or Thomistic conservation.
- **Sacred Scripture:** supplies the revealed grammar of creation through the Word, sustaining presence, and creaturely goodness. Short biblical phrases are not made to carry an independently scholastic demonstration.
- **Conciliar and catechetical teaching:** governs one free Creator really distinct from the world, the creature's total dependence, divine transcendence and intimacy, and genuine secondary causality. It does not canonize each adopted Thomistic explanation.
- **Patristic witness:** establishes the Christian field of uncreated Creator, creatures from nothing, continued existence by gift, the Word as Creator, and apophatic restraint. Later technical terms are not silently placed in earlier texts.
- **Thomistic theology:** supplies the paper's principal metaphysical articulation. The real distinction of essence and existence and the creature's one-sided real relation are identified as Thomistic school metaphysics where they are not themselves dogmatic formulas.
- **Mystical witness:** shows metaphysical dependence reflexively received in trembling, wonder, love, littleness, and union without absorption. Images illustrate apprehension; they do not demonstrate the thesis or guarantee an experience.
- **Project synthesis:** the definition of ontological vertigo, its phenomenological description, and the final formulations belong to this article.

## Material qualifications

1. The definition states the article's adopted Thomistic conclusion; it is not itself a proof. Brute-fact and existential-inertia objections remain live disagreements about causal explanation.
2. “First” is metaphysical, not necessarily chronological. Aquinas expressly denies that natural reason demonstrates a first temporal instant (ST I, q. 46, a. 2).
3. The argument is not “everything has a cause.” It concerns received, participated, or actualized being. A source that itself received existence would not be the explanatory terminus described.
4. Essence and existence are principles, not two physical objects or substances. Their real distinction in creatures and *ipsum esse subsistens* are adopted Thomistic claims, not definitions binding every Catholic school.
5. Creation from nothing is not change in pre-existing matter. Conservation is present causation of received being, not repeated temporal creation or a succession of decisions in God.
6. Participation in being does not make creatures fragments, modes, or material parts of God. Creator and creature remain really and essentially distinct.
7. Aquinas's real relation in the creature and relation of reason in God do not make divine knowing, willing, creating, preserving, or loving fictional. They deny a creature-caused accident or increase in God.
8. The relevant asymmetry is ontological. It does not deny covenantal relations, divine knowledge and will, or the reality of creaturely response.
9. Primary causality does not entail occasionalism. The whole effect may be from God and from a creature according to distinct causal orders, so created nature, agency, and responsibility remain real.
10. Divine concurrence does not make God the author of the defect constituting sin: God causes the act's actuality, not its defective mode (ST I–II, q. 79, a. 2). The fuller moral analysis lies outside this article.
11. Dependence in being is not identical with corruptibility or modal contingency. Even an incorruptible creature, or one necessary through another, possesses being from another (ST I, q. 44, a. 1, ad 2).
12. Mystical union by grace differs from the sustaining divine presence common to every creature; neither is identity of essence.
13. Pseudo-Dionysius is identified as an anonymous late-fifth- or early-sixth-century Christian author, not the convert of Acts 17.
