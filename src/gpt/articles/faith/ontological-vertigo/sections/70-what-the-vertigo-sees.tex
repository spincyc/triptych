\section{The Argument Finds the Reader}

Every creature receives and is conserved in being. Its nature and actions are genuinely its own. The first cause neither receives actuality from those actions nor acquires perfection by producing them. The dependence is present, total in the order of being, noncompetitive, and asymmetric.

But the knower has not stood outside that statement for a single moment. The act by which he follows the argument is itself among the received acts the argument describes. The conclusion is not merely before him. It includes him.

\begin{center}
\emph{I am.}\\[0.65em]
\emph{I am not from myself.}
\end{center}

Existence is not added to a self already there. There is no more original self behind received being to which the mind can retreat.

To say \emph{I am not from myself} is not to retract \emph{I am}. It is to understand it. Recoil would protect not the creature but the fiction that the creature first possessed itself and was afterward subjected to God. Proper abasement relinquishes that fiction. Consent does not create the dependence; it freely agrees to it. Humility makes that agreement stable. Magnanimity accepts the good given. Adoration turns the freed attention toward God.

Press the asymmetry further. God does not become more perfect by creating. The world fills no absence in him. I do not complete God. If the first source required the creature in order to possess his perfection, dependence would finally run both ways.

It does not.

Yet I am.

No creature possesses a prior claim to existence. Creaturely being is freely bestowed.

What is neither compelled by lack nor owed by justice is gift. For Aquinas, to love is to will the good of another; in God, that willing does not discover a goodness already there but causes it. Reason can therefore say more than \emph{I am caused}. It can say: \emph{I am because God wills that I be} (ST I, q.~20, aa.~1--2).

Divine non-need is therefore not divine indifference. The creature supplies no missing good, answers no deficiency, and completes no unfinished life in God. Nevertheless, God freely wills created good. The loss of imagined aseity need not end in recoil. The self that does not complete God is nevertheless willed by God.

Reason can now reach a precise conclusion, and a precise limit. \emph{God loves} and \emph{God is love} are not the same proposition. The first can be known analogically from God's free willing and causing of created good. The second names the inner life of God. It is not the conclusion of the First Way, nor of the argument from participated being.

Revelation does not repair a defective proof or supply an omitted premise. It discloses the giver whom reason has reached without comprehending. The Father sends the Son so that sinners may live. Love begins not in the creature's return but in God: ``he first loved us'' (1 John 4:9--10, 19).

The asymmetry remains entire. The creature's answer is received and unequal, yet it can be whole.

I give God nothing he lacks. The One whom nothing completes gives himself.

\begin{center}
\bfseries God is love.
\end{center}
