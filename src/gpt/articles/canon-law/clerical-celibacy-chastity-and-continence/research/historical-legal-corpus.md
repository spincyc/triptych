# Historical Legal Corpus

## Late antiquity and East–West differentiation

| Act | Rule / significance | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elvira can. 33 | Explicit Western higher-clerical marital continence. | Provincial, exact date/collection debated. |
| Siricius, Directa (385) | Authoritative Roman continence from ordination; applies to Spanish inquiry. | Records violation and claims apostolic basis; does not independently prove first-century rule. |
| African canons, c. 390 onward | Repeated bishops/presbyters/deacons continence, claimed apostolic/ancient. | Multiple enactments/compilation layers; numbering differs. |

| Trullo 6, 12–13, 30, 48 | No marriage after orders; marital life for priests/deacons with periodic service abstinence; bishops separate. | Rome’s reception nuanced; decisive Byzantine development. |

## Medieval Western consolidation

- Gregorian reform joined sexual discipline with reform of simony, ecclesial freedom, clerical identity, sacramental/ascetical purity, household succession, inheritance, and church property.
- Repeated legislation demonstrates both durable norms and enforcement failure.
- Lateran I (1123) can. 7 reinforced separation from wives/concubines and continence.
- Lateran II (1139) cann. 6–7 made attempted unions contrary to the higher-order discipline no marriage and required separation: decisive universal diriment-impediment consolidation.
- Lateran IV (1215) can. 14 strengthened continence but acknowledged clerics lawfully using marriage by regional custom.
- Property/inheritance is a real factor but cannot explain earlier law, ascetic exegesis, altar/prayer rationales, or Eastern/Western theological treatment alone.

## Reformation and Trent

- Protestant churches broadly normalized clerical marriage and attacked compulsory celibacy.
- Trent Session XXIV can. 9 defended the invalidity of marriage attempted by sacred-order clerics despite ecclesiastical law.
- Session XXIV can. 10 praised virginity/celibacy without making marriage evil.
- Session XXIII reform can. 18 created seminaries, materially changing formation/enforcement.
- Trent does not contradict Vatican II’s later explicit statement that celibacy is not intrinsic to priesthood’s nature.

## Eastern Catholic diaspora boundary

Union traditions often protected Eastern married clergy, but Latinization and migration produced restrictions. Ea semper (1907) and Cum data fuerit (1929) constrained married Eastern clergy in the United States and contributed to severe communal conflict and departures. Vatican II, the CCEO, and the 2014 decree reasserted the equal legitimacy and wider territorial exercise of Eastern discipline. This background is used only as contextual synthesis; a full diaspora legal history is outside the article’s scope.
