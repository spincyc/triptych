## Historiographic schools

- Apostolic-continuity: Christian Cochini, Stefan Heid, Roman Cholij, Alfons Stickler. Reads later explicit apostolicity claims and geographically dispersed witnesses as preservation of an oral rule; generally doubts Paphnutius and reads Trullo as divergence.
- Development/late-antique boundary: Roger Gryson, David G. Hunter, Daniel Callam. Stresses sparse pre-fourth-century direct evidence, ascetic and clerical-boundary development, and regional conflict.
- Methodological critique: Roger Balducelli. Warns that later apostolic claims and inferred universality cannot substitute for early public evidence.
- Broader ascetic context: Peter Brown. Explains renunciation within ancient Christian social and theological worlds without reducing it to hostility to marriage.

## Confidence conclusions

- High: married ministers; no explicit NT universal law; late-fourth-century authoritative Western continence; early/durable no-marriage-after-orders rule; Trullan divergence/settlement.
- Moderate: some earlier regional ministerial-continence practice, supported by Origen/Eusebius but limited by genre/transmission.
- Unresolved by historical method: one universally binding apostolic oral law of perfect clerical continence.
