# Taxonomy Matrix — Natural, Positive, Divine, and Human Law

## Governing warning

The two pairs ask different questions and do not yield four equal species.

- **Divine/human** principally concerns the proximate source or author of the norm; every just human authority's ultimate dependence on God does not erase human secondary causality.
- **Natural/positive** principally concerns rational ground versus authoritative positing or historical promulgation.
- **Natural law** is divine in source, naturally knowable, and participated by human reason.
- **Divine positive law** is revealed and historically given by God.
- **Human positive law** includes civil and ecclesiastical enactment and legally received custom.
- **“Human natural law”** names human knowledge, formulation, recognition, or application; it is not an independently authored norm.

## Core cells

| Cell | Ultimate source | Mode of access or promulgation | Typical content | Ordinary subjects | Mutability or dispensation | Principal failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Divine natural | God as Creator; eternal law participated by rational creatures | Practical reason, conscience, reflection on human goods and ends | First practical principles, basic duties, dignity and common-good norms | Human persons as rational creatures | Human formulation/application changes; underlying norm is not repealed or dispensed by humans | Treating intuition, biology, custom, or ideology as self-evident “nature” |
| Divine positive | God freely revealing and commanding in salvation history | Revelation received in Scripture and Tradition, authoritatively taught | Old and New Law; revealed covenant; divinely instituted elements | Depends on covenant and norm; New Law ordered to all in Christ | No human repeal or dispensation; salvation-historical fulfillment must be distinguished | Treating every earlier juridical form as perpetually binding or revelation as arbitrary will |
| “Human natural” | No independent source | Human discovery, reasoning, adjudication, or recognition | A sound natural-law conclusion or positive recognition of an inherent right | Depends on the human formulation’s forum | The formulation and remedy can change; the underlying truth does not depend on the human act | Making the knower author of the known norm |
| Human positive—civil | Competent temporal authority or legally capable community | Constitution, statute, regulation, judgment, custom, agreement, public promulgation | Public order, rights, procedures, contracts, remedies, crimes, administration | Persons and entities within the civil jurisdiction | Mutable under constitutional competence; morally bounded by natural/divine law | Equating validity, enforceability, and justice |
| Human positive—ecclesiastical | Competent Church authority or legally capable ecclesial community | Canon, decree, custom, statute, administrative act, judgment, promulgation | Discipline, office, sacraments’ juridic ordering, procedure, temporal goods, penalties | Defined Catholic subjects, offices, persons, territories, or communities | Mutable or dispensable only within competence; always bounded by divine/higher law | “Merely ecclesiastical” falsely treated as optional or every canon treated as divine |

## Related but nonidentical categories

| Term | Proper use | Not equivalent to | Source control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eternal law | Divine wisdom ordering all creation | A fifth enacted code | Aquinas, ST I–II q. 93; CCC 1950–1953 |
| Revealed law | Old and New Law disclosed by God | Every proposition in a canon code | Aquinas q. 91; CCC 1961–1974 |
| *Ius naturale* | Context-dependent natural right/order; in Catholic moral use, rational participation | Ulpian’s animal-wide category in every respect | Digest 1.1; Gratian D.1; Rufinus; Aquinas q. 94 |
| *Ius gentium* | Context-dependent law/common practice of peoples | Modern public international law or natural law simpliciter | Gaius 1.1; Digest 1.1; Justinian Institutes 1.2; later scholastics |
| Written/unwritten law | Form of expression | Natural/positive or divine/human source | Aristotle, Roman sources, custom canons |
| Custom | Unwritten positive norm introduced by a capable community and approved under law | Mere frequency or natural law | CIC cc. 23–28; CCEO cc. 1506–1509 |
| Ecclesiastical law | Law within the Church’s juridical order | Divine law merely because Church text contains it | CIC/CCEO proposition-by-proposition audit |
| Canonized civil law | Civil norm given canonical effects by canonical reference | Canonization of saints; automatic merger of orders | CIC cc. 22, 1290; CCEO cc. 1034, 1504 |
| Legal positivism | Modern family of theories about social sources and law/morality | The existence or use of positive law | Hart, Kelsen, Austin, and successors require theory-specific treatment |
| Equity | Law’s reason applied where general wording fails a case | Private suspension or favoritism | Aristotle, Aquinas, CIC c. 19/CCEO c. 1501 contexts |
| Dispensation | Relaxation of merely ecclesiastical law in a particular case by competent authority | Repeal, interpretation, or dispensation from divine law | CIC cc. 85–93; CCEO cc. 1536–1539 |
| Moral non-obligation | Lack of binding force in conscience | Automatic juridical nullity or immunity from consequence | Aquinas q. 96 a. 4; *Evangelium vitae* 72–74 |

## Classification test

1. State the person's proposed act, material facts, roles, and affected third parties.
2. Isolate each exact proposition or juridical act on its own row.
3. Identify source, author, genre, jurisdiction, authoritative text, and date.
4. State whether access or manifestation is rational, revealed, promulgated, notified, customary, adjudicated, contractual, or received.
5. Verify subjects, factual trigger, rank, exception, and the competent interpreter, legislator, dispenser, adjudicator, and remedy.
6. Separate moral obligation, validity and status, liceity, enforceability, culpability, penalty, evidence, remedy, and restitution.
7. Check higher, special, later, particular, and proper law within the relevant order, then identify any treaty, reception, or recognition rule joining an effect to another order.

## Three nonidentical hierarchies

| Relation | Meaning | Practical control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moral priority | Natural and revealed moral norms measure the justice of every human act and enactment | A morally impermissible choice cannot become good through official permission |
| Normative rank within an order | A constitution, code, universal or common law, particular law, administrative act, judgment, and contract occupy positions assigned by that legal order | Apply that order's competence and conflict rules; moral criticism alone does not supply external invalidity |
| Allocation among orders | Civil and ecclesiastical authorities can govern different aspects of the same person or event | Neither is a universal appellate forum for the other; each gives effects within competence unless treaty, reception, or recognition law connects them |

## Confidence statement

The asymmetric map is high-confidence synthesis from Aquinas ST I–II qq. 90–97, CCC 1950–1974, and current divine-law boundary canons. The exact scope of individual natural-law conclusions, identity of some norms as divine positive law, and historical use of *ius gentium* remain proposition-specific and sometimes disputed.
