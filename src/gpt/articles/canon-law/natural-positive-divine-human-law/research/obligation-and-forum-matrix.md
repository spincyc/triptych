# Person–Obligation–Forum Matrix — Concurrent Orders of Law

This is an abstract audit method, current through 2026-07-16. It is not civil or canonical advice and does not determine the rights, status, culpability, or obligations of a named person.

## Governing proposition

Natural and revealed norms measure the morality of a person's act. Civil and ecclesiastical orders give juridical effects within their own competence. Status, office, contract, vow, family, and fiduciary relations can add duties. These norms can govern one event without forming one human appellate hierarchy. Conscience judges the concrete moral choice; it does not enact, dispense, or adjudicate.

## Norm ledger

Create one row for each claimed norm. Never merge a moral principle, canon, statute, singular act, contract, and office instruction into one paraphrase.

| Exact norm | Authoritative text and date | Proximate author and competence | Order and jurisdiction | Subjects and factual trigger | Required act or omission | Rank or conflict rule | Claimed effect | Exception, dispensation, or excuse | Interpreter, forum, remedy, and deadline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Natural moral norm | Reasoned proposition and any authoritative moral teaching used | Not humanly enacted; known by practical reason | Universal moral order | Persons and relations defined by the act or good | Concrete good to pursue or evil to avoid | Measures every human act and enactment | Objective moral duty or right | No human dispensation from the norm; culpability remains person-specific | Well-formed conscience assisted by sound moral and pastoral counsel; no automatic external remedy |
| Revealed divine norm | Scripture and Tradition as authoritatively received | God revealing; ecclesiastical authority teaches and applies within competence | Depends on covenant, baptism, sacrament, or office | Exact addressees and conditions in the proposition | Revealed duty, institution, or boundary | Human authority cannot repeal or dispense divine law | Moral, doctrinal, sacramental, or constitutional effect | Fulfillment and application must be distinguished from human dispensation | Competent teaching and juridical authority; forum depends on effect sought |
| Ecclesiastical general norm | Official promulgated Latin or controlling particular text | Competent legislator | Latin, Eastern common, particular, special, or proper law | Canonical subjects, territory, status, time, and factual trigger | Conduct or juridical condition specified | Higher, special, later, and more-particular rules under the applicable code | Validity, liceity, duty, right, penalty, presumption, or remedy | Exception, privilege, dispensation, doubt, ignorance, or excuse only as law provides | Legislator or authentic interpreter; executive or tribunal; verify recourse and appeal periods |
| Ecclesiastical singular act | Authenticated decree, precept, rescript, privilege, or dispensation and notification | Competent executive authority | Jurisdiction stated by the act and enabling law | Determined person or case and established facts | Concrete command, decision, or favor | Must conform to law and competence | Case-specific external or internal effect | Revocation, cessation, correction, recourse, or appeal as applicable | Author, hierarchical superior, tribunal, or internal forum; verify notification and suspensive rules |
| Civil positive norm | Official current constitution, statute, regulation, judgment, or order | Competent civil authority | Identified state, territory, person, property, or transaction | Civil subjects and factual predicates | Civil command, prohibition, permission, status, or procedure | Governing constitutional, statutory, conflicts, later/special-law rules | Civil validity, liability, penalty, evidence, enforcement, or remedy | Exemption, defense, accommodation, appeal, or review under civil law | Competent agency or court; local counsel and deadlines required |
| Role-based or assumed norm | Contract, bylaws, office terms, vow, professional rule, fiduciary instrument, family relation | Parties or authority capable of creating the relation | Civil, canonical, or both, as the instrument provides | Person validly holding the role or relation | Performance, loyalty, care, confidentiality, reporting, or support | Higher law, public policy, statutes, canons, and the instrument's conflict rules | Contractual, disciplinary, fiduciary, moral, or status effect | Release, modification, delegation, impossibility, or excuse under governing law | Counterparty, superior, board, arbitrator, tribunal, or court |

## Effects ledger

| Effect | Required conclusion | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moral obligation | Whether this person's concrete choice is required, permitted, or forbidden | Permission or enforceability in one order is moral approval |
| Validity or status | Whether the juridical or sacramental act produced the claimed effect | Good intention or reduced culpability cures invalidity |
| Liceity or discipline | Whether a valid or invalid act complied with the governing norm | Every illicit act is invalid or penal |
| External enforceability | Which authority can command performance or give an enforceable remedy | Moral non-obligation automatically removes external consequences |
| Moral culpability | Knowledge, freedom, intention, duress, error, and circumstances affecting personal fault | Civil guilt or canonical imputability uses identical elements |
| Canonical penal attribution | External violation, grave imputability, presumptions, excuses, mitigation, jurisdiction, and current penal norm | Moral gravity alone creates a delict or penalty |
| Civil penal or regulatory liability | Elements, burden, defenses, procedure, and jurisdiction under applicable civil law | Canonical disposition or pastoral judgment decides civil guilt |
| Evidentiary effect | Admissibility, burden, presumption, credibility, and weight in the particular forum | A fact found under one procedure is automatically conclusive everywhere |
| Remedy and suspension | Authority, relief, deadline, exhaustion, and whether filing suspends execution | A correct substantive claim is self-executing or recourse is generally suspensive |
| Restitution and third-party protection | Harm attributable to the person, continuing care, notice, safety, return, or repair | A justified refusal cancels every residual duty |

## Authority and forum

| Authority or forum | Can do | Cannot do merely by acting in that capacity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conscience | Judge what concrete act the person ought to choose after responsible formation | Enact a law, dispense, determine another party's rights, or create civil/canonical immunity |
| Pastoral, theological, or legal adviser | Supply reasons, sources, risk assessment, and counsel | Bind a tribunal or alter external status without delegated authority |
| Sacramental internal forum | Exercise faculties and give remedies assigned by sacramental and penal law | Disclose the seal, decide a civil case, or alter external status except as law provides |
| Nonsacramental internal forum | Give a favor or resolution assigned by law to that forum | Operate as a general appeal from external governance |
| Canonical executive authority | Issue or review administrative acts and dispense within competence | Dispense divine law, legislate without power, or decide a judicial cause reserved to a tribunal |
| Canonical tribunal | Decide causes within jurisdiction through the assigned process | Enact universal law or decide civil liability outside recognition rules |
| Legislator or authentic interpreter | Enact, amend, repeal, or authentically interpret within competence | Establish disputed individual facts without a lawful fact-finding process |
| Civil agency or court | Determine civil effects and remedies within jurisdiction | Determine sacramental validity or canonical status merely from civil power |
| Treaty, reception, or recognition mechanism | Coordinate specified effects between orders | Merge the orders or supply effects outside the text and implementing law |

## Conflict sequence

1. Establish the proposed act, material facts, capacities, and affected persons.
2. Map canonical, civil, family, office, contractual, and other status gates.
3. List each norm separately and verify the authoritative text and currentness.
4. Test competence, promulgation or notification, scope, rank, factual trigger, and exception.
5. Separate every moral, validative, disciplinary, penal, evidentiary, and remedial effect.
6. Interpret and harmonize concurrent duties where text, sequencing, delegation, or accommodation permits.
7. Identify any true moral incompatibility and analyze the person's act, cooperation, alternatives, duress, and third-party effects.
8. Seek relief from the authority competent to give it and verify deadlines and suspension.
9. Refuse intrinsically evil or otherwise morally impermissible action; perform all compatible duties and protect affected persons.
10. Preserve a reviewable decision record and reopen it when facts, law, or available relief change.

## Controlling anchors and cautions

- CIC: cc. 7–22, 35–112, 129–144, 208–223, 1321–1325, and 1732–1752.
- CCEO: cc. 7–38, 909–930, 979–1006, 1414–1416, and 1488–1539.
- Internal/external forum: CIC c. 130; CCEO c. 980; *Praedicate Evangelium* arts. 190–193.
- Conscience and civil authority: CCC 1776–1802 and 2238–2243.
- Cooperation and resistance in the specific life-law context: *Evangelium vitae* 73–74.
- Filing hierarchical recourse is not generally suspensive; CIC c. 1736 and CCEO c. 1000 must be read with any applicable special law.
- The cross-order framework is project synthesis grounded in these sources. It is not quoted from a single authority and supplies no universal choice-of-law rule.
