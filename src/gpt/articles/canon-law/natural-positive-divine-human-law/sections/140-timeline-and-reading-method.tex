\subsection{The source-first reading sequence}

For any claim about law, proceed in this order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Quote or isolate the proposition.} Do not classify an entire document when only one clause is disputed.
\item \textbf{Identify genre and author.} Scripture, philosophical dialogue, juristic excerpt, conciliar canon, papal teaching, statute, rescript, judgment, and commentary do not establish the same thing.
\item \textbf{Establish text and translation.} Use the controlling language, disclose fragmentary transmission, and distinguish convenient translations from authoritative texts.
\item \textbf{Locate jurisdiction and date.} Universal, common, particular, proper, territorial, personal, current, historical, superseded, or merely proposed?
\item \textbf{Classify source and mode.} Natural rational ground, divine revelation, human enactment, custom, reception, agreement, judgment, or a mixture?
\item \textbf{Determine rank and competence.} Who may legislate, interpret, dispense, execute, or adjudicate, and what approval or promulgation is required?
\item \textbf{Separate effects.} Moral obligation, canonical or civil validity, liceity, enforceability, penalty, remedy, and evidentiary effect.
\item \textbf{Check development and conflict.} Later amendments, authentic interpretations, higher law, parallel civil order, doctrinal development, and changed facts.
\item \textbf{State confidence and limits.} Distinguish verified conclusion, reasonable synthesis, disputed interpretation, and unverified lead.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{A compact notation for research records}

A research note can record each proposition as:

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.90\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{Claim} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{source/author} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{genre} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{controlling text/translation} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{jurisdiction and date} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{natural/revealed/positive classification} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{competent interpreter/forum} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{effect} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{currentness and confidence}.}}
\end{center}

This notation prevents the most common drift: a historical source becomes current law, a teaching becomes a penalty, a local judgment becomes universal legislation, or a moral conclusion becomes an unproved civil remedy.
