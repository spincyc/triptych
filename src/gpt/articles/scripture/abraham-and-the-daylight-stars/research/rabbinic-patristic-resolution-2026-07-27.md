# Abraham and the Daylight Stars — rabbinic and patristic resolution

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Scope:** source-control disposition for the held GPT edition
**Review character:** repository evidence review, not credentialed rabbinics
or patristics review

## Disposition

The exact Abarbanel and Augustine bindings may remain. The other named Jewish
interpreters are edition-identified leads, not publication-verified witnesses,
until an exact artifact or passage is acquired, inspected, fingerprinted, and
bound locally. The negative patristic claim must be narrowed to Augustine
rather than presented as a result about the Fathers or early Christianity.

This disposition preserves useful research without promoting catalog records
or an unrecorded search to evidence.

## Patristic boundary

The registered Augustine passages at *City of God* XVI.23–24 support only the
following positive findings:

- Augustine treats the stars as practically innumerable;
- he calls the Genesis 15 episode a vision;
- he reads the sunset, darkness, and fire allegorically; and
- he does not formulate the exact daylight dilemma in those inspected loci.

No artifact-defined patristic corpus or search receipt establishes a broader
absence. Philo and Josephus are Jewish witnesses, not Fathers; Cornelius a
Lapide and Haydock are later commentators. Jerome's *Hebrew Questions*,
Ambrose's *De Abraham*, Chrysostom's Genesis homilies, Theodoret, Ephrem, and
other Christian witnesses remain uninspected for this publication.

Accordingly, reader-facing prose should say that the exact dilemma was not
found in the inspected Augustine loci. It should not retain these broader
claims:

- “Why So Few Fathers Ask”;
- “the Greek textual form available to many Fathers”;
- “the narrative hour was not a major Christian controversy”; or
- any tradition-wide or author-wide silence.

A bounded search may replace this narrowing only after its exact corpus
members, hashes, queries or reading method, inspected contexts, date, and
limitations are recorded.
