# Hebrew edition verification — 2026-07-27

## Witness and method

Genesis 15:5, 12, and 17–18 were inspected directly in Rudolf Kittel and
Georg Beer's *Biblia Hebraica*, volume 1 (Leipzig: J. C. Hinrichs, 1909),
printed pages 18–19. Exact Internet Archive BookReader deliveries of both
public-domain scan pages are now retained and registered in the provider-neutral
source library. The printed Hebrew, verse numbers, adjacent context, and page
apparatus were visible at full size.

This first-edition *Biblia Hebraica* follows the ben Hayyim/Bomberg textual
base; it is not BHS, BHQ, or a direct facsimile of Codex Leningradensis. The
verification therefore closes the missing identified-print-witness gap but is
not a collation of the Masoretic tradition.

## Exact grammatical findings

- Genesis 15:5 advances the represented action with wayyiqtol forms:
  *wayyotse*, *wayyomer*, and a second *wayyomer*. That sequence supports
  narrated progression but does not measure elapsed clock time.
- Genesis 15:12 prints *wayhi hashemesh labo*. *Labo* is the Qal infinitive
  construct with prefixed *lamed*. “Infinitival expression” is accurate but can
  be made more precise as “infinitive construct”; the clause places the sun in
  the approach to setting.
- Genesis 15:17 prints *wayhi hashemesh ba'ah*. *Ba'ah* is a feminine singular
  Qal active participle agreeing with *hashemesh*. It is **not** a completed
  verbal form. The article's present statement that verse 17 uses a “completed
  form” must be corrected before release.
- The rest of Genesis 15:17 reports that thick darkness was present, and verse
  18 begins “on that day.” These clauses sustain the narrative's movement into
  darkness and same-day formula, but the participle alone does not prove that
  sunset was already complete.

## Versional reconciliation

- The Clementine Vulgate remains an independently verified Latin witness. Its
  *iam occubuisset* at verse 17 expresses completed sunset more strongly than
  the Hebrew participle. It should not be projected backward as the morphology
  of the Hebrew.
- Swete's printed Greek transcription remains inspected but uncollated against
  its page image. Its narrower claims should continue to be identified as
  Swete's printed text rather than “the Septuagint” without qualification.
- The dated peshitta.eu Syriac witness preserves the outside/look/count command,
  the sun's movement toward setting at verses 12 and 17, and “on that day” at
  verse 18, without an explicit interval. Because the site's textual base is
  unidentified, it is comparison evidence below an edition-critical Peshitta
  claim.
- Jubilees 14 is reception through rewritten Scripture, not a version of
  Genesis. Charles's 1913 translation has Abram look at the stars, relocates
  and expands the covenant scene, and later reports completed sunset; it does
  not identify the hour of the star sight. It should be discussed separately
  from Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and Syriac textual controls.

## Editorial consequence

The conditional thesis survives: in the presented order, the star command
precedes the chapter's explicit movement to sunset and darkness, while neither
the verbal sequence nor the ancient versions rule out represented vision or an
unmarked interval. The Hebrew section must replace “completed form” with the
participial analysis above. The evidence map, source audit, and scope should
also stop listing the Hebrew print witness, Peshitta, and Jubilees as wholly
unresolved. This verification is an internal source audit, not the qualified
Hebraist or Septuagint-specialist review still required by the hold.
