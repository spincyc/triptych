## Findings

### Hebrew

The article distinguishes narrated sequence from elapsed time and expressly
denies that wayyiqtol forms measure hours. That is the correct kind of
qualification. Its claims about a chain of wayyiqtol forms and the forms at
Genesis 15:12 and 15:17 nevertheless depend on Hebrew morphology. No Masoretic
Hebrew artifact or focused passage is bound in `source-bindings.toml`, and the
evidence map itself says that a printed critical Hebrew witness remains
uncollated.

The daylight result may therefore remain a conditional observation about the
presented order of verses 5, 12, and 17–18, but the publication may not claim
the Hebrew verbal analysis as source-closed until an identified Hebrew edition
and exact loci are inspected and bound. A specialist should also check whether
the prose description “infinitival expression” at verse 12 and “completed
form” at verse 17 is the most precise account of the respective constructions.

### Greek

The checked Swete transcription supports the bounded observation that its
Genesis 15:17 does not reproduce the Hebrew darkness phrase and that verse 18
has a spatial expression rather than “on that day.” The article properly
limits this to one identified printed Greek text and uses Augustine's Old
Latin as contrary evidence against generalizing across Greek transmission.

The binding is only `acquired` and `inspected`; its own context and the source
audit say that the page image remains uncollated. The evidence map incorrectly
calls the exact GPT binding outstanding even though the transcription is now
bound. Replace that stale statement with the actual remaining gate: collation
of the checked transcription against the identified Swete page image, followed
by specialist review of the narrow textual inference. Until then, retain
“Swete's printed text” and do not broaden the claim to “the Septuagint.”

### Latin

The Clementine comparison is supported by a verified Hetzenauer page image and
checked transcription. The article correctly treats the Douay–Rheims as an
English witness to the Vulgate line rather than as an independent ancient
authority. Augustine's exact Latin provides a useful versional control, but it
proves an Old Latin reading used by Augustine, not the wording of every Greek
exemplar or Latin tradition.

No correction is required to the article's bounded Latin conclusion. The
references should continue identifying the Clementine, Douay–Rheims, and
Augustinian witnesses separately.

### Syriac and rewritten Scripture

The bindings now include a focused Syriac Peshitta web passage and Jubilees
14:1–20. The source audit still lists both the Peshitta and Jubilees as
unresolved, while the article does not use either witness. This is a record
state contradiction.

Either incorporate a narrowly qualified comparison and update the evidence
map, audit, scope, and references, or remove these unused publication bindings.
If retained, the Syriac evidence must remain explicitly below an
edition-critical claim because the registered web witness has an unidentified
textual base. Jubilees is a rewritten-scripture reception witness, not a direct
version of Genesis, and must not be grouped with Hebrew, Greek, Latin, or
Syriac textual controls.
