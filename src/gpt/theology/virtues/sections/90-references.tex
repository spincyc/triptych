\subsection{Aristotle}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline,itemsep=0.45em]
  \item[\emph{Nicomachean Ethics}.]
  Greek loci and the public-domain W.~D. Ross translation (1925), consulted through the Perseus \href{https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0053}{Greek} and \href{https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054}{English} editions, the \href{https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics_(Ross)}{Wikisource transcription}, and the \href{https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.html}{Internet Classics Archive}. Governing loci: II.1--9 (habit, definition, mean, map, shame and indignation); III.6--12 (courage and temperance); IV.1--9 (liberality, magnificence, magnanimity, ordinary honor, gentleness, affability, truthfulness, eutrapelia, shame); V.1--10 (general, distributive, commutative justice, equity); VI.3--13 (five intellectual virtues, euboulia, synesis, gnome, connection, and natural virtue's incompleteness without prudence at 1144b1--17); VII.1--10 (heroic virtue, continence, incontinence); VIII--IX (friendship).

  \item[\emph{Eudemian Ethics}.]
  III.7, 1234a24--34, for the uncertain status of shame and righteous indignation, and VIII.3, 1248b8--1249a17, for \emph{kalokagathia} as an integrative capstone, using Joseph Solomon's public-domain translation in W.~D. Ross's 1925 edition, consulted through the \href{https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Eudemian_Ethics}{Wikisource transcription}. Both uses are qualified rather than allowed to enlarge the strict virtue count.

  \item[\emph{Categories}.]
  Chapter 8 for durable habit and less stable disposition, using E.~M. Edghill's public-domain translation through the \href{https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/categories.1.1.html}{Internet Classics Archive}.
\end{description}

The Ross and Solomon renderings guide English terminology but do not settle every Greek ambiguity. In \emph{Nicomachean Ethics} IV.4 Aristotle leaves the ordinary-honor mean unnamed and observes that ambition or love of honor and unambitiousness can each attract praise or blame according to how the desire is measured. For the appendix's Aristotelian flank display, \emph{aphilotimia} and \emph{philotimia} mark deficient and excessive desire in that context. Aquinas, by contrast, calls the virtue itself \emph{philotimia} in ST I--II, q.~60, a.~5. The publication states this cross-author difference rather than fabricating one ancient technical name common to both. In VI.4, Aristotle's \emph{atechnia}, rendered by Ross as “lack of art,” supplies art's named contrary; because it is a false productive state rather than a quantitative deficiency, the excess side remains unpaired.

\subsection{Augustine}

The following nine works and their stated loci are the exact bounded Augustinian corpus for this edition; no additional direct Augustinian witness is implied. Most are public-domain English translations from the nineteenth-century \emph{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers} through New Advent's transcriptions; the two entries marked as Latin checks use the identified Augustinus.it witness:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.35em]
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1401.htm}{\emph{On the Morals of the Catholic Church}} I.15.25, for the four cardinal virtues as ordered forms of the love of God;
  \item \href{https://www.augustinus.it/latino/libero_arbitrio/libero_arbitrio_2_libro.htm}{\emph{On Free Choice of the Will}} II.18.47--50 and II.19.51--53, checked in Latin, for virtue as the right use of goods that can otherwise be misused;
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/12021.htm}{\emph{On Christian Doctrine}} I.22--27 and 35--40, for the object and order of charity, the end of Scripture, and the governing relation of faith, hope, and charity;
  \item \href{https://www.augustinus.it/latino/vera_religione/vera_religione_libro.htm}{\emph{On True Religion}} 55.108--113, checked in Latin, for the worship owed to God alone as contextual support for religion's object;
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1302.htm}{\emph{Enchiridion on Faith, Hope, and Love}} 3--8, 114, and 117--121, for the theological triad, pilgrim hope, charity's primacy, and the end of the commandments;
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120101.htm}{\emph{City of God} I.18}, for chastity as a virtue of the soul governed by the will; \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120105.htm}{V.12--20}, for the limits of civic glory; \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120109.htm}{IX.5}, for compassionate affection governed by reason; \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120114.htm}{XIV.13}, for pride and creaturely order; \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120115.htm}{XV.22}, for virtue as the order of love; and \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120119.htm}{XIX.4}, for the incompleteness of earthly virtue and happiness;
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1308.htm}{\emph{On Continence}}, especially 1--3 and 26--27, for resistance to disordered desire under grace and the boundary from false continence;
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1315.htm}{\emph{On Patience}} 1 and 8 (within 1--8 and 14--20 consulted), for endurance specified by a good end; and
  \item \href{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1310.htm}{\emph{On Holy Virginity}} 8 and 18, for virginity's dedicated purpose and marriage's goodness. Its anti-pride use at 31--32 was identified through Aquinas's citations and is marked mediated in the research records.
\end{itemize}

Augustine does not offer a single scholastic census corresponding to Aquinas's question sequence. His contribution is therefore used where direct: theological orientation, ordered love, Christian patience, continence, virginity, humility, and the fourfold interpretation of cardinal virtue. Later classifications are not retroactively placed in Augustine's mouth.

\subsection{Thomas Aquinas}

The controlling Latin work is the \emph{Summa theologiae}. English was checked through the Fathers of the English Dominican Province translation (1920), consulted in New Advent's \href{https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2.htm}{Prima secundae index} and \href{https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3.htm}{Secunda secundae index}. Principal loci are ST I--II, qq.~22--70 and 109--114; ST II--II, qq.~1--170; and ST III, qq.~85 and 90. Exact dossier-level loci and the classification controls governing their use are recorded in the terminal apparatus and source audit.

\subsection{Scripture and present Catholic synopsis}

Principal scriptural texts are Wisdom 8:7; Matthew 5:1--12; Romans 5:1--5; 1 Corinthians 13; Galatians 5:6 and 5:22--23; Hebrews 11:1; and 2 Peter 1:5--7.

The present Catholic synopsis was checked in the Holy See's English text of the \href{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_one/article_7/the_virtues.html}{\emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, 1803--1845}: human virtues (1804), prudence (1806), justice (1807), fortitude (1808), temperance (1809), formation and grace (1810--1811), theological virtues (1812--1829), gifts and fruits (1830--1832), and the summary (1833--1845). Supplemental official checks cover the \href{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_one/article_2.html}{beatitudes, 1716--1729}, and the \href{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_two/chapter_two/article_6/ii_the_vocation_to_chastity.index.html}{definition of chastity, 2337}. The \emph{Catechism} confirms the received Catholic frame; it does not supply every historical species or Aristotelian name in the expanded census.
