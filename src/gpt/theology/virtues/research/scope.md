# The Virtues — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Identity and governing profile

- **Provider:** `gpt`
- **Collection:** theology / faith
- **Genre:** comprehensive philosophical and theological reference
- **Governing profile:** `guidance/theology/virtues.md`
- **Canonical source:** `src/gpt/theology/virtues/main.tex`
- **Language:** English, with Greek and Latin terms where a translation choice affects the taxonomy
- **Jurisdiction and as-of date:** no mutable legal discipline is applied. The official English *Catechism of the Catholic Church* was checked on 2026-07-16 as a current catechetical synthesis; the historical philosophical and theological claims are edition- and locus-bound rather than jurisdiction-bound.

The work is a study instrument for readers who want a rigorous map of classical and Catholic virtue language. It is not an official catechism, a complete moral theology, spiritual direction, a clinical instrument, or a method for assigning another person's character or culpability.

## Question, thesis, and intended use

The governing question is: **What is a virtue, which distinct virtues receive secure Aristotelian, Augustinian, or Thomistic treatment, how are they ordered, and in what precise sense can each be related to defect and excess?**

The thesis has four parts:

1. virtue is a stable perfection of a human power for good action, not a mood, isolated deed, talent, reputation, or merely frequent behavior;
2. the Aristotelian mean is the measure of right reason in passions and actions, relative to the person and circumstances, not an arithmetic average or universal template for every kind of virtue;
3. Christian theology receives the acquired moral and intellectual order while distinguishing infused theological and moral virtues directed to supernatural beatitude; and
4. a comprehensive table must preserve asymmetry: theological virtues admit no intrinsic excess in God, intellectual virtues are measured by truth, and justice has a special objective mean of equality.

The reference is designed for continuous study and retrieval. Definitions therefore identify each virtue's class, formal object, characteristic act, principal sources, opposition structure, and boundary from nearby acts or counterfeits.

## Controlling corpus and meaning of “comprehensive”

The controlling corpus is deliberately reproducible:

- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* I--X, with II--VI controlling the strict virtue taxonomy; the authentic *Eudemian Ethics* III.7, 1234a24--34 and VIII.3, 1248b8--1249a17 are used only to expose qualified or integrative classifications;
- Augustine, *On the Morals of the Catholic Church* I.15.25; *On Free Choice of the Will* II.18.47--50 and II.19.51--53; *Enchiridion on Faith, Hope, and Love* 3--8, 114, and 117--121; *On Christian Doctrine* I.22--27 and 35--40; *On True Religion* 55.108--113; *City of God* I.18, V.12--20, IX.5, XIV.13, XV.22, and XIX.4; *On Patience* 1--8 and 14--20; *On Continence* 1--3 and 26--27; and *On Holy Virginity* 8 and 18, with 31--32 used only through Aquinas's mediated citations; and
- Thomas Aquinas, *Summa theologiae* I--II, questions 49--70; II--II, questions 1--170, whose organization controls principal, subjective, integral, and potential parts; and III, question 85 on penance as a special virtue, with question 90 controlling its distinction from the sacrament and its context-conditioned acts.

ST I--II, questions 109--114 are consulted as supplemental grammar for grace, merit, and the relation of free acts to divine assistance. They do not enlarge the strict virtue census.

The official *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 1803--1845 supplies the present Catholic catechetical synopsis. Scripture supplies the revealed vocabulary and order, especially Wisdom 8:7, Matthew 5--7, 1 Corinthians 13, Galatians 5:13--26, Philippians 4:4--9, Colossians 3:12--17, 2 Peter 1:3--11, and the passages cited by the controlling authorities.

“Comprehensive” means that every candidate found in this corpus's governing taxonomic loci is accounted for in `virtue-inventory.md` as a counted virtue, nested species or application, qualified cognate state, integral condition or act, alias, or reasoned exclusion. It does not mean every praiseworthy biblical noun, every later spiritual-school vocabulary, every virtue named once in a sermon, every modern character strength, or every act and gift that Aquinas treats alongside a virtue.

## Authority and provenance classes

- **Revealed and authoritative Catholic teaching:** Scripture and the official catechetical synthesis are identified as such; no scholastic taxonomy is presented as though every detail were dogma.
- **Aristotelian philosophical account:** a natural teleology of rational flourishing, habituated choice, passions, action, and contemplation. It is received critically rather than baptized without qualification.
- **Augustinian theological synthesis:** virtue as ordered love under the supreme good, with the cardinal virtues gathered into love of God and Christian life governed by faith, hope, and charity.
- **Thomistic synthesis:** a systematic account of habit, powers, acquired and infused orders, theological and cardinal architecture, annexed virtues, gifts, and opposed vices.
- **Editorial synthesis:** English dossier definitions, cross-source ordering, relation codes, and any marked descriptors for an unnamed extreme. These are not attributed to the sources as quotations or as their own fixed nomenclature.

## Sorting algorithm

The body and appendix use the same stable order:

1. faith, hope, and charity in the order of generation, with charity separately identified as first in perfection;
2. a dedicated overview explicitly naming prudence, justice, fortitude, and temperance, followed by their families in received cardinal order;
3. within each cardinal family, the principal virtue, integral conditions nested under it, subjective species, and potential or annexed virtues, with classification controlling before question order: equity follows legal justice, particular justice visibly parents distributive and commutative justice, penance follows commutative justice, and personal prudence appears in the prudential species map;
4. mercy and Aristotle's unnamed mean concerning ordinary honor as strict moral virtues without a non-editorial parent in the governing Thomistic parts-lists, ordered alphabetically by their preferred English titles;
5. speculative intellectual habits from first principles (understanding), through demonstrated conclusions (science), to highest causes (wisdom), followed by productive art, with prudence cross-referenced rather than duplicated; and
6. cognate states in a pedagogical sequence from natural beginning and incomplete self-command, through affective safeguard, virtuous relation, and mean-like passion, to extraordinary mode and integrative whole; the exclusion ledger follows them.

This is an order of exposition. It is not a single descending rank. Logical order, order of acquisition, order of generation, and order of excellence are stated independently. The fifty-two strict rows are treated taxonomic entries, not fifty-two coordinate habits.

## Defect and excess method

The appendix states relations in words and distinguishes six conditions that must not be collapsed:

- **mean flank:** a deficient or excessive response in a moral domain;
- **named contrary:** a real opposed vice that need not be one half of a quantitative pair;
- **objective outcome:** loss, gain, shortfall, or surplus in justice's equality, not a vice in the party receiving the outcome;
- **counterfeit or corruption:** a state resembling the virtue while changing its object, end, or rule;
- **unnamed or undedicated:** a source-recognized but unnamed flank, or a broad field with no single dedicated vice; and
- **not applicable:** no intrinsic defect--excess comparison governs that side.

Named historical terms control even when their English forms are privative. Thus *imprudence*, *disobedience*, *ingratitude*, and *irreligion* are not suppressed to achieve artificial lexical independence. The prohibition remains against “too little” or “too much” plus a virtue name. Source accuracy also overrides global word uniqueness: the same term can stand in different formal relations, and source alternatives can appear together when one English selection would create a false taxonomy. The table uses no unexplained dash. Aristotle's unnamed mean concerning ordinary honor remains visibly unnamed, with the source-language extreme terms retained and their ambiguity explained.

## Included distinctions and excluded expansions

The work includes acquired and infused virtue, the connection of virtues, the difference between virtue and continence, the gifts and fruits of the Holy Spirit, the beatitudes, friendship, shame, martyrdom, heroic virtue, and formation by acts and grace because those distinctions are necessary to understand the census.

The work does not:

- treat gifts, beatitudes, fruits, charisms, counsels, passions, acts, or states of life as coordinate virtues merely because they are praiseworthy;
- count synonyms or source-language translations as new species;
- count prudence's eight integral conditions as coordinate habits or collapse the four distinct social species in ST II--II, q. 50 into an undifferentiated governing scope;
- multiply the four cardinal virtues by the social, perfecting, perfect, and exemplar modes reported in ST I--II, q. 61, a. 5;
- multiply the cardinal names when ST I--II, q. 61, aa. 3--4 uses them for general conditions or for one virtue's qualities overflowing into another;
- copy rival lists in ST II--II, q. 80 without applying Aquinas's own reductions, or count broad-sense dulia and hyperdulia in ST II--II, q. 103, a. 4 as additional species of strict dulia;
- count natural aptitude or inchoation as a perfected habit, or turn the three context-conditioned exercises of penance and the sacrament's integral parts in ST III, q. 90 into additional stable virtue rows;
- count purity separately from chastity, since Aquinas treats it as a circumstance or alternate designation;
- promote Aristotle's shame, righteous indignation, continence, friendship, heroic virtue, or *kalokagathia* without their express qualifications;
- claim that marriage is a defect of virginity, abstention from alcohol a defect of sobriety, suffering injustice a vice, or maximal love of God an excess; or
- derive moral diagnoses from a reader's score, temperament, illness, disability, sex, social station, or isolated action.

## Review state and unresolved limits

The source loci and taxonomy were internally audited during generation. Independent philosophical, Augustinian, Thomistic, moral-theological, pastoral, and clinical review has not occurred and is not claimed. The virtue names are historical terms whose English connotations can shift; the dossiers define rather than assume their sense.

The controlling inventory records the principal unresolved boundary questions. In particular, Aristotle's *nemesis* is an unstable praiseworthy mean passion rather than a secure coordinate virtue; continence is virtue only in a broad or qualified sense; friendship “is or involves” virtue; and Aquinas sometimes reports earlier enumerations that he subsequently reduces to another principal or annexed virtue. Those cases remain visible rather than being used to inflate the headline count.
