# Virtue Inventory

Status: controlling working census for the comprehensive virtues reference, 16 July 2026. This record controls names, classification, order, dossier coverage, and appendix rows. It is not a claim that the listed habits are mutually exclusive units or that every ancient use of *aretē* or *virtus* names another species.

The census is bounded by the corpus declared in `guidance/theology/virtues.md`: Aristotle's securely attributed ethical works, principally the *Nicomachean Ethics*; Augustine's systematic treatments of Christian virtue and ordered love; and Aquinas's *Summa theologiae* I--II, questions 49--70; II--II, questions 1--170; and III, question 85 on penance as a virtue, with question 90 controlling its virtue--sacrament boundary and contextual divisions. Loci below are control references, not quotations. The source audit must identify the Greek or Latin witness and the human translation actually checked before publication; no source-language collation or independent philosophical or theological review is claimed here.

## Census rules

1. A counted row requires a stable habit, formally distinguished virtue, subjective species, or potential part expressly treated as virtue in the controlling corpus. Acquired and infused modes do not create duplicate rows.
2. A principal heading and its species may both have rows when the source gives each a distinct formal account. The resulting total is therefore a publication-row count, not a count of mutually exclusive psychological entities.
3. An act, passion, gift, fruit, beatitude, counsel, state of life, integral condition, circumstance, or authorial alias receives no strict census row unless a controlling source also establishes it as a distinct virtue. A separately labeled qualified appendix row may expose a necessary boundary case without promoting it into the strict census.
4. A species stated only under a term taken in a wide or analogical sense receives no new row unless the source also establishes a distinct univocal habit. Thus wide dulia groups already distinguished human-reverence virtues, and hyperdulia names the highest reverence within that wide usage without dividing strict dulia, which Aquinas says has no species.
5. A source's context-conditioned species of an act does not create another habit row unless the source separately stabilizes the habit by a proper name and formal object. ST III, q. 90, a. 4 expressly calls three exercises of penance species according to the intended change; they remain nested under the one special habit established in q. 85.
6. Prudence appears once in its cardinal family although it is also an intellectual virtue. Mercy and Aristotle's unnamed mean concerning ordinary honor are counted strict moral virtues, but neither receives a fabricated theological or fortitude-family parent. They follow all four cardinal families in a residual moral section.
7. The strict appendix order is the order of the counted entries below. Nested entries do not acquire appendix rows; the seven qualified entries appear only in a separate, visibly qualified appendix table.
8. The appendix keeps `Defect-side opposition | Virtue | Excess-side opposition` in that physical order and adds taxonomic status, parent, and a prose relation. Its vice cells use the shortest source-faithful headword that preserves the formal object; precision belongs in the relation field. It distinguishes mean flanks, named contraries, justice outcomes, counterfeits, **Unnamed** departures recognized without a proper noun, **Unpaired** sides with no dedicated opposite proper to the row, and **Inapplicable** axes. It uses no unexplained dash. Labels and markers must agree with `terminology-audit.md`; editorial qualifiers and translation controls may not be attributed to an ancient or medieval author.

## Stable sorting method

The numeric sort key is never inferred from the English alphabet. Its components fix the exposition sequence: `01` theological virtues; `02` prudence; `03` justice; `04` fortitude; `05` temperance, with `05.900--05.910` reserved for the residual strict moral section that follows all cardinal families; `06` remaining intellectual virtues; `07` qualified or cognate states; `08` nested non-row candidates; and `09` exclusions and reductions. Within `01`, faith, hope, and charity follow the order of generation, while charity is separately first in perfection. Within each cardinal family, classification controls before question order: equity follows legal justice; particular justice visibly parents distributive and commutative justice; severity remains with legal justice; penance follows commutative justice; and personal prudence is visible as the first prudential species. The residual moral entries are mercy and the unnamed ordinary-honor mean, in English alphabetical order. The intellectual order moves from first principles through demonstrated conclusions to highest causes, then to production: understanding, science, wisdom, art. Qualified entries move from natural beginning and incomplete self-command, through affective safeguard, virtuous relation, and mean-like passion, to extraordinary mode and integrative whole.

### 06: Remaining intellectual virtues

These habits are specified by truth or right making, not by a quantitative mean of passion. Aquinas nevertheless calls truth the mean of intellectual virtue: false denial and false affirmation can be defect and excess at the level of acts, without becoming two stable vice habits. Aristotle gives art the named contrary *atechnia*; its placement on the defect side is lexical, not quantitative, and no excess-side mate is supplied.

## Unresolved review controls

- The unnamed ordinary-honor mean is counted because Aristotle's account and Aquinas's elevenfold moral-virtue catalogue give it sound provenance. It belongs in the residual moral section, not a fabricated fortitude “honor cluster,” and the cross-author use of *philotimia* must remain explicit.
- Mercy is counted because Aquinas explicitly determines it to be a moral virtue. It belongs in the residual moral section after the four cardinal families; it is not a fourth theological virtue and has no manufactured two-flank pair.
- Legal justice, justice as principal heading, and the two species of particular justice produce nested rows. Counts must never be used as though each row named a wholly separate appetite or subject.
- Purity must remain a circumstance of chastity, notwithstanding the preliminary parts-list in ST II--II q.143 a.1; q.151 a.4 supplies Aquinas's express resolution.
- Natural or inchoate virtue, continence, shame, friendship, nemesis, heroic virtue, and *kalokagathia* remain qualified. Moving any one into the counted census requires a documented change to the comprehensive rule, counts, sort keys, body architecture, appendix, and terminology audit.
- Intellectual virtues receive truth-relative defect and excess only at the level of false acts or judgments, not an invented pair of passion-vices. No theological virtue receives an intrinsic excess toward God. No justice row receives an invented agent-side vice of “excess justice.”
