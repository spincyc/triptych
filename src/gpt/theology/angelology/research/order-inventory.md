# Nine-order inventory for GPT angelology

This inventory records the nine orders common to the Dionysian and Gregorian schemes. It does not turn every angelic epithet, scriptural image, personal name, or hostile principality into an additional order. Stable keys identify the nine comparison rows. The table ranks from highest (1) to lowest (9). The three Dionysian triads are ranks1–3,4–6,7–9. Scripture supplies the names; the ordered synthesis is patristic exposition.

## Meaning and biblical anchors

- Seraphim: burning/enkindling, purifying, communicated charity (Isa6:2,6). All angels love God; special name expresses eminence.
- Cherubim: fullness of knowledge, receptive contemplation and communication (Gen3:24; Ezek10). No inference of created omniscience.
- Thrones: stable receptivity/bearing God; Gregory divine judgment through them (Col1:16). Not literal seats supporting a divine body.
- Dominions: freedom from servility, eminent rule (Col1:16; Eph1:21).
- Virtues: steadfast strength in Dionysius; ministers of signs/miracles in Gregory (Eph1:21). Latin virtus here means power/strength, not an extra class of moral qualities.
- Powers: ordered exercise of authority in Dionysius; restraint of hostile spirits in Gregory (Col1:16; Eph1:21).
- Principalities: leadership of subordinate ministers; Gregory places the name above powers (Col1:16; Eph1:21).
- Archangels: mediating position in Dionysius; greater announcements in Gregory (Jude9 explicitly names Michael archangel). Do not infer a strict nine-order assignment of Gabriel/Raphael from every use of angel as common noun.
- Angels: common name of heavenly messengers, proper name of lowest order; nearness to human ministry (Luke1:26; Heb1:14). CHV explains common/proper naming; CHXI explains a different common/special use of powers.

No Scripture passage presents this entire ranked table. Neither the table nor its biblical names alone determines the meaning of every hostile “power” in Paul's writings. The opening Scripture exposition supplies the relevant narrative contexts.

## Additional patristic witnesses and limits

- Basil, DSSXVI.38: names ranks and heavenly rational natures beyond our naming; gives a theology of Spirit-given holiness and concord, not an independent exhaustive rival rank list.
- Augustine, City of GodXI9–10,13,29: gives creation, participation, knowledge, city and blessedness; those selected passages do not provide a competing ranked nine-order scheme.
- Damascene, DFOII3: explicitly receives Dionysius's three triads. Translation uses Dominions/Powers/Authorities and Rulers/Archangels/Angels; terms must be normalized before comparison.
- Gregory, HomXXXIV.11: human analogies for the nine ministries concern growth in grace and charity; do not create nine human biological species or claim bodily humans turn into angelic substances.
- ST I108a5: proper name according to excellence, lower perfections contained more eminently in higher, common gifts participate in different modes. I108a6 reconciles offices while preserving printed sequence differences. I108a8: human participation in angelic orders by grace.

## Witness and verification controls

The Dionysian ranks and meanings are read in Parker's 1899 translation, with chapter headings and selected quotations checked against the facsimile. Greek source terms above are conventional transliterations, not fresh manuscript readings. PG3 supplies the inspected Greek/Latin chapter and section locators. Gregory's sequence is directly read in Latin at Homily XXXIV.7; the definitions are at §§8–10. Aquinas's I.108.6, read in its entirety, explicitly compares the lists. These are the two complete ranked lists printed in appendix01; all eighteen rank cells and their associated characterizations appear in this inventory.

Damascene, Orthodox Faith II.3, read completely in Salmond's Edinburgh1898 witness, receives Dionysius's three triads. His translated middle triad is Dominions/Powers/Authorities, corresponding to normalized Dominions/Virtues/Powers; his Rulers/Archangels/Angels correspond to Principalities/Archangels/Angels. His nine ranks therefore follow the Dionysian column, not the Gregorian exchange of Principalities and Virtues. Translation differences do not create additional orders.

Scriptural anchors in the meaning list identify canonical names and images. They do not independently prove a nine-rank sequence or a particular mission for every member of an order.
