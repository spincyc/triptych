# Angels and the Gift of Being

## Recorded user deliverable (2026-09-29)

An original GPT/Codex document following the systematic teaching of Thomas
Aquinas, including the fifth-/sixth-century *Celestial Hierarchy*, expanding
the corpus through relevant patristic and saintly sources, with robust
exposition and ample appendices, installed and linked in the web library.
The Claude edition's source leads may be reused; its authored content is
not the source of this edition's composition. No page quota was requested.

## Question, reader, and argument

How can creatures without bodies know, love, receive grace, and serve God's
providence? The study addresses serious lay readers willing to learn the
necessary philosophical terms, with reference apparatus for advanced study.
Its organizing argument is that angelic excellence remains wholly received:
immaterial nature is not divinity, natural intelligence is not the beatific
vision, and ministry does not compete with God's causality.

The reader begins with Scripture and the Church's confession, then encounters
patristic witnesses and all fifteen Dionysian chapters, then follows the
Summa's order. Concrete explanatory puzzles introduce difficult distinctions.
Question dossiers and the complete article census support slower rereading.

## Corpus and completeness

The controlling systematic corpus is ST I qq.50–64 and 106–114, supplemented
by III q.8 a.4 and q.30 and named parallels actually read. Completeness means
every article in those two ranges appears exactly once in the canonical
inventory and its reader projection, not that every objection or parallel
work is reproduced. The Dionysian corpus is represented by a chapter-by-
chapter exposition of the Celestial Hierarchy, not its full reproduction.
Patristic, scholastic, Byzantine, magisterial and liturgical reception is
selective and identified by exact witness. No exhaustive history of all
angelology is promised or implied.

## Authority, language, and rights

The profile's grades apply to propositions, never to an author as a whole.
Defined and Church teaching require a named act; Common teaching is bounded
by identified witnesses; school determinations and open disputes remain
attributed. Grade assignments are editorial synthesis. The printed terminal
appendix states this rule without making readers cross it before the study.

English scriptural quotations use the registered Douay–Rheims/Challoner
witness; Vulgate book and verse numbering is retained with Hebrew psalm
numbering where different. Historical human translations are identified;
modern official teaching is primarily paraphrased. Received prayers will
not be composed or adapted. Project prose is CC BY 4.0; external wording
retains its separately recorded rights. The study supplies no case diagnosis,
exorcism procedure or deliverance instructions.
