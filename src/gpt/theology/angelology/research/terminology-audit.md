# Terminology audit: GPT angelology

Consolidated 29 September 2026 from the reader glossary, the directly read primary loci, and the [order inventory](order-inventory.md). English exposition uses established Catholic terms with a definition where ordinary usage could mislead. Latin and Greek forms identify conceptual or translation correspondences; they are not claims that every author uses the terms with identical scope. Greek appears in conventional unaccented transliteration, not as a diplomatic transcription.

| Preferred English | Latin / Greek source term | Translation choice and collision control | Principal control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Angel | angelus / angelos | Messenger by office; general designation of spiritual creatures; proper designation of the lowest order. State the relevant sense. | Gregory, Gospel homily XXXIV.8; CH V, IX; ST I.108.5 |
| Spirit | spiritus / pneuma | In angelology a created personal subject. The word does not itself assert deity, omnipresence, or an impersonal force. | Damascene II.3; CCC328–330 |
| Intellect; understanding | intellectus / nous | Power of understanding and its acts distinguished from substance. “Intellectual” does not mean merely learned or clever. | ST I.54 |
| Will | voluntas | Intellectual appetite, including enjoyment of possessed good. Distinguish willing from bodily passion and choice from deliberative delay. | ST I.59 |
| Substance | substantia / ousia | Subsisting subject, not a material stuff. The English word does not make a spirit corporeal. | ST I.50,54 |
| Essence | essentia | What the creature is; distinct from its received existence. Avoid interchangeable use with operation or power. | ST I.50.2; De ente, local caps.3–4 |
| Existence; act of being | esse | Received act of being, distinguished from the angel's act of understanding. | ST I.50.2;54.2 |
| Act and potency | actus; potentia | Actuality and capacity for actuality. Potency is not necessarily matter, bodily strength, or a future possibility. | ST I.50.2; De ente |
| Species: metaphysical kind | species | The kind constituted by a substantial form. “One angel per species” is metaphysical, not a biological population claim. | ST I.50.4 |
| Species: intelligible likeness | species intelligibilis | Form through which an object is known. It is neither the object's natural existence inside the knower nor an imaginative photograph. | ST I.55–56 |
| Natural / intentional being | esse naturale / esse intentionale | Actual existence of the other creature versus its presence as known. “Intentional” here does not mean imaginary or a future purpose. | ST I.56.2 ad3 |
| Natural knowledge | cognitio naturalis | Knowledge proportioned to created intellectual nature and its connatural forms. Distinguish from revelation and beatific vision. | ST I.55–58 |
| Beatific vision | visio Dei per essentiam | Seeing God's essence by divine elevation; seeing does not imply comprehensive knowledge of all God knows. | ST I.56.3;57.5;62 |
| Morning / evening knowledge | cognitio matutina / vespertina | Knowing creatures in the Word and in their created being. Distinguish the aspect known from the cognitive medium; avoid literal hours. | Augustine City of God XI.29; ST I.58.6–7 |
| Illumination | illuminatio | Communication of intelligible truth proportioned to the recipient, with strengthening of intellect. Not every communication is illumination. | CH VII–XIII; ST I.106–107 |
| Speech | locutio | A will's ordering its mental conception toward another spirit. No necessary vocal sound, distance in air, or bodily organ. | ST I.107 |
| Hierarchy | hierarchia | Sacred order, knowledge and activity directed toward likeness to God; a triad in the nine-order scheme. Do not flatten it into administrative rank alone. | CH III, VI; ST I.108.1 |
| Order; rank | ordo / taxis | A rank in spiritual communion distinguished by gifts and ministries. Common participation does not erase proper naming by eminence. | CH V–IX; ST I.108.5–6 |
| Purification | purgatio / katharsis | In blessed angels, removal of ignorance or lesser participation, not cleansing of personal moral sin. Respect analogical context. | CH III,VII,XIII; ST I.106 |
| Perfection | perfectio / teleiosis | Fulfillment according to the creature's participation and activity. Distinguish natural completeness from supernatural beatitude. | CH III; ST I.62 |
| Charity; natural love | caritas; amor naturalis | Natural orientation toward good is not interchangeable with supernatural charity. Both remain creaturely gifts. | Augustine City of God XII.9; ST I.60,62 |
| Definitive presence | definitive | Finite presence through application of angelic power. “Definitive” does not mean a final doctrinal ruling. | ST I.52 |
| Circumscriptive presence | circumscriptive | Bodily presence through dimensions corresponding to place, distinguished from angelic operative presence. | ST I.52 |
| Assumed body | corpus assumptum | Bodily instrument of representation and action; distinct from a human body naturally animated by a soul and from the Incarnation's personal union. | ST I.51 |
| Mission | missio | Ministry under divine command; compatible with continued contemplation. Sending may be mediated; higher orders' direct missions are a separately discussed question. | Gregory XXXIV.12–13; ST I.112 |
| Spiritual matter | materia spiritualis | In Bonaventure's account, a broad metaphysical receptive principle, not bodily quantity. Do not label the position corporealism or equate it without argument with Damascene's relative incorporeality. | Bonaventure II Sent. d.3 p.1 a.1 qq.1–2; Breviloquium II.6 |
| Pure spirit / pure act | spiritus; actus purus | An angel's immateriality does not remove composition of essence and existence. Pure act belongs to God. | ST I.50.2; De ente |
| Incorruptibility / immutability | incorruptibilitas; immutabilitas | Absence of natural dissolution differs from absolute unchangeability and from confirmation in blessedness. All created permanence is received. | Damascene II.3; ST I.50.5;62.8 |
| Fall; fixation | lapsus; obstinatio | Voluntary departure from good, followed by an irrevocable fundamental orientation. It does not destroy natural intellect or eliminate every choice of means. | Damascene II.4; ST I.63–64; De malo16.5 |

The [order inventory](order-inventory.md) gives the full nine-row rank and function comparison. The glossary and order tables use these choices consistently; source quotations retain witness wording, including Parker's printed *anthromorphic* in the chapterXV title.

## Citation and naming choices

The narrative may use familiar English biblical book names, while exact loci preserve Vulgate numbering with Hebrew numbering where different. Tobias/Tobit and Apocalypse/Revelation identify the same biblical books, not alternative witnesses. Biblical quotation wording belongs to its registered translation: Exodus3:2 in the opening's Douay–Rheims witness reads “the Lord,” and must not be silently replaced with another translation's “angel.”

“Dionysius the Areopagite” records traditional attribution and reception; modern dating of the corpus is stated separately. “Celestial Hierarchy” is the usual work name; Parker's title “Heavenly Hierarchy” identifies his translation. Chapter and section numbers are those established in the chapter audit: Parker prints no internal divisions in V, VI or XIV, while PG divides VI. A PG section locator is not silently represented as printed in Parker.

“Common teaching,” “Thomist position,” and “Disputed” are grades of propositions, not synonyms for confidence. The governing profile and terminal scope state their rule; a reader-facing term is not upgraded merely because a great authority uses it. No new theological definition or philological consensus is claimed by this terminology table.
