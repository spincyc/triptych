\section{Calendar and scriptural names}

The Roman entries describe the general calendars of the identified books.
Occurrence and local calendars can alter a celebration in a particular year
or place. The Byzantine entry belongs to the Pittsburgh Menaion witness.

672, 695; \emph{Ordo lectionum Missae} (1981), nn.~647, 650,

\begin{longtable}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.16\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.39\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.35\linewidth}}

Michael & Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Jude 9; Apocalypse 12:7--9 & Defense of God's people; combat against the dragon\\
Gabriel & Daniel 8:16; 9:21--23; Luke 1:19, 26--38 & Interpretation and announcement; the Annunciation\\
Raphael & Tobias 3:25; 12:14--15, in the Vulgate numbering & Healing, deliverance, and companionship\\
\end{longtable}

The Directory, n.~217, exempts these three scriptural names from its
direction discouraging the assignment of names to angels. The list does
not identify a Christian's particular guardian angel. Nor does it identify
all seven angels mentioned in Tobias 12:15. The distinction is between
what Scripture names and what devotion is permitted to supply on its own.
