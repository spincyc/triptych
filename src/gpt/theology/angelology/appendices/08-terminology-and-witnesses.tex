\section{Terminology}
\label{app:terms}

\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.19\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.51\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
English & Source term & Meaning in this treatise\\
\midrule\endhead
Angel & Latin \emph{angelus}; Greek \emph{angelos} & A messenger; also the
customary general name of the spiritual creatures and the particular name
of the lowest order. The context distinguishes these uses.\\
Spirit & \emph{spiritus}; \emph{pneuma} & A spiritual being. In angelology
the term denotes a created personal subject, not an impersonal force.\\
Intellect & \emph{intellectus}; \emph{nous} & The power of understanding.
Angelic intellect has no bodily organ and does not abstract from sensory images.\\
Will & \emph{voluntas} & Intellectual appetite: inclination toward the
good apprehended by intellect.\\
Substance & \emph{substantia}; \emph{ousia} & That which subsists, as
distinct from a quality or operation inhering in a subject.\\
Essence & \emph{essentia} & What a thing is. In a created angel essence
receives existence; it is not the unreceived divine act of being.\\
Existence & \emph{esse} & The act of being. Aquinas's angelic immateriality
retains the composition of essence and received existence.\\
Act and potency & \emph{actus}, \emph{potentia} & Actuality and capacity
for actuality. A creature without matter can still stand in potency to
received perfection.\\
Species & \emph{species} & Either a metaphysical kind or an intelligible
likeness by which something is known. The latter does not name a second
angelic creature within the knower.\\
Natural knowledge & \emph{cognitio naturalis} & Knowledge proportioned
to the created intellect and its connatural forms.\\
Beatific vision & \emph{visio Dei per essentiam} & Seeing God's essence,
made possible by divine elevation. It exceeds every created natural power.\\
Morning/evening knowledge & \emph{cognitio matutina/vespertina} & Augustine's
and Aquinas's distinction between knowing creatures in the Word and in
their created being; not hours on an angelic clock.\\
Illumination & \emph{illuminatio} & Communication of intelligible truth
proportioned to a recipient, with strengthening of that intellect.\\
Speech & \emph{locutio} & A will's directing its mental conception to
another angel; broader than illumination and independent of vocal sound.\\
Hierarchy & \emph{hierarchia} & Sacred order, knowledge and activity
directed toward likeness to God; in the nine-order arrangement, a triad.\\
Order & \emph{ordo}; \emph{taxis} & A rank within ordered spiritual
communion, distinguished through gifts and ministries.\\
Purification & \emph{purgatio}; \emph{katharsis} & In blessed angels,
release from a lack of knowledge or a lesser participation, not cleansing
from moral fault. Context governs Dionysius's analogical use.\\
Perfection & \emph{perfectio}; \emph{teleiosis} & Fulfillment in the
participation and activity proper to a creature.\\
Definitive presence & \emph{definitive} & An angel's finite operative
presence, without the dimensional extension by which bodies occupy place.\\
Mission & \emph{missio} & Ministry proceeding under God's command toward
another creature; consistent with the blessed vision.\\
Assumed body & \emph{corpus assumptum} & A bodily instrument of appearance
and action, distinct from the natural body animated by a human soul.\\
Dominion; domination & \emph{Dominatio}; \emph{kyriotes} & The same order
under customary English alternatives.\\
Virtue/Power & \emph{Virtus}; \emph{dynamis} & ``Virtues'' in the Latin
order names; Parker's ``Powers.'' Here \emph{virtus} denotes strength or
power rather than a moral habit.\\
Power/Authority & \emph{Potestas}; \emph{exousia} & ``Powers'' in the
Latin order names; Parker's ``Authorities.''\\
Principality & \emph{Principatus}; \emph{arche} & The order Parker calls
``Principalities''; its rank differs between the Dionysian and Gregorian lists.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The principal controls are Augustine, \emph{City of God} XI.9, 29;
Basil, \emph{On the Holy Spirit} XVI; Dionysius, \emph{Celestial Hierarchy}
III, VI--IX; John Damascene, \emph{Orthodox Faith} II.3; and Aquinas,
\ST{I qq.~50--60, 106--108, 112}. The paired order names retain the distinction
between a translated word and a rank in a particular witness's arrangement.

\section{Witness register}

\toprule
Witness & Work used & Contribution to the account\\
\midrule\endhead
Sacred Scripture & Douay--Rheims/Challoner witness & The angels' creation,
worship, personal ministry, service of Christ and participation in judgment.\\
Basil of Caesarea & \emph{On the Holy Spirit}, XVI & The heavenly powers'
holiness received through the Spirit.\\
Gregory Nazianzen & Oration 38.9 & The first intellectual creation and the

Augustine & \emph{City of God}, XI--XII; \emph{On the Trinity}, III &

Dionysius the Areopagite, traditional attribution & \emph{Celestial Hierarchy},
I--XV & Sacred order, participation, the nine ranks, illumination and

Gregory the Great & \emph{Homilies on the Gospels}, II.34 & The orders,
their ministries and the ethical fruit of their contemplation.\\
John Damascene & \emph{Exposition of the Orthodox Faith}, II.3--4 &
Spiritual nature, finite presence, received immortality, ministry and the fall.\\
Origen & \emph{Commentary on Matthew}, XIII.26--29 & Inquiry into the

John Chrysostom & \emph{Homilies on Matthew}, 59.4 & Care for the little

Bonaventure & \emph{Commentary on the Second Book of Sentences}, d.~3 &

Thomas Aquinas & \emph{Summa theologiae}, I qq.~50--64, 106--114;

knowledge, love, grace, fall, hierarchy and ministry.\\
Lateran IV and Vatican I & \emph{Firmiter credimus}; \emph{Dei Filius} &

Pius XII & \emph{Humani generis}, 26 & Angelic personality and the

The Catholic Church & \emph{Catechism}, 328--336, 391--395 & The doctrinal

Congregation for Divine Worship & \emph{Directory on Popular Piety}, 213--217 &
The Church's direction of devotion to the angels.\\
Roman and Byzantine liturgical witnesses & Identified Missals and calendar
witness in the liturgical appendix & The celebration of the angels within
the Church's worship.\\
\bottomrule

The bibliography identifies editions and translations. The table gives the
role of each principal witness in the exposition; an author quoted within
