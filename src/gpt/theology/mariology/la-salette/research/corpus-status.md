## Exact controlling Grenoble formula

The original French, not an editorial shorthand, controls the legacy status:

> Nous jugeons que l'apparition de la sainte Vierge à deux bergers, le 19 septembre 1846, sur une Montagne de la chaîne des Alpes, située dans la paroisse de la Salette, de l'Archiprêtré de Corps, porte en elle-même tous les caractères de la vérité, et que les fidèles sont fondés à la croire indubitable et certaine.

Source: de Bruillard, mandement of 19 September 1851, article 1; reproduced in the nineteenth-century record and independently cited in the DDF's 2024 presentation.

Editorial English sense: the bishop judges that the apparition of the Blessed Virgin to two shepherds on the named date and mountain bears in itself all the characteristics of truth, and that the faithful have grounds to believe it indubitable and certain. This translation aids reading; the French is the historical juridical formula.

### Status consequences

- **Positive:** the formula identifies an event by person, date, place, and witnesses, and gives a real favorable diocesan judgment. Article 3 authorizes the cult and preaching, article 4 forbids publication of any particular prayer formula, hymn, or devotional book without de Bruillard's written approval, and article 7 recognizes the principal evangelical purpose.
- **Bounded:** it does not name the 1851 secret manuscripts, the 1879 text, a critical transcript, each alleged miracle, or a witness's whole subsequent life.
- **Legacy:** it belongs to the ecclesiastical vocabulary and procedure of 1851. DDF 2024 quotes it precisely to illustrate older formulations and reiterate that the faithful are not obliged to accept an apparition's authenticity.
- **Current:** no public DDF act located through 2026-07-16 reclassifies the case into the six determinations introduced in 2024. Therefore the publication retains the historical formula and adds current doctrinal context; it does not retrofit `Nihil obstat` or `constat de supernaturalitate`.

Grenoble 1851 judgment + cult ──► 1846 event/public reception
Grenoble 1854 act             ──► defends judgment; separates later predictions
Rome 1915/1916/1923           ──► secret discussion and named books

No arrow runs from “Rome received a sealed paper” to “Rome authenticated every proposition.” No arrow runs from “local permission to print” to “Grenoble's 1851 judgment includes the 1879 text.”

## 1854 act and 1855 pastoral-source control

Modern summaries sometimes compress two different interventions:

| Date | Genre | Object | Proper use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 Nov. 1854 | Formal episcopal instruction and mandement | Déléon's *Affaire de La Salette, Mémoire au Pape*; defense of the prior judgment and devotion; treatment of posterior predictions | Source for Ginoulhiac's juridical/episcopal response |
| 18–19 Sept. 1855 | Near-contemporary report and anniversary allocution | Completion of the children's finite role, succession by new instruments, and the pilgrims' mission | Source for the pastoral substance later compressed into the shepherds-and-Church maxim |

The monograph may present the familiar sentence only as a later custodial synthesis. It may not call the wording a stenographic quotation or the anniversary preaching a “new inquiry decree.”

## Roman-discipline control

The legal and pastoral record must be read object by object:

1. **1915:** universal discipline directed at secret discussion and application; explicit devotional exception.
2. **1916:** action against one named two-volume work, with two official author-name forms.
3. **1923:** action against a named 1922 reprint; anomalous 1845 title date preserved with `[sic]`.
4. **1966:** the Index loses ecclesiastical-law force and attached censures; the CDF notification nevertheless appeals to conscience under natural law.
5. **1983:** unretained older Apostolic See penal laws are abrogated under CIC c. 6 §1, 3°.
6. **Present:** these sources do not, without further competent interpretation, settle whether the 1915 decree's standalone non-penal command remains binding in its original breadth or has lapsed. No private writer may claim that the old censures still attach automatically, or that their abrogation makes the underlying works doctrinally approved. Concrete present questions belong to competent authority.
