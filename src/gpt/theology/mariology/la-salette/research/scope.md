# La Salette: Apparition, Public Message, and Contested Secrets — Research Scope

## Identity and governing question

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/la-salette/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** single-apparition historical and theological reference monograph; `guidance/theology/mariology.md` governs, especially its rules for judgment objects, text strata, currentness, and private revelation. The universal editorial standard also controls. This is not a liturgical proper or a general discursive article.
- **Rite, jurisdiction, and competent acts:** Latin Church; Diocese of Grenoble (now Grenoble–Vienne); Bishop Philibert de Bruillard's mandement of 19 September 1851, followed by Bishop Jacques-Marie-Achille Ginoulhiac's formal instruction and mandement of 4 November 1854. Later Roman acts are stated only for the objects they address.
- **Question:** What was reported at La Salette on 19 September 1846, what is the complete public message, exactly what did Grenoble judge and authorize, how do the two short 1851 secret manuscripts differ from Mélanie Calvat's expanded 1879 publication, and how should the event, symbols, witnesses, Roman discipline, and continuing Catholic reception be understood together?
- **Reader:** a serious Catholic reader who wants devotion, historical criticism, documentary precision, and ecclesial obedience held together.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-16. The DDF document index, 2024 procedural norms, 2025 Marian doctrinal note, current Diocese/Shrine/Institute pages, and relevant Holy See records were rechecked through this date. No public DDF act superseding or reclassifying Grenoble's legacy event judgment was located.
- **Primary thesis:** the controlling ecclesial reception concerns the reported 1846 encounter and its public evangelical message, together with authorized cult. The separately delivered 1851 manuscripts and Mélanie's much longer 1879 apocalypse belong to distinct textual and juridical strata. La Salette's received center is Christic reconciliation expressed in conversion, worship, prayer, reverence for the holy Name, penitential discipline, solidarity in hunger, and communication of the Gospel—not possession of a secret timetable.

## Included corpus and evidence strata

The monograph includes the following strata and keeps their dates, objects, and authority distinct:

1. **The 1846 encounter and public discourse:** the event reported by Maximin Giraud and Mélanie Calvat at Mont Planeau on 19 September 1846, including the complete public-message content reconstructed from early evidence. The study distinguishes stable content from wording affected by recollection, translation, and the children's local speech.
2. **Near-event records:** Jean-Baptiste Pra's lost early relation as preserved through an attested copy, the accounts associated with Abbé Lagier, the children's interrogations, correspondence, sermons, and other material assembled chronologically by Jean Stern. These establish early content and reception; they are not stenographic recordings.
3. **The 1847 diocesan inquiry:** the conference process organized under de Bruillard, including the work of Canon Pierre-Joseph Rousselot and seminary superior Jacques-Philippe Orcel, testimony, objections, and the bishop's developing judgment.
4. **The 1851 diocesan act and cult:** the exact positive legacy formula, its stated grounds, its authorization of cult, and its limits. It is neither a modern `Nihil obstat` nor a definition requiring divine and Catholic faith.
5. **The 1852 institutional reception:** the diocesan decision to build the mountain sanctuary and found the Missionaries of Our Lady of La Salette, read as an ecclesial response to the judged event rather than proof of a hidden rule.
6. **Ginoulhiac's 1854 and 1855 interventions:** the formal *Instruction pastorale et Mandement* of 4 November 1854 is distinguished from the anniversary preaching of September 1855. The first answers Déléon's attack and separates the judged event from posterior predictions. A near-contemporary 1855 report establishes the second intervention's pastoral substance—the children's mission completed and others sent to continue the Church's work—while the polished shepherds-and-Church epigram is identified as a later custodial synthesis rather than a stenographic formula.
7. **The two 1851 personal manuscripts:** short texts written separately by Maximin and Mélanie for Pius IX, with their custody, contents, dates, expired predictions, and documentary limits. The fact that the manuscripts are authentic witness documents is not treated as proof that every sentence came from Mary.
8. **Mélanie's expanded secret corpus:** especially the first-person account dated at Castellammare on 21 November 1878 and printed at Lecce in 1879 with local permission. Its new clerical denunciations, chronology, apocalyptic figures, ecclesial crisis language, new institute rule, and last-things material are compared directly with 1851 rather than retrojected into 1846.
9. **Roman discipline and book actions:** qualified 1880 withdrawal evidence; the Holy Office decree of 21 December 1915; the 1916 Holy Office and Index actions against a named two-volume work; the 1923 action against a named 1922 reprint; and the later legal context supplied by the Index's loss of ecclesiastical-law force and the 1983 Code. The study preserves the 1915 exception for devotion to Mary as Reconciliatrix. It distinguishes the demonstrably obsolete penalty machinery from the unresolved present status of the 1915 decree's standalone non-penal command.
10. **Papal, liturgical, institutional, and current reception:** Pius XII's centenary reception, John Paul II's 1996 and 2000 interventions, the Missionaries' charism, diocesan and sanctuary custody, and French liturgical memory. These establish substantial ecclesial reception according to their own objects; none enlarges the 1851 act to cover the 1879 text.
11. **The witnesses' later lives and controversies:** the Ars episode, Maximin's unsettled adult life and continued testimony, Mélanie's religious itinerancy and expanding claims, and their deaths. Biography is evidence about reception and credibility, not a canonization process or substitute for competent judgment.

## Exclusions

- No attempt is made to create a universal apparition registry, pronounce a new supernatural-origin judgment, or replace the bishop, DDF, shrine, or institute as competent custodian.
- The monograph does not certify every reported word, cure, prodigy, prediction, devotional title, later locution, or biographical legend.
- It does not claim that a local printing permission authenticates a revelation, that a Roman archive's receipt is papal approval, or that a saint's favorable or unfavorable private opinion is the competent judgment.
- It does not decode the expanded secret as a timetable of popes, nations, dynasties, wars, Antichrist, or the end of the world. Expired dates and failed-looking predictions are disclosed rather than rescued through elastic reinterpretation.
- It does not reproduce wholesale modern translations or the entire 1879 booklet. The public message and both 1851 manuscripts receive complete-content analytical maps; the 1879 expansion receives a complete map of consequential additions with short anchors and a link to the scan.
- It does not settle the witnesses' sanctity, moral culpability, mental state, or causes of death; nor does it use devotion to excuse calumny against clergy or contempt for ecclesial authority.
- It does not treat the optional memorial, papal praise, a shrine, a religious institute, an imprimatur, or popular fruits as an automatic enlargement of the judgment's object.

## Material uncertainties and editorial controls

### Early wording and source transmission

The earliest public-message wording survives through several witnesses, interrogations, and copies rather than an audio record. The original Pra relation is not known to survive in its original hand; its content is accessed through the Lagier-associated transmission and the critical documentary dossier. French renderings also mediate children who used a local Alpine speech form. Consequently, the study publishes the complete stable content, identifies memorable wording as received wording, and does not claim syllable-level inerrancy.

### Secret manuscript custody and dating

The 1851 texts were written for Pius IX and later became difficult for researchers to locate. Michel Corteville reported finding them in the historical Holy Office archive in 1999; René Laurentin and Corteville published facsimiles and transcriptions in 2002. This project inspected public facsimiles/transcriptions and their scholarship, not the physical Vatican archival leaves. Accounts differ over whether Maximin's surviving text is the 2 July draft or the rewritten 3 July version; the monograph discloses the discrepancy and does not derive theology from the day difference.

### Later Roman evidence

The 1915, 1916, and 1923 actions were checked in the official *Acta Apostolicae Sedis*. The 1880 episode is known through an archival dossier as published in later scholarship rather than a located AAS decree, so it is described as qualified evidence and not made to bear subsidiary chronological precision. Later literature mentions additional Holy Office correspondence, but no reproducible publication and page locus was established and no conclusion depends on it. The 1966 Index notification and 1983 Code remove old legal machinery and unretained penalties; those sources neither transform the prohibited books into approved revelations nor, without further competent interpretation, settle every non-penal command's present status.

### Witness biography

The Ars exchange has no agreed verbatim transcript, and later recollections differ. Maximin's and Mélanie's adult lives have been written through polarized apologetic and hostile lenses. The study retains facts supported by documentary work, attributes disputed explanations, and refuses both character assassination and hagiographic immunity.

### Currentness

The statement that no superseding DDF act was located is a dated negative-search result, not a claim that competent authority could never act later. Any future edition must recheck the DDF index, Diocese of Grenoble–Vienne, sanctuary, and relevant Roman records.

## Theological controls

- Vatican II, *Dei Verbum* 2–4 and CCC 65–67 govern the finality of Revelation in Christ and the subordinate role of private revelation.
- Vatican II, *Lumen gentium* 56 and 60–62, CCC 970, and DDF, *Mater Populi Fidelis* (2025), govern Marian cooperation: entirely received from Christ, dependent, participatory, and never competitive with the one Mediator.
- DDF, 2024 norms, govern current procedural vocabulary and explicitly use La Salette's older formula to explain why a faithful person's freedom of assent must remain clear.
- Documentary authenticity, ecclesiastical permission to print, disciplinary tolerance, papal reception, and supernatural-origin judgment are separate predicates throughout.
