# La Salette Secret-Text Audit

Checked through 2026-07-16. This record prevents the most consequential source error in La Salette literature: collapsing the public message, two separately written 1851 communications, and Mélanie Calvat's much longer 1879 publication into a single “approved secret.”

## Audit question and controlling conclusion

The audit asks four separate questions of every statement:

1. **Which document contains it?**
2. **When was that document written or printed?**
3. **Who authored, copied, permitted, received, or judged it?**
4. **What does the relevant ecclesiastical act actually do to that object?**

The result is clear. The early dossier establishes that each child claimed a private communication in 1846. The two short July 1851 manuscripts establish what each witness then wrote for Pius IX. Mélanie's 1878/1879 text is materially expanded in scale, tone, chronology, politics, ecclesiology, and apocalyptic detail. Grenoble's positive 1851 act concerns the apparition and authorized cult but does not authenticate a secret text. Roman reception later distinguished authorized La Salette devotion from secret publication and application.

## Documentary chain and custody

### The 1846 private communications

Within the reported event the public crop warning pauses. The Lady is said to speak to Maximin while Mélanie sees moving lips but does not hear, then to Mélanie while Maximin does not hear. Public speech resumes with the condition of conversion, prayer, Mass, Lent, the Coin memory, and the command to communicate the message. Early questioners therefore knew that two individually received communications were claimed, but did not possess their wording.

This order matters: the public message was examinable and preached for nearly five years before a secret text was delivered to Rome. The later printed apocalypse cannot be made the hidden textual center of every earlier inquiry simply because the narrative says a private exchange occurred.

### July 1851 writing and delivery

- Maximin wrote under adult observation at the episcopal residence. The accessible clean manuscript is dated 3 July. Documentary summaries that say 2 July appear to count an earlier draft/session; the displayed date controls quotation while the discrepancy remains disclosed.
- Mélanie first wrote at Corenc. She subsequently clarified the sequence of the predicted disasters of Paris and Marseille and produced the extant text dated Grenoble, 6 July.
- The writings were sealed separately. Canons Jean-Baptiste Gérin and Pierre-Joseph Rousselot carried the bishop's letter and packets to Rome and delivered them for Pius IX on 18 July 1851.
- Accounts report papal reading or reaction. No located public dispositive act declares either text supernatural. Receipt, reading, sealing, or archival preservation is custody—not approval.

### Rediscovery and accessible text

The manuscripts became difficult for later scholars to locate. Michel Corteville reported finding them in the historical Holy Office archive in 1999. René Laurentin and Corteville published facsimiles/transcriptions in *Découverte du secret de La Salette* (2002), especially pp. 46–49 and the manuscript plates. This audit compared that publication's reported text with publicly displayed facsimile-based transcriptions. It did not inspect the physical archival leaves and therefore does not call itself a Vatican critical edition.

## Complete content map: LS-PUB

The public message contains the following stable elements, none of which requires the later secret corpus:

| Sequence | Content | Textual/status note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Concern that the Lady's people may not understand; warning that continued conduct may oblige her to release the Son's arm | Theological interpretation remains subordinate to Christ's unique mediation; Mary is not a rival restraining deity |
| 2 | Complaint about six days given for work and the seventh reserved, with people continuing Sunday labor | Received in the historical setting of Catholic Sunday obligation; not a license to condemn the poor without context |
| 3 | Misuse of the Son's Name, described as weighing heavily | Direct moral call to reverence, not a magical explanation of catastrophe |
| 4 | Spoiled crops and warning that the next harvest may fail; seed eaten or reduced to dust | Concrete subsistence imagery from 1845–1847 |
| 5 | Famine; small children trembling and dying; others doing penance through hunger; walnuts and grapes failing | The tears direct readers toward mercy and solidarity, not victim-blaming |
| 6 | Separately spoken private communications to each child | Establishes the boundary, not their wording |
| 7 | Conditional promise that conversion can transform scarcity into abundance | The condition resists fatalism |
| 8 | Question about prayer; counsel to pray morning and evening, at minimum an Our Father and Hail Mary when unable to do more | Ordinary Christian practice, not a secret technique |
| 9 | Few older women at Mass; others absent or mocking; eating meat in Lent “like dogs” | Historical moral rhetoric; apply through charity and current Church discipline |
| 10 | The Coin episode in which Maximin remembers seeing spoiled wheat with his father | Narrative recognition cue personal to Maximin; not a universal prediction |
| 11 | Repeated command to make the message known to all the Lady's people | Public commission; it does not say to publish the separately unheard communications |

## Complete content map: LS-MAX-1851

Maximin begins by distinguishing perception from later identification: he says they saw a “beautiful Lady,” that they had not originally said she was the Blessed Virgin, and that he now believes she was. He then attributes the following sequence to her:

| Clause | Content | Date/fulfillment control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | If the people continue as before, events come sooner; if they change somewhat, events come somewhat later | A timing condition, not an unlimited cancellation clause |
| M2 | France has corrupted the world and will be punished | Broad moral/national proposition; no competent fulfillment decree located |
| M3 | Faith will become nearly extinguished in France: most will cease religious practice and the remainder practice badly | Resemblance to secularization is insufficient to verify the whole sequence |
| M4 | Nations will afterward convert and faith will be rekindled everywhere | Universal scale must not be reduced to isolated examples |
| M5 | A large northern European country, then Protestant, will convert and help all other countries convert | No country is named; no competent act identifies one; political guesswork remains private |
| M6 | Great disturbances will precede this in the Church and everywhere | Highly general unless read in the full ordered sequence |
| M7 | The pope will be persecuted and succeeded by an unexpected pontiff | No pope is named; resemblance does not authenticate the manuscript |
| M8 | A great but brief peace will come, then a “monster” will disturb it | The “monster” is not identified as Antichrist in this text |
| M9 | All of the foregoing will happen in the next century, at the latest by the year 2000 | Plain terminal horizon has passed without a transparently complete fulfillment |
| M10 | Parenthetical note: she told him to disclose it some time beforehand; request for the pope's blessing | No specific 1858 publication date appears in Maximin's manuscript |

### Editorial assessment

The text is historically authentic as Maximin's 1851 writing. It is not transparently fulfilled in its ordinary total sequence by 2000. Selecting wars, persecuted popes, secularization, conversions, or temporary peaces after the fact cannot establish the ordered whole, especially the unnamed northern Protestant country's universal role. The passed horizon is a material credibility limit for the private text. It does not retroactively annul the competent judgment of the separately bounded event/public message.

## Complete content map: LS-MEL-1851

Mélanie's short text begins with J.M.J., identifies itself as the secret given on the mountain, and says not to tell anyone. Its complete consequential sequence is:

| Clause | Content | Date/fulfillment control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | The time of divine anger has arrived | Apocalyptic introduction, subordinate to the explicit condition that follows |
| L2 | If people do not convert, do penance, cease Sunday work, and stop blaspheming after hearing the public message, divine chastisement follows | Real condition linked directly to the public moral call; not permission to blame victims |
| L3 | Paris will perish and Marseille be destroyed soon afterward; disorder and impious passions spread | The rewriting reportedly clarified succession rather than simultaneity; ordinary reading is not transparently fulfilled within the stated horizon |
| L4 | The pope will be persecuted and shot at, but efforts to kill him will fail and he will triumph “this time” | No pope named; no official one-to-one application located |
| L5 | Priests, women religious, and true servants of Christ will be persecuted; several will die for the faith | Positive/martyrial treatment of clergy as a class, unlike the 1879 opening denunciation |
| L6 | Famine occurs at the same time | Conditional-sequence claim, not an independent proof from any famine |
| L7 | Many then recognize God's hand, convert, and do penance | Conversion remains the evangelical aim |
| L8 | A great king reigns for several years; religion and abundance flourish | No ruler identified by competent authority |
| L9 | Prosperity leads again to disorder and criminal passions | Moral cycle, but still part of an ordered prophecy |
| L10 | Some ministers of God and spouses of Christ fall into disorder | Limited “some,” alongside faithful martyrs; not the 1879 sweeping opening |
| L11 | An infernal period follows; Antichrist is born of a woman religious and deceives many by claiming heavenly origin | Antichrist appears here, but without the elaborate 1879 genealogy, armies, ten kings, cosmic disruptions, Enoch/Elijah sequence, or Rome sentence |
| L12 | The time is not distant: “twice fifty years” will not pass | Ordinary outer horizon from the nineteenth-century utterance has passed |
| L13 | Mélanie must say nothing further until told; she asks the pope's blessing | No 1858 publication permission appears in this July 1851 manuscript |

### Editorial assessment

The opening conditionality must be retained, but it cannot make every subsequent mismatch unfalsifiable. Paris and Marseille did not undergo the plainly predicted destruction within less than one hundred years, and no competent authority has identified the great king, Antichrist, or complete sequence. A later assassination attempt on a pope can resemble L4 without proving L1–L13 or identifying Marian authorship. The passed terminal phrase is disclosed without polemical exaggeration.

## Complete addition map: LS-MEL-1879

The 1879 booklet does not merely polish L1–L13. It embeds a much longer “secret” within a retrospective apparition narrative, then claims that a rule for a new religious order was given before the public discourse resumed. Its consequential additions include:

| Addition family | 1879 content | Comparison control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publication permission | Mélanie is told the content will not always remain secret and may be published in 1858 | Absent from both July 1851 manuscripts; printed only in 1879, after the asserted release date |
| Clerical indictment | Long opening condemnation of priests for bad life, irreverence in sacred mysteries, money, honors, pleasures, and impurity; famous “cesspools of impurity” wording | Material change from L5/L10, where persecuted priests die for Christ and only some ministers fall |
| Named ecclesial/political actors | Direct warnings involving Pius IX, Napoleon, Italy, civil rulers, and ecclesiastical princes | Names and political specificity absent from Mélanie's short 1851 text |
| Retrospective dates | Warnings tied to 1859; demons released in 1864; abomination in holy places in 1865; events extending more than thirty-five years | These dates were already past or underway when the 1878 manuscript was dated |
| Demonic and pseudo-religious phenomena | Demonic transportation, false resurrections, false gospel, extraordinary prodigies, churches serving spirits, religious houses losing faith | Extensive new narrative architecture |
| Political and natural catastrophe | France, Italy, Spain, and England at war; civil conflict; Paris burned; Marseille swallowed; cities and countries lost in earthquakes; famine, plague, slaughter | Enlarges L3/L6 far beyond the short manuscript and adds numerous mechanisms |
| Interim renewal | Enemies destroyed; peace and reconciliation; new kings support a humble Church; Gospel preached; twenty-five years of abundance followed by relapse | Elaborated millenarian-looking chronology absent from 1851's short sequence |
| Antichrist complex | Forerunner, ten kings, false peace, parentage through a Hebrew religious woman, demonic father, monstrous childhood, brothers commanding armies, cosmic disorder | Dramatically expands L11 |
| Famous Rome sentence | “Rome will lose the faith and become the seat of Antichrist” | 1879 layer only; absent from LS-PUB, LS-MAX-1851, and LS-MEL-1851 |
| Last-times apostles | Appeal to apostles of the last times, poverty, humility, prayer, mortification, suffering, and spiritual combat | New summons, frequently detached from its late textual source |
| Church eclipse | “The Church will be eclipsed” amid world consternation | 1879 layer only; not the approved public message |
| Enoch and Elijah | Prophetic preaching, death, heavenly judgment, cosmic purification, and defeat of Antichrist | Absent from the short 1851 manuscript |
| New-order rule | Claim that Mary next gave, in French, the rule of a new religious order before resuming the public discourse | Not in the early public-message dossier or de Bruillard's 1852 foundation act |

### Textual stability warning

The audit anchors the named 1879 Lecce booklet, including its bracketed matter, notes, and reported marginal additions as printed in the scan. Later reprints, editions, commentaries, and composite “secrets” may alter punctuation, add marginal phrases, omit controversial passages, or merge other Mélanie texts. Any quotation must therefore carry an edition and page/image locator. “Mélanie later said” is not a sufficient textual citation.

### Editorial assessment

The 1879 text is a primary source for what Mélanie publicly attributed to the apparition at that late date. It is not a primary 1846 transcript. The twenty-seven-year gap, retrospective chronology, textual growth, passed dates, new institute claim, and later Roman discipline all materially limit confidence. The evidence does not require a psychological diagnosis or accusation of conscious fraud; it requires exact dating and refusal to enlarge the positive judgment.

## Deadline and fulfillment protocol

The monograph applies the same rule to favorable and unfavorable claims:

1. Read the ordinary grammatical horizon before proposing symbolism.
2. Preserve explicit conditions, but do not turn them into universal immunity from evidence.
3. Test the whole ordered sequence, not a selected resemblance.
4. Require a named source and competent ecclesial application before calling a pope, nation, ruler, disaster, or present event “the fulfillment.”
5. Disclose passed dates and failed-looking particulars.
6. Distinguish spiritual fruit from prediction verification.

Applied here:

- Maximin's “at the latest” 2000 horizon has passed; the full conversion/persecution/peace/monster sequence is not transparently fulfilled.
- Mélanie's “twice fifty years” horizon has passed; Paris and Marseille did not undergo the plainly described annihilation within it, and no competent act identifies the promised king or Antichrist sequence.
- The 1879 publication's 1858 release permission and 1859/1864/1865 chronology were already retrospective when printed. Later broad correspondences cannot cure that source fact.
- These limits reduce confidence in the secret texts. They do not logically erase every element of the separately judged 1846 event or the public evangelical message.

## Exact Roman bibliography controls

- **1915:** *Decretum circa vulgo dictum “Secret de La Salette”*, AAS 7 (1915), printed p. 594. Its final sentence preserves devotion to the Blessed Virgin under the title Reconciliatrix commonly called La Salette.
- **1916:** AAS 8 (1916), printed p. 175, gives “Dr Henry Mariam”; the Index decree at pp. 178–179 gives “Dr Henri Mariavé” for the same titled work. Both official forms must remain visible.
- **1923:** AAS 15 (1923), pp. 287–288. The named booklet's printed title says 19 September 1845 `[sic]`, although the historical event date is 1846. Silent correction would corrupt the legal citation.
- **Later correspondence lead:** later documentary literature mentions further Holy Office correspondence concerning the 1922 reprint. No sufficiently reproducible publication and page locus was established here, so no published conclusion depends on that lead; the named 1923 AAS entry controls.

## Imprimatur, archive, and authenticity predicates

These propositions are not interchangeable:

| Predicate | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manuscript/facsimile authenticity | A witness wrote or signed a particular text at a particular stage | That Mary dictated every sentence |
| Sealed delivery for Pius IX | Chain of custody and intended recipient | Papal approval, papal authorship, or a supernatural-origin decree |
| Preservation in Holy Office archive | Institutional custody | Doctrinal endorsement |
| Local permission to print | A local ecclesiastical permission under the review then applied | Extension of Grenoble jurisdiction or authentication of prophecy |
| Grenoble event judgment | Favorable judgment on the identified apparition and authorized cult | Inerrancy of every later witness document |
| Papal devotional reception | Ecclesial fruit and public-message encouragement | Secret chronology or approval of the expanded text |

## Rejected composite texts

The following are not admissible as textual proof:

- web pages headed “The Full Approved Secret of La Salette” that silently splice LS-PUB, LS-MEL-1851, LS-MEL-1879, and commentary;
- translations without a French edition, page, or manuscript locator;
- prophecy charts that omit the 2000 and “twice fifty years” horizons;
- texts that move the 1879 Rome or Church-eclipse sentences into 1846's public discourse;
- claims that both children wrote the same secret;
- assertions that Pius IX's receipt, a bishop's imprimatur, or the Holy Office archive constitutes revelation approval;
- later marginalia or alternate editions quoted as if present in the July 1851 autographs;
- contemporary political applications unsupported by a competent ecclesial act.

## Rights and quotation boundary

The nineteenth-century witness texts and acts are public-domain source material, but modern editions, translations, introductions, photographs, and critical apparatus may carry separate rights. The publication therefore uses short French anchors where exact provenance matters and supplies complete-content analytical restatements in original editorial language. It links readers to the public facsimiles/scans and does not reproduce Laurentin–Corteville's modern apparatus or an entire modern English translation.

## Audit lock

The only publication-safe shorthand is:

> La Salette has a publicly received 1846 message, two materially different short witness manuscripts written for Pius IX in 1851, and Mélanie's much longer 1879 expansion. Documentary authenticity and ecclesial permission must be stated object by object; none converts the expanded secret into the approved public message.
