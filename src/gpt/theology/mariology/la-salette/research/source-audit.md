# La Salette Monograph — Claim-Level Source Audit

All mutable online sources were last rechecked on 2026-07-16. Primary acts and official Holy See texts control ecclesial status and law. Jean Stern's chronological documentary edition controls historical sequence where a primary scan is unavailable. Current shrine, diocesan, and Missionaries pages are authoritative custodial witnesses but do not replace the competent bishop's act, retroactively approve a secret, or erase material discrepancies.

## Source hierarchy and handling

1. **Public Revelation and universal doctrine:** Scripture, Vatican II, the Catechism, current DDF norms, and current DDF Marian teaching.
2. **Competent judgments and disciplinary acts:** the Grenoble episcopal acts of 1851, 1852, and 1854; official AAS decrees of 1915, 1916, and 1923; official 1966 Index notification/decree; the 1983 Code.
3. **Primary event and text witnesses:** near-event relations and interrogations in Stern; the two 1851 secret manuscripts in facsimile; Mélanie's 1879 booklet; contemporaneous correspondence where available.
4. **Official custody and reception:** Holy See papal texts; Diocese of Grenoble–Vienne, Shrine, Missionaries, and French Church records.
5. **Critical scholarship:** Stern; Laurentin and Corteville; Stern on Ars; historical studies used for context and witness biography.
6. **Discovery aids only:** OCR, catalog metadata, search snippets, unaffiliated transcriptions, and devotional sites. Consequential wording found through these aids was checked against a page image, primary scan, official transcript, or multiple independent controls.

## Textual, translation, and OCR notes

- The exact 1851 French judgment formula was checked across the nineteenth-century reprint and DDF 2024 quotation. English renderings in the monograph are identified as editorial translations; the French controls.
- Event discourse survives in French renderings and locally mediated speech, not a recording. Complete-content mapping is therefore preferred to claims of stenographic precision.
- Public 1851 manuscript transcriptions were checked against displayed facsimile images and Laurentin–Corteville's published comparison. The physical Vatican archival leaves were not independently inspected.
- The 1879 booklet was downloaded and OCR-extracted for discovery; every consequential phrase used in the study was checked against the scan. OCR hyphenation and accents do not control quotations.
- AAS searches used the Vatican's OCR PDFs, but exact names, titles, verbs, printed page numbers, and the anomalous 1845 date were checked in the official page images/text context.
- Modern official English and French web pages are cited in their published language. No unofficial English secret translation is treated as a critical edition.

## Rejected leads and non-controlling evidence

Rejected as proof: anonymous “full approved secret” pages; social-media prophecy images; texts that splice the public message, both 1851 manuscripts, the 1879 expansion, and later commentary into one speech; claims that Pius IX's receipt equals papal approval; claims that an imprimatur certifies supernatural origin; political or conclavist applications; elastic fulfillment systems that move expired dates without textual warrant; accounts that call the 1855 homily a new decree; lists that silently harmonize “Henry Mariam” and “Henri Mariavé”; citations that correct AAS's 1845 title without `[sic]`; and any assertion that the 1966 Index notification approves the previously listed books.
