\section{The Prayer}

The Angelus is a brief pious exercise of the Latin Church for dawn, midday, and evening. Its three exchanges move from Gabriel's announcement, through Mary's consent, to the Word made flesh; each is followed by the Hail Mary. The collect carries the Incarnation through the Cross to the Resurrection. During Easter Time the Regina Coeli ordinarily takes its place.

The bilingual form below reproduces Dom Gaspar Lefebvre's 1925 \textit{Daily Missal with Vespers for Sundays and Feasts}, printed p.~13. That witness abbreviates each repeated prayer as ``Hail, Mary, etc.'' To make the form complete, this publication prints immediately afterward the complete Hail Mary found on p.~5 of the same edition; it is to be recited at each marked place. No wording has been modernized or translated by this project.

\bilingualprayer{The Angelus}{Received Latin and historical English from Lefebvre's 1925 \textit{Daily Missal}, p.~13. Its abbreviation markers are retained; the complete same-edition Hail Mary to be recited at each marker is printed below.}{%
\VR{Angelus Dómini nuntiávit Maríæ.}{Et concépit de Spíritu sancto.}
\PrayerRepeat{Ave María, etc.}
\VR{Ecce ancílla Dómini.}{Fiat mihi secúndum verbum tuum.}
\PrayerRepeat{Ave María, etc.}
\VR{Et Verbum caro factum est.}{Et habitávit in nobis.}
\PrayerRepeat{Ave María, etc.}
\VR{Ora pro nobis, sancta Dei Génitrix.}{Ut digni efficiámur promissiónibus Christi.}
\textbf{Orémus.}\par
Grátiam tuam, quaesumus Dómine, méntibus nostris infúnde: ut qui, Angelo nuntiánte, Christi Fílii tui incarnatiónem cognóvimus, per passiónem ejus et crucem, ad resurrectiónis glóriam, perducámur. Per eúndem Christum Dóminum nostrum.\par
\textbf{R.} Amen.}{%
\VR{The Angel of the Lord announced unto Mary.}{And she conceived of the Holy Ghost.}
\PrayerRepeat{Hail, Mary, etc.}
\VR{Behold the handmaid of the Lord.}{Be it done unto me according to Thy word.}
\PrayerRepeat{Hail, Mary, etc.}
\VR{And the Word was made flesh.}{And dwelt among us.}
\PrayerRepeat{Hail, Mary, etc.}
\VR{Pray for us, O holy Mother of God.}{That we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ.}
\textbf{Let us pray.}\par
Pour forth, we beseech Thee, O Lord, Thy grace into our hearts, that we, to whom the incarnation of Christ Thy Son was made known by the message of an Angel, may, by His passion and cross, be brought to the glory of His resurrection. Through the same Christ our Lord.\par
\textbf{R.} Amen.}

One may pray the Latin or the historical English devotionally; a group may alternate languages without doubling the prayer. The printed English is not presented as a currently approved translation for an indulgenced recitation or for liturgical use. Readers seeking the indulgence in another language should use a form approved by competent ecclesiastical authority. The 1999 \textit{Enchiridion} also prints a current Latin form with modern \textit{eius} and a shorter \textit{Per Christum} conclusion; it is not silently substituted for this 1925 witness.
