# The Angelus — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/angelus/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** repeatable Marian-prayer reference and usable bilingual
  prayer aid. `guidance/editorial.md` and the Marian-prayer addendum in
  `guidance/theology/mariology.md` govern it. The novena profile does not.
- **Rite, language, and locale:** Catholic popular piety of the Latin Church in
  the Roman tradition; English editorial exposition; received Latin and historical
  English prayer text. The publication is not an edition of the Roman Missal or
  the Liturgy of the Hours and does not claim one current vernacular text for
  every territory.
- **Governing question:** What is the Angelus; what exact prayer does this
  publication reproduce; how do its scriptural, Christological, Trinitarian,
  Marian, Paschal, historical, and devotional dimensions fit together; and
  which current seasonal and indulgence rules actually govern its use?
- **Intended reader:** a Catholic reader, household, catechist, study group, or
  community seeking both a usable prayer and a source-audited account of its
  meaning and status.
- **Thesis:** the Angelus is a Christ-centered pious exercise that interrupts
  the day with the one economy of salvation: the Father sends, the Holy Spirit
  overshadows Mary, the eternal Word truly becomes flesh, Mary's graced consent
  receives God's initiative, and the collect opens the Incarnation through the
  Cross to the Resurrection.
- **Checked-through date:** 21 July 2026. The fourth *Enchiridion
  Indulgentiarum*, the current Roman-calendar boundary of Easter Time, and the
  current doctrinal safeguard for Marian intercession were checked as mutable
  or current claims on that date.

## Included and excluded scope

Included are the complete received bilingual prayer form selected for this
publication; the three Gospel exchanges and repeated Hail Mary; the concluding
versicle, response, and collect; the prayer's biblical and doctrinal
architecture; its staged Western development; its daily rhythm at dawn,
midday, and evening; its relation to bells and prayer for peace; its Easter-Time
handoff to the Regina Coeli; and the current universal grant as printed in the
Latin typical edition of the *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum*.

Excluded are a critical edition of every historical Angelus witness; a catalog
of all territorial translations, melodies, bell patterns, religious-family
customs, local additions, or former indulgence grants; a liturgical order; a
claim that every Catholic is bound to pray at three exact clock times; and a
general history of Marian devotion. The Regina Coeli has its own complete leaf
and audit records rather than being treated as an appendix or fragment here.

No apparition, private revelation, automatic promise, guaranteed political
result, or private chronology is part of the evidence or the devotion's
authority.

## Source hierarchy and evidence method

1. The reproduced vocal text is governed by the page images of Dom Gaspar
   Lefebvre's 1925 *Daily Missal with Vespers for Sundays and Feasts*, not by
   memory, OCR, a current website, or a project translation.
2. Current indulgence discipline is governed by the Apostolic Penitentiary's
   fourth *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum* (1999), concession 17 §2, 2° and its
   printed Latin form.
3. The nature and responsible ordering of the pious exercise are governed by
   Paul VI's *Marialis cultus* 41 and the *Directory on Popular Piety and the
   Liturgy* 195–196.
4. Scripture, *Lumen gentium*, the Catechism, and the DDF's *Mater Populi
   Fidelis* govern the Incarnation, Mary's graced consent, intercession, and
   Christ's unique mediation.
5. Papal historical reflections and source-critical historical scholarship
   support a staged development. A secondary report of a primary witness is
   identified as such and never promoted to direct collation.

The publication distinguishes the received 1925 prayer text from current Latin
discipline. Historical rubrics adjacent to that prayer remain evidence for the
1925 edition only. The current official Latin is used to verify the present
grant, not silently substituted into the chosen historical witness.

## Material qualifications

1. The Angelus is a pious exercise, not Mass, a liturgical hour, a sacrament, or
   a universally binding precept. Paul VI's description of its rhythm as
   “quasi-liturgical” is an analogy of daily sanctification, not a juridical
   reclassification.
2. Dawn, midday, and evening are the received daily rhythm. The current grant
   names early morning, midday, or toward evening; it does not require completion
   of all three recitations as one indulgenced set or impose exact civil clock
   times.
3. Bells, standing or kneeling, communal alternation, sung Hail Marys, and a
   proclaimed Annunciation Gospel can be venerable or fitting customs. They are
   not conditions printed in the current universal indulgence grant.
4. The grant must be read with the *Enchiridion*'s general norms. A partial
   indulgence requires at least a contrite heart; the recipient must be
   baptized, not excommunicated, and in the state of grace at least when the
   prescribed work is completed; and at least a general intention and due
   performance are required. A vernacular prayer used to gain the indulgence
   requires competent ecclesiastical approval. The printed historical English
   is offered devotionally but is not represented as holding that approval in
   every territory.
5. The historical hundred-days and monthly-plenary regime printed in the 1925
   witness is superseded. *Indulgentiarum doctrina* abolished measurement of
   partial indulgences in days or years; the fourth *Enchiridion* now grants a
   partial indulgence for the stated pious recitation.
6. The current Roman devotional handoff is the Regina Coeli in Easter Time. The
   1925 witness's older seasonal rubric is historical evidence, not the current
   calendar boundary.
7. The prayer's history is composite. Evening bell-and-Ave customs, later
   morning use, peace and noon bells, a developed versicle sequence, and papal
   and popular reception are not collapsed into one invention by one pope,
   friar, council, or date.
8. Mary's fiat is free, personal, and grace-enabled creaturely cooperation. It
   neither makes her a divine cause nor places God's saving work in dependence
   on an autonomous human power.
9. Mary's intercession participates wholly in Christ's one mediation. The
   petition does not make her an independent source of grace, a rival advocate,
   or a restraint upon the Father.
10. The collect joins Incarnation and Pasch. Its vocal recitation is no automatic
   mechanism, guarantee of salvation, or substitute for faith, conversion,
   sacramental life, charity, or justice.

## Text, translation, and rights boundary

The governing scan is Dom Gaspar Lefebvre, O.S.B., *Daily Missal with Vespers
for Sundays and Feasts* (Lophem near Bruges: St. Andrew's Abbey; St. Paul,
Minnesota: E. M. Lohmann, 1925), printed pp. 5 and 12–14. The Angelus itself is
on p. 13; the complete Hail Mary used to resolve its three explicit
abbreviations is on p. 5; p. 12 supplies historical contextual rubrics. The
checked scan has SHA-256
`28c9046c5acfa19297ab9aeaa14ac4f8d8fcffab1307b51215b94fa4ca6172a2`.
`research/prayer-text-audit.md` controls every reproduced unit and resolution
of an abbreviation.

The 1925 edition is public domain in the United States by publication date. Its
title matter says “All rights reserved,” and this audit did not establish the
translator or author of the exact historical English. Lefebvre's 1966 death
therefore does not by itself establish an exact life-plus-70 endpoint for that
translation; non-US rights remain unresolved rather than cleared through 2036.
The historical Latin and English prayer wording remains excluded from
Triptych's CC BY 4.0 grant. The project-created exposition, source organization,
and design fall within that grant. Historical ecclesiastical permissions
printed in the edition govern that edition only and do not approve this
publication or make its English a current liturgical translation.

No current proprietary vernacular Missal, Liturgy of the Hours, Bible, or
official prayer translation is reproduced. Current official texts are cited
and narrowly paraphrased. Scripture is normally cited by locus rather than
reproduced from a modern copyrighted version.
