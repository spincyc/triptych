\subsection{A detailed graphite study of the commanded Ark}

The first plate is a source-controlled graphite illustration keyed to the
features commanded in Exodus, not a recovered portrait of the lost object.
The scriptural facts are stated in the live caption below; perspective,
surface ornament, and every other uncommanded visual detail are interpretive.
Generated pixels supply no evidence and do not enlarge what the text warrants.
A fine gray hairline deliberately marks the edge of each graphite study plate;
it is an editorial frame, not an archaeological or architectural boundary.

\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Commanded Ark form --- textual features, interpretive rendering.}
The chest is made of acacia---the Douay and Vulgate tradition's setim
wood---and overlaid with gold inside and out.  It is $2\frac12$ cubits long
and $1\frac12$ cubits wide and high.  A pure-gold cover, the mercy seat or
propitiatory, bears two hammered-gold cherubim whose wings overshadow the
cover and whose faces turn toward it.  Four gold rings receive two
gold-overlaid wooden poles, which remain in the rings (Ex 25:10--22; 37:1--9).
The modern length of a cubit, the cherubim's appearance, the surface pattern,
and the fittings seen here are not reconstructed; the drawn proportions and
ornament are illustrative rather than evidentiary.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\begin{studybox}{What was within, before, and beside it}
Exodus and Deuteronomy place the covenant tablets within the Ark.  The
Pentateuch places the manna vessel and Aaron's rod before the Testimony and the
book of the law beside the Ark.  Hebrews 9:4 gathers the golden manna vessel,
Aaron's rod, and tablets within its retrospective sanctuary description.  At
Solomon's dedication, 1 Kings 8:9 says that only the two tablets were inside.
The drawing does not invent when or how any item entered or left.
\end{studybox}

\subsection{Four sanctuary settings}

These detailed graphite plates help the reader imagine four attested settings
while the source-controlled titles and captions mark the boundary of each
claim.  They are not archaeological reconstructions, are not drawn to a
common scale, and never make a pictured architectural or decorative detail
into evidence.

\begin{center}
{\bfseries Wilderness Tabernacle --- commanded arrangement, interpretive reconstruction\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
\arkgraphiteplate{theology/mariology/ark-of-the-covenant/artwork/tabernacle-graphite-v2.png}

\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\footnotesize
The commanded Tent stands within the court, and the Ark is screened by the
veil in the Most Holy Place (Ex 26--27; 30:17--21; 40:2--8, 18--21, 29--38 in
modern numbering).
This cutaway asserts only the east-to-west nesting and ordered relation of
gate, altar, laver, Holy Place, veil, Most Holy Place, and Ark.  Its apparent
court and Tent proportions, post counts, single visible roof layer,
construction, textiles, ornament, and furniture forms are illustrative
simplifications; Exodus controls wherever the view omits or compresses a
commanded feature.
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.8em}
{\bfseries Shiloh --- attested sanctuary, architectural form deliberately unresolved\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
\arkgraphiteplate{theology/mariology/ark-of-the-covenant/artwork/shiloh-graphite-v2.png}

\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\footnotesize
Scripture explicitly places the Tent of Meeting and the Ark at Shiloh, then
uses house or temple language and names doors and a lamp (Jos 18:1; 1 Sm 1:9;
3:3, 15; 4:3--4).  The centered Ark is an emblem of its attested presence,
not a plan coordinate or a claim that it stood exposed.  The plate's
separated, fading cloth, threshold, doorway, and erased-line possibilities are
invented and mutually incompatible uncertainty cues, not traces copied from a
Shiloh excavation.  They refuse to select one recovered structure: exact plan,
material, sequence of change, footprint, and the Ark's position within it
remain unknown.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

{\bfseries David's tent on Zion --- attested resting place, unrecovered form\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
\arkgraphiteplate{theology/mariology/ark-of-the-covenant/artwork/david-tent-graphite-v2.png}

\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\footnotesize
David pitches a tent, brings the Ark into it, and appoints worship before it;
the older Tabernacle and altar remain at Gibeon (2 Sm 6:17; 1 Chr 16:1,
39--40).  The finely drawn tent is nevertheless only a schematic,
interpretive sign.  The isolated white-paper setting does not depict or locate
Zion.  The plate recovers neither David's structure nor its site and does not
identify his tent with the Mosaic Tent or a later Temple building.
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.8em}
{\bfseries Solomon's Temple --- textual arrangement, interpretive cutaway\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
\arkgraphiteplate{theology/mariology/ark-of-the-covenant/artwork/solomon-temple-graphite-v2.png}

\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\footnotesize
The Ark is brought up from the City of David into the inner sanctuary beneath
the two great cherubim, whose wings span the chamber wall to wall and touch
one another above it; only the tablets are inside; cloud and glory fill the
house (1 Kgs 6:19--28; 8:1--11; 2 Chr 5:2--14).  The cutaway helps distinguish
porch, Holy Place, and inner sanctuary, but its plan and elevation, masonry,
ornament, furnishings, and the bodily forms and viewing angle of Ark and
cherubim are an interpretive reconstruction rather than archaeological or
historical evidence.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Continuity:} In every attested setting, the Ark remains the Lord's
covenant sign, never a ruler's possession; Scripture judges the attempt to use
it as a talisman rather than submit to the God whose presence it signifies.
\par
\textbf{Change:} The commanded movable sanctuary, the textually complex Shiloh
sanctuary, David's newly pitched tent, and Solomon's permanent house are not
four interchangeable buildings.
\par
\textbf{Boundary:} Scripture securely identifies the Ark's presence and, in
some cases, its liturgical relation to veil or inner sanctuary.  It does not
preserve the Shiloh structure, David's tent, the Ark's artistic appearance, or
the precise First-Temple footprint for modern reconstruction.
\end{studyframe}
