\subsection{Text equivalent for the journey map}

The plate has two panels.  A small whole-journey locator runs from the
Sinai wilderness northward to the land of Israel and Nazareth.  A larger
central panel enlarges the Jordan valley, central highlands, Philistine plain,
Judean hill country, and Jerusalem.  North is toward the top in both panels,
but the drawing is schematic rather than a measured road map.

\textbf{How to read the marks.} A solid line means Scripture narrates movement
of the Ark or an explicit departure; its printed curve is not a recovered
road.  A long-dashed line means that an endpoint, the Ark's accompaniment, or
the plotted identification is inferred.  A dotted line joins two separately
attested Ark locations when the intervening transfer is not narrated.
A dotted area marks a region whose exact place is unknown.  Filled circles
identify strongly located ancient sites; hollow circles identify a probable,
candidate, or region-level location.  A double-outlined square marks a later
Christian traditional site.  Mary's movement is a white-cored double line so
that it remains distinct in monochrome print and does not merge with the old
Ark's route.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.0em]
  \item \textbf{Stations 1--3: Sinai/Horeb and the wilderness.} The Ark is placed within the
    tabernacle, and Numbers records one initial three-day journey on which it
    goes before Israel (Ex 40:20--21; Nm 10:33--36).  The biblical mountain is
    not securely located.  The map therefore begins in a broad Sinai region,
    then uses a dashed wilderness course past the Kadesh region toward Moab.
    At Kadesh, Moses and the Ark explicitly remain in camp during Israel's
    presumptuous ascent (Nm 14:44).  Numbers 21 and 33 list Israel's itinerary,
    not continuous Ark accompaniment; explicit following resumes in Joshua 3.
  \item \textbf{Stations 4--5: Jordan, Gilgal, and Jericho.} Priests carry the Ark into the
    Jordan and remain in the riverbed until the people cross; Israel camps at
    Gilgal on Jericho's eastern limits (Jos 3--4).  Neither the crossing nor
    Gilgal has an established point, so the map uses an area.  The small loop
    at Jericho represents the narrated procession around the city, not a
    surveyed wall circuit (Jos 6).
  \item \textbf{Stations 6--7: Ebal and Gerizim, then Shiloh.} Joshua 8 places the Ark between
    the two assemblies facing the paired mountains.  No route to that ceremony
    is narrated.  Joshua 18 establishes the tent of meeting at Shiloh, and
    1 Samuel 3 later explicitly places the Ark there.  Dotted connectors keep
    those transitions distinct from narrated travel.
  \item \textbf{Side episode: Bethel/Shiloh witness difference.} In Judges
    20:18, 26--28 the Hebrew, Greek, and most modern witnesses place the Ark at
    Bethel, while the Clementine Vulgate and Douay tradition read the house of
    God as Shiloh.  No witness gives a dated transfer into or out of Bethel, so
    the map prints the difference without drawing another route.
  \item \textbf{Stations 8--11: capture and Philistine circuit.} The Ark is brought from
    Shiloh to the Israelite camp at Ebenezer and captured (1 Sm 4).  Ebenezer's
    location remains uncertain, and Aphek is the opposing Philistine camp, not
    an Ark stop.  The Hebrew, Vulgate, and Douay branch takes the Ark to Ashdod,
    Gath, and Ekron; the registered Brenton Septuagint names Ashdod, Gath, and
    Ashkelon.  The figure plots the first branch and prints the second in its
    key.  Gaza occurs only in the five-lord offering list.  The departure of
    the cow-drawn cart from the last-named city is implied by narrative
    continuity rather than freshly named at 1 Samuel 6:7.
  \item \textbf{Stations 12--13: return to the highlands.} The cart goes toward Beth-shemesh;
    the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim then take the Ark to Abinadab's house on
    the hill (1 Sm 6:7--7:1).  The archaeological sites give regional anchors,
    not the locations of Joshua's field or Abinadab's house.
  \item \textbf{Stations 14--15: Perez-uzzah and Obed-edom.} David's first Jerusalem procession
    is broken at the Nacon/Nodan--Chidon threshing floor, afterward called
    Perez-uzzah; the Ark then remains in Obed-edom's household for three months
    (2 Sm 6:6--12; 1 Chr 13:9--14).  Neither place is located securely.
    They therefore appear in a narrative box over the broad
    Kiriath-jearim--Jerusalem corridor, not as map pins.  ``Gittite'' is not
    treated as a coordinate for Gath.
  \item \textbf{Stations 16--17: Zion and the Temple.} David brings the Ark into the City of
    David, biblical Zion, and places it in the tent prepared for it
    (2 Sm 6:12--17).  Solomon later brings it from Zion into the Temple's inner
    sanctuary (1 Kgs 8:1--11).  The short solid connector represents this
    narrated transfer, while the points remain area markers rather than a
    claim to know the tent or Holy of Holies footprint.
  \item \textbf{Unmapped side notices and the earthly terminus.} Uriah's
    wartime statement may imply an Ark field presence but gives no route or
    camp (2 Sm 11:11).  During Absalom's revolt the Ark is carried across the
    Kidron and returned to Jerusalem, but the text gives no new station
    (2 Sm 15:23--29).  Second Chronicles 35:3 gives
    Josiah's later command concerning the Ark at Solomon's house.  The exile
    inventories do not name it.  Jeremiah 3:16 is prophecy; 2 Maccabees 2:1--8
    preserves a canonical concealment tradition whose place is expressly to
    remain unknown; Revelation 11:19 is a heavenly vision.  None supplies a
    recoverable terrestrial route beyond the Temple, so the map ends there.
  \item \textbf{N1--N2 and tradition: Mary, the living Ark.} Luke names Nazareth, but identifies
    Elizabeth's home only as a town in Judah's hill country (Lk 1:26, 39--56).
    The double route therefore ends in a broad hill-country area.  Ein Karem is
    drawn separately as the received Christian traditional identification,
    not as a coordinate supplied by Luke.  Luke closes the Visitation with
    Mary's return home before beginning the later Nazareth--Bethlehem--Jerusalem
    sequence; the map consequently draws no Ein Karem-to-Temple procession.
\end{enumerate}

The typological reading depends on the scriptural pattern, not on identical
geography: divine overshadowing, ascent into Judah, the question about the
Lord's coming, David's dance and John's leap, a three-month household sojourn,
and blessing in the presence of the Lord.  Mary fulfills the type personally
because she bears Jesus Christ, the incarnate Covenant and divine Presence.
