\section{The Whole Journey at a Glance}

This synopsis is a retrieval aid, not a substitute for the biblical narratives,
the checked witnesses, or the Church's definitions.  It gathers the road into
one view while preserving the difference between event, typology, dogma, and
spiritual application.

\begin{threestable}{Movement}{Scriptural center}{What the movement discloses}
Sinai and wilderness & Exod 25; 37; 40; Num 10 & God gives the sign of his
presence, appoints its service, and leads his people; the Ark is received, not
invented or activated.\\
Jordan and Jericho & Josh 3--6 & The Lord goes before Israel.  The waters and
the city yield to his action rather than to a sacred mechanism under human
control.\\
Shiloh and Philistia & 1 Sam 4--7 & Israel's presumption is judged, Dagon is
humbled, and God's freedom is manifested even while the Ark is in exile.\\
Kiriath-jearim to Zion & 2 Sam 6; 1 Chr 13; 15--16 & Disordered handling gives
way to appointed service; fear, household blessing, dancing joy, sacrifice,
and ascent belong to one recovered procession.\\
Temple and expectation & 1 Kgs 8; Ps 132; 2 Macc 2 & The glory fills the house,
yet no earthly sanctuary exhausts the promised dwelling or the hope of final
rest.\\
Annunciation and Visitation & Luke 1:26--56 & The Spirit overshadows Mary; she
bears the incarnate Lord into Judah's hill country; wonder, leaping, blessing,
and three months receive their Christological fullness.\\
Heavenly horizon & Rev 11:19--12:6; 21:1--4, 22 & Ark, woman, people, Temple,
and city converge without becoming identical.  God and the Lamb are the final
sanctuary, and God's dwelling is with humanity.\\
\end{threestable}

\subsection*{Five truths to carry forward}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Christ is the Covenant and definitive divine Presence in person.  The
  Ark type and every Marian privilege are ordered to him.
  \item Mary is a personal, believing, grace-enabled mother, not an object or
  passive container.  She receives the Word and bears him in charity.
  \item Typology is a real Catholic reading within the unity of God's economy.
  It preserves the literal narratives and the differences between type and
  fulfillment.
  \item Joseph's holy custody is obedient marital and paternal love.  He takes
  Mary as his wife, names Jesus, guards the household, and serves without
  claiming the Child's origin.
  \item The pattern reaches the Church and the Christian life by participation:
  receive Christ in faith, remain in him, worship in truth, and carry his
  charity into the world.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{studybox}{Memory formula}
The Lord gives his holy Presence, prepares a believing Mother to bear the
incarnate Son, gathers a Spirit-filled people around him, and leads creation
toward the dwelling where God and the Lamb are its sanctuary.
\end{studybox}

\subsection*{Dogma synopsis I: Divine motherhood}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Object:} Mary is truly \emph{Theotokos}, Mother of God, because the
one whom she conceived and bore according to his humanity is the eternal Son,
one divine person in two natures.\par
\textbf{Mode and authority:} Ecumenical-conciliar Christological dogma.
Ephesus received Cyril's Second Letter (431; DS 250--251); Cyril's first
anathema expresses the same consequence.  Chalcedon gives the confession its
balanced one-person, two-natures grammar (451; DS 301--302).\par
\textbf{Biblical center:} Matthew 1:18--25 names Mary's child Jesus and
Emmanuel; Luke 1:31--35, 43 identifies him as Son of God, Davidic king, Lord,
and Mary's son; Galatians 4:4 says God sent his pre-existent Son, born of a
woman; John 1:1--14 confesses that the Word who is God became flesh.  Scripture
gives the reality before it gives the later technical noun.\par
\textbf{Principal early witnesses:} Ignatius joins Mary's real virgin birth
to Christ's true flesh (\emph{Ephesians} 18--19; \emph{Smyrnaeans} 1--3).
Irenaeus teaches that the Word truly takes flesh from Mary and that her obedient
faith answers Eve (\emph{Against Heresies} III.21.10; III.22.4).  Gregory
Nazianzen makes \emph{Theotokos} a test of the Incarnation (Epistle 101).
These are stages of substance and terminology, not advance transcripts of
Ephesus.\par
\textbf{Thomistic support:} ST III, q.35, a.4 explains that maternity
terminates in the person: because the person conceived and born according to
human nature is God the Son, Mary is Mother of God.  Questions 31--32 preserve
his true derivation from Mary while distinguishing the Spirit's creative
action from fatherhood.\par
\textbf{Development hinge:} The Nestorian controversy forced the Church to
state whether Mary's child is a human personal subject beside the Word.
Ephesus received Cyril's answer; the 433 reunion and Chalcedon preserved both
personal unity and distinction of natures.\par
\textbf{Doctrinal fruit:} The title safeguards Christ's true divinity, true
humanity, and saving personal unity.  It grounds every Marian privilege in the
identity of her Son and confirms that salvation truly enters gestation, birth,
family, suffering, death, and resurrection.\par
\textbf{Required assent:} Divine and Catholic faith.\par
\textbf{Exclusions and open questions:} Mary does not originate the divine
nature, precede the Trinity, become divine, or supply a human person later
joined to the Word.  The dogma does not by itself define every later Marian
privilege.  Conciliar procedure and later historical reconstruction remain
historical questions; the defined Christological object does not.\par
\textbf{Ark light:} This is the typology's constitutive center: Mary is the
living Ark because she bears the incarnate Lord, not merely another covenant
sign.
\end{studyframe}
\endgroup

\subsection*{Dogma synopsis II: Perpetual virginity}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Object:} Mary conceived Jesus virginally by the Holy Spirit, truly
gave birth while her virginal integrity remained undiminished, and remained
ever-virgin: virgin before, in, and after the birth.\par
\textbf{Mode and authority:} Dogma of the Church's universal Tradition and
ordinary universal Magisterium, repeatedly proposed.  Constantinople II uses
\emph{Aeiparthenos} (553; DS 422, 427); Lateran 649 under Pope Martin I states
the threefold scope in canon 3 (DS 503); Vatican II and CCC 496--507 receive
it.  Lateran 649 is a Roman papal synod, not an ecumenical council.\par
\textbf{Biblical center:} Matthew 1:18--25 and Luke 1:26--38 directly attest
conception without a human father and from the Spirit; Luke 2:7 narrates a
real birth.  The full in-birth and post-birth confession is known from
Scripture received in the Church's Tradition, not from forcing one verse to
state all three moments.\par
\textbf{Principal early witnesses:} Ignatius defends Mary's virginity and
Christ's real birth against docetism.  Epiphanius uses \emph{Aeiparthenos};
Jerome answers the arguments from ``brothers,'' ``until,'' and ``firstborn''
(\emph{Against Helvidius} 5--17); Augustine confesses the Virgin conceiving,
bearing, carrying, and nursing while remaining virgin (Sermon 186.1).  Their
historical reconstructions are not all identical.\par
\textbf{Thomistic support:} ST III, q.28, aa.1--4 treats virginity before, in,
and after birth and Mary's virginal purpose through arguments of theological
fittingness.  ST III, q.29, a.2 simultaneously protects her wholly true
marriage to Joseph, grounded in consent and mutual self-gift.\par
\textbf{Development hinge:} The ancient title \emph{ever-Virgin} becomes the
explicit three-moment formula at Lateran 649 and continues through conciliar,
papal, liturgical, and catechetical confession.  The formula develops
precision without turning a later wording into a recent invention.\par
\textbf{Doctrinal fruit:} The dogma confesses God's initiative, Christ's real
and singular birth, Mary's embodied and undivided vocation, and the Church's
virginal fidelity and maternal fruitfulness.\par
\textbf{Required assent:} Divine and Catholic faith.\par
\textbf{Exclusions and open questions:} It licenses neither anatomical
speculation nor contempt for marriage, intercourse, childbirth, or bodies.
Jesus' ``brothers'' are not later children of Mary, but the Church does not
define one exclusive kinship reconstruction.  Joseph's age, prior history, and
lifelong virginity before this marriage are not separate dogmas.\par
\textbf{Ark light:} Consecrated dwelling and fruitful overshadowing support
the dogma as received typology; Uzzah neither causes nor proves the spouses'
continence.
\end{studyframe}
\endgroup

\subsection*{Dogma synopsis III: Immaculate Conception}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Object:} From the first instant of her conception, Mary was, by a
singular grace and privilege of Almighty God and in view of the merits of
Jesus Christ, preserved immune from every stain of original sin.\par
\textbf{Mode and authority:} Ex cathedra papal definition of a divinely
revealed doctrine: Pius IX, \emph{Ineffabilis Deus}, 8 December 1854, concluding
definition (DS 2803).\par
\textbf{Biblical center:} Genesis 3:15, read through Christ the New Adam and
the New Eve tradition; Luke 1:28's graced address and the Spirit-filled
blessings of Luke 1; Ephesians 1:3--10 on election and grace in Christ; and
Romans 5 on the universal need for the one Redeemer form the scriptural matrix.
No single verse states the entire 1854 formula.\par
\textbf{Principal early witnesses:} Justin contrasts Eve's disobedience with
the Virgin's believing obedience (\emph{Dialogue} 100); Irenaeus teaches that
Mary's obedience unties Eve's knot (\emph{Against Heresies} III.22.4).
Ephrem and the liturgical tradition praise extraordinary Marian holiness.
Augustine's exception for Mary in \emph{On Nature and Grace} 36.42 concerns
personal sin and grace, not an explicit first-instant doctrine.  The Fathers
supply a matrix, not verbal unanimity around the final definition.\par
\textbf{Thomistic support and limit:} ST III, q.27, a.2 places Mary's
sanctification after animation and rejects the later first-instant conclusion
because Thomas wishes to protect Christ as universal Savior.  Articles 4--5
teach preservation from actual sin and fullness of grace.  His Christological
principle endures, but he did not teach the 1854 formula.\par
\textbf{Development hinge:} Conception feasts preceded settled Latin
explanation.  Medieval dispute culminated in the logic of preservative
redemption; Sixtus IV, Trent, and Alexander VII marked stages of reception and
clarification before Pius IX defined the exact object.\par
\textbf{Doctrinal fruit:} Mary is not outside redemption but its most radical
recipient: Christ's merits save her preventively.  Grace perfects rather than
erases freedom, preparing her free fiat and maternal mission.\par
\textbf{Required assent:} Divine and Catholic faith.\par
\textbf{Exclusions and open questions:} This is Mary's conception, not Jesus'
virginal conception or a virginal conception by Anne; it does not deny her
need for Christ or define embryological mechanism.  Lifelong freedom from
personal sin is closely related authoritative teaching, not the exact 1854
object.\par
\textbf{Ark light:} Christ's prevenient grace prepares his living Ark.
Acacia, ``incorruptible wood,'' and gold are fitting images, not proofs of the
first instant.
\end{studyframe}
\endgroup

\subsection*{Dogma synopsis IV: Bodily Assumption}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Object:} The Immaculate Mother of God, the ever-Virgin Mary, having
completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul into
heavenly glory.\par
\textbf{Mode and authority:} Ex cathedra papal definition of a divinely
revealed dogma: Pius XII, \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 44, 1 November 1950
(DS 3903), following the 1946 episcopal consultation.\par
\textbf{Biblical center:} First Corinthians 15 presents Christ as firstfruits
and the bodily victory promised to those who belong to him; Romans 8:11, 23 and
Philippians 3:20--21 confess the Spirit's life-giving work and transformation
of the body.  Luke 1, Psalm 132:8, and Revelation 11:19--12:1 supply Marian,
Ark, and ecclesial fittingness, not a canonical narrative of Mary's final day.\par
\textbf{Principal early witnesses:} Scripture contains no Assumption
narrative, and Epiphanius reports uncertainty about Mary's end
(\emph{Panarion} 78.11).  Diverse \emph{Transitus} texts witness late-antique
reception but are not apostolic memoir.  Dormition liturgy and the mature
homilies of Germanus of Constantinople and John Damascene clearly confess
bodily glorification.  Their later date and narrative settings remain visible.\par
\textbf{Thomistic support and limit:} Thomas gives no dedicated proof.
\emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 31 cautiously notes occasional texts affirming
that Mary's body as well as soul is in heaven.  Thomistic principles concerning
the unity of body and soul and Christ's Resurrection illuminate the dogma under
the Church's judgment; they are not independent historical evidence.\par
\textbf{Development hinge:} Eastern Dormition and Roman Assumption worship,
medieval theological consensus, papal teaching, and Pius XII's consultation of
the bishops culminate in the 1950 definition.  Consultation witnesses to the
Church's faith; it does not create Revelation by vote.\par
\textbf{Doctrinal fruit:} Mary's glory manifests Christ's Paschal victory,
the dignity and destiny of the body, and the Church's promised consummation.
She is the pre-eminent created recipient of resurrection glory, never a rival
source of it.\par
\textbf{Required assent:} Divine and Catholic faith.\par
\textbf{Exclusions and open questions:} ``Was assumed'' names God's action,
not an Ascension by Mary's power.  The definition leaves her death, date,
place, tomb, and mechanism open; it canonizes no apocryphal narrative.
Revelation's Woman includes Israel, Church, and Mary and is not exclusively
Marian or photographic.\par
\textbf{Ark light:} This dogma has the strongest dogma-specific documentary
Ark application: Psalm 132 and the living Ark's entry into God's rest illuminate
bodily glory without replacing the definition.
\end{studyframe}
\endgroup
