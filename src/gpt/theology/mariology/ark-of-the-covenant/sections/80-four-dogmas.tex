\section{The Four Dogmas: One Living Ark}

The old Ark bore the signs of covenant presence.  Mary bore the Covenant in
person: the eternal Son who took flesh from her.  For that reason the Ark is
not a fifth Marian dogma laid beside four others.  It is a Christ-centered
scriptural, patristic, liturgical, and magisterially received pattern that
gathers the four dogmas into one luminous form.

The relations are real but unequal.  Divine motherhood is the constitutive
center of Mary's identity as living Ark, because it tells us whom she bore.
The Assumption has the most explicit dogma-specific papal application of Ark
imagery.  Perpetual virginity and the Immaculate Conception are also illuminated
by the Ark, but neither is derived from Uzzah, acacia, gold, or a single image.
Each dogma rests upon Revelation as authoritatively proposed by the Church and
requires divine and Catholic faith; their historical modes of proposal differ.

\subsection{Divine motherhood: whom she bore}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Exact object:} Mary is truly \emph{Theotokos}, Mother of God, because
the one born of her according to his humanity is the eternal Word: one divine
person, truly God and truly man.\par
\textbf{Authority and locus:} Ephesus (431), reception of Cyril's Second
Letter and first anathema (DS 250--251), and Chalcedon's Definition (451;
DS 301--302).\par
\textbf{Ark relation and strength:} Constitutive Christological center and
direct papal, catechetical, and liturgical reception; especially
\emph{Marialis cultus} 6, CCC 2676, and Benedict XVI's Assumption homily of
15 August 2011.\par
\textbf{Necessary boundary:} Mary does not originate Christ's divine nature,
become divine, or contain God as an inert object.
\end{studyframe}

The Ark contained the tablets of the covenant; Mary bore the Lawgiver.  It
held manna; she bore the living Bread.  It sheltered the memorial signs of
God's covenant; from her the Son took the humanity in which he ratified the New
Covenant in his blood.  The fulfillment exceeds the type at every point
because the treasure is not another sacred thing but the divine person of the
Son incarnate.

This is why divine motherhood governs every other Marian privilege.  Mary is
not Mother of the divine nature considered apart from Christ's humanity.  A
mother bears a person, and the person she truly bore is God the Son according
to the humanity he received from her.  The title protects the unity of Christ.
The living-Ark title protects the same center when it insists that all Marian
glory is relative to the Lord who dwells in her.

Yet Mary is more than a container.  She believes, asks, consents, conceives,
bears, nurses, journeys, ponders, suffers, and remains faithful.  Paul VI's
description of her as true Ark and true Temple begins from her free reception
of the one Mediator.  The living fulfillment is personal because the
Incarnation is personal communion, not divine use of disposable matter.

\subsection{Perpetual virginity: the undivided form of her motherhood}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Exact object:} Mary is virgin before, in, and after Christ's birth:
she conceived by the Holy Spirit without a human father; truly gave birth
without loss of virginal integrity; and remained ever-virgin.\par
\textbf{Authority and locus:} The Church's universal Tradition and ordinary
universal Magisterium; Constantinople II's title \emph{Aeiparthenos} (553;
DS 422, 427), Lateran 649 under Pope Martin I, canon 3 (DS 503), Vatican II,
\emph{Lumen gentium} 57, and CCC 496--507.  Lateran 649 was a Roman papal
synod, not an ecumenical council.\par
\textbf{Ark relation and strength:} Received, supportive, and
non-derivational.  \emph{Marialis cultus} 26 joins the Spirit's consecration
and fruitfulness of Mary's virginity to the Fathers' Ark, Temple, and dwelling
titles.\par
\textbf{Necessary boundary:} No anatomical reconstruction, contempt for
marriage or childbirth, or claim that Uzzah caused or proves Joseph's
continence.
\end{studyframe}

The shadow of the Most High that consecrates the living Ark makes her
fruitful.  Her virginity is therefore not emptiness but the Spirit-given,
embodied, lifelong form of her maternity.  It confesses divine initiative in
the conception, the singular holiness of the real birth, and an undivided
vocation after it.  The patristic closed gate, the enclosed garden, and the Ark
reserved for God's covenant service contemplate this mystery without
replacing its proper scriptural and traditional grounds.

Mary's perpetual virginity is lived within her true marriage to Joseph.  The
fullness of the Ark image is not a dangerous object kept from a frightened
man.  It is the overshadowed Virgin freely receiving the Son, and a husband
and wife freely giving themselves in marital and virginal love around him.
Grace neither despises marriage nor makes persons untouchable.  It gives this
marriage its unrepeatable form and fruit.

\subsection{The Immaculate Conception: the dwelling prepared by Christ}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Exact object:} From the first instant of her conception, Mary was, by
a singular grace and privilege of Almighty God and in view of the merits of
Jesus Christ, preserved immune from every stain of original sin.\par
\textbf{Authority and locus:} Pius IX, \emph{Ineffabilis Deus}, concluding
definition, 8 December 1854 (DS 2803), an ex cathedra definition of a divinely
revealed doctrine.\par
\textbf{Ark relation and strength:} Explicit papal theological application
and fittingness of moderate strength.  \emph{Misericordiae vultus} 24 says
that Mary was prepared from the outset by God's love to be the Ark of the
Covenant between God and humanity.\par
\textbf{Necessary boundary:} Ark materials do not prove the first-instant
definition; this is not Christ's virginal conception or a virginal conception
of Mary; Mary is saved by Christ through preservation.
\end{studyframe}

If Mary is the living Ark because she bears the all-holy Son, the Church
rightly contemplates how the Redeemer prepared his mother.  The answer is not
that she stood outside redemption.  Christ saved her more perfectly: the
merits of his Cross reached her beginning as prevenient, preservative grace.
Her holiness is wholly received, wholly Christ's victory, and ordered to her
free fiat and maternal mission.

Gold within and without, purity of dwelling, and the Greek scriptural phrase
``incorruptible wood'' can serve later Christian contemplation of integrity.
They cannot be converted into a botany lesson or an independent demonstration
of 1854.  In particular, Pius XII's use of incorruptible wood in
\emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 26 belongs explicitly to Assumption reception,
not to a proof of the Immaculate Conception.  The exact first-instant object
must come from \emph{Ineffabilis Deus}, not be smuggled into an ancient image.

\subsection{The Assumption: the living Ark enters God's rest}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Exact object:} The Immaculate Mother of God, the ever-Virgin Mary,
having completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul
into heavenly glory.\par
\textbf{Authority and locus:} Pius XII, \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 44,
1 November 1950 (DS 3903), an ex cathedra definition.\par
\textbf{Ark relation and strength:} The strongest dogma-specific documentary
Ark application.  \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 26 receives Psalm 132:8 and
the Ark of incorruptible wood as figures of Mary's incorrupt body and heavenly
glory; John Damascene and Benedict XVI explicitly contemplate the living Ark's
entry into the heavenly sanctuary.\par
\textbf{Necessary boundary:} Typology is not the definition or a solitary
proof.  Mary's death, date, place, tomb, and the mechanism remain undefined;
she is assumed by God's power and does not ascend by her own.
\end{studyframe}

The Ark's long road sought rest before the Lord.  Psalm 132 sings, ``Arise,
O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your might.''  In the Church's
Dormition and Assumption reception, the image reaches its eschatological
horizon.  The mother who bore the risen Lord is brought, body and soul, into
his glory.  She is not the source of resurrection; she is its pre-eminent
created recipient, a sign of what Christ promises to his whole Body.

Revelation 11:19--12:1 strengthens this canonical horizon by placing the
heavenly Ark beside the woman clothed with the sun.  The vision should not be
flattened into a photograph or an exclusively Marian code.  The woman bears
Israel, the Church, and Mary within the sign's polyvalent ecclesial and
messianic depth.  Read in the Church's faith, Mary appears personally within
that horizon without exhausting it.

Nor does the definition settle the manner in which Mary's earthly life ended.
The ancient Dormition tradition and many Catholic teachers hold that she died;
the words ``having completed the course of her earthly life'' deliberately do
not define that question.  What is defined is bodily consummation in heavenly
glory through the risen Christ.

\subsection{One vocation, four dogmas}

The fourfold synthesis can now be said without hesitation.  Divine motherhood
tells us whom the living Ark bore.  Perpetual virginity names the Spirit-given,
undivided form of her maternal vocation.  The Immaculate Conception names
Christ's prevenient, preservative preparation of his mother.  The Assumption
names the bodily glory to which his Paschal victory brought her.  Beginning,
vocation, maternity, and consummation are gathered around one center: Jesus
Christ.

This synthesis does not make the four definitions interchangeable or pretend
that a single image mechanically proves each one.  It reveals their organic
coherence.  The all-holy Son prepares his mother, takes true flesh from her,
consecrates her whole vocation, and brings her bodily into the glory he won.
Mary remains a creature at every point.  Her preservation, motherhood,
virginity, and glorification are gifts of the Father through the Son in the
Holy Spirit.

The final order is therefore doxological.  Christ is the Covenant, the divine
Presence, the one Mediator, the High Priest, and the cause of salvation.  Mary
is his uniquely graced mother and living Ark.  Joseph serves with her in the
covenantal household.  The Church receives Christ, confesses him, and awaits
the glory already displayed in his mother.  The more clearly the type leads to
Christ, the more fully it can disclose what grace has accomplished in Mary.
