The map is a confession before it is a reconstruction.  The Lord who chose to
make his presence known above the mercy seat truly led his people; the same
Lord truly took flesh from Mary and was borne by her into Judah's hill
country.  The drawing therefore distinguishes what Scripture locates from
what no surviving source permits us to locate.  That restraint lets the
providential correspondence stand in its own strength instead of making it
depend on an invented road.

\textbf{Line key.}
Solid arrows mark narrated movement or departure; dashed arrows mark an
inferred endpoint, departure, narrative continuity, or accompaniment; dotted lines connect attested places when
the intervening leg is not narrated; the wide gray corridor marks a narrated
destination with its road and intervening events unlocated; and the white-cored
double route marks Mary's inferred Nazareth--unnamed-Judah-region movement.
The map follows Ekron in the Hebrew/Vulgate/Douay branch and prints the
Brenton-Septuagint Ashkelon difference here rather than drawing a false second
road.

\textbf{Figure: The journey of the Ark and its New-Covenant fulfillment.}
The dark route follows stations Scripture narrates for the Ark; dashed and
dotted marks preserve movements or transitions whose exact geography is not
recoverable.  The white-cored double route follows Mary from Nazareth into
Judah's hill country, where Luke leaves the town unnamed; Ein Karem is marked
separately as the received Christian traditional identification.  The
connectors join narrative stations and do not claim recovered ancient roads.
Archaeological anchors locate sites, not the sacred object, a house, or an
event within them.  The earthly Ark route ends at Solomon's Temple: later
Scripture gives a final attestation, prophecy, a canonical concealment
tradition whose place is said to remain unknown, and a heavenly vision rather
than a recoverable onward itinerary.

\begin{studybox}{The correspondences the map is meant to reveal}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The glory overshadows the tabernacle; the Holy Spirit overshadows
        Mary (Ex 40:34--35; Lk 1:35).
  \item David arises and goes to Baale-Judah to bring up the Ark; Mary arises
        and goes in haste into Judah's hill country (2 Sm 6:2; Lk 1:39).
  \item David asks how the Ark of the Lord can come to him; Elizabeth asks
        why the mother of her Lord should come to her (2 Sm 6:9; Lk 1:43).
  \item David dances before the Lord; John leaps for joy before the Lord whom
        Mary bears (2 Sm 6:14--16; Lk 1:41, 44).
  \item The Ark remains in a household for three months and brings blessing;
        Mary remains with Elizabeth about three months while the household
        receives the hidden Messiah (2 Sm 6:11; Lk 1:56).
\end{itemize}
These are canonical and received Catholic correspondences.  They identify
Mary as the living Ark because of Christ within her; they do not claim that
the two journeys followed the same road.
\end{studybox}
