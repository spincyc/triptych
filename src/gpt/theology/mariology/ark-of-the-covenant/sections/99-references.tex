\subsection{Councils, Magisterium, and Catechism}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Council of Ephesus (431), St.~Cyril of Alexandria's Second and Third Letters to Nestorius and the first anathema: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3810.htm}{working English conciliar text}.  The divine-motherhood chapter uses the received conciliar Christological confession, not a free-standing slogan.
  \item Council of Chalcedon (451), Definition of Faith, on the one and the same Son born before the ages according to divinity and, in the last days, of Mary the Virgin Theotokos according to humanity: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3811.htm}{working English conciliar text}.
  \item Second Council of Constantinople (553), especially the conciliar confession and anathemas that name the holy, glorious, ever-Virgin Mary as Theotokos; critical loci DS 422 and 427.  This ancient universal confession forms part of the doctrinal control for perpetual virginity.
  \item Lateran Synod of 649, canon 3, under Pope Martin I, on Mary's virginal conception, birth without corruption, and enduring virginity; critical locus DS 503.  The study does not turn the conciliar formula into an anatomical reconstruction.
  \item Sixtus IV, \emph{Cum praeexcelsa} (1476) and \emph{Grave nimis} (1483); Council of Trent, Session V, Decree on Original Sin, §6 (17 June 1546; DS 1516); and Alexander VII, \emph{Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum} (1661).  These control the Conception feast's authorized reception, the prohibition of premature mutual heresy charges, Trent's deliberate Marian exception and renewal of Sixtus IV, and the later clarification of the first-instant object.  They are stages toward, not substitutes for, the 1854 definition; Pius IX's historical survey in \emph{Ineffabilis Deus} supplies the principal documentary orientation.  No exact day is asserted for \emph{Cum praeexcelsa}.
  \item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Dei Verbum}, especially 11--12, on inspiration, truth, literary attention, canonical unity, living Tradition, and the analogy of faith: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html}{Holy See text}.
  \item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Lumen gentium}, chapter VIII, especially 52--69: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html}{Holy See text}.  This is the broad conciliar control for the study's Christological, ecclesial, and Marian synthesis.
  \item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Gaudium et spes} 48--49, on the dignity, covenantal reality, mutual self-gift, and embodied love of marriage: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html}{Holy See text}.   \item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church} 2676, on Daughter Zion in person, Ark of the Covenant, and dwelling of the Lord's glory: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_four/section_one/chapter_two/article_2/the_way_of_prayer.html}{Holy See text}.

These are early anti-docetic witnesses to Mary's real virgin conception and Christ's real birth and flesh, not the later three-moment formula or an advance transcript of Ephesus.
  \item St.~Justin Martyr, \emph{Dialogue with Trypho} 100.5--6, on the Virgin's believing obedience against Eve's disobedience; and St.~Irenaeus of Lyons, \emph{Against Heresies} III.21.10 and III.22.4, on the Word's true humanity from Mary and the New-Eve recapitulation.  Bibliographic critical edition for Irenaeus, book III: Adelin Rousseau and Louis Doutreleau, eds., SC 210--211 (Paris, 1974).  These loci supply an early doctrinal matrix, not the later conciliar or 1854 formulas verbatim.
  \item St.~Gregory Nazianzen, Epistle 101 to Cledonius, especially the \emph{Theotokos} test within his anti-Apollinarian defense of the Incarnation; bibliographic critical edition: Paul Gallay, ed., \emph{Lettres théologiques}, SC 208 (Paris, 1974).  The study paraphrases the argument; critical Greek/translation comparison remains pending, so no verbatim quotation depends on it.
  \item St.~Ephrem the Syrian, \emph{Hymns on the Nativity} 4.112--13 and 16.16--17; Edmund Beck, ed. and trans., \emph{Des heiligen Ephraem des Syrers Hymnen de Nativitate}, CSCO 186--187 (Louvain, 1959).  The genuine Syriac passages govern the bounded priest-before-Ark and pure-tablet images; later Greek Ephremiana are not substituted.
  \item St.~Epiphanius of Salamis, \emph{De mensuris et ponderibus / On Weights and Measures} §35, CPG 3746; James Elmer Dean, \emph{The Syriac Version of Epiphanius' Treatise on Weights and Measures}, Studies in Ancient Oriental Civilization 11 (Chicago, 1935), 52--54, Syriac fols.~65b--65d: \sourceurl{https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Epiphanius_Treatise_Weights_Measures.pdf}{working edition and translation; rights status unresolved}.  The relevant complete passage is cited as preserved in Syriac, not as checked Greek; the study publishes independently worded paraphrase rather than Dean's English.
  \item St.~Epiphanius of Salamis, \emph{Ancoratus} 119.5, as a witness to the received \emph{ever-Virgin} title, and \emph{Panarion} 78.11, as a witness to ancient uncertainty about Mary's earthly end.  The latter limits historical reconstruction; it is not evidence against the later Assumption definition, and disputed death-language quotations are not used.

  \item St.~Proclus of Constantinople, \emph{Homily 5 on the Theotokos}, CPG 5804, especially §3; PG 65.716--721; Nicholas Constas, \emph{Proclus of Constantinople and the Cult of the Virgin in Late Antiquity} (Leiden: Brill, 2003), 245--264.  The homily's authenticity is distinguished from Pseudo-Proclus material.

  \item St.~Germanus of Constantinople, \emph{Dormition Sermon I}, received in Pius XII, \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 22--23.  It is used as a mature seventh-/eighth-century Dormition witness to bodily glorification and incorruption, not as apostolic memoir or as the source of the 1950 definition.
  \item St.~John Damascene, \emph{Three Homilies on the Dormition}, CPG 8061--8063, especially Homily II §§2 and 12--13; Bonifatius Kotter, ed., \emph{Die Schriften des Johannes von Damaskos}, vol.~5, PTS 29 (Berlin, 1988), 461--555; Mary H. Allies, trans., \emph{St.~John Damascene on Holy Images, Followed by Three Sermons on the Assumption} (London, 1898), a public-domain working translation.
  \item St.~Ambrose of Milan, \emph{Exposition of the Gospel according to Luke} II.2 and II.5, CCSL 14, on the Visitation and on the spousal title as witness to true marriage without surrender of virginity. Exact uses are bounded by the patristic audit and are not made to prove a word-for-word Ark identification Ambrose does not state.

  \item St.~Augustine, \emph{Sermon} 186.1, PL 38:999, on the Virgin conceiving, bearing, carrying, and nursing while remaining virgin; and \emph{On Nature and Grace} 36.42, on excepting Mary from the discussion of personal sin because of the Lord's honor and superabundant grace.   \item St.~Bernard of Clairvaux, \emph{In Praise of the Virgin Mother / Super Missus est} II.16, \emph{Sancti Bernardi Opera} IV, 33--34, on Joseph's affectionate custody of Christ.
  \item St.~Thomas Aquinas, \emph{Summa theologiae} III, q.29, a.2, on the true marriage of Mary and Joseph: \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth4027.html}{Corpus Thomisticum Latin text}.

Bougerol (1993), 517--530.  The richer \emph{De Nativitate BMV}, Sermo V, q.~IX, Quaracchi IX, 715a--719a, is cited only as a thirteenth-century Marian sermon printed in the older Bonaventure corpus because modern critical work did not retain it as secure Bonaventure.
