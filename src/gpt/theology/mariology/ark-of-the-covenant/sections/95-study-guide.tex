\section{A Six-Session Study Guide}

This guide serves either a group or an individual reader.  Each session is
designed for approximately forty-five to sixty minutes: begin with the named
Scripture, read the corresponding portion of the study, close the book for the
brief recall, and then reopen it to correct or deepen the answer.  The recall
is not an examination of faith or holiness.  Its purpose is to keep the route,
the controlling texts, and the limits of each comparison available for
contemplation.

For group use, one reader may summarize the biblical movement before the
discussion.  For self-study, write no more than a sentence for each prompt.
In either form, the recurring actions are the same: \textbf{observe} what the
text narrates, \textbf{recall} its consequential details, \textbf{compare} the
canonical pattern, \textbf{distinguish} warrant and limit, \textbf{contemplate}
the Catholic truth disclosed, and \textbf{live} that truth in communion with
Christ.

The final question in each session may be kept private.  No facilitator should
ask participants to disclose sins, trauma, marital details, or private
spiritual experience.  A reader may name a general Christian application or
form a silent resolution without reporting it to the group.

\subsection{Session One: A dwelling made for the Presence}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} Exodus 25:10--22; 37:1--9; 40:34--38;
Numbers 10:33--36; Joshua 3:7--17; 6:1--20.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} What does the Ark signify, and which details of its
  construction and appointed movement keep it from being treated as an idol
  or charm?
  \item \textbf{Recall.} Without looking, name the three movements from Sinai
  to the entry into the land considered in this session.
  \item \textbf{Compare.} How do the cloud, the crossing, and the fall of
  Jericho reveal divine initiative rather than human control of sacred power?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} Why is later Christian meditation on acacia wood a
  fittingness rather than an independent proof of a Marian dogma?
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Reread Exodus 40:34--38 in silence.  Attend to
  the Lord who freely makes his dwelling and directs both departure and rest.
  \item \textbf{Live.} Identify one place where religious practice is being
  used to secure control rather than to receive and obey God.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Check the recall against} Exod 40:34--38; Num 10:33--36; Josh 3--6.
The controlling truth is that the Lord gives and leads; Israel does not
activate him by carrying the Ark.

\subsection{Session Two: Defeat, exile, and the Lord's freedom}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} 1 Samuel 4:1--7:2.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} Trace the Ark from the battlefield through the
  Philistine cities to Beth-shemesh and Kiriath-jearim.
  \item \textbf{Recall.} What two false conclusions are overturned by the
  capture and the fall of Dagon?
  \item \textbf{Compare.} How can the same Presence expose Israel's
  presumption, judge Philistia's gods, and bless those who receive the Ark
  rightly?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} Explain why holiness includes judgment without
  making God a rival deity confined inside the Ark.
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Reread 1 Samuel 5:1--5 in silence.  Let the
  Lord's freedom correct every attempt to reduce him to a possession.
  \item \textbf{Live.} Where does repentance require surrendering a religious
  image of success, prestige, or immunity from suffering?
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Check the recall against} 1 Sam 4:3--11 and 5:1--5.  Israel's defeat
does not defeat the Lord; Dagon's fall does not excuse Israel's misuse of the
gift.

\subsection{Session Three: Fear, blessing, dance, and Zion}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} 2 Samuel 6; 1 Chronicles 13; 15:1--16:6;
1 Kings 8:1--13; Psalm 132.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} What changes between the first and second attempts
  to bring the Ark to Zion?
  \item \textbf{Recall.} Complete the sequence after the breach at
  Perez-uzzah: whose house holds the Ark for three months, and at what city
  does the renewed procession arrive?
  \item \textbf{Compare.} Hold together David's fear, Obed-edom's blessing,
  David's joy, and the Temple cloud.  What fuller account of reverence emerges?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} Why must Samuel and Chronicles be allowed to supply
  their distinct emphases rather than being blended into an invented account?
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Reread 2 Samuel 6:12--15.  Hold obedient
  reverence and joy together before the Lord.
  \item \textbf{Live.} Name one concrete way reverence can become more
  obedient and more joyful at the same time.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Recall key:} Obed-edom; Zion or the City of David.  The appointed
service of the Levites in 1 Chronicles 15 clarifies the recovered procession.

\subsection{Session Four: The Mother of the Lord in the hill country}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} Luke 1:26--56; then reread Exodus 40:34--35 and
2 Samuel 6:2--16.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} List the Visitation correspondences before consulting
  the study's comparison: journey into Judah's hill country, wonder before the
  Lord's coming, leaping joy, blessing, and the three-month stay.
  \item \textbf{Recall.} What does the overshadowing language confess first
  about divine initiative and the identity of Mary's child?
  \item \textbf{Compare.} Which correspondences are verbal, which are
  narrative, and which belong to the Church's theological reception?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} Explain this sentence: Mary is Ark because she bears
  the incarnate Presence, and the type serves Christology.
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Reread Luke 1:35, 43--45 in silence.  Adore the
  hidden Lord and honor the singular grace given to his Mother.
  \item \textbf{Live.} How does Mary's movement from receiving the Word to
  serving Elizabeth distinguish apostolic charity from restless activism?
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Check the comparison against} Luke 1:35, 39--45, 56; Exod 40:34--35;
2 Sam 6:2, 9, 11, 14--16.  Similar sequence is not the same thing as identity
of every Greek verb.

\subsection{Session Five: The Fathers and the four dogmas}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} Luke 1:26--45; John 1:1--14; Galatians 4:4--7;
1 Corinthians 15:20--28, 42--49; Revelation 11:19--12:6.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} Choose one authenticated patristic or liturgical Ark
  witness from the study.  What does it actually say, and what later conclusion
  does it not yet formulate?
  \item \textbf{Recall.} Name the four Marian dogmas and state the exact object
  of each in one sentence.
  \item \textbf{Compare.} Which relation to the Ark is strongest for divine
  motherhood, and why are the relations to virginity, the Immaculate
  Conception, and the Assumption real but unequal?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} Why does typology illuminate the coherence of dogma
  without becoming the formal ground of faith?
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Choose one dogma and remain with the truth it
  protects about Christ before turning to its personal fruit.
  \item \textbf{Live.} Which dogma most clearly calls you to wonder at what
  grace in Christ accomplishes in a creature, and what truth about Christ does
  it protect?
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Recall key:} divine motherhood---Ephesus and Chalcedon; perpetual
virginity---Lateran 649 within the Church's ancient universal confession;
Immaculate Conception---Pius IX's \emph{Ineffabilis Deus}; bodily
Assumption---Pius XII's \emph{Munificentissimus Deus}.  Their controlling
objects and authorities, not the vividness of an analogy, govern the synthesis.

\subsection{Session Six: Holy custody, interior life, and mission}

\textbf{Read in Scripture:} Matthew 1:18--25; 2:13--23; Luke 1:38--56;
1 Corinthians 3:16--17; Ephesians 2:19--22; Revelation 21:1--4, 22.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Observe.} List Joseph's positive acts of marital and paternal
  service.  What distinguishes his vocation from Uzzah's act?
  \item \textbf{Recall.} Reconstruct the study's spiritual movement in four
  verbs.  What fourth verb follows receive, contemplate, and carry?
  \item \textbf{Compare.} How is Mary's unique divine maternity related to,
  but never repeated by, the Church's reception and communication of Christ?
  \item \textbf{Distinguish.} How do indwelling, sacramental communion, holiness,
  and charity preserve the difference between Creator and creature while
  establishing real participation by grace?
  \item \textbf{Contemplate.} Reread Galatians 4:4--7 or Revelation 21:1--4
  in silence.  Receive adoption and resurrection hope as gifts of the triune
  God in Christ.
  \item \textbf{Live.} Choose one modest act of worship, repentance, service,
  or fidelity that answers a truth received in this study.  Let the act follow
  from the truth rather than substitute for it.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Recall key:} serve.  The pattern is not a ladder by which activity
earns divine presence.  Christ acts first; contemplation receives his gift;
charity becomes its grace-enabled fruit.

\begin{studybox}{A final return to the map}
Close the study and trace the journey from Sinai to Zion in memory.  Then name
the Annunciation and Visitation correspondences, the four dogmas, and Joseph's
positive vocation.  Reopen the synopsis and correct the route.  End not with
the number remembered, but with the one governing confession: the glory toward
which every true correspondence points is the incarnate, crucified, risen, and
returning Lord.
\end{studybox}
