# Ark Journey Narrative Audit

## Purpose and status

This focused audit controls the literal Ark narrative in:

- `sections/00-prologue.tex`;
- `sections/10-fashioned-for-presence.tex`;
- `sections/20-ark-goes-before.tex`;
- `sections/30-glory-in-captivity.tex`;
- `sections/40-fear-blessing-dance.tex`; and
- `sections/45-rest-hiddenness.tex`.

It records the exact biblical loci, the difference between explicit movement
and inference, material textual and translation branches, claims deliberately
withheld, and the remaining source work. The audit was checked through
2026-08-15. It supplements rather than replaces `research/source-audit.md` and
`research/source-bindings.toml`.

The narrative is written from a faithful Catholic standpoint. Variant and
geographic controls protect the truth of the inspired witnesses; they do not
presume against divine inspiration, miracle, providence, canonical unity,
typology, Tradition, or Marian doctrine.

## Material textual and translation controls

### Exodus numbering and order

Reader citations use modern/Hebrew numbering. Douay/Vulgate numbering for the
installation and cloud in Exodus 40 differs; their 40:15, 18–19 and 32–36
correspond to modern 40:17, 20–21 and 34–38. Brenton's reordered Exodus places
some construction material at different verse labels. References should name
the edition whenever wording or numbering, rather than only the event, matters.

The Hebrew timber term is commonly translated acacia or retained as
shittim/setim. Swete's Septuagint reads *xula asepta*, “incorruptible” or
non-decaying wood. The publication treats this as a translation and reception
datum, not a botanical claim or an independent proof of the Immaculate
Conception or Assumption.

Swete's Exodus uses the *episkiazō* lemma for the cloud overshadowing the Tent;
Luke 1:35 uses the same lemma in another inflected form. The verb is neither
exclusive to Ark contexts nor applied directly to the Ark in Exodus.

### The Ark's contents

- Tablets are explicitly inside: Exod 25:16, 21; 40:20; Deut 10:1–5.
- Manna is placed before the Lord/Testimony: Exod 16:32–34.
- Aaron's staff is placed and returned before the Testimony: Num 17:1–11.
- The Torah scroll is beside the Ark: Deut 31:24–26.
- At the Temple dedication only the tablets are said to remain inside:
  1 Kgs 8:9; 2 Chr 5:10.
- Hebrews retrospectively describes the manna vessel, staff, and tablets in
  relation to the Ark as interior contents: Heb 9:3–5.

No undocumented entry, removal, second Ark, or temporary Ark was created to
harmonize these witnesses. Deuteronomy's Mosaic first-person recollection and
Exodus' naming of Bezalel are both retained.

### Joshua and Judges

The Ebal–Gerizim unit follows Ai in the Masoretic/Douay sequence, appears after
Josh 9:2 in the Old Greek sequence, and is placed earlier by 4QJosha. The
publication uses the scene's theological and narrative content without making
its literary position an exact road chronology.

Judges 20 has a Bethel/Shiloh branch. The publication states the variant rather
than manufacturing two stops or selecting one silently.

### Philistia and Beth-shemesh

The stable Hebrew/Vulgate route is Ashdod–Gath–Ekron. Registered Brenton gives
Ashdod–Gath–Ashkelon. Gaza occurs only in the five-lord offering inventory.
Ashkelon is therefore not an Ark stop in the Hebrew/Vulgate line, but it is a
narrated stop in the registered Greek translation branch.

The casualty number and immediate offense in 1 Sam 6:19 vary materially across
witnesses and translations. Reader prose states only the stable narrative
minimum: judgment falls, the people mourn, and they ask who can stand before
the holy Lord.

### Davidic procession

The threshing-floor name varies among Nacon/Nachon, Nodan, Nachor, and Chidon.
No precise site is claimed. Uzzah's motive is not supplied. Chronicles governs
the interpretation of the corrected procession through appointed Levitical
bearing, sanctification, shoulder carriage, and poles (1 Chr 15:2, 11–15).

Samuel's sacrifice after six paces and Chronicles' seven bulls and seven rams
are retained as separate account details. The publication does not infer a
sacrifice after every six steps. David is described as wearing a linen ephod;
Michal's accusation is not turned into a narrator's claim of nudity.

## Geography and cartographic controls

- The work maps narrative geography, not a reconstructed road network.
- Exact Ark presence is not extended to every wilderness station.
- Aphek, Ai, Gaza, and Gibeon in the David/Solomon period are not Ark stations.
- Gilgal is a direct Israelite camp and an inferred Ark setting, not a recovered
  Ark installation.
- The Ark stands between the Ebal–Gerizim assembly groups, not on either summit.
- Ebenezer's battle site is not equated with Samuel's later memorial stone in
  1 Sam 7:12.
- The Kiriath-jearim–Jerusalem road, the threshing floor, Perez-uzzah,
  Obed-edom's house, and David's tent are not precisely located.
- “Gittite” does not locate Obed-edom's house at Philistine Gath.
- The possible Rabbah notice and Absalom episode do not justify invented field
  or Mount of Olives coordinates.
- No route to Babylon is drawn.
- The mountain and cave in 2 Macc 2 are not assigned recoverable coordinates.
- Revelation is shown, if at all, as a canonical/heavenly horizon rather than a
  geographic station.

## Catholic and Marian interpretive controls

1. The Ark is a God-given covenant meeting-place and throne-sign; God is not
   contained by it (Exod 25:22; 1 Kgs 8:27).
2. Christ is the incarnate Son and mediator of the New Covenant, not another
   sacred object. Mary is the living Ark because she personally and maternally
   bears him.
3. The old journey is narrated literally before Marian fulfillment is drawn.
   Luke never explicitly calls Mary “Ark”; the Church receives the cumulative
   canonical pattern.
4. Acacia/incorruptible wood is later typological fittingness, not direct
   scriptural proof of any Marian dogma.
5. David's dance and John's leap are a strong narrative-image correspondence.
   The standard Greek texts do not use the same verb.
6. Uzzah's death concerns sacred handling contrary to appointed cultic order.
   It neither condemns marriage nor makes Joseph a second Uzzah. A bounded
   project contrast may illuminate unappointed handling versus commanded
   reception; it does not explain Joseph's continence. Joseph's vocation is
   personal, marital, virginal guardianship in a true marriage.
7. The three months with Obed-edom and Mary's approximately three months with
   Elizabeth are exact narrative-duration correspondences within a larger
   cumulative pattern.
8. The Ark–Woman sequence in Revelation supports Marian contemplation within
   the Woman's Israel and Church horizons; it is not an exclusive photographic
   proof.

## Withheld dramatic details

The publication uses only action and sensory details supplied by Scripture:
cloud and fire, river at flood, feet in water, silence and trumpets, the earth-
shaking camp shout, the fallen idol, the lowing cows, the wheat harvest, the
stumbling oxen, sacrifice, music, dance, cloud-filled Temple, and the heavenly
storm. It does not invent weather, dust, facial expression, glow from the Ark,
audible speech from the chest, dialogue, motives, roads, or coordinates.

## Remaining source and review gaps

1. Before any verbatim Greek is printed, collate the relevant Swete Exodus
   passages against page images or another identified critical Greek edition;
   the registered remote OCR is a finding aid. The owned narrative paraphrases
   rather than prints the Greek.
2. Publication bindings should include the exact registered Douay or other
   controlling artifacts for 2 Chr 5 and 35, Jer 3 and 52, and 2 Macc 2.
3. If 2 Sam 11:11 and 15:23–29 remain in reader prose, expand the 2 Samuel
   binding beyond chapter 6.
4. Because the Bethel/Shiloh and Ekron/Ashkelon branches are reader-facing,
   bind the exact Brenton and Clementine/Douay witnesses used for those
   comparisons.
5. The official Catholic comparison for the difficult 1 Sam 6:19 wording should
   be recorded in the general source audit if it is used to support more than
   the stable minimum retained here.
6. The map's site identifications, coordinates, route grammar, and pencil-art
   provenance are owned by `research/map-provenance.md` and require their own
   visual review; this audit controls only their biblical station status.
7. No authenticated patristic source equating Joseph with Uzzah was identified.
   The publication therefore labels the ordered-custody contrast as project
   synthesis rather than patristic testimony.
8. The Ark's post-Josiah earthly fate remains unresolved by the inspected
   historical narratives. This is a substantive source boundary, not a prompt
   to speculate.

The final narrative was reconciled to this matrix before the 42-page PDF and
web edition were built, visually reviewed, and installed on 2026-08-16. The
source boundaries above remain substantive qualifications, not uncompleted
production work: the reader prints no uncollated verbatim Greek, invents no
missing route, and claims no external specialist or ecclesiastical approval.
