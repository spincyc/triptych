## Controlling thesis

Christ is the Covenant and definitive divine Presence in person. The old Ark
reaches its living New-Covenant fulfillment in Mary because the eternal Son
takes flesh in her and is borne by her. Divine motherhood names whom she bears;
perpetual virginity names the Spirit-given and undivided form of her motherhood;
the Immaculate Conception names Christ's prevenient preparation of his mother;
and the Assumption names her bodily consummation in his Paschal glory. These
truths are received from Revelation as authoritatively proposed by the Church;
the Ark typology gathers and illuminates them rather than replacing their
proper grounds.

- Joseph and Mary's true, mutually given virginal marriage within a positive,

  embodied Presence, with Joseph's Davidic sonship, obedient reception,

## Governing distinctions

The work distinguishes literal narrative, canonical typology, patristic or
liturgical reception, magisterial teaching, later theological fittingness,
project synthesis, and spiritual application. These distinctions protect a
confident Catholic reading from false attribution and brittle overstatement;
they do not impose a secular presumption against miracle, providence, canonical
unity, typology, Tradition, or Marian doctrine.

Joseph is not presented as a second Uzzah. Uzzah's death concerns the Ark's
handling contrary to the appointed cultic order. Joseph is commanded to take
Mary as his wife and receives a positive vocation of marital and virginal love.
The publication's positive, bounded pastoral synthesis concerns ordered,
commanded reception: holy Presence is welcomed, named, protected, and served
in a real covenantal household. This is not a strict Ark typology, the literal
sense of Uzzah's episode, or a claim that Joseph fulfills Uzzah.

## Exclusions and limits

The publication will not invent biblical roads, event coordinates, dialogue,
motives, patristic unanimity, a post-Temple Ark itinerary, a direct scriptural
proof of every dogma, or a Father who equates Joseph with Uzzah. It will not
make Mary divine, reduce her to a container, imply that marriage or conjugal
union is unclean, or treat geography and literary correspondence as the same
kind of evidence. It supplies no ecclesiastical approval or independent human
specialist review.
