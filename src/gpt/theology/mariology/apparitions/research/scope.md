# Marian Apparitions in Catholic Ecclesial Judgment — Research Scope

## Identity and governing question

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/apparitions/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** historical, doctrinal, and authority-qualified Mariological reference work; `guidance/theology/mariology.md` governs. The universal editorial standard remains controlling; the general articles profile is not the document profile.
- **Question:** Which Marian apparition claims have received what kind of positive Catholic ecclesial judgment, how far does each judgment extend, what messages are actually within its documentary scope, and how should the cases be interpreted together without confusing private revelation with the definitive Revelation in Christ?
- **Intended reader:** a serious lay reader who needs an authority-qualified map rather than a devotional internet list.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-13. Current DDF judgments, diocesan acts, and doctrinal norms were checked through this date.
- **Primary thesis:** “Approved Marian apparition” is not one canonical status. A responsible corpus must distinguish a judgment about the alleged event, a judgment that testimony is credible or visions are worthy of belief, authorization of cult or devotion, a post-2024 `nihil obstat`, papal or liturgical reception, precautionary status, and a negative determination. No private revelation completes Christ, binds the faithful with divine and Catholic faith, or licenses a message outside the exact scope examined by competent authority.

## Corpus rule

The study uses four affirmative or reception tiers and one boundary tier.

1. **Selected legacy event judgments and qualified positive receptions:** fifteen high-confidence cases individually checked against a competent act or the closest authoritative diocesan or shrine record located. A 2020 Vatican News survey supplied a historical candidate baseline, not an official register or sufficient authority. Amsterdam was removed after the DDF disclosed its controlling negative act; San Nicolás was added on the independent strength of its 2016 decree. The retained cases are Laus; Alphonse Ratisbonne at Sant'Andrea delle Fratte; La Salette; Lourdes; Champion; Pontmain; Gietrzwałd; Knock; Fátima; Beauraing; Banneux; Akita; Betania; Kibeho; and San Nicolás. These are not assigned one identical legal formula. Knock, Gietrzwałd, and Akita receive conspicuous qualifications.
2. **Post-2024 `nihil obstat` Marian experiences:** Placanica, Montichiari, Chandavila, Pellevoisin, Medjugorje, and Litmanová. These are included because the DDF authorized positive pastoral reception under the 2024 Norms. They are not called authenticated supernatural apparitions.
3. **Ancient, medieval, and major received traditions without a located modern positive fact decree:** the Pillar at Zaragoza, Our Lady of the Snows / Saint Mary Major, Walsingham, Mount Carmel / Simon Stock, Guadalupe, Rue du Bac / the Miraculous Medal, and Vailankanni. Their papal, liturgical, sacramental, shrine, or devotional reception is documented without inventing a canonical act. Their provenance is graded: the official source's own words “tradition,” “dream,” or chronological qualification are retained.
4. **Ambiguous positive reception:** Cuapa is treated as a documented diocesan welcome whose surviving text does not justify an unqualified claim of a formal `constat de supernaturalitate`.
5. **Boundaries and exclusions:** Amsterdam is negative; Mount S. Onofrio is `prae oculis habeatur`; representative unresolved, negative, non-Catholic, or merely tolerated cases are named only to prevent false inclusion. A controlling act is required for an assigned status; a case included only because no positive act was located must instead carry a dated, reproducible negative-search record and may not be called negative on that basis.
6. **Bounded index of moderately renowned claims:** an appendix consolidates the core cases and adds fifteen index-only cases whose status is commonly confused, including the early and nationally received traditions of Šiluva, Coromoto, La Vang, and Licheń. “Moderate renown” is operational, not intuitive: a row must satisfy at least two of four documented signals—sustained national/international shrine or devotional reception; a public competent judgment or repeated intervention; treatment in a Holy See, episcopal, or national-liturgical source; or recurrent public confusion serious enough to prompt authoritative correction. The table is not a census of every local or internet claim. Boldface marks only a located positive Catholic judgment about the alleged event itself; it excludes a shrine, feast, title, prayer, sacramental, cult, pilgrimage, cure, or post-2024 `nihil obstat` standing alone.

No claim is made that the Catholic Church maintains a universal master registry. The DDF stated in 2024 that no more than six cases since 1950 had been officially resolved, while most major shrines never received a declaration of supernatural origin. Published lists vary because they use different meanings of “recognized.” This is therefore a selected authority-qualified legacy and received-tradition corpus supplemented by the complete set of positive Marian determinations located in the DDF index from the 2024 Norms through the as-of date and by a reproducibly bounded, broader status index. It is not an exhaustive register of every historical diocesan judgment, Marian legend, shrine, locution, image, dream, miracle, or local tradition in Christian history.

## Authority and terminology

- **Public Revelation:** Sacred Scripture and apostolic Tradition, fulfilled in Jesus Christ; normative and binding.
- **Dogma and authoritative teaching:** the finality of public Revelation; Christ's unique mediation and redemption; Mary's divine motherhood, holiness, maternal intercession, and subordinate cooperation; the Church's authority and duty to discern charisms.
- **Ecclesiastical administrative or prudential judgment:** the exact local or Holy See determination on an alleged phenomenon. Such acts are quoted or closely paraphrased and are not upgraded in rank.
- **Historical witness:** official shrine chronologies, diocesan records, and contemporary documents establish event history and reception, not divine origin by themselves.
- **Project synthesis:** the cross-case thematic comparison and historical interpretation are source-grounded editorial synthesis.

The study normally says “reported message,” “alleged apparition,” or “message within the recognized scope.” It uses direct declarative language only when describing what a competent act itself judges, and even then states the act's non-infallible and non-obligatory character.

## Material limitations

1. The original decrees for every early case are not uniformly available online. When an official diocesan or shrine page transcribes the formula, that is identified. Where only an authoritative summary is available, the study does not pretend to have inspected an unseen original.
2. A positive judgment about an event does not authenticate every alleged message, later secret, later seer, later appearance, translation, miracle claim, or interpretation. Kibeho and Betania make this limitation explicit and govern the editorial method elsewhere.
3. Akita is not described as “Vatican approved” or given an unqualified positive label. The current Bishop of Niigata states that the Holy See has issued no judgment and that the operative 1984 provisions are an ambiguous double-negative finding plus diocesan non-prohibition pending Rome.
4. Knock is not described as a modern `constat`. Its documented basis is two favorable witness commissions, the archbishop's reception, and sustained authoritative ecclesial reception.
5. Guadalupe is not demoted by the absence of a located modern fact decree; it is classified by the different form of its exceptionally strong papal and continental liturgical reception.
6. The study does not repeat unsourced claims that Kibeho “predicted the genocide,” that every later La Salette secret was approved, that every San Nicolás text online falls within the decree, that Ratzinger privately approved Akita, or that a papal visit by itself settles supernatural origin.
7. Historical context may illuminate why a message was heard as urgent, but crisis is not proof of supernatural origin and later events do not retroactively enlarge the authority's judgment.
8. Apparition narratives are treated as private revelation. They are not used as independent premises to define dogma, settle disputed theology, forecast dates, direct politics, diagnose illness, or replace Scripture, liturgy, sacraments, prudence, medicine, law, or competent pastoral care.
9. The Pillar is presented exactly as papally received ancient local tradition, not as a fact established by apostolic-era evidence. The Saint Mary Major foundation memory is distinguished from the fifth-century basilica fabric; Walsingham's official liturgical chronology qualification is retained; and the Brown Scapular's approved sacramental meaning is distinguished from the disputed Simon Stock origin narrative.
10. The appendix does not use type alone to flatten unlike statuses. Its legend defines boldface narrowly; every unbolded row must be read from its status cell, which may indicate strong reception, `nihil obstat`, uncertainty, another Church's jurisdiction, precaution, or a negative act.

## Doctrinal guardrails

The governing sources are CCC 65–67; the CDF's 2000 theological commentary on Fátima; the 1978 and 2024 discernment norms; and the DDF's 2025 `Mater Populi Fidelis`.

- Christ is the Father's definitive Word; no new public revelation is expected before his return.
- An approved private revelation is offered for prudent human faith, not divine and Catholic faith; its use is not obligatory.
- The criterion is orientation to Christ, the Gospel, the theological virtues, ecclesial communion, liturgy, and sacramental life.
- Prophetic imagery is not a photographic script of an inevitable future. Its primary function is conversion in the present.
- Christ is the sole Redeemer and Mediator. Mary's cooperation is graced, maternal, participated, subordinate, ecclesial, and wholly dependent on him.
- Mary is never a merciful alternative to an insufficiently merciful God, a “lightning rod” restraining Christ, or the source of grace apart from the Trinity and Christ's Paschal Mystery.
