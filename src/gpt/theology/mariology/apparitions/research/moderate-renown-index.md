# Bounded Moderate-Renown Index — Inclusion and Typography Record

**As of:** 2026-07-13
**Consumer:** `sections/95-moderate-renown-index.tex`
**Controlling status inventory:** `research/corpus-status.md`

## Why this is not an “all apparitions” registry

Neither the Holy See nor the worldwide college of diocesan bishops publishes one universal list of every claimed Marian apparition. “Moderate renown” has no canonical definition, and search-engine popularity is unstable, language-biased, and easily manufactured. The appendix therefore makes a bounded editorial claim: it indexes cases with enough documented public ecclesial presence that a serious Catholic reader is reasonably likely to encounter or misclassify them.

## Inclusion rule

A case is included only when it satisfies at least two of these independently documented signals:

1. sustained national or international shrine, feast, sacramental, pilgrimage, or devotional reception documented by a competent or official Church source;
2. a public judgment or repeated intervention by competent Catholic authority, whether positive, negative, precautionary, or restrictive;
3. treatment in a Holy See, episcopal-conference, diocesan, eparchial, national-liturgical, official-shrine, or other competent ecclesial publication; or
4. recurring public status confusion serious enough to produce an authoritative clarification or correction.

The complete appendix corpus is the fifteen legacy cases, six post-2024 positive-pastoral cases, eight received/ambiguous traditions, the named boundary cases in `corpus-status.md`, and the fifteen appendix-only cases recorded there. An index candidate is not promoted into the narrative corpus merely because it passes this renown threshold.

## Boldface rule

Boldface is a semantic claim and is generated manually only after checking the controlling inventory. It means:

> a located positive Catholic judgment is directed to the alleged event itself and says, in its own historical vocabulary, that the event or apparition is true, authentic, supernatural, or worthy of belief.

Accordingly, the bold rows are Laus, Ratisbonne, La Salette, Lourdes, Champion, Pontmain, Fátima, Beauraing, Banneux, Betania, Kibeho, and San Nicolás.

The following do **not** trigger boldface by themselves: a canonized recipient; papal visit or address; feast; coronation; indulgence; imprimatur; approved prayer, title, medal, scapular, cult, public worship, shrine, pilgrimage, or miracle; a favorable witness commission without a located event-origin judgment; a double-negative non-exclusion; diocesan non-prohibition; or a post-2024 `nihil obstat`. This is why Guadalupe, Walsingham, Mount Carmel, Rue du Bac, Pellevoisin, Gietrzwałd, Knock, Akita, Medjugorje, and the other post-2024 cases remain unbolded despite real positive forms of reception.

## Coverage and exclusions

- The index includes ancient and medieval received traditions only where an official papal, basilica, national-liturgical, episcopal, or Carmelite source supplies both the tradition and its reception boundary.
- Zeitoun is included because of extraordinary international renown and a clear positive Coptic Orthodox judgment; its row names that authority and refuses to transform it into a Roman Catholic act.
- Negative and restricted cases are included when omitting them would predictably turn silence into imagined approval.
- Local claims lacking two signals, cases known principally through commercial or devotee sites, and cases whose competent jurisdiction or public act could not be established are excluded pending a new audit. Exclusion is not a negative judgment.
- The 2026 re-audit added Šiluva, Coromoto, La Vang, and Licheń because each has both sustained national shrine reception and direct papal or official-shrine documentation. It did not add Good Success at Quito, Ngome, Soufanieh, Anguera, or Itapiranga: their popular renown supplied a research lead, but this audit did not locate two independent qualifying signals with a sufficiently clear competent-status record. Miraculous-image traditions without an apparition claimant, such as Aparecida, Luján, and Częstochowa, lie outside this apparition index as such.
- “Moderate renown” is reevaluated when the document is substantively revised. A new case requires a controlling-inventory row and claim-level source audit before it enters typeset output.
