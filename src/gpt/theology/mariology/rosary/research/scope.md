# The Holy Rosary — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/rosary/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** foundational Mariological reference with discursive historical and doctrinal exposition. `guidance/editorial.md` and `guidance/theology/mariology.md` govern it; `guidance/articles.md` supplies compatible general argument standards but is not its primary profile.
- **Question:** What is the Rosary in Catholic doctrine and practice; how did its form develop; how should claims of revelation and tradition be classified; and how do its four groups and twenty mysteries form one Christological, Trinitarian, Marian, ecclesial, and moral whole?
- **Intended reader:** a serious Catholic lay reader, catechist, or study group seeking an authoritative foundation rather than a short prayer leaflet.
- **Thesis:** the Rosary is Marian in contemplative mode and radically Christological in content. The Church remembers the one saving economy in communion with Mary's believing memory so as to be conformed by the Spirit to the Son and led through him to the Father.
- **As-of date for online verification:** 2026-07-12. Indulgence discipline, the DDF's current discernment norms, and current doctrinal safeguards for Marian titles were checked in the Apostolic Penitentiary's fourth *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum*, the DDF's 2024 *Norms*, and its 2025 *Mater Populi Fidelis*; mutable claims are date-qualified.

## Included and excluded scope

Included are the form and theological anatomy of a decade; the origins of its prayers; medieval and modern development; the received St. Dominic tradition and its historical qualification; Pius V's stabilization; modern papal teaching; the relation of Fatima to the existing devotion; group-level and mystery-level biblical, doctrinal, patristic, typological, spiritual, and moral analysis; the Rosary's relation to liturgy, lectio divina, family prayer, private revelation, and Christian freedom.

Excluded are a complete general mariology; an exhaustive catalogue of every Rosary confraternity, variant, indulgence, saintly testimony, or national custom; a critical edition of medieval Rosary texts; a judgment on every claimed apparition; instructions for liturgical celebration; and spiritual direction for an individual. The companion project on approved Marian apparitions has its own scope and must not be silently merged into this document.

## Authority classes

- **Public Revelation:** Scripture and the apostolic deposit supply the mysteries' substance. The Rosary adds no revelation to Christ.
- **Dogma and authoritative doctrine:** conciliar Christology, the Incarnation, Pasch, Eucharist, Church, Assumption, the completion of public Revelation, and Mary's maternal cooperation and intercession within Christ's unique mediation govern all synthesis.
- **Authoritative devotional teaching:** *Marialis cultus*, *Rosarium Virginis Mariae*, and the Directory on Popular Piety identify the Rosary's Christological, contemplative, Trinitarian, and ecclesial character and its proper relation to liturgy.
- **Patristic reception:** exact works and loci illuminate individual mysteries. They are not represented as evidence that the Fathers prayed the later Rosary form.
- **Historical synthesis:** the document uses the official sources' restrained claims; checked late-medieval and sixteenth-century witnesses; Barile's 2003 synthesis; and modern manuscript, art-historical, and institutional scholarship for Dominic of Prussia, Alain, and Cologne. Historical conclusions are not raised to doctrinal rank.
- **Private revelation and ecclesial judgment:** reported apparitions can commend living the Gospel but cannot supplement the deposit or bind theological faith. Each judgment is stated according to its exact act; under the 2024 DDF norms a *nihil obstat* permits positive pastoral reception without certifying supernatural origin. Fatima fosters rather than originates the Rosary.
- **Project synthesis:** the fourfold whole-cycle map, repeated eight-axis mystery analysis, and several transitions between mysteries are editorial theological syntheses grounded in cited sources.

## Material qualifications

1. RVM 1's statement that the Rosary “gradually took form in the second millennium” governs the history. The document does not claim a single complete origin.
2. The detailed claim that Mary delivered the complete modern Rosary to St. Dominic is treated as a later devotional tradition first securely linked to him in the audited fifteenth-century evidence, not contemporary documentary history or required belief. Leo XIII's repetition of the received attribution is reported rather than suppressed; the study does not infer that Dominic had no Marian or counted-prayer role.
3. Pius V did not invent every component or list the fixed fifteen mysteries in *Consueverunt Romani Pontifices*. The bull's exact object—confraternity privileges and indulgences—and its description of 150 salutations, Our Fathers, and whole-life meditation are preserved. MC 42's phrase that he “in a certain sense established” the traditional form governs the synthesis.
4. The Hail Mary's two biblical greetings, the name Jesus, the varied late-medieval petitions, and the stabilized full form have distinct histories. Savonarola's c. 1495–1496 near-form omits *nostrae*; the 1566 Roman Catechism explains the ecclesial petition without printing the full later formula at that locus; the Pian Breviary stabilized the liturgical form in 1568. The Our Father, doxology, concluding prayers, and beads likewise retain distinct histories and functions.
5. The historic full Rosary of fifteen decades and the expanded twenty-mystery cycle are distinguished from an ordinary five-decade recitation. The Luminous Mysteries remain a papal proposal left to devotional freedom (RVM 19, 38).
6. The Rosary is distinct from the liturgy and is not treated as sacramental anamnesis, a substitute for Mass or the Liturgy of the Hours, or a prayer ordinarily to be recited concurrently during liturgy (MC 48).
7. Mary's maternal intercession and cooperation are always subordinate and dependent participation within Christ's unique mediation; any use of “mediation” for her is analogical and cannot add to Christ's saving office or interpose her in God's immediate communication of sanctifying grace (LG 60–62; CCC 970; *Mater Populi Fidelis* 27–33, 45–55).
8. Typologies—Mary as Ark or New Eve, Cana's Eucharistic horizon, thorns and Genesis, Pentecost and Babel, the woman of Revelation 12—are labeled as such and are not presented as exhaustive literal exegesis.
9. The Assumption is defined doctrine. The defining formula does not settle whether Mary died. Scriptural texts used for it are received within Tradition and are not treated as standalone narrative proof.
10. Mary's Queenship is authoritative doctrine derived from Christ. ACR 39's “limited and analogous” participation governs the claim. A separately narrated visual coronation ceremony is not called a defined dogma.
11. Passion analysis distinguishes sinful human agency, Christ's free self-offering, and the Father's saving will. It rejects collective Jewish guilt (CCC 597), glorification of pain, and any use of “carry the cross” to prevent protection, treatment, reporting, or justice.
12. Private revelation is interpreted through CCC 66–67, the DDF's 2024 *Norms*, and the CDF Fatima commentary. Legacy judgments retain their own scope; a current *nihil obstat* is not a declaration of supernatural origin. No judgment is equated with public Revelation, universal obligation, or a guarantee that every popular retelling is exact.
13. Exact wound counts, thorn species, alleged speeches, fixed temporal promises, and mechanically saving “Rosary promises” were rejected as governing evidence.
14. The indulgence paragraph follows the fourth *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum*, general norms 18 and 20 and concession 17 §1, as checked on the as-of date. It directs future editors to recheck rather than perpetuate a mutable claim.

## Copyright and quotation review

The document chiefly paraphrases Scripture, magisterial sources, and historical scholarship. Its few compact quotations are claim-driven and located. It does not reproduce complete prayers beyond naming their ordinary components, nor does it reproduce a modern translation or historical article in bulk. The linked patristic English texts are used as working translations, not claimed as critical editions.
