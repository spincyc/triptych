# Definition Inventory — The Marian Dogmas

This inventory controls the exact corpus and prevents the narrative from treating unlike forms of proposal as interchangeable. It supports `main.tex` and is subordinate to the primary acts identified below.

## Corpus overview

| Dogma | Exact object in compact form | Controlling act or formulation | Mode of proposal | Defined or required content | Deliberate boundary / open question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Divine Motherhood | Mary is truly *Theotokos* because the one born from her according to humanity is the eternal Word. | Ephesus (431), reception of Cyril's Second Letter and first anathema; Formula of Union (433); Chalcedonian Definition (451). | Solemn ecumenical conciliar Christological judgment, received in the Church's universal faith. | One divine personal subject; true divinity and true humanity; real birth from Mary; maternity terminates in the divine person according to his humanity. | Mary does not originate the divine nature, precede the Trinity, or become divine. Ephesus does not independently define all later Marian privileges. |
| Perpetual Virginity | Mary is virgin before, in, and after Christ's birth. | Ancient universal confession; Constantinople II (553); Lateran 649, canon 3; later conciliar/papal confession; LG 57; CCC 496–507. | Dogma of the Church's universal Tradition and ordinary universal Magisterium, repeatedly and authoritatively proposed; Lateran 649 gives explicit threefold content. | Conception without human father by the Spirit; true miraculous birth preserving virginal integrity; no later children or marital consummation. | No dogmatic microscopic physiology; no contempt for marriage or childbirth; no requirement to define every “brother” as cousin rather than Joseph's earlier child. |
| Immaculate Conception | From the first instant of her conception Mary was preserved immune from every stain of original sin by singular divine grace in view of Christ's merits. | Pius IX, *Ineffabilis Deus* (8 Dec. 1854), concluding solemn definition. | Ex cathedra papal definition of a divinely revealed doctrine. | First instant; singular grace and privilege; Almighty God as giver; Christ's merits as redemptive ground; preservation from every stain of original sin; divine revelation and required belief. | Not Christ's virginal conception; not a virginal conception of Mary; not exemption from needing Christ; not identical to the separate doctrine of lifelong personal sinlessness; no claim that every Father used the final formula. |
| Assumption | Mary, having completed her earthly course, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory. | Pius XII, *Munificentissimus Deus* (1 Nov. 1950), §44. | Ex cathedra papal definition of a divinely revealed dogma. | Identity of the subject as Immaculate Mother of God and ever-Virgin; completed earthly course; passive divine Assumption; body and soul; heavenly glory. | Does not define whether she died, a date, a place, a tomb, a detailed mechanism, or an apocryphal narrative; not Ascension by her own power. |

## Divine Motherhood: controlling textual chain

### Scriptural reality

- Luke 1:31–35: Mary's child is Son of the Most High and Son of God.
- Luke 1:43: Elizabeth calls Mary “the mother of my Lord.”
- Galatians 4:4: God sends his pre-existent Son, born of a woman.
- John 1:1–14: the Word is God and becomes flesh.
- Matthew 1:18–25: the one born from Mary is Jesus and Emmanuel.

### Conciliar chain

1. **Cyril's Second Letter to Nestorius:** Mary is Mother of God not because the Word's divine nature begins from her, but because the Word personally unites flesh from her and is born according to the flesh.
2. **Cyril's First Anathema:** denial that Emmanuel is truly God and therefore that Mary is *Theotokos* is condemned.
3. **Formula of Union (433):** perfect God and perfect man, one Christ; *Theotokos* because of the union without confusion.
4. **Chalcedon (451):** one and the same Son, acknowledged in two natures, born eternally from the Father according to divinity and in the last days from Mary *Theotokos* according to humanity.

### Assent and exclusion

The dogma is received with divine and Catholic faith. Its Marian title is inseparable from the hypostatic union. “Mother of Christ” is inadequate when used to divide the human subject from the Word; it can be materially true only when Christ unambiguously names the one divine person.

## Perpetual Virginity: controlling textual chain

### Three moments

| Moment | Direct and received evidence | Exact content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Ante partum* | Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 1:26–38; ancient rule of faith and creeds. | Jesus is conceived from Mary by the Holy Spirit without a human father. |
| *In partu* | Luke 2:7 retains a real birth; patristic and liturgical confession; Lateran 649; LG 57; CCC 499. | Christ's real birth did not diminish but consecrated Mary's virginal integrity. |
| *Post partum* | Universal patristic and ecclesial confession; Epiphanius; Jerome; Augustine; Constantinople II's *ever-Virgin*; Lateran 649. | Mary remained virgin and bore no later children. |

### Controlling historical acts

- **Constantinople II (553):** repeatedly uses *ever-Virgin* in conciliar confession.
- **Lateran 649, canon 3 (DS 503):** explicit conception without seed by the Spirit, birth without corruption, and indissoluble virginity after birth.
- **Fourth Lateran (1215) and Lyons II (1274):** continuing confession of the Incarnation from the ever-Virgin.
- **Paul IV, *Cum quorundam hominum* (1555):** papal condemnation of denial of the threefold truth; primary critical wording still merits specialist verification before any future verbatim quotation.
- **LG 57 / CCC 499–501:** modern authoritative synthesis.

### Exegetical decisions not made into dogma

- The “brothers” are not Mary's later children, but the Church does not define one exclusive genealogy for every named relative.
- Matthew's “until” does not assert later intercourse.
- Luke's “firstborn” indicates legal/cultic status and does not require later siblings.
- Luke 1:34 is compatible with and traditionally read through a prior intention of virginity but is not made a documentary canonical-vow formula.
- Ezekiel 44 is typology, not the literal prophetic or biological description.

## Immaculate Conception: controlling textual chain

### Definition clauses

| Clause in *Ineffabilis Deus* | Dogmatic function |
| --- | --- |
| “in the first instant of her conception” | Excludes an interval in which Mary first contracts original sin and is then cleansed. |
| “by a singular grace and privilege of Almighty God” | Identifies the gift as exceptional, created, and wholly divine in origin. |
| “in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, Savior of the human race” | Preserves Christ's universal mediation; Mary is redeemed by preservation. |
| “preserved immune from every stain of original sin” | Identifies the exact evil prevented. |
| “revealed by God” and firmly believed | Identifies dogmatic authority and required assent. |

### Development chain

1. New Eve and singular-holiness matrix in Justin, Irenaeus, Ephrem, Augustine, and liturgy.
2. Eastern Conception feast and later Western reception, without treating one local origin story as universally established.
3. Medieval dispute: Eadmer's preservation logic; Bernard's opposition to the feast as then proposed; scholastic original-sin and animation questions.
4. Aquinas: sanctification after animation, explicit concern for Christ as universal Savior; no final-form Immaculate Conception.
5. Scotus: perfect mediation includes preservation; Mary remains wholly indebted to Christ.
6. Sixtus IV: liturgical promotion and prohibition on mutual heresy accusations before definition.
7. Trent session V: deliberate non-inclusion of Mary and renewal of Sixtus IV.
8. Paul V (1617) and Gregory XV (1622): progressively restrict assertion of the contrary thesis while leaving the doctrine not yet solemnly defined.
9. Alexander VII, *Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum* (1661): clearer first-instant object.
10. Pius IX, *Ubi primum* (1849): episcopal consultation.
11. Pius IX, *Ineffabilis Deus* (1854): solemn definition.

### Exclusions

The dogma neither teaches a virgin conception of Mary nor removes her from Christ's redemption. “Immaculate” in an older source is not automatically technical. Augustine's exception concerns personal sin; Aquinas's praise of lifelong holiness cannot be substituted for first-instant preservation.

## Assumption: controlling textual chain

### Definition clauses

| Clause in *Munificentissimus Deus* §44 | Dogmatic function |
| --- | --- |
| “the Immaculate Mother of God, the ever Virgin Mary” | Identifies the subject through the other dogmas without redefining them. |
| “having completed the course of her earthly life” | Affirms completion and leaves the fact of death undefined. |
| “was assumed” | Confesses God's action upon a redeemed creature, unlike Christ's Ascension by his own power. |
| “body and soul” | Excludes merely spiritual survival or honor to an absent body. |
| “into heavenly glory” | Confesses transformed eschatological consummation, not ordinary spatial relocation. |

### Evidence chain

1. Biblical resurrection and bodily-glory horizon: 1 Corinthians 15; Romans 8; Philippians 3.
2. Marian canonical fittingness: Luke 1; Genesis 3:15 / New Eve; Ark and Revelation 12 as typologies.
3. Consequential silence: no canonical Assumption narrative; Epiphanius reports uncertainty concerning Mary's end.
4. *Transitus Mariae* traditions: evidence of late-antique reception, not apostolic eyewitness proof.
5. Feast of Dormition/Assumption and Roman reception; later patristic homilies.
6. Mature witnesses: Pseudo-Modestus, Germanus of Constantinople, John Damascene.
7. Medieval consensus, including Aquinas's occasional affirmative references without a dedicated proof.
8. Pius XII, *Deiparae Virginis Mariae* (1946), episcopal consultation.
9. Pius XII, *Munificentissimus Deus* (1950), definition.

### Open question

Mary's death remains a weighty traditional conclusion, especially in Eastern Dormition liturgy and John Paul II's 25 June 1997 catechesis. It is not part of the defined object. Neither mortality nor non-mortality may be represented as the meaning of Pius XII's deliberate silence.

## Inventory control result

The narrative's dogma count is exactly four. Every exact definition in the publication agrees with this inventory. The final titles chapter does not propose a fifth dogma, and the definition inventory contains no private-revelation evidence as a dogmatic source.
