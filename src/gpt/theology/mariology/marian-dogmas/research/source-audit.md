# Source Audit — The Marian Dogmas

All online sources were last checked 2026-07-13 unless a more specific status is stated. This audit supports `main.tex`, the section files, and `definitions-inventory.md`.

## Verification legend

- **O — official / primary online:** checked in a Holy See, conciliar, or authority-owned text at the stated locus.
- **P — primary working text:** exact ancient or medieval work and locus checked in a searchable transcription or working translation; not represented as a critical edition.
- **C — critical-locus controlled:** exact critical-series, Denzinger, or edition locus identified, but the full critical text was not directly collated in this drafting task; no disputed verbatim translation is published from it.
- **M — magisterial reception:** an official magisterial source quotes, locates, or summarizes the earlier witness; the publication states the earlier claim no more broadly than that reception supports.
- **S — source-grounded synthesis:** editorial synthesis drawn from multiple identified sources; not attributed verbatim to any one authority.
- **L — bounded lead:** potentially useful locus retained with an explicit verification limit; it does not carry a consequential claim requiring a critical reading.

## Scripture and interpretive mode

| Claim / text | Literary and canonical use | Evidence class / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Luke 1:26–38 | Directly narrates virginal conception, identifies the child as Son of God, gives Trinitarian structure, and records Mary's free fiat. | Direct scriptural assertion. It does not independently state the complete Immaculate-Conception definition or a later canonical vow formula. |
| Luke 1:41–45 | Elizabeth, filled with the Spirit, calls Mary blessed and “mother of my Lord.” | Direct biblical substance for divine maternity and blessedness; not a substitute for fifth-century terminology. |
| Luke 1:46–55 | Mary's praise centers the Mighty One, covenant mercy, Abrahamic promise, and reversal of proud and lowly. | Direct scriptural norm for doxological Marian devotion. |
| Luke 2:7, 22–24 | Real birth and firstborn legal/cultic status. | Direct assertion of birth. “Firstborn” is not proof of later siblings. |
| Luke 2:19, 50–51 | Mary ponders and grows in the mystery she does not exhaustively understand. | Direct support for real pilgrim faith; excludes an inference that sinlessness means earthly omniscience. |
| Matthew 1:18–25 | Conception before cohabitation and from the Spirit; Joseph's legal role; Emmanuel fulfillment. | Direct assertion of virginal conception. “Until” establishes the prior period but does not assert later intercourse. |
| Matthew 13:55–56 / Mark 6:3 | Names Jesus' brothers and sisters. | Direct kinship language. Received dogma excludes later children of Mary; exact kinship reconstruction remains historical rather than dogmatic. |
| Galatians 4:4–7 | The Father sends the already-identifiable Son, born of a woman, then sends the Spirit of the Son for adoption. | Direct scriptural grammar for pre-existent Son, real maternity, redemption, and Trinitarian order. |
| John 1:1–14 | The Word is God and becomes flesh. | Direct foundation for the divine subject of Mary's maternity. |
| John 2:1–11 | Mary notices need, intercedes, and orders servants to Christ; Christ produces the sign and manifests glory. | Direct evangelical pattern for maternal intercession. It does not assign Mary efficient causality over the sign or grace. |
| John 19:25–37 | Mary stands at the Cross; Jesus establishes a real relation with the beloved disciple; Jesus completes his work and dies. | Direct evidence for faithful maternal presence and spiritual-motherhood synthesis. It does not make Mary co-priest or a second victim. |
| Acts 1:14 | Mary prays within the apostolic community awaiting Pentecost. | Direct ecclesial placement: unique mother and receptive member, not source of the Spirit. |
| Genesis 3:15 | Enmity between serpent, woman, and offspring; Christian canon sees fulfillment in Christ and New Eve reception. | Literal primordial judgment and promise; Marian application is canonical/patristic. The dogma does not depend on Vulgate *ipsa*. |
| Romans 5:12–21 / 1 Corinthians 15:20–49 | Christ is the new Adam; all require his redemption; he is firstfruits of resurrection. | Direct Christological and corporate framework. Mary never becomes a second new Adam or independent exception to Christ. |
| Ephesians 1:3–10 | Election, grace, adoption, redemption, and recapitulation are entirely “in Christ.” | Direct canonical grammar for preservative redemption and receptive Marian position. |
| Romans 8:11, 23 / Philippians 3:20–21 | The Spirit gives life to mortal bodies; Christ transforms the lowly body. | Direct common-resurrection horizon. Mary's anticipated glorification is known through Tradition and definition, not narrated here. |
| 2 Samuel 6 / Exodus 40 | Ark transfer and overshadowing correspond to Luke's Annunciation and Visitation. | Canonical typology; not literal Marian prediction or proof of every dogma. |
| Zephaniah 3:14–17 / Zechariah 9:9 | Daughter Zion rejoices because the King is present. | Canonical typology in Luke's annunciation pattern; Mary does not exhaust corporate Zion. |
| Ezekiel 44:1–3 | Closed eastern sanctuary gate through which the Lord enters. | Literal restored-sanctuary prophecy; Marian virginity application is patristic/liturgical typology only. |
| Psalm 132:8 | The Lord and ark arise to the resting place. | Assumption typology reported in *Munificentissimus Deus*; not narrative proof. |
| Revelation 12 | Apocalyptic woman, messianic child, dragon, and other offspring. | Polyvalent Israel–Church–Mary reading. Not photographic Assumption or Coronation evidence. |
| 1 Timothy 2:5–6 / Hebrews 7–10 | Christ is the one mediator and once-for-all priestly self-offering. | Direct governing limit for every Marian cooperation title. |
| 1 John 2:1 / John 14–16 | Christ is advocate; the Spirit is Paraclete. | Primary biblical title use. “Advocate” for Mary is analogical and intercessory, not a third divine Paraclete. |

## Consequential negative results

The following negative findings are part of the evidence state and are intentionally visible in the publication:

1. **No identical definition mode:** the four dogmas are not all separate modern papal definitions.
2. **No scriptural noun *Theotokos*:** Scripture supplies the reality but not that technical noun.
3. **No universally simple Ephesus story:** conciliar procedure and reception are more complex than a popular crowd anecdote.
4. **No full threefold formula in one Gospel verse:** virginal conception is direct; the lasting and in-birth dimensions are received through Tradition and authoritative teaching.
5. **No dogmatic anatomical reconstruction:** “without corruption” and undiminished integrity are not made a speculative medical chart.
6. **No exact 1854 patristic sentence:** New Eve, holiness, and liturgy are real roots but not the final first-instant formula.
7. **No Augustine shortcut:** *On Nature and Grace* 36.42 concerns personal sins rather than explicit immunity from original sin at conception.
8. **No Thomistic Immaculate Conception:** Thomas explicitly held contraction followed by sanctification; praising him does not reverse the evidence.
9. **No purely biological explanation of Thomas:** delayed animation mattered, but his universal-redemption concern was theological and consequential.
10. **No verified Scotist slogan quotation:** the popular “could/fitting/did” formula is not assigned to Scotus verbatim.
11. **No canonical Assumption narrative:** the publication says so directly.
12. **No apostolic status for *Transitus* texts:** they document reception and imagination.
13. **No early unanimity on Mary's end:** Epiphanius's uncertainty is retained.
14. **No definition of Mary's death:** common Dormition tradition remains a weighty opinion, not §44's object.
15. **No dedicated Thomistic Assumption proof:** Pius XII's careful wording controls.
16. **No photographic Revelation 12:** Israel, Church, and Mary remain within a polyvalent apocalyptic sign.
17. **No fifth Marian dogma:** sinlessness, Mother of the Church, Queenship, and the disputed cooperation titles retain distinct authority classes.
18. **No dogma from private revelation:** apparition reception cannot complete public Revelation.
19. **No suitable Co-redemptrix title:** current DDF judgment, not historical popularity, governs use.
20. **No created universal dispenser:** not even Mary immediately causes or distributes sanctifying grace to every soul.

## Rejected or bounded leads

- Unattributed quotation sites for conciliar formulas were rejected in favor of official reception, identified council texts, or DS loci.
- The Vulgate *ipsa* of Genesis 3:15 was rejected as the sole or necessary textual foundation for the Immaculate Conception.
- Ezekiel's closed gate was rejected as literal obstetrical proof and retained only as patristic typology.
- Assertions that every early Father explicitly taught the Immaculate Conception were rejected.
- The *potuit–decuit–fecit* maxim was rejected as a verbatim Scotus citation.
- Claims of an empty, universally known Marian tomb as historical proof were not used.
- Apocryphal narratives were rejected as eyewitness evidence.
- Apparitional phrases, devotional promises, and petitions for a new dogma were rejected as formal doctrinal evidence.
- Older papal uses of Co-redemptrix and the 1921 Mediatrix liturgy were rejected as cumulative solemn definitions.
- “Every grace passes through Mary's hands” was rejected as an unqualified causal proposition.
- “Suppliant omnipotence” was not used as literal power-language; its defensible intention is covered by subordinate intercession.

## Translation, quotation, and source limits

- Holy See English texts are treated as official web translations where supplied. *Ineffabilis Deus* was checked on the official Italian/Latin page; its dogmatic clauses are tightly paraphrased rather than reproduced at length.
- New Advent and comparable patristic pages are working translations. The publication cites work and locus, does not call them critical editions, and keeps quotations short.
- Lateran 649, Epiphanius, Gregory Nazianzen, Ephrem, Bernard, Eadmer, Scotus, and some Dormition sources still merit comparison with their critical editions before any future expansion of direct quotation.
- The current document does not reproduce a modern biblical translation in bulk. Short conventional phrases identify passages and arguments.
- No complete third-party article, scan, corpus, or OCR is vendored.
