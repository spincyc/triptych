# The Marian Dogmas — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/marian-dogmas/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** comprehensive Mariological reference governed by `guidance/editorial.md`, `guidance/repository.md`, and `guidance/theology/mariology.md`. It is not a Mass proper, Ordinary exposition, sacramental-reference fragment, apparition-status dossier, or general discursive article.
- **Question:** What exactly does the Catholic Church confess in the four truths commonly called the Marian dogmas; how is each grounded in Scripture and Tradition, witnessed and developed historically, treated by the Fathers and St. Thomas Aquinas, and defined or authoritatively proposed; how do the four relate as one Christological and ecclesial whole; and how must the titles Co-redemptrix, Mediatrix, and Advocate be judged under current doctrine?
- **Intended reader:** a serious Catholic lay reader, catechist, seminarian, clergy member, or study group needing a source-audited theological reference deeper than a catechetical summary but not pretending to be a critical monograph or magisterial act.
- **Thesis:** the four dogmas confess what the triune God accomplishes in one created woman because the eternal Son truly became man from her, redeemed her by his merits, incorporated her free maternal cooperation within his mission, and brought her to the bodily glory promised to the Church. Every Marian privilege is received; every authentic Marian title preserves Christ's sufficient redemption and unique mediation.
- **Online verification date:** 2026-07-13. Stable ancient doctrine is not given artificial legal currentness, but the current DDF judgment on Marian titles was checked in the official 2025 text of *Mater Populi Fidelis* through this date.

## Four-dogma corpus

The corpus is deliberately limited to:

1. **Divine Motherhood:** Mary is truly *Theotokos*, Mother of God, because the person born from her according to his humanity is God the Son.
2. **Perpetual Virginity:** Mary is virgin before, in, and after Christ's birth.
3. **Immaculate Conception:** from the first instant of her conception Mary was preserved from every stain of original sin by singular grace in view of Christ's merits.
4. **Assumption:** having completed her earthly course, Mary was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory.

The common taxonomy does not flatten their modes of proposal. Ephesus and Chalcedon define divine motherhood within Christology. Perpetual virginity is ancient dogma taught through universal Tradition, councils, papal acts, and the ordinary universal Magisterium, with Lateran 649 supplying an especially exact threefold formulation. The Immaculate Conception and Assumption have explicit ex cathedra definitions in 1854 and 1950.

Other Marian teachings and titles are included only to classify them correctly and explain their relation to the dogmas. Lifelong personal sinlessness, spiritual motherhood, Mother of the Church, and Queenship are not promoted into fifth, sixth, or later dogmas. New Eve, Ark, Daughter Zion, Woman of Revelation 12, and *Panagia* are classified as patristic, biblical, liturgical, or typological expressions according to the claim. The weighty tradition that Mary died is distinguished from the deliberately non-defining language of 1950.

## Included scope

Included are:

- an account of dogma, levels of authority, development, definition, and assent;
- a common biblical economy placing Mary within Israel, the missions of Son and Spirit, Christ's Pasch, and the Church;
- for each dogma, its exact object, controlling authority, necessary exclusions, principal biblical loci in context, patristic roots and mature witnesses, liturgical reception, historical controversy, magisterial development, Thomistic treatment, doctrinal consequences, and common errors;
- a candid treatment of pre-definition disagreement and historical silence;
- the relation of the four dogmas to Trinity, Christology, grace and freedom, anthropology, ecclesiology, and eschatology;
- categorical distinction among dogma, authoritative doctrine, traditional title, typology, theological opinion, devotional narrative, and private revelation;
- a final sustained treatment of maternal cooperation, Co-redemptrix, Mediatrix, Mediatrix of All Graces, and Advocate under *Lumen gentium* 56, 58, 60–62 and *Mater Populi Fidelis*;
- exact source loci in prose, a consolidated reference chapter, a definition inventory, and this source audit.

## Excluded scope

Excluded are:

- a complete history of every Marian feast, local liturgy, prayer, iconographic school, confraternity, or devotional title;
- a critical edition or manuscript stemma for Ephesus, Lateran 649, medieval conception treatises, the *Transitus Mariae* traditions, Dormition homilies, or Thomas's occasional Marian opuscula;
- a comprehensive history of Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, or evangelical Mariology;
- a judgment on alleged apparitions or an expansion of the repository's apparition corpus;
- use of a private revelation to prove, complete, or reinterpret a dogma;
- a biological reconstruction of virginity *in partu* or a settlement of questions the Church has not defined;
- a proposed new Marian dogma or advocacy campaign for Co-redemptrix, Mediatrix of All Graces, or Advocate;
- an assertion of ecclesiastical approval, independent specialist review, or juridical authority.

## Authority and evidence classes

### Sacred Scripture

Scripture supplies inspired testimony. The document first reads texts in literary context, then within the complete canon and the Church's received rule of faith. Direct assertion, canonical synthesis, and typology remain distinct. Genesis 3:15, Ark correspondence, Ezekiel 44, Psalms 45 and 132, and Revelation 12 are not represented as modern narrative reports of later definitions.

### Dogmatic and magisterial authority

The received Cyrillic letters and Chalcedonian Definition govern divine motherhood. The ancient conciliar, papal, creedal, and ordinary universal teaching governs perpetual virginity, especially DS 503 and CCC 496–507. *Ineffabilis Deus* and *Munificentissimus Deus* govern the exact objects of the last two dogmas. *Lumen gentium*, the Catechism, and official papal catecheses supply authoritative synthesis and clarify limits. The CDF's 1998 *Professio fidei* commentary is used to distinguish forms and levels of proposal.

The DDF's *Mater Populi Fidelis* is controlling for current constructive use of disputed Marian titles. Its numbered paragraphs were checked against the official English text. In particular:

- §14 calls Mary the first redeemed and places her cooperation after prevenient redemption;
- §§17–22 give the history and present negative judgment on Co-redemptrix;
- §§23–29 explain Mediatrix and participated mediation;
- §§34–44 treat maternal and intercessory cooperation;
- §§45–55 reserve the immediate communication of sanctifying grace to God and call Mary an advocate of grace only as intercessor;
- §§65–70 reject instrumental or universal-dispenser accounts and delimit actual graces and maternal preparation.

### Patristic evidence

Fathers are used as exact witnesses to particular realities, terminology, controversies, or stages of reception. The document does not infer the complete 1854 definition from New Eve language or the complete 1950 definition from every ancient praise of Mary. Online patristic translations are marked working translations. Exact critical-edition comparison remains pending where stated, especially Gregory Nazianzen, Epiphanius, Ephrem, and some Dormition homilies.

### St. Thomas Aquinas and scholastic evidence

Thomas is treated by exact question or chapter:

- ST III, q.35, a.4 and *Compendium theologiae* I, cc.222–223 for divine motherhood;
- ST III, q.28 and *Compendium* I, cc.221 and 225 for virginity;
- ST III, q.27, a.2, *Compendium* I, c.224, and related loci for his real disagreement with the later Immaculate Conception, together with q.27, aa.4–5 for personal holiness and fullness of grace;
- the occasional Assumption loci identified by *Munificentissimus Deus* §31 and note 31, without inventing a dedicated proof;
- ST III, q.26; I–II, q.114, a.6; II–II, q.83, a.11; and III, q.64, a.1 for unique mediation, merit, intercession, and divine causality of grace.

Scotus is used for preservative redemption through *Ordinatio* III, d.3, q.1. The popular *potuit–decuit–fecit* formula is not presented as a checked verbatim Scotus quotation.

### Historical and liturgical evidence

Conciliar texts, papal constitutions, official papal historical catecheses, the historical surveys within the definitions, and exact patristic or scholastic loci govern the published history. Apocrypha are evidence of reception and narrative imagination only. Liturgy is evidence of ecclesial worship and belief according to the date, locality, and scope that can actually be supported; it is not made a detailed eyewitness record.

### Project synthesis

The fourfold Christological map, the nature–grace–glory sequence, several transitions among dogmas, the repeated doctrinal test for titles, and the concluding fourfold prayer application are source-grounded editorial syntheses. They are not attributed to a council or Father as exact schemes.

## Material qualifications and negative results

1. Scripture does not use the noun *Theotokos*, but directly supplies the reality protected at Ephesus.
2. Ephesus's procedural history is complex. The document grounds the dogmatic claim in the received Cyrillic texts, 433 Formula of Union, and Chalcedon rather than a romanticized crowd narrative.
3. Scripture directly teaches virginal conception. The full *ante/in/post partum* articulation is received through the Church's Tradition and later explicit acts.
4. “Brothers,” “until,” “firstborn,” John 19, and Luke 1:34 are treated cumulatively and according to their linguistic scope rather than as isolated knock-down proofs.
5. Virginity *in partu* is confessed without an intrusive anatomical reconstruction or contempt for ordinary marriage and childbirth.
6. No pre-Nicene Father states the exact 1854 first-instant formula. New Eve, singular holiness, liturgy, and developing original-sin theology form a matrix rather than a verbatim definition.
7. Augustine's *On Nature and Grace* 36.42 concerns exemption from discussion of personal sins; it is not mislabeled an explicit Immaculate-Conception definition.
8. Aquinas did not teach the later-defined Immaculate Conception. Delayed-animation premises mattered, but his explicit universal-redemption concern is not erased. The definition answers that concern through preservative redemption in view of Christ's merits.
9. The dogma concerns original sin at the first instant; Mary's lifelong freedom from personal sin is a distinct, closely related authoritative doctrine.
10. The New Testament contains no Assumption narrative. Scriptural evidence establishes the Paschal, bodily, canonical, and typological horizon within which Tradition receives the dogma.
11. *Transitus Mariae* literature is not treated as apostolic eyewitness testimony. Epiphanius's uncertainty about Mary's end is retained as consequential early negative evidence.
12. *Munificentissimus Deus* deliberately does not settle whether Mary died. The common Dormition tradition is reported as weighty, not defined.
13. Aquinas does not supply a dedicated Assumption proof. His affirmative occasional references and general resurrection principles are reported according to Pius XII's own caution.
14. Revelation 12 retains its Israel–Church–Mary polyvalence and is not a photographic Assumption or Coronation narrative.
15. Queenship is authoritative and derived; a separate visible coronation ceremony is not another dogma.
16. Co-redemptrix, Mediatrix, and Advocate are not additional dogmas. Historical papal use or limited liturgical concession does not create a solemn definition.
17. Under *Mater Populi Fidelis* §22, constructive use of Co-redemptrix is always inappropriate. Historical discussion is retained only to understand the judgment.
18. Mediatrix is acceptable only in a distant, analogical, subordinate, participated sense. No creature is a universal dispenser or immediate cause of sanctifying grace.
19. Advocate means maternal intercession within Christ's mediation, never protection from a merciless Father or reluctant Christ.
20. Apparitions, even when positively received, cannot establish a dogma or make every reported title an object of faith.
