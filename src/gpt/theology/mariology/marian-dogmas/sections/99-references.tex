\subsection{Sacred Scripture, councils, and catechism}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Principal scriptural loci: Genesis 3:15; 2 Samuel 6; Psalms 45 and 132; Isaiah 7:14; Ezekiel 44:1--3; Zephaniah 3:14--17; Matthew 1--2 and 12--13; Mark 3 and 6; Luke 1--2; John 1--2 and 19; Acts 1; Romans 5 and 8; 1 Corinthians 15; Galatians 4; Ephesians 1 and 5; Philippians 2--3; 1 Timothy 2; Hebrews 7--10; 1 John 2; and Revelation 5, 6, and 12. Individual chapters distinguish direct assertion, canonical synthesis, and typology.
  \item Council of Ephesus (431), St.~Cyril of Alexandria's Second and Third Letters to Nestorius, including the first anathema: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3810.htm}{working English conciliar text}. The doctrine is checked through the received Cyrillic texts rather than a free-standing modern slogan.
  \item Council of Chalcedon (451), Definition of Faith, especially the one-and-the-same-Son formula and ``Mary the Virgin, Mother of God, according to the humanity'': quoted in the CDF's \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20061126_notification-sobrino_en.html}{official doctrinal notification}; a \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3811.htm}{working English conciliar text} was also consulted.
  \item Second Council of Constantinople (553), anathemas and conciliar confession of the holy, glorious Mother of God and ever-Virgin Mary; critical locus DS 422, 427, and related conciliar texts.
  \item Lateran Synod of 649, canon 3, under Pope Martin I, on virginal conception, birth without corruption, and enduring virginity; DS 503. The study paraphrases the critical conciliar locus rather than relying on an unattributed online translation.
  \item Fourth Lateran Council (1215), constitution 1, \emph{Firmiter credimus}; Second Council of Lyons (1274), profession of faith; received as subsequent conciliar confession of the Incarnation from the ever-Virgin.
  \item Council of Trent, session V, Decree on Original Sin, including its express non-inclusion of Mary and renewal of Sixtus IV; session VI, canon 23, on lifelong avoidance of venial sin by special privilege. Cited by session, decree, and canon; exact doctrinal use is bounded in the text.
  \item First Vatican Council, \emph{Dei Filius}, especially chapters 3--4, on faith and reason: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-ix/en/documents/constitutio-dogmatica-dei-filius-24-aprilis-1870.html}{Holy See text}.
  \item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Dei Verbum}, especially 2--4, 8, and 10: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html}{Holy See text}.
  \item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Lumen gentium}, especially 8 and 52--69: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html}{Holy See text}.
  \item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, especially 396--421, 456--511, 668--682, 946--975, 1618--1620, and 2673--2679: \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/_INDEX.HTM}{Holy See index}; \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_two/artcile_3/paragraph_2_conceived_by_the_power_of_the_holy_spirit_and_born_of_the_virgin_mary.html}{virginal conception and birth}; \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_9/paragraph_6_mary_-_mother_of_christ\%2C_mother_of_the_church.html}{Mary and the Church}.
\end{itemize}

The defining clauses were checked at the constitution's concluding definition.

  \item St.~Justin Martyr, \emph{Dialogue with Trypho} 100, on the Eve--Mary contrast: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/01287.htm}{working English translation}.
  \item St.~Irenaeus of Lyons, \emph{Against Heresies} III.21.10 and III.22.4, on real derivation from Mary, recapitulation, and the New Eve: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103321.htm}{book III, chapter 21}; \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103322.htm}{book III, chapter 22}; V.19.1 on Mary as advocate of Eve: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103519.htm}{working English translation}.

  \item St.~Epiphanius of Salamis, \emph{Ancoratus} 119.5 on \emph{Aeiparthenos}; \emph{Panarion} 78.11 on the historical uncertainty surrounding Mary's end. These exact loci are used without claiming that a freely available English text is a critical edition.
  \item St.~Jerome, \emph{Against Helvidius}, especially 5--17, on ``brothers,'' ``until,'' and continuing virginity: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3007.htm}{working English translation}.
  \item St.~Augustine, \emph{On Nature and Grace} 36.42, on excluding Mary when discussing personal sin: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1503.htm}{working English translation}; \emph{Holy Virginity} 3, 4, and 6, respectively on believing discipleship, Mary's prior purpose, and ecclesial motherhood: \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1310.htm}{working English translation}; Sermon 186.1, cited through CCC 510, on perpetual virginity.

  \item St.~Ephrem's Marian holiness language is used as a bounded patristic lead through \emph{Hymns on the Nativity}, with the need for comparison against \emph{Sources Chr\'etiennes} 459 expressly retained; no disputed English line is quoted as a critical translation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{St.~Thomas Aquinas and the scholastic question}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Summa theologiae} III, qq.2, 26--35, especially q.26, aa.1--2 on Christ's unique mediation; q.27 on Mary's sanctification and Thomas's disagreement with the later Immaculate Conception; q.28 on threefold virginity; q.30, a.1 on consent; q.32, a.3 on the Holy Spirit and paternity; and q.35, a.4 on divine motherhood. Latin critical text: \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth4000.html}{Corpus Thomisticum, Tertia Pars}; q.27 was checked at \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/sth4027.html}{the exact question}.
  \item \emph{Compendium theologiae} I, cc.221--225, on virginity, divine motherhood, and Mary's sanctification: \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/ott1185.html}{Corpus Thomisticum Latin text}. Chapter 224 is used as an additional unambiguous witness that Thomas did not teach the 1854 formula.
  \item \emph{Quodlibet} VI, q.5, a.1, on Mary's conception, original sin, redemption, and the feast: \sourceurl{https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/q06.html}{Corpus Thomisticum Latin text}.
  \item ST I--II, q.114, a.6, on merit for another's first grace; ST II--II, q.83, a.11, on heavenly intercession; ST III, q.64, a.1, on God as principal cause of the sacrament's interior effect. These loci are used with MPF 47--55, which explicitly receives the same Thomistic distinctions.
  \item Aquinas's occasional Assumption loci are identified according to \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} 31 and note 31: \emph{Exposition of the Angelic Salutation}; \emph{Exposition of the Apostles' Creed}, art.~5; \emph{In IV Sententiarum}, d.12, q.1, a.3, sol.3, and d.43, q.1, a.3, sols.1--2. No dedicated Thomistic proof is claimed.
  \item Bl.~John Duns Scotus, \emph{Ordinatio} III, d.3, q.1, on preservative redemption and Christ's perfect mediation. The study summarizes the argument and deliberately does not attribute the popular \emph{potuit--decuit--fecit} slogan to Scotus as an exact quotation.
  \item St.~Bernard of Clairvaux, Epistle 174 to the canons of Lyons, and Eadmer, \emph{Tractatus de conceptione sanctae Mariae}, are used to locate opposed medieval stages in the conception controversy; their views are neither presented as magisterial judgments nor collapsed into the later definition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Historical source limits}

The dogmatic constitutions, conciliar texts, official catecheses, liturgical evidence summarized in them, and identified patristic and scholastic loci govern the historical narrative. Apocryphal \emph{Transitus Mariae} texts and the \emph{Protoevangelium of James} are used only as evidence of developing reception and narrative imagination. They are not treated as apostolic eyewitness sources. Exact manuscript genealogy, local feast origins, disputed authorship, and the full critical history of Dormition and Conception texts remain matters for specialist historical review rather than claims silently resolved by this study.

Online sources were last checked 13 July 2026. Patristic web links are working English translations unless otherwise stated; they are not represented as critical editions. Short phrases in the body are claim-driven and remain well below reproduction of complete third-party texts.
