# Champion Source-Strata Control

Checked through 2026-07-16. This record prevents the publication from converting a developing tradition into a fictitious single 1859 transcript.

## Stratum A — Eliza Allen Starr, 1871

**Witness and access.** Eliza Allen Starr published the account in the first series of *Patron Saints* in 1871. A page-image scan of the 1883 reissue was checked at printed pp. 298–301 through Internet Archive (`patronsaints00star`). Starr says Adele and a younger English-speaking companion visited her during fundraising; a French letter from a Robinsonville priest introduced Adele, and the younger woman interpreted.

**What this establishes strongly.** By 1871 Adele personally presented a three-appearance narrative; the local priest and a Catholic writer knew it; the commission to teach children was central; Adele was already operating free instruction, seeking support for a school, chapel, and home; and an annual Assumption procession commemorated the apparition.

**Mediations and limits.** Starr was not present in 1859. The exchange passed through an interpreter and literary shaping. Starr announces her own belief (“undoubtedly”) and inserts the episode into an edifying life of St. Lioba. It is not an ecclesiastical deposition or neutral reportage.

**Material content.** Starr places the first appearance on a summer day while Adele returned from Mass, describes the figure as veiled and visually resembling Immaculate Conception imagery, places another appearance a few weeks later on the way to Mass and a third that afternoon, and reports only the catechetical commission: gather neglected children, teach what they need for salvation, catechism, the Sign of the Cross, and approach to the sacraments.

**Non-harmonization rule.** Do not silently change “summer day” to October, returning from Mass to going to a grist mill, a veil to visible golden hair and a crown, or Starr's shorter dialogue to the full later text. The 2010 decree controls the judged month; it does not erase the existence of Starr's textual differences.

## Stratum B — Peter Pernin, 1874

**Witness and access.** Peter Pernin published *The Finger of God Is There! or, Thrilling Episode of a Strange Event Related by an Eye-Witness* at Montreal in 1874. The English first-edition scan at Canadiana (`oocihm.04805`) was inspected at printed pp. 94–102, images 100–108. Pernin survived the Peshtigo fire and wrote the main memoir as an eyewitness to Peshtigo; his appendix shifts to Robinsonville.

**What this establishes strongly.** It is early published evidence for Adele's identity and reputation, three appearances, the instruction/First Communion mission, arduous itinerant catechesis, school, chapel, approximately six-acre enclosure, pilgrimage, collaborators, and the reported 1871 preservation. It also preserves a contemporary act of epistemic restraint.

**Mediations and limits.** Pernin did not witness the 1859 apparitions. He does not identify every informant for the Champion fire and was not at the shrine during that night. His theological and fundraising purposes are explicit. Approbation for publication is not a diocesan judgment authenticating every event.

**Material content.** Pernin gives three successive return-from-Mass appearances and a large escort for the third. He says the conversation was long but reports only what serves his subject: instruct the children, remedy Eucharistic ignorance, prepare First Communions, and trust divine help. This differs from both Starr and the later grist-mill narrative.

**Fire restraint.** Pernin reports that Adele's “pious girls” hastened to the chapel, carried a Marian statue in procession, and prayed; he says they waited for a lull or turned in another direction when wind and flame blocked them. He describes a charred fence, spared school, chapel, and enclosure, and surrounding destruction. The checked passage does not supply the later generalized refugee crowd, possessions, livestock, or downpour. On the next page he explicitly says he has no intention of pronouncing the event a miracle or passing judgment on the apparition, because ecclesiastical authority had not spoken. That caveat must accompany every use of his evocative “emerald island amid a sea of ashes” image.

## Stratum C — Later oral-history and custodial narrative

**Witness and access.** M. Dominica's *The Chapel: Our Lady of Good Help, A Shrine of Mary on the Green Bay Peninsula* (De Pere: Journal Publishing Co., 1955), prepared within the Bay Settlement custodial tradition, is repeatedly identified by the Shrine as a historical source. The original volume was not independently collated during this pass. The checked National Shrine 2023 history PDF gives the current custodial presentation and expressly states on every page that it is based on the Shrine history book, diocesan archived information, and word-of-mouth testimony.

**What this establishes.** It establishes the form in which the complete narrative is currently received and taught at the competent diocesan shrine. It supplies the grist-mill beginning; maple and hemlock; white dress, yellow sash, crown of stars, and golden hair; priest's test; companions' questions; identification as Queen of Heaven; conversion, confession, Communion, and conditional warning; beatitude; vineyard question; catechetical commission; and assurance of help.

**Limits.** “Official shrine history” means custodial and pastoral authority, not contemporaneity or critical-edition status. The complete oral chain is not exposed in the public PDF. A claim sometimes repeated online connects Sister Pauline LaPlante directly to Adele. The Shrine timeline's 1902 Chapel assignment does not make that contact impossible: Pauline's own congregation says that she had returned from Racine to Bay Settlement by 1868, and her life overlapped Adele's until 1896. But no located Pauline writing, dated interview, deposition, or documented chain ties a particular sentence to her. The publication therefore records contact as possible but unproved and calls the fuller text later received wording, not Adele's certified verbatim deposition.

## Stratum D — Bishop Ricken's 2010 decree

**Function.** The decree is the competent ecclesiastical judgment, not the earliest historical witness. It acknowledges sparse early documentation, reports a three-expert study of extant records, judges the accounts free of doctrinal error and consistent with Catholic faith, weighs Adele's character and fruits, quotes selected portions of the fuller received wording, and defines the positive status.

**Exact object.** “the events, apparitions and locutions given to Adele Brise in October of 1859.”

**Exact effect.** They “do exhibit the substance of supernatural character,” and the bishop approves “these apparitions as worthy of belief (although not obligatory) by the Christian faithful.”

**Limit.** The decree judges substance. It does not announce discovery of an 1859 transcript, enumerate every sentence as verbatim, resolve the early chronology and visual variants, or separately declare the fire and claimed healings miracles. Its own recital expressly says none of the favors had been officially declared a miracle.

## Publication rule

Every narrative claim is written at the strength of its stratum:

- “Starr reports” for the 1871 mediated account;
- “Pernin reports” for the 1874 apparition or fire appendix;
- “the later received/shrine narrative says” for expanded wording and imagery;
- “Bishop Ricken judged” only for the decree's actual findings and operative object; and
- “this study synthesizes” for theological or historical conclusions drawn across sources.

Agreement can establish a stable core without proving verbal identity. Difference can identify human transmission without automatically negating the competent bishop's positive judgment. Neither agreement nor difference is itself proof of supernatural origin.
