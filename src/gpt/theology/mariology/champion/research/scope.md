# Champion: Apparitions, Catechetical Mission, and Ecclesial Judgment — Research Scope

## Identity and governing question

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/champion/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; Mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** a single-apparition historical, theological, and authority-conscious reference monograph governed by `guidance/theology/mariology.md`, especially its apparition-judgment, corpus, and currentness rules, together with `guidance/editorial.md`. It is not a liturgical proper, a cause-of-saints position paper, or an official shrine history.
- **Place and jurisdiction:** the reported events occurred near Robinsonville (now Champion), Wisconsin, in October 1859. The territory then belonged to the Diocese of Milwaukee under Bishop John Martin Henni. The Diocese of Green Bay was erected from Milwaukee territory in 1868; the controlling apparition act is the 8 December 2010 decree of David L. Ricken, Bishop of Green Bay. Historical jurisdiction and later territorial competence are not collapsed.
- **Question:** What can be responsibly said about the reported 1859 apparitions and locutions to Adele Brise; how do the 1871 Starr account, the 1874 Pernin account, and later shrine oral-history tradition differ; what did Adele's catechetical mission become; how should the 1871 fire preservation be reported; exactly what did the competent bishop judge in 2010; and what did the 2022–2023 liturgical and naming acts change or leave unchanged?
- **Reader:** a serious Catholic reader seeking devotion, historical criticism, doctrinal safety, and obedience to ecclesial judgment in one account.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-16. The DDF document index, 1978 and 2024 procedural norms, current Diocese of Green Bay and National Shrine presentations, the 2023 signed decree, and Adele Brice cause records through the 27 February 2026 first session were checked through this date. No later DDF act reclassifying or superseding Bishop Ricken's 2010 judgment was located.
- **Primary thesis:** Champion's durable center is a mission of conversion through sacramental life and patient catechesis. Bishop Ricken positively judged the October 1859 events, apparitions, and locutions to exhibit the substance of supernatural character and approved the apparitions as worthy of belief, explicitly without obliging the faithful. That judgment deserves exact reception; it does not turn every later narrative detail into a contemporaneous transcript, declare the 1871 preservation or any healing a canonically verified miracle, or add anything to Christ's definitive Revelation.

## Included corpus and evidence strata

1. **The reported October 1859 events.** Three appearances are presented in the received narrative: an initial silent encounter, a second silent encounter while going to Mass, and a third encounter after Mass in which the Lady identifies herself and gives Adele a mission. The exact day of every appearance is not established uniformly. The 2010 decree's operative object is the events, apparitions, and locutions given to Adele in October 1859.
2. **Eliza Allen Starr's near-contemporary account.** Starr met Adele and a younger interpreter during a fundraising visit and published the account in the first series of *Patron Saints* in 1871. The checked 1883 reissue preserves the passage at printed pp. 298–301. It is mediated through an interpreter and openly devotional, but it is the closest published narrative located. It reports the catechetical commission and Adele's already visible work. It also differs materially from later narrative in season, journey sequence, visual description, and the extent of the reported words.
3. **Peter Pernin's 1874 account.** Pernin's *The Finger of God Is There!* is an early original publication and an eyewitness memoir of the Peshtigo catastrophe, with an appendix about Robinsonville. Its apparition account is not direct eyewitness testimony to 1859, and its Champion fire narrative is not an eyewitness account of what occurred at the shrine during the night. It nonetheless supplies early evidence for Adele's reputation, mission, site, school, chapel, pilgrimage, and the reported preservation. The checked fire passage specifically describes Adele's women collaborators praying and processing with a statue; it does not itself supply the later full refugee, possessions, livestock, or rain scene. Printed pp. 94–102 of the 1874 English edition were checked in the Canadiana scan.
4. **Later oral and custodial tradition.** The fuller received dialogue and visual sequence appear in later shrine history, associated especially with Sister M. Dominica's 1955 *The Chapel: Our Lady of Good Help* and later diocesan/shrine presentations. The 2023 National Shrine history PDF expressly says its recount draws on the Shrine history book, diocesan archived information, and word-of-mouth testimony. That current official custodial narrative was checked; the 1955 book itself was not independently collated in full during this pass. The publication therefore attributes this layer instead of treating it as a stenographic 1859 deposition.
5. **The 1871 regional fire and site preservation.** Pernin's 1874 appendix, the later shrine tradition, and the 2010 decree are included with distinct functions. The fire episode may evidence historical survival and devotional reception. It is not treated as a prediction contained in the reported 1859 message, a controlled scientific experiment, proof that victims lacked faith, or a separately adjudicated miracle.
6. **The 2010 diocesan inquiry and decree.** The signed four-page decree controls the status. Its recitals, expert review, doctrinal finding, character finding, spiritual-fruit evidence, and operative clause are distinguished. Only the operative object and exact formula are used to state the ecclesial judgment.
7. **Later institutional, liturgical, and naming reception.** National-shrine designation, including inconsistent public dates and the unlocated underlying approval act; the Dicastery for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments' 15 December 2022 acknowledgment; Bishop Ricken's 20 April 2023 decree; the shrine's annual 9 October solemnity; and the title change from Our Lady of Good Help to Our Lady of Champion are included according to their objects. Canons 1230–1234, USCCB national-shrine norms, and particular-calendar law establish the relevant legal categories. None is treated as a new DDF supernatural judgment.
8. **Adele's cause.** The 2024 USCCB consultation, October 2025 Dicastery for the Causes of Saints *nihil obstat*, 28 December 2025 diocesan edict, 30 January 2026 opening decree, and 27 February first session are included only to state current reception. “Servant of God” means that a cause has opened; it is not a judgment of heroic virtue, beatification, canonization, or an enlargement of the apparition corpus. The Causes-of-Saints *nihil obstat* is kept distinct from both the 1978 apparition-law phrase and the DDF's 2024 conclusion of the same name.
9. **Doctrinal interpretation.** Scripture, *Dei Verbum*, *Lumen gentium*, the Catechism, the 2024 DDF norms, and *Mater Populi Fidelis* govern the distinction between public and private revelation, Christ's unique mediation, Marian intercession, conversion, judgment, catechesis, Eucharist, penance, mission, and pastoral freedom.

## Exclusions

- No claim is made to reconstruct an exact 1859 French-language transcript, identify the language of every reported utterance, determine the natural or supernatural mechanism of perception, or harmonize every early and later detail.
- The publication does not reproduce the complete 1955 shrine book, modern copyrighted shrine prose, devotional prayers, or a full transcription of the official decrees. Quotations are short and claim-driven; complete content is supplied by original analytical paraphrase.
- No attempt is made to authenticate every healing, favor, relic, crutch, weather report, pilgrimage story, later image description, or internet compilation. The 2010 decree itself says no claimed favor had been officially declared a miracle.
- The fire section does not settle disputed casualty totals, acreage, fire mechanics, meteorology, or a universal theory of providence. It does not call the east-side fire at Champion the identical flame front that burned Peshtigo across Green Bay.
- The study does not infer that the 1859 warning predicted the October 1871 fires. No checked early source makes that identification.
- It does not use private revelation to bind conscience, replace ordinary catechetical formation, supersede sacramental discipline, justify fear commerce, or excuse neglect of emergency planning, medical care, safeguarding, education, or works of justice.

## Material disagreements and interpretive controls

### Chronology and visual description

Starr's 1871 account calls the first encounter a “summer day” while Adele was returning from Mass, places another appearance a few weeks later on the way to Mass and a third that afternoon, and describes a veiled figure. Pernin's 1874 appendix gives three successive Sunday-return encounters and no grist-mill episode. The later received narrative begins with a silent encounter on a grist-mill errand, then places the second and third on the journey to and from Mass on what is believed to be 9 October; it adds the maple and hemlock, yellow sash, crown of stars, and golden hair. The 2010 decree defines the month as October but does not adjudicate every narrative variance. The publication preserves rather than silently harmonizes these differences.

### The reported words

The catechetical core is early: Starr records gathering neglected children, teaching what they need for salvation, catechism, the Sign of the Cross, and approach to the sacraments. Pernin records instruction, Eucharistic understanding, preparation for First Communion, and the assurance of help. The fuller identification as Queen of Heaven, prayer for sinners, general confession, conditional punishment warning, beatitude addressed to the companions, vineyard question, and expanded commission belong to the later received wording; excerpts of that fuller wording are quoted in the 2010 decree. Approval of the substance of the accounts is not treated as a declaration that every later sentence is an inerrant verbatim transcript.

### The fire

Pernin reports that Adele's women collaborators prayed and carried a Marian statue in procession; he describes a charred fence, surrounding destruction, and spared buildings. The wider refugee crowd and its possessions and livestock belong to later Shrine narration in the sources checked. Pernin expressly refuses to pronounce either the Champion preservation or his Peshtigo tabernacle preservation a miracle and refuses to forestall ecclesiastical judgment. Later shrine history adds or develops details, including a downpour. The 2010 decree treats preservation as part of the evidence and says the fire stopped at the shrine's parameters, but its operative clause judges the October 1859 events and apparitions. The fire is therefore reported as an early documented preservation tradition with strong ecclesial reception, not a separately declared miracle.

### Punishment, mercy, and prophecy

The later wording's warning about conversion, penance, and punishment is read through Scripture's conditional calls to repentance, not as a portrait of Mary restraining a merciless Christ or as a timetable for disaster. The private-revelation form may exhort; it does not add a doctrine of divine judgment, identify a later catastrophe without evidence, or remove human freedom. God's justice and mercy are one in the saving mission of Christ.
