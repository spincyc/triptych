# Champion 1871 Fire-Claim Audit

Checked through 2026-07-16. This audit governs every reference to the October 1871 fire in the publication.

## Minimum verified claim

On the night of 8–9 October 1871, destructive fires affected northeastern Wisconsin on both sides of Green Bay. A fire threatened the Robinsonville chapel, school, community, and enclosed grounds associated with Adele Brise. Peter Pernin's 1874 appendix reports narrowly that Adele's “pious girls” hastened to the chapel, carried a Marian statue in procession, and prayed; he says they waited for a lull or turned another way when wind and flame blocked them. By morning the school, chapel, and surrounding fence remained while neighboring houses and fences had burned. He says the fence was charred in several places and the fire did not cross the narrow path into the enclosure. The wider refugee crowd, possessions, livestock, and rain belong to the later shrine narrative unless another early source is produced. That later narrative and Bishop Ricken's 2010 decree receive the preservation as a significant answer-to-prayer tradition and spiritual fruit.

## Geographic safeguard

Pernin distinguishes the fire at Robinsonville from the conflagration he personally survived at Peshtigo. He locates it roughly nine or ten leagues away and separated from Peshtigo by the waters of Green Bay, while describing it as a similar wind-and-fire storm at the same time. The publication may use the conventional regional label “Peshtigo fire” when directly reflecting the 2010 decree or shrine usage, but explanatory prose calls the Champion event part of the regional October 1871 fires. It must not imply that one flame front physically crossed Green Bay.

## Witness safeguard

- Pernin is an eyewitness to Peshtigo, not to the shrine's night.
- His Champion appendix is early testimony but does not name every informant, identify every person present, or supply a modern investigative protocol.
- The current shrine history is a later custodial synthesis drawing on history-book, archival, and word-of-mouth material.
- The 2010 decree is competent evidence of ecclesial reception and of the role the episode played in the bishop's judgment; it is not a wildfire-science report.

## Physical-detail safeguard

The following may be stated from Pernin: school and chapel survived; the enclosing fence survived but was charred in several places; surrounding buildings and fences were destroyed; a narrow path bounded the enclosure; and Adele's women collaborators prayed and processed with a Marian statue. Do not attribute the later full refugee scene to him, call the property literally untouched, or describe every object as unscorched. Do not give casualty or acreage figures unless tied to a separately audited civil or scientific source; exact totals are unnecessary to the theological argument and vary across summaries.

## Downpour safeguard

The 1874 English appendix inspected at printed pp. 99–101 does not include the later sentence that relief arrived as a downpour before its morning description. The 2023 Shrine history includes the downpour within a passage it attributes to its history book and labels as a recount of Pernin. The publication therefore treats rain as a later shrine-tradition detail and does not quote it as wording verified in Pernin's 1874 passage.

## Miracle-status safeguard

Pernin's “Important consideration” expressly refuses to pronounce either the Champion preservation or his tabernacle's preservation at Peshtigo a miracle and refuses to forestall ecclesiastical judgment. Bishop Ricken's 2010 recitals use the Champion preservation as favorable evidence, but the operative clause judges the October 1859 events, apparitions, and locutions. No separate canonical miracle decree for the fire was located. Use “reported preservation,” “received answer-to-prayer tradition,” or “providential preservation in the shrine's devotional reception”; do not state “the Church declared the fire miracle authentic.”

## Providence and victims safeguard

Catholic providence does not license a moral ranking of survivors and victims. The survival of persons or buildings cannot establish greater merit, while death or loss cannot establish punishment, unbelief, or divine rejection (cf. Lk 13:1–5; Jn 9:1–3). The episode may call forth gratitude, intercession, solidarity, and hope. It may not become an accusation against those killed, a promise that shrine devotion prevents disaster, or a reason to neglect evacuation and emergency authority.

## Prophecy safeguard

No checked early source identifies the reported 1859 warning as a prediction of the 1871 fires. The twelve-year interval and thematic resemblance between a conditional warning and later catastrophe do not establish a prophecy-fulfillment relation. The publication explicitly rejects this inference.

## Rights and source status

Pernin's 1874 text is public domain. Only short phrases are quoted. The Canadiana page images and OCR remain external evidence. Modern shrine wording and official decree text retain their own status and are quoted only as needed for source and juridic precision.
