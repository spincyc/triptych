## Material findings

CCC 1667–1673 supplies an exact official control for the distinction between
ordinary grotto water and sacramentals instituted by the Church and used with
her intercession and prayer. It does not deny that water may be blessed
according to an approved rite. Its exorcism treatment does not control a
Lourdes claim in this edition.

CCC 1700–1706 controls human dignity, reason, freedom, conscience, and
responsibility in the treatment of Bernadette, sick and disabled pilgrims, and
other vulnerable persons. CCC 1734–1735 controls voluntariness and recognizes
that coercion, fear, immaturity, and other factors may diminish or nullify
responsibility. They strengthen agency, consent, and safeguarding without
proving supernatural origin or deciding any person's subjective guilt.

CCC 2111 controls the rejection of efficacy attributed to mere external
performance, quantity, dosage, or a curative guarantee apart from interior
disposition. CCC 2116–2117 controls the rejection of magic and attempted
occult domination when devotion is distorted into a controllable technique.
Its divination material is not independently developed here. These passages
govern distortions; they do not classify authorized Lourdes devotion, ordinary
prayer, source criticism, or every water gesture as superstition, magic, or
divination.

## Assessed passages not used

CCC 391–395 concerns the fall, choice, finitude, and providentially limited
action of Satan and the demons. CCC 550 concerns Christ's exorcisms as signs
of the Kingdom. Lourdes develops neither a demonology nor an exorcism thesis.
Its general account of Christ's healings as signs of the Kingdom remains
governed by existing broader doctrinal sources, not exorcism-specific CCC 550.

CCC 1857–1859 states the conditions for mortal sin. The study discusses
sinners, conversion, penance, and responsibility without classifying any
person's subjective mortal guilt. CCC 2846–2854 offers compatible teaching on
temptation, vigilance, and deliverance from the Evil One, but no consequential
claim depends on that technical treatment. These four passages are not bound
merely because exact records are available.
