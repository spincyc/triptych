## Material findings

CCC 1857–1859 requires a correction in two places. The hell synthesis must
name grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent rather than reduce
mortal sin to generic free persistence. The whole-moral-law comparison must
distinguish serious moral objects and circumstances from the additional
conditions required for personal mortal sin.

CCC 1700–1706 and 1734–1735 strengthen the already bounded treatment of
dignity, agency, coercion, fear, immaturity, and diminished responsibility.
They do not authorize a judgment about any named person's subjective guilt.

CCC 2111 and 2116–2117 strengthen the work's rejection of automatic-promise
thinking, merely external performance, magic, divination, and occult control.
They govern distortions of Fátima devotion; they do not classify the approved
devotion, Marian entrustment, or popular piety itself as superstition.

CCC 391–395 and 550 concern fallen angels and Christ's exorcisms. The Fátima
study maps demons as elements of a reported vision but does not develop a
demonology or exorcism argument. CCC 1667–1673 likewise supplies no needed
sacramental or exorcism control here. CCC 2846–2854 offers compatible teaching
on temptation and deliverance from the Evil One, but no present consequential
claim depends on that technical treatment. These four passages are therefore
not bound merely because they are available.
