# Fátima: Event, Message, and Ecclesial Reception — Research Scope

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/fatima/main.tex`.

- **Genre and profile:** single-apparition historical and theological reference monograph; `guidance/theology/mariology.md` governs, especially its apparition-judgment and currentness rules. - **Jurisdiction and competent act:** Latin Church; Diocese of Leiria (now Leiria-Fátima); Bishop José Alves Correia da Silva's pastoral letter of 13 October 1930.
- **Question:** What occurred and was reported at Fátima, how did the documentary corpus develop, exactly what did the competent authority judge, what is contained in the three-part secret and later Fátima cycle, and how has the Church authoritatively interpreted and received it?
- **Reader:** a serious Catholic reader who wants devotion, history, source criticism, and ecclesial obedience held together.

- **Primary thesis:** Fátima is a Church-received private revelation whose center is evangelical conversion: prayer, penance, Eucharistic reparation, intercession for sinners, Marian discipleship, and peace in history. Its worthy-of-belief status permits prudent human assent; it neither adds to Christ's definitive Revelation nor turns every remembered phrase, later experience, political application, or private interpretation into an inerrant oracle.

## Included corpus and evidence strata

The publication includes the following, but keeps their dates and authority distinct:

1. **Contemporaneous 1917 records:** parish-priest interrogations beginning 27 May 1917; other clerical and lay interviews; letters; pro-, anti-, and secular press; and witness testimony in the Shrine's critical documentary selection. These are the strongest evidence for what was publicly said, asked, observed, and remembered close to the six events. They do not by themselves prove supernatural origin.
2. **The six 1917 apparitions:** 13 May, 13 June, 13 July, 19 August after the children's detention on 13 August, 13 September, and 13 October. These are the object of the 1930 decree's operative worthy-of-belief formula and permission of cult.
3. **The diocesan process and decision:** the 1922 commission, Lúcia's 1924 canonical interrogation, the commission report, and the exact 1930 operative clauses. The document does not upgrade “worthy of belief” into an infallible definition, an obligation of faith, or a certification of every later text.
4. **Later retrospective records:** Lúcia's 1922 report, memoirs of 1935, 1937, and 1941, correspondence, and later authorized narratives. These provide the fullest account of the Angel cycle, the 1917 dialogue, and the first two parts of the secret, but are identified as later recollection and spiritual interpretation rather than contemporaneous transcripts.
5. **The three-part secret:** the first and second parts as written in the Third Memoir of 31 August 1941, with the Fourth Memoir addition of 8 December 1941; the third part written at Tuy on 3 January 1944; and the CDF's complete 2000 documentary publication and theological commentary. The publication gives a complete element-by-element content map and short verbal anchors, not a wholesale reproduction of the Holy See's modern English translation.
6. **The wider Fátima spiritual family:** the reported 1915 occurrences, 1916 Angel cycle, 1921 seventh encounter, Pontevedra experiences of 1925–1926, and Tuy experience of 1929. Their ecclesial reception is documented, but none is silently placed inside the exact 1930 operative clause, which names the children's Cova da Iria visions from May through October 1917.
7. **Papal and ecclesial reception:** Pius XII's 1942 and 1952 acts, John Paul II's 1981–1984 entrustments and 2000 publication, Benedict XVI's 2010 interpretation, Francis's 2017 canonization and 2022 act, the seers' causes, and the Shrine's pastoral development. These acts establish reception according to their objects; canonization does not authenticate every claimed event, and a consecration does not create dogma.

## Exclusions

- No attempt is made to certify a physical mechanism for the 13 October solar phenomenon, adjudicate every witness report, prove a global astronomical displacement, or resolve every natural, psychological, or miraculous hypothesis.
- The study does not reproduce complete third-party modern translations, every later locution attributed to Lúcia, every devotional promise, every papal Fátima reference, every political appropriation, or every controversy in polemical literature.
- It excludes “fourth secret,” unpublished-text, date-setting, and automatic-salvation theories except where a controlling public source directly answers them.
- It does not declare the present spiritual or political “conversion of Russia,” calculate a promised period of peace, or treat any nation as the unique embodiment of evil.
- It does not use private revelation to settle doctrine, bind consciences, replace Scripture or liturgy, direct political allegiance, or justify harmful penances.

## Material discrepancies and interpretive controls

### The October war statement

The parish priest's 16 October 1917 interview records Lúcia as reporting `a guerra acaba ainda hoje` (“the war ends today”), and the 19 October Formigão interview presses the problem because fighting continued. Lúcia replied that she did not know and may not have understood the Lady well; other early witnesses remembered “soon” or a conditional ending, while the later memoir gives “the war is going to end.” The First World War ended in November 1918, not on 13 October 1917.

This discrepancy is published because it is consequential. It does not warrant secretly correcting the early record or pretending all sources say the same thing. Nor does it automatically negate the bishop's later global judgment. Cardinal Ratzinger's 2000 account of interior vision supplies the controlling theological frame: a real divine impulse, if present, is received and translated through the visionary's finite faculties, images, language, memory, and historical setting. A worthy-of-belief judgment is not verbal inerrancy.

### Secret chronology

The detailed first and second parts were written in 1941, after the Russian revolutions, the emergence of Soviet communism, the 1938 aurora, and the opening of the Second World War. The third part was written in 1944. The early July 1917 record verifies that a secret existed and a miracle was promised, but does not contain its later detailed wording. The study therefore treats the ecclesially received secret seriously while refusing to use the 1941 text as if it were a stenographic sheet independently deposited in 1917.

### Symbol, condition, and freedom

The 2000 CDF commentary is controlling for Catholic interpretation: private revelation is subordinate to public Revelation; interior visions are symbolic rather than photographic; prophecy calls the present will rather than satisfying curiosity; time and events may be compressed; and the future is not fatalistically fixed. John Paul II's 1981 survival is a privileged papal application of the third part, not necessarily an exhaustive one-to-one decoding of every image.
