# Guadalupe: Tradition, Image, and Ecclesial Reception — Research Scope

## Identity and governing question

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/guadalupe/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** single-apparition received-tradition, historical, theological, and ecclesial-status reference monograph; `guidance/theology/mariology.md` and the universal editorial standard govern. This is not a Mass proper, a devotional prayer book, or a new canonical process.
- **Rite and jurisdiction:** Latin Church; the originating cult belongs to the Archdiocese of Mexico. Later papal and continental liturgical reception has a wider object. No modern diocesan or Holy See decree formally adjudicating supernatural origin was located and none is invented.
- **Question:** What does the received Tepeyac narrative say, what evidence survives for the narrative and early cult, how should the image and Nahuatl text be read, what has competent ecclesial authority actually received, and which historical or scientific claims remain disputed or unverified?
- **Reader:** a serious Catholic reader who wants devotion, historical criticism, Indigenous dignity, theology, and obedience to ecclesial authority held together.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-16. The current DDF document index, 2024 procedural norms, Holy See texts, the Archdiocese of Mexico's jurisdiction-and-custody presentation, Basilica sources, and the newest papal reception located (Leo XIV, 12 December 2025) were rechecked. No later DDF reclassification or formal event-origin decree was located.
- **Primary thesis:** Guadalupe is one of the strongest received Marian traditions in Catholic life: Popes narrate the appearance, Juan Diego is canonized, the shrine and image are deeply received, and the feast is celebrated throughout America. That reception warrants confident Catholic devotion and theological reflection. It does not erase the late and disputed transmission of the full narrative, create a decree that has not been located, or authenticate every popular scientific claim about the image.

## Included evidence strata

1. **The received 1531 tradition:** four encounters of Mary with the Indigenous Christian Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin at Tepeyac, the healing of Juan Bernardino, Castilian roses, the image on the tilma, and the request for a sacred house. The complete content is mapped from the 1649 *Huei tlamahuiçoltica* without presenting that publication as a contemporaneous transcript.
2. **Early cult and image evidence:** the chapel and cult are documentable by the mid-1550s; the 1556 Montúfar inquiry records controversy over an image and claims of miracles. It establishes an early Guadalupan cult, not by itself the complete Juan Diego narrative.
3. **Foundational printed narratives:** Miguel Sánchez's Spanish *Imagen* (1648) and Luis Laso de la Vega's Nahuatl *Huei tlamahuiçoltica* (1649), including the *Nican mopohua*. Their relation, genre, dating, authorship, and intended audiences remain materially disputed and are not silently harmonized.
4. **Later historical inquiries and devotion:** the 1666 *Informaciones*, later patronage, liturgical offices, canonical coronation, national and continental reception, and political appropriations. A late juridical inquiry is not converted into a 1531 deposition.
5. **Modern authoritative reception:** Pius X, Pius XII, the continental feast decree, *Ecclesia in America* 11, Juan Diego's canonization, and the teachings of Benedict XVI, Francis, and Leo XIV. Each act is used according to its object: cult, liturgy, holiness, papal teaching, or pastoral interpretation.
6. **The sacred image:** visible iconographic features and authoritative theological readings are distinguished from claims about pigment, fabric, stars, eyes, temperature, preservation, or an alleged agency endorsement.

## Exclusions and limits

- The work does not publish a project translation of the *Nican mopohua*, reproduce a complete protected modern translation, or claim competence to settle Classical Nahuatl philology. Narrative content is analytically restated and tied to the Basilica's current numbered Nahuatl/Spanish presentation (1–218), checked alongside the public-domain 1649 witness; the modern division and translation are custodial aids, not a project critical edition.
- It does not adjudicate supernatural origin, identify an artist, establish the physical mechanism of the image, or infer a miracle merely from an unresolved material question.
- It does not repeat the popular `coatlaxopeuh`/“she who crushes the serpent” etymology as established Nahuatl, assign every color or star one compulsory Indigenous meaning, or treat modern symbolism charts as sixteenth-century depositions.
- It does not use papal devotion, canonization, feast, basilica status, coronation, or pastoral fruit as a substitute for a modern fact decree. Nor does it use the absence of such a decree to deny the real force of the Church's exceptional reception.
- It excludes unsupported claims that NASA authenticated the image; that the eyes contain a proved photographic scene; that the cloth has a heartbeat, body temperature, music, or a hovering image; that the stars uniquely encode the sky of 12 December 1531; or that no human pigment, brushwork, repair, or intervention exists.
- Historical trauma—including conquest, forced conversion, epidemic, and exploitation—remains part of the theological account, as does Indigenous agency.

## Claim and authority controls

- “Received narrative” names the literary story in the 1649 witness and subsequent ecclesial reception; it does not assign that wording to 1531 as a transcript.
- “Appearance” may be used when accurately reporting papal teaching or ordinary devotional speech inside the already qualified tradition. Historical analysis otherwise uses “reported” or “tradition.”
- Juan Diego's canonization authoritatively proposes his holiness and public cult. It does not make every biographical detail or technical image claim an object of canonization.
- Christ alone is Redeemer and the one mediator. Guadalupan maternity, help, and intercession are wholly received from Christ, subordinate to him, and governed by *Lumen gentium* 56, 60–62, CCC 970, and the DDF's 2025 *Mater Populi Fidelis*.
- Papal and liturgical reception is stated positively and in full. Its juridic object is not renamed a `constat de supernaturalitate` or a post-2024 `Nihil obstat`.
- The title's precise linguistic route is unresolved. The printed Nahuatl text uses `Guadalupe`; proposals deriving it from Nahuatl are treated as proposals, not as the message's secret key.
