## Controlling historical disagreement

The Basilica's documentary presentation argues for Antonio Valeriano and a sixteenth-century *Nican mopohua*. Rodrigo Martínez Baracs (UNAM, 2016) records substantial scholarly division and concludes that documentary and philological evidence does not permit a secure choice between a sixteenth-century composition and an early-seventeenth-century composition or reworking. Austin Cruz (2025) reads the 1649 work as a unified Guadalupan hagiography shaped by Laso de la Vega for a Nahua-Christian audience and summarizes the authorship dispute. The publication reports this disagreement rather than choosing a devotional or skeptical certainty that the evidence does not supply.

## Negative evidence discipline

“Silence” is not proof that an event did not occur, especially where archives are lost and colonial transmission was unequal. It is nevertheless evidence when expected, extensive writings—especially those of Bishop Zumárraga and early chroniclers—do not preserve the story later told. The monograph therefore uses two propositions together:

1. the mid-sixteenth-century cult and image are historically documentable; and
2. the detailed Juan Diego narrative has a materially later and disputed surviving textual history.

Neither proposition cancels the other.
