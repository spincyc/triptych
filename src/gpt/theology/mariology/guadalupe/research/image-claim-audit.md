# Guadalupe Image-Claim Audit

Checked through 2026-07-16. This is an evidence-control record, not a declaration that the sacred image is or is not miraculous.

| Recurring claim | Audit result | Publication treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “NASA examined the image and declared it inexplicable” | No NASA report, laboratory record, mission, or official agency statement was located. | Rejected as attribution. No agency claim appears in the publication. |
| The eyes contain a photographically exact room scene or identifiable persons | Public claims rely on enlargements and interpretive pattern matching; no transparent, independently replicated imaging protocol with preregistered identification criteria was located. | Not used as evidence. The visible downcast eyes are treated iconographically. |
| The image or eyes reproduce the Purkinje–Sanson optical effect | A physiological analogy is not the same as a controlled identification in a small, aged painted surface. No independently replicated protocol controlling resolution, restoration, and observer bias was located. | Classified as unverified technical apologetics. |
| The tilma maintains human body temperature, a heartbeat, or music | No competent conservation or biomedical protocol was located; such claims frequently circulate without primary data. | Excluded. |
| The image floats above the fibers or contains no pigment, binder, brushwork, sketch, or human intervention anywhere | Published examinations and art-historical discussion do not support such an absolute bundled claim; visible additions, aging, and intervention history complicate it. | No categorical material-origin claim is made. A future transparent conservation study could refine the evidence. |
| The garment is certainly a short-lived cactus-fiber `ayate` whose survival is physically impossible | Terminology, fiber identification, environmental history, mounting, and conservation require documented analysis. Age and survival alone do not specify cause. | The work uses “cloth,” “mantle,” or traditional “tilma” without turning a lifespan estimate into proof. |
| The stars exactly reproduce the sky of Mexico on 12 Dec 1531 | Proposed matches depend on orientation, date/calendar, location, selection, and projection choices; no unique, independently validated encoding was located. | Stars are read first through received Marian iconography and papal inculturation, not as a planetarium. |
| Every visual feature has one fixed Aztec meaning that Juan Diego and all Nahua viewers would have read identically | Iconographic proposals vary; cultures are neither static codebooks nor homogeneous audiences. | Indigenous resonances are presented as disciplined possibilities, not compulsory decoded speech. |
| Preservation, the acid-spill incident probably dating to about 1784–1785, and the 1921 bombing prove supernatural origin | Survival and restoration history are relevant to reception and conservation, but causation requires evidence and a competent judgment. The acid event survives in a later 1820–1823 file with uncertainty over date, liquid/concentration, exposure, and damage; a next-day newspaper report bounds the observable 1921 damage. | Reported as history only where sourced; not used as a substitute apparition decree or technical impossibility claim. |
| A human artistic contribution would disprove Guadalupe's spiritual or ecclesial meaning | Catholic veneration is directed through an image to the holy person represented; the Church routinely receives human sacred art. | Rejected as a false dilemma. Material questions remain open without making devotion depend on a laboratory outcome. |

## Technical-source boundary

Philip S. Callahan's *The Tilma under Infra-red Radiation* (1981) is a limited published infrared/artistic analysis, not a comprehensive modern conservation campaign. Art-historical studies, including Jeanette Favrot Peterson's work, offer human-craft genealogies and report contested conservation evidence. The monograph uses these sources to defeat categorical internet claims, not to announce a definitive artist or material mechanism.

No sampling, new imaging, or laboratory work was performed for this project. Independent textile, pigment, imaging, conservation, and art-historical review remains outstanding.
