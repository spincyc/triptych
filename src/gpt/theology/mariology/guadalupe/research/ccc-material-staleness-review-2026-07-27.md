## Material findings

CCC 1700–1706 supplies an exact official control for the study's theological
account of human dignity, reason, freedom, conscience, and responsibility.
Those principles bear on Indigenous agency, colonial coercion, adult
responsibility, and care for vulnerable persons. They do not decide the
subjective culpability of an individual conqueror, missionary, bishop, or
witness; establish a disputed historical event; or prove supernatural origin.

CCC 2111 supplies an exact official control for the work's distinction between
embodied relational veneration and a distortion that assigns efficacy to bare
external performance, treats the object as a manipulable power, or purchases
guaranteed protection apart from interior conversion. It does not classify the
Guadalupan tradition, an approved sacred image, kneeling, candles, touch,
flowers, songs, or processions as superstition.

## Assessed passages not used

CCC 1734–1735 addresses imputability and diminished responsibility. The
Guadalupe study names agency and coercion but makes no technical judgment of
diminished personal guilt requiring that locus. CCC 1857–1859 concerns the
conditions of mortal sin; no present claim classifies personal mortal guilt.
CCC 2116–2117 concerns divination, recourse to demons, conjuring, magic, and
occult domination. The study's references to secret decoders, constellation
codes, “magical” poverty, and manipulation are evidentiary analogies rather
than an argument about those practices.

CCC 391–395 and 550 concern fallen angels and Christ's exorcisms. CCC
1667–1673 concerns sacramentals and exorcism. CCC 2846–2854 concerns
temptation and deliverance from the Evil One in the Lord's Prayer. None
controls a consequential claim in this edition. These seven passages are not
bound merely because exact records are available.
