\section{A practical test for Marian claims}

Before repeating a consequential Marian claim, ask:

\begin{enumerate}
\item What exact proposition or practice is asserted?
\item Is its object doctrine, history, liturgy, devotion, private revelation,
discipline, or theological synthesis?
\item What competent source proposes it, in what genre and at what locus?
\item Is the proposal definitive, authentic non-definitive, juridical,
administrative, permissive, or merely theological?
\item What assent or conduct follows, and what does not follow?
\item Does the claim preserve Christ's unique mediation and the completeness
of public Revelation?
\item Are historical disagreement, translation, rights, jurisdiction, and
currentness stated where material?
\end{enumerate}

This test is not suspicion toward Mary. It is the discipline by which Catholic
speech remains worthy of her fiat: receptive to God's word, truthful about
what has been given, and unwilling to substitute invention for grace.
