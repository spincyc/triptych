## Governing witness

The sole reproduced prayer witness is Dom Gaspar Lefebvre, O.S.B., *Daily
Missal with Vespers for Sundays and Feasts* (Lophem near Bruges: St. Andrew's
Abbey; St. Paul, Minnesota: E. M. Lohmann, 1925), printed pp. 13–14.

- Complete Regina Coeli Latin and facing historical English: beginning at the
  foot of printed p. 13 and continuing on p. 14, corresponding to PDF pp. 47–48.
- Adjacent historical daily, seasonal, posture, and indulgence context: printed
  pp. 12–14; the paired Angelus also appears on p. 13.
- Same edition's complete Hail Mary: p. 5, recorded to identify the whole checked
  prayer cluster but not imported into the Regina Coeli, which contains no Hail
  Mary abbreviation.
- Checked scan SHA-256:

## Orthography, punctuation, and comparison witnesses

- The publication title follows the requested conventional English form
  *Regina Coeli*. The selected Latin prayer retains Lefebvre's `Regina coeli`.
  The 1999 *Enchiridion* prints `Regina caeli`; its
  orthography is not retrofitted into the 1925 witness.
- Historical accent marks; the spellings `coeli`, `laetáre`, `Jesu`, `ejus`,
  `praesta`, `quaesumus`, `perpétuae`, and `vitae`; punctuation; and the
  source's collect conclusion remain as printed by Lefebvre rather than being
  normalized to current `caeli`, `Iesu`, and `eius` Latin.
- The four Latin antiphon lines end, in order, `;`, `:`, `:`, `.`; the four
  historical-English lines end `!`, `,`, `;`, `.`. The collect retains
  `dignátus es: praesta, quaesumus; ut`, and the fourth English line remains
  exactly “Pour for us to Him thy prayer.”
- The fourth *Enchiridion* prints `Per Christum Dominum nostrum`; any historical
  `Per eundem Christum Dominum nostrum` in Lefebvre remains the selected
  witness's conclusion. The current book controls present discipline, not the
  historical prayer text.
- Lefebvre's historical English—including its capitalization, second-person
  singular pronouns, verbal forms, and wording for Mary's bearing, Christ's
  Resurrection, intercession, and eternal joy—is retained without modernization.
- The 2005 *Compendium* prints distinct UK and USA English versions. Neither is
  chosen, blended, corrected against the other, or used to modernize Lefebvre.
- The modern Latin comparison witness places no `V.` and `R.` before the four
  antiphon lines but does identify the following Easter versicle and response.
  Typography does not turn the antiphon into an invented dialogue.
- Line breaks and column layout are editorial presentation only; the word order
  and vocal content remain those of pp. 13–14.

## Collation performed

The following internal checks were completed against the page images:

1. every Latin antiphon, alleluia, versicle, response, collect word, conclusion,
   and Amen on printed pp. 13–14 (PDF pp. 47–48) was compared to the rendered
   source text;
2. the facing historical English was checked line by line without modernization;
3. accents, spelling, punctuation, and capitalization were retained;
4. the p. 12–14 historical rubrics were separated from vocal text and current
   discipline;
5. the selected text was compared with the fourth *Enchiridion*'s current Latin;
6. the Compendium's UK and USA forms were checked only to prevent accidental
   conflation; and
7. no legendary wording, musical direction, or unreceived project prayer was
   introduced.

Independent human line-by-line collation and chant review remain outstanding.

## Status and rights

The antiphon has a Roman liturgical use; the displayed antiphon-plus-versicle-
and-collect form is also used as an Easter-Time popular exercise in place of the
Angelus. Printing it here does not make this publication a liturgical book or
authorize it for any ritual context.

The 1925 edition's historical ecclesiastical permissions govern that edition
only. The edition is public domain in the United States by publication date.
It says “All rights reserved,” and this audit did not establish the translator
or author of the exact historical English; Lefebvre's 1966 death therefore does
not itself establish a non-US rights endpoint. Rights outside the United States
remain unresolved. The received Latin and historical-English prayer text is
excluded from Triptych's CC BY 4.0 grant.

No current proprietary English Missal, Liturgy of the Hours, Bible, Compendium,
or devotional translation is reproduced. Current official texts are cited for
discipline, doctrine, and comparison only. The historical English may be prayed
devotionally, but it is not represented as a competent-authority-approved
vernacular text for gaining the indulgence in every territory; *Enchiridion*
norm 22 governs that question.
