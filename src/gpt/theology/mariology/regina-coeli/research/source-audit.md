# Source Audit — The Regina Coeli

Online and mutable sources were checked through 21 July 2026. The reproduced
prayer is governed by `research/prayer-text-audit.md`; official modern forms are
comparison and discipline sources, not permission to conflate translations.

## Scripture and doctrine

| Claim | Source and exact locus | Authority, use, and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Christ truly rose, fulfilling his own announcement; the Gospel proclamation, not a later Marian story, is the prayer's center. | Matthew 16:21; 28:1–10; Mark 8:31; 16:1–8; Luke 9:22; 24:1–12; John 20:1–18; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, 20–28; CCC 638–658. | Public Revelation and authoritative doctrine. The prayer does not assert a canonical Easter appearance to Mary. |
| The invitation to joy echoes Gabriel's greeting and is fulfilled within Paschal joy. | Luke 1:28; *Directory on Popular Piety* 196. | The Directory expressly links the antiphon to Gabriel's invitation. This is ecclesial reception, not a claim that Luke contains the medieval antiphon. |
| Mary's title as Queen is derived from Christ the King and is limited and analogous rather than parallel sovereignty. | Pius XII, *Ad caeli Reginam* 8–9, 30, 34, 39; *Lumen gentium* 59; CCC 966. [Holy See encyclical](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_11101954_ad-caeli-reginam.html). | Governs “Queen of Heaven.” *Ad caeli Reginam* 39 expressly reserves kingship in the full and strict sense to Christ and describes Mary's share as limited and analogous. |
| Mary's intercession is created, maternal, subordinate, and dependent upon Christ's unique mediation. | *Lumen gentium* 60–62; CCC 970; DDF, *Mater Populi Fidelis* 27–33 and 45–55. [DDF text](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20251104_mater-populi-fidelis_en.html). | Governs `Ora pro nobis Deum` and the collect's `per eius Genitricem`. It excludes an independent source of grace or a rival advocate. |
| The Easter prayer asks for joy grounded in Christ's Resurrection and eternal life, not emotional compulsion. | Fourth *Enchiridion*, printed collect; Benedict XVI, Regina Caeli address, Easter Monday, 24 Mar. 2008. [Holy See text](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/angelus/2008/documents/hf_ben-xvi_reg_20080324_easter-monday.html). | The papal address interprets the prayer as an Easter announcement of joy to Mary centered on Christ's Pasch. It does not turn devotional joy into proof about Mary's unrecorded experiences or a promise of constant affect. |

## Historical formation and reception

| Claim | Source and exact locus | Evidentiary role and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A twelfth-century Old Roman antiphoner transmits the substantially recognizable antiphon at second Vespers within the Octave of Easter, with the final-line variant `Ora pro nobis ad Deum`. | Vatican Apostolic Library, `Arch.Cap.S.Pietro.B.79`, fol. 110r, [digital facsimile](https://digi.vatlib.it/view/MSS_Arch.Cap.S.Pietro.B.79/227); [Cantus chant record](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/218556) and [source description](https://cantusdatabase.org/source/123661). | Directly inspected primary manuscript witness with catalog records used for date, liturgical placement, and variant. It proves transmission by that time, not an author, place of composition, or exact earliest date. No manuscript image is reproduced. |
| The antiphon's author is unknown; the Directory says it “probably” dates from the tenth or eleventh century, while documentary evidence securely establishes a twelfth-century witness and later Franciscan reception. | *Directory on Popular Piety* 196; St. Peter's antiphoner `B.79`, fol. 110r; Hugh Henry, “Regina Coeli (Queen of Heaven),” *Catholic Encyclopedia* 12 (1911). [Historical transcription](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/12718b.htm). | Official pastoral synthesis, direct primary witness, and dated historical secondary study. The approximate formation date and secure documentary threshold are preserved as different claims; none identifies an author. |
| Benedict XIV's action of 20 Apr. 1742 belongs to the regulated Easter-Time substitution of the Regina Coeli for the Angelus. The official English Directory's “2 April” differs from the official Italian and Spanish forms' “20 April.” | *Directory on Popular Piety* 196: [official English](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/wss/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_en.html), [official Italian](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_it.html), and [official Spanish](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_sp.html); Apostolic Penitentiary, decree of 20 Feb. 1933, *AAS* 25 (1933), 70–71, [official volume scan](https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-25-1933-ocr.pdf). | The non-English official Directory forms and the historical indulgence record support 20 April; the shortened English date is treated as a typographical error. The original 1742 instrument was not independently recovered, so no title or formula is invented. |
| Papal indulgence grants attached to the Angelus and Regina Coeli developed before the present discipline. | Apostolic Penitentiary, decree of 20 Feb. 1933, *AAS* 25 (1933), 70–71. | Primary official historical survey of earlier grants. Its quantified concessions and conditions are superseded and are not presented as current law. |
| The story that Gregory the Great heard angels sing the first lines and supplied the last is legend, not established authorship. | Henry, “Regina Coeli,” historical attribution discussion. | Evidence for a received origin story and its classification. Henry also reports a Gregory V attribution “without good reason.” Neither is used as fact. |
| The antiphon is received in Latin liturgical tradition and in later popular Easter prayer. | Fourth *Enchiridion* source notes; *Directory* 196; Lefebvre p. 14; Pius XII, *Ad caeli Reginam* 30. | Multiple forms of reception establish their own objects. They do not prove one author, an apostolic text, or approval of this publication. |

## Translation comparison, rights, and excluded sources

- The 2005 *Compendium of the Catechism of the Catholic Church*, Appendix A,
  prints the Latin and separate UK and USA English forms. It was checked as an
  official modern comparison witness only. Neither English version is
  reproduced or merged with Lefebvre. [Official Compendium](https://press.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_en.html).
- The fourth *Enchiridion* supplies the current Latin and discipline. Its
  `caeli`, `Iesu`/`eius`, punctuation, and conclusion are not silently
  substituted for Lefebvre's accented historical text and English.
- Vatican News's current explanatory page was checked only as a contemporary
  public explanation of the Easter handoff and daily rhythm, not as a normative
  source or prayer witness. [Vatican News explanation](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/utility/text-sources/what_is_regina_coeli.html).
- No current proprietary vernacular Missal, Liturgy of the Hours, Bible,
  Compendium, or devotional translation is reproduced.
- Lefebvre's 1925 prayer wording is public domain in the United States by
  publication date and excluded from Triptych's CC BY 4.0 grant. The edition
  says “All rights reserved,” and the audit did not establish who authored or
  translated the exact historical English. Lefebvre's 1966 death therefore
  does not itself clear the translation abroad; non-US rights remain unresolved.

## Consequential negative results and unresolved review

- No author is established. The Gregory story remains legend, and the Gregory V
  attribution lacks a sound basis in the checked secondary record.
- The St. Peter's antiphoner page image and its Cantus records were directly
  checked, but no full critical edition, every medieval witness, or chant
  recension was collated. The Directory's probable formation date and the
  directly attested twelfth-century threshold remain distinct.
- The original Benedict XIV instrument reported by the Directory was not
  independently recovered and inspected; no exact act title or unverified
  quotation is supplied.
- The concession itself does not print standing, bells, chant, a church,
  communal recitation, or completion of all three daily times as conditions;
  the *Enchiridion*'s general norms 4, 17, and 22 nevertheless apply.
- No current proprietary English prayer translation was cleared for
  reproduction, so none is printed.
- Independent historical, liturgical, chant, Latin, Mariological, theological,
  legal, pastoral, rights, and ecclesiastical review remains outstanding.
- Final production completed on 21 July 2026: 8 pages; clean two-pass log and
  PDF/font/text/geometry checks; every page visually inspected; terminal rights
  colophon verified; and installed/build copies byte-identical at SHA-256
  `74789568a76ef0f25bc58cd6ad6e119dbd4bde43ffad47cbf6b55747c634b30f`.
