# The Regina Coeli — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/gpt/theology/mariology/regina-coeli/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; theology; mariology.
- **Genre and profile:** repeatable Marian-prayer reference and usable bilingual
  prayer aid governed by `guidance/editorial.md` and the Marian-prayer addendum
  in `guidance/theology/mariology.md`.
- **Rite, language, and locale:** Roman tradition of the Latin Church; English
  editorial exposition; received Latin and historical English prayer text. The
  work distinguishes the liturgical antiphon from the popular exercise that
  uses it with a versicle, response, and collect.
- **Stable title and textual name:** the requested English title is *The Regina
  Coeli*. The selected 1925 Latin witness spells the incipit `Regina coeli`; the
  current typical Latin comparison spells it `Regina caeli`. These forms are
  recorded rather than silently treated as one orthographic edition.
- **Governing question:** What is the Regina Coeli; what exact prayer does this
  publication reproduce; how does it join Incarnation and Resurrection; what
  does “Queen of Heaven” mean under Christ's primacy; how did the antiphon and
  popular exercise develop; and what current seasonal and indulgence discipline
  governs its use?
- **Intended reader:** a Catholic reader, household, catechist, study group, or
  community seeking a usable Easter-Time prayer and a source-audited account of
  its meaning, history, and status.
- **Thesis:** the Regina Coeli is centered on the risen Christ. It invites Mary
  and the Church to Easter joy because the Son whom she bore has risen as he
  said, asks her subordinate intercession, and petitions God through Christ for
  the joy of eternal life.
- **Checked-through date:** 21 July 2026. Current indulgence discipline,
  Easter-Time boundaries, papal devotional practice, and current doctrinal
  safeguards for Marian titles and intercession were checked through that date.

## Included and excluded scope

Included are the complete selected Latin and historical-English antiphon,
versicle, response, collect, conclusion, and Amen; the prayer's scriptural,
Christological, Paschal, Marian, and eschatological structure; Mary's title as
Queen under Christ's kingship; the antiphon's historical liturgical reception;
its popular use in place of the Angelus during Easter Time; the recommended
daily rhythm; and the current universal grant as printed in the Latin typical
edition of the *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum*.

Excluded are a critical edition or musical edition of every medieval witness;
an exhaustive history of the four Marian antiphons; all chant melodies,
polyphonic settings, territorial translations, local customs, and old grants;
an edition of the Liturgy of the Hours; and any claim that a legendary origin
narrative supplies authorship. The Angelus remains a separate complete leaf and
is not reduced to introductory material here.

The study does not claim a canonical Gospel scene in which the risen Christ
appears to Mary, treat Mary's joy as public Revelation beyond the scriptural
Pasch, or make the prayer a guarantee of emotional consolation, salvation, or a
temporal result.

## Source hierarchy and evidence method

1. The reproduced vocal text is governed by Lefebvre's 1925 printed page image.
2. Present universal indulgence discipline and its Latin form are governed by
   the Apostolic Penitentiary's fourth *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum* (1999),
   concession 17 §2, 2°.
3. The antiphon's devotional classification, Easter-Time replacement, probable
   broad dating, and responsible solemnization are governed by the *Directory
   on Popular Piety and the Liturgy* 196.
4. Scripture, *Lumen gentium*, the Catechism, *Ad caeli Reginam*, and *Mater
   Populi Fidelis* govern Resurrection, queenship, intercession, and Christ's
   unique mediation.
5. The 2005 *Compendium* is a current official comparison witness with distinct
   UK and USA English forms. Neither modern form is reproduced.
6. The Vatican Apostolic Library facsimile of
   `Arch.Cap.S.Pietro.B.79`, fol. 110r, together with its Cantus records,
   governs the direct twelfth-century manuscript-attestation claim and the
   recorded `ad Deum` variant. The Directory's probable earlier dating and
   references to other witnesses remain qualified rather than silently merged
   with this directly checked witness.
7. Historical secondary scholarship is used only with its limits. Reported
   Franciscan use and the Gregorian story are not promoted to facts of
   authorship or substituted for the direct manuscript evidence.

## Material qualifications

1. `Regina caeli` is a received liturgical antiphon in the Roman tradition. The
   antiphon combined with a versicle, response, and collect for daily recitation
   is also a popular exercise. Liturgical and devotional uses remain distinct.
2. In the current Roman devotional discipline it replaces the Angelus during
   Easter Time, from Easter Sunday through Pentecost Sunday inclusive. The
   1925 witness's older Holy-Saturday-to-before-Trinity rubric is reported only
   as historical practice.
3. The daily dawn, midday, and evening rhythm follows the paired Angelus custom
   and current indulgence grant. It is recommended rather than a universal
   obligation or three-exact-clock-times rule.
4. Bells, standing, communal alternation, chant, and a proclaimed Resurrection
   Gospel are liturgical, historical, or pastoral customs as applicable. The
   current universal grant does not make them indulgence conditions.
5. The grant must be read with the *Enchiridion*'s general norms. A partial
   indulgence requires at least a contrite heart; the recipient must be
   baptized, not excommunicated, and in the state of grace at least when the
   prescribed work is completed; and at least a general intention and due
   performance are required. A vernacular prayer used to gain the indulgence
   requires competent ecclesiastical approval. The printed historical English
   is offered devotionally but is not represented as holding that approval in
   every territory.
6. “Queen of Heaven” is derived from Mary's relation to Christ the King and her
   glorification by grace. Only Christ is King in the full and strict sense;
   Mary's queenship is received, maternal, limited, and analogous.
7. `Quem meruisti portare` follows the received prayer. It names Mary's unique
   graced dignity and vocation; it does not mean that she earned the Incarnation
   by autonomous merit or placed God in her debt.
8. `Resurrexit, sicut dixit` centers the prayer on Christ's real Resurrection
   and fulfilled promise. The prayer does not assert an otherwise unattested
   private Easter appearance to Mary.
9. `Ora pro nobis Deum` invokes subordinate creaturely intercession. It does
   not make Mary a rival source of mercy or grace.
10. The collect asks God, through Christ, for eternal joy. Joy is a theological
   fruit and hope grounded in the Pasch, not a command to suppress grief or a
   guarantee of a particular emotion.
11. Authorship is unknown. The Directory's probable tenth- or eleventh-century
    dating and historical scholarship's later documentary thresholds remain
    distinct. The story associating the antiphon with Gregory the Great is
    received legend, not verified composition history.

## Text, translation, and rights boundary

The governing scan is Dom Gaspar Lefebvre, O.S.B., *Daily Missal with Vespers
for Sundays and Feasts* (1925), printed pp. 5 and 12–14. The Regina Coeli begins
at the foot of printed p. 13 and continues on p. 14, corresponding to PDF
pp. 47–48 of the checked scan. Printed p. 12 supplies historical contextual
rubrics; p. 5 supplies the same edition's complete Hail Mary for the companion
Angelus but is not imported into the Regina Coeli. The checked scan has SHA-256
`28c9046c5acfa19297ab9aeaa14ac4f8d8fcffab1307b51215b94fa4ca6172a2`.

The 1925 edition is public domain in the United States by publication date. Its
title matter says “All rights reserved,” and this audit did not establish the
translator or author of the exact historical English. Lefebvre's 1966 death
therefore does not by itself establish an exact life-plus-70 endpoint for that
translation; non-US rights remain unresolved rather than cleared through 2036.
The received Latin and historical-English prayer wording remains excluded from
Triptych's CC BY 4.0 grant. Historical ecclesiastical permission for the source
edition is not approval of this publication.

The 2005 *Compendium* supplies separate modern UK and USA English versions.
Neither is reproduced or conflated with the 1925 historical English. No current
proprietary vernacular Missal, Liturgy of the Hours, Bible, or official prayer
translation is reproduced. Current sources are cited and narrowly paraphrased.

Page-image collation retains the Latin antiphon's line-end punctuation
`;`, `:`, `:`, `.` and the historical English line endings `!`, `,`, `;`, `.`;
the collect's `dignátus es: praesta, quaesumus; ut`; and the exact historical
English line “Pour for us to Him thy prayer.” These are witness readings, not
project translations or normalizations.
