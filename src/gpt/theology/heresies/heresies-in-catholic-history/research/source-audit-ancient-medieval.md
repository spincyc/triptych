# Ancient and medieval dossier source-audit map

## Purpose and audit state

This record maps every dossier in sections 10--50 to its principal primary or official anchors and to the critical or target-side editions still needed for publication. It is a routing and negative-audit record, not a claim that the cited corpus has been fully collated.

The audit state abbreviations are:

- **T** — the stated locus was spot-checked in an accessible transcription or translation during this drafting pass;
- **R** — the source and locus were routed from established editions or scholarship, but the controlling original-language text was not collated in this pass;
- **S** — the evidence is source-limited, substantially hostile, dependent, late, or lacks a target-side text;
- **M** — act boundaries, numbering, attribution, recension, or censure distribution require a further critical check.

An entry marked T is not thereby a critical-edition verification. Accessible patristic translations, Denzinger transcriptions, and Tanner-derived council translations served as finding and sense controls. They must not silently replace the critical Greek, Latin, Syriac, Armenian, Coptic, or vernacular witness.

## Checks actually performed in this pass

- The early-school loci in Irenaeus, Justin, Eusebius, and the *Refutation of All Heresies* were spot-checked in accessible English transcriptions for chapter routing and witness separation.
- Ambrose, Letter 42, was checked for the Milanese reception of Siricius's judgment against Jovinian and for the propositions on virginity, fasting, reward, and Mary's virginal childbirth.
- Gregory the Great, *Register* 10.39 in the older English numbering, was checked for the Agnoete argument and its stated agreement with Eulogius.
- The submission of Lucidus was located in an accessible Denzinger transcription; its critical conciliar setting and numbering were not collated.
- The Tanner-derived English text of the Council of Vienne was checked for the soul-form definition, the express condition of obstinate future adherence, the Beguard errors, and the Templar suppression by provision rather than definitive sentence.
- The censure distribution in John XXII's *In agro dominico* was checked in accessible transcriptions: the first fifteen and last two propositions are treated as heretical; the intervening eleven are evil-sounding, rash, and suspect of heresy; Eckhart's submission is recorded.
- Lateran V's *Apostolici regiminis*, the Anagni Joachimite protocol, the Reims and Sens proceedings, and several source-poor eastern and medieval dossiers were routed but not critically collated.

## Checks explicitly not performed

1. No complete original-language collation was performed against the selected SC, GCS, CSEL, CCSL, ACO, MGH, CCCM, papal-register, or council-act editions.
2. No complete manuscript or dependency stemma was constructed for Irenaeus, the *Refutation*, Pseudo-Tertullian, Epiphanius, Philastrius, Augustine, Theodoret, John Damascene, or the medieval anti-heretical catalogues.
3. No exhaustive registry search proves that an unlocated local act never existed. The narrative uses the narrower formulation that no extant or identified act was found in the present audit.
4. No full target-text attribution audit was completed for recovered Gnostic works, Basilidean fragments, the *Great Declaration*, Priscillian's tractates, Paulician texts, Cathar materials, the *Introductorius*, or all extracted university and trial propositions.
5. Conciliar and papal act numbering, promulgation history, confirmation, recensions, and censure syntax remain to be checked in the controlling editions, especially 543/553 Origenism, Constantinople 869--870 and 879--880, Sens, Reims, Anagni 1255, Vienne, Constance, Basel, and Lateran V.
6. Civil and military consequences were not exhaustively checked against each kingdom's or empire's law and archives. The dossiers separate them conceptually but require specialist jurisdictional audit.
7. No comprehensive rights review was made for source translations. The document therefore paraphrases propositions and uses only short act titles and formulas.
8. The publication reference section links the modern Catholic--Oriental Orthodox and Catholic--Assyrian common declarations from official sources; paragraph-level concordance remains necessary before expanding the short present-day claims.
9. The controlling census, timeline, catalogue accounting, and references are reconciled separately; this companion does not replace their independent checks.
