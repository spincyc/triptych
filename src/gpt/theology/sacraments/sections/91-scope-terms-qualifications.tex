\section{Scope, Terms, and Qualifications}

\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Corpus and method.} The study uses Scripture; general councils and papal constitutions; both Catholic codes; universal catechetical and Eastern liturgical norms; the principal Thomistic questions; direct or closely related Greek, Latin, and Syriac patristic witnesses; and records for all twenty-four Catholic Churches \latin{sui iuris}. Doctrinal definition, ritual essence, current discipline, patristic reception, scholastic synthesis, and editorial cross-sacramental synthesis remain distinct.

\textbf{Authority, jurisdiction, and currentness.} Scripture, defined doctrine, and the identified official law and liturgical sources govern; patristic and scholastic witnesses illuminate them without acquiring magisterial force from citation here. Universal norms are distinguished from particular law and documented local practice. Canonical and ecclesial-identity material was checked through 11 July 2026. Individual questions of validity, faculty, impediment, initiation status, or matrimonial nullity require competent ecclesiastical judgment.

\textbf{Limits, rights, and review.} Patristic survival is uneven; the \emph{Supplementum} is posthumous; hylomorphic assignments are not dogmatically uniform; Eastern particular law and parish practice vary; and no exhaustive patristic or global ethnographic coverage is claimed. Exact sacramental-form excerpts reproduce identified official or historical human witnesses and remain third-party text outside the project's license. No independent theological, canonical, liturgical, or patristic review is claimed. The complete audit and unresolved questions are recorded in \nolinkurl{src/gpt/theology/sacraments/research/scope.md}.

\textbf{Initiation-practice boundary.} Particular practice can diverge from the restored inherited norm because of local law, pastoral history, and Latinization. The initiation-practice table in Appendix~\ref{app:initiation-table} therefore states common law and marks named delay or restoration examples as local or particular rather than treating one timetable as universal parish practice.
\end{studyframe}
