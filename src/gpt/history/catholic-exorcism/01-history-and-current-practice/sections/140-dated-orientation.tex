\section{Dated Orientation}

\noindent\textbf{Ancient witnesses.} \emph{Jubilees} and Josephus preserve two unlike
Second Temple or early Roman-period literary settings; their later English
editions are evidence for the translations used here, not the dates of the
underlying works.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{First century.} Gospel and Acts narratives place expulsion of hostile
spirits within Jesus' kingdom ministry and apostolic mission.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Second--fourth centuries.} Apologetic and initiatory witnesses attest
different Christian settings. Cornelius's c.~251 Roman clergy list survives in
fourth-century Eusebius; no unbound conciliar lead supplies a reader claim.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Early medieval witnesses.} The Gelasian Sacramentary survives through
layered manuscripts; Wilson's 1894 edition controls the passages used here.
The tenth-century \emph{Pontificale Romano-Germanicum} is identified
bibliographically through the Vogel--Elze 1963--1972 edition; no exact ordo or
page supports a publication claim here.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Thirteenth century.} Aquinas, \emph{Summa theologiae} III, q.~71,
distinguishes baptismal exorcistic acts within sacramental theology.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{1614.} Paul V issues the \emph{Rituale Romanum}; the princeps witness prints
\emph{De exorcizandis obsessis a daemonio} as one section at pp.~198--219.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{1917--1918.} The first Latin Code is promulgated in 1917; the 1918 Vatican
Polyglot book witness controls the canons cited here.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{1952.} A later Roman Ritual edition arranges the material in title XII; it
does not authorize present use by its historical existence.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{1972--1973.} Paul VI issues \emph{Ministeria quaedam} on
15 August 1972; its norms take effect on 1 January 1973. The former minor
orders are called ministries, lector and acolyte remain universal Latin
ministries, and other ministries require a conference request and an
Apostolic-See decision.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{1983.} John Paul II promulgates the revised Latin Code on
25 January; it takes force on 27 November and states the reservation and
priestly qualifications in canon 1172.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{29 September 1985.} The CDF restricts unauthorized use of exorcism formulas
and direct interrogation in assemblies for liberation.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{1998--2004.} A decree dated 22 November 1998 promulgates the revised book;
the Latin typical edition appears in 1999, the dicastery presents it on
26 January 1999, and an emended reprint appears in 2004.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{14 September 2000.} The CDF distinguishes exorcism from healing services
and reaffirms dependence on the diocesan bishop.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{2014--2017.} The USCCB approves the English edition in 2014, the Holy See
confirms it in December 2016, and it takes effect in U.S. Latin dioceses on
29 June 2017.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{22 October 2021.} The competent dicastery issues current implementation
for amended canon 838, including the exorcism book's preliminary norms.
\medskip\noindent\textbf{27 July 2026.} Current-law and additional historical-source
checks close on this date.
