# Deep-and-broad redevelopment audit — 2026-07-29

## User trigger and prior-edition finding

The user required a full pass because the work was perceived as lacking
content and deep, broad research. The installed study was already extensive
(116 physical pages and more than 64,000 reader-facing words), so page count
was not accepted as evidence of adequacy.

The audit found a different qualitative deficiency: the source corpus is broad,
but the reader often has to reconstruct the cross-period argument from
successive dossiers. The conclusion states “continuity without identity,” yet
the body lacked one sustained comparison that tests continuity across the
same variables in every period. This made a heavily researched paper feel
accumulative rather than decisively synthetic.

## New substantive synthesis

The reader-facing chapter “Seven Cross-Period Tests: What Actually Continues?”
compares:

1. recipient;
2. act;
3. minister;
4. book or artifact;
5. authority;
6. evidence for practice or efficacy; and
7. care under uncertainty.

This is not a ritual or intake sequence. It is a historical method that makes
the governing thesis reproducible. It also consolidates consequential negative
results: title recurrence does not prove office identity; normative books do
not prove frequency; permission does not prove cause; narrative outcome does
not prove efficacy; current Latin law does not govern Eastern Catholics; and
uncertainty never licenses coercion or withdrawal of care.
