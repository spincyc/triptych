## Findings

100 now says explicitly that a person with capacity may refuse or withdraw
from a non-emergency religious inquiry or rite and that refusal must not be
relabeled as spiritual resistance. It preserves the separate,
jurisdiction-specific treatment of minors, incapacity, surrogate decisions,
clinical emergencies, reporting, and protective disclosure. The duplicated
item in the continuing-care list has also been removed.

The competence boundary is appropriately stated. Medical and mental-health
assessment is assigned to appropriately qualified professionals; pastoral and
ecclesial judgments are kept distinct from diagnosis and treatment; clinicians
are not asked to certify a metaphysical conclusion; and pastoral personnel are
not licensed to diagnose. The WHO CDDR is used only for bounded differential
domains and cultural and religious exclusions. Its clinical category is not
treated as proof for or against a preternatural cause or as a ritual criterion.

The territorial and juridical limits remain visible. The USCCB material is
identified as United States guidance rather than universal law or a clinical
protocol. The child-safeguarding source is expressly limited to England and is
not used to infer Catholic prevalence or a worldwide rate. Emergency,
reporting, privacy, capacity, surrogate, and protective duties remain tied to
applicable law, policy, professional ethics, and jurisdiction rather than
being stated as one universal civil rule.

The pastoral conclusion remains non-operational and proportionate. It directs
immediate danger to appropriate emergency or protective services, preserves
prescribed treatment and continuing professional care, rejects amateur tests
and confrontation, and routes Catholic pastoral concerns through accountable
parish, diocesan, or eparchial authority. It does not provide ritual words,
alleged diagnostic signs, interview scripts, or instructions for coercive
action.
