# Ancient, biblical, and patristic audit

This workstream distinguishes comparative context, canonical witness,
apologetic claim, initiatory practice, and later institutional reception.

Primary loci requiring final edition-level binding are: 1 Samuel 16; Tobit
6–8; Jubilees 10; Genesis Apocryphon 20; Josephus, *Antiquities* 8.45–49;
Matthew 4:24 and 12:22–45; Mark 1:21–28, 3:22–27, 5:1–20, and 9:14–29; Luke
4:31–37, 10:17–20, and 11:14–26; Acts 8:7, 16:16–18, and 19:11–20; Justin,
*Second Apology* 6; Tertullian, *Apology* 23; Origen, *Against Celsus* 1.6,
1.25, and 7.4; *Apostolic Tradition* 20–21; Cyril of Jerusalem,
*Procatechesis* 9 and *Mystagogical Catechesis* 2.3; and Laodicea canon 26.

Material qualifications:

- comparative resemblance does not establish borrowing or a continuous rite;
- Gospel classifications are not modern clinical diagnoses;
- Mark 9:29 has a material prayer/fasting variant;
- apologetic challenge scenes are claims by advocates, not neutral case files;
- the *Apostolic Tradition* is composite and its attribution and dating remain
  disputed;
- baptismal exorcism, extraordinary exorcism, and a later ordained ministry
  must not be conflated;
- Greek, Latin, Syriac, and other Eastern sequences require edition-specific
  treatment before a universal chronology is asserted.

No display translation has been created. Final publication requires exact
public-domain or licensed translation control, original-language collation for
consequential lexical claims, reusable bindings, and independent historical,
patristic, biblical, liturgical, and theological review.

## Exact repository controls added in this tranche

- The normalized Challoner/Gutenberg Douay-Rheims corpus supplies exact, hashed
  English controls for 1 Samuel (1 Kings) 16, Tobit (Tobias) 6–8, Matthew,
  Mark, Luke, and Acts. It controls Catholic English reception, not Hebrew,
  Aramaic, or Greek critical text.
- Exact Robinson–Pierpont 2018 Byzantine-textform artifacts control the Greek
  wording in Matthew, Mark, Luke, and Acts. They represent one textform and
  include inline divergence notes; they are not a full critical apparatus.
- At Mark 9:29 the Robinson–Pierpont artifact includes fasting with prayer and
  its inline divergence note reports omission in the Nestle–Aland text. The
  exact Douay–Rheims/Challoner artifact also includes fasting, at Mark 9:28 in
  its numbering. No separately identified eclectic artifact or full critical
  apparatus is bound, so the draft states the variant but does not reconstruct
  its manuscript history or weight.
- Josephus's exact Whiston VIII.2.5 web witness is normalized, acquired, and
  inspected. Niese 8.45–49 is retained only as a cross-reference; no Niese
  Greek witness has been registered.
- Justin's *Second Apology* and a dated public-domain ANF translation state are
  normalized; chapter 6 still needs a bounded exact passage record.

Exact records remain necessary for Tobit's Greek recensions, Genesis
Apocryphon 1Q20 XX, Qumran apotropaic texts, Tertullian
23, Origen's three loci, the *Apostolic Tradition*, Cyril, and Laodicea 26.
Prose concerning them remains narrow and does not claim original-language
verification.

The matrix closes a methodological gap but not the remaining edition gaps.
Father counts are not doctrine tallies, and the lead-only rows are not cited as

## Bounded negative-search rule

No author-wide or patristic-wide absence claim was established in this tranche.
The exact ANF volume 1 OCR was inspected only at the registered Irenaeus
passage; it was not treated as an index of all early Christianity. Dated web
editions of Justin and Chrysostom identify delivery states, not complete
searched corpora. Consequently the publication may say that an enumerated
locus does not contain a later Roman formulary or canon, but it may not say
that a Father, language tradition, or “the early Church” never supplied one
without a separately replayable corpus search.

## Original-language controls

The draft makes no argument from a novel translation. Its consequential Greek
controls are the exact Robinson–Pierpont loci, not a freestanding lexical
theory. Forms conventionally rendered “unclean spirit” and *daimonizomai*
remain contextual narrative designations, and Acts 16:18 issues a command “in
the name of Jesus Christ.” The Latin reception's *exorcista* and *exorcizare*
do not by themselves identify whether a locus concerns initiation,
extraordinary affliction, or an ecclesiastical grade.

The exact Byzantine-textform artifacts permit inspection of wording but do not
prove lexical meaning. The publication therefore makes no claim that
“possessed,” “demonized,” and “afflicted” are interchangeable technical or
clinical labels.

No exact Hebrew or Syriac artifact is bound in this tranche. The tracked
Douay–Rheims/Challoner artifact controls the English reception of 1 Samuel 16,
not the Masoretic wording. No claim about Hebrew morphology or a Syriac
textual tradition supports the publication. Syriac witnesses to Mark 9:29
remain a future edition-specific research question rather than evidence
silently inferred from the Greek or Latin reception.

## Consequential negative results

- The enumerated biblical witnesses contain no later Roman major-exorcism
  formulary and no canon reserving its celebration to a bishop's permit.
- The checked apologetic loci do not provide names, dates, or independently
  corroborated histories of the afflicted persons invoked rhetorically.
- The initiatory witnesses do not justify treating every catechumen as a case
  of extraordinary possession.
- No enumerated witness establishes a straight textual descent from a
  Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Greek-magical, or Solomonic formula to the Christian
  rite.
- Laodicea 26 alone does not establish a universal minor order, its functions,
  or uninterrupted practice across Greek, Latin, Syriac, Armenian, Coptic, and
  Ethiopic churches.

These are corpus-bounded findings, not claims about every surviving manuscript
or Christian community.
