# Patristic second-pass research, 2026-07-27

## Question and boundary

This pass re-tested every ancient or patristic claim narrowed during final
review: Tertullian, Origen, the Jerusalem catecheses, Laodicea 26, Eusebius
6.43.11, and the church order conventionally called the *Apostolic
Tradition*. It searched three families separately:

1. public-domain primary texts and translations capable of identifying the
   passage;
2. critical-edition catalogs or digitized critical texts capable of
   identifying the textual control; and
3. specialist studies capable of testing provenance, authorship, genre, and
   the evidentiary ceiling.

The result below is a reproducibility finding, not an assertion that a text
does not exist. A web page located and read in this pass is not promoted to an
“inspected” repository source until its exact delivery state or a lawful exact
derivative is registered, hashed, and bound.

## Tertullian, *Apologeticum* 23

- **Primary/translation family:** the complete public-domain ANF translation
  was located in New Advent and CCEL delivery states. Chapter 23 contains the
  forensic challenge and the claim that spirits would submit at the naming of
  Christ.
- **Original-language/critical family:** the Tertullian Project supplies an
  exact Latin chapter sequence and identifies its text as Oehler revised by
  Glover and compared with CETEDOC text 24/3/6. Its critical-edition survey
  identifies CCSL 1 as the modern Latin control; BiblIndex independently
  catalogs *Apologeticum* in CCSL 1.
- **Specialist family:** the Tertullian Project's work bibliography and
  critical-edition survey were checked for the edition history.
- **Disposition:** evidence for the narrow literary claim was found, so there
  is no historical negative result. No exact artifact for this work is
  presently retained in the repository, and the web delivery state was not
  normalized during this pass. The reader claim therefore remains removed
  pending an exact Latin or translation passage record.

## Origen, *Contra Celsum* 1.6, 1.25, and 7.4

- **Primary/translation family:** Crombie's public-domain English translation
  was located and the three numbered loci were checked in New Advent's
  book-level delivery states. The loci do not make one identical argument:
  1.6 and 7.4 concern Christian means in contrast with magical apparatus,
  while 1.25 participates in the broader argument about names and their
  efficacy.
- **Original-language/critical family:** searches located the digitized
  Koetschau GCS edition (Leipzig, 1899) and a modern discussion identifying
  Koetschau as its Greek base. Sources Chrétiennes/Borret and Chadwick were
  identified as later critical/translation controls but no lawful exact
  repository artifact was already present.
- **Specialist family:** bibliographic surveys of *Contra Celsum* and the
  Koetschau edition were checked to prevent the ANF translation from being
  described as a critical Greek control.
- **Disposition:** evidence for the differentiated literary claims was found;
  no historical absence is claimed. The repository has edition records for
  another Origen work, not exact *Contra Celsum* artifacts or passages. The
  claims remain removed until an exact edition, artifact, and the three
  bounded passages are registered and bound.

## Jerusalem catecheses

- **Primary/translation family:** Gifford's NPNF translation was checked at
  *Procatechesis* 9 and at the first Mystagogical Catechesis. The latter,
  numbered Catechetical Lecture 19 in the host sequence, places westward
  renunciation in section 4 and continues the renunciation sequence through
  section 9. This confirms that 2.3 was an erroneous locus.
- **Critical family:** the Catholic University of America edition listing and
  the Sources Chrétiennes edition family were located as modern controls; no
  exact lawful repository artifact was already retained.
- **Specialist family:** Edward Yarnold's authorship study and later
  specialist summaries were checked. They confirm that attribution of the
  Mystagogical Catecheses has been disputed and cannot be silently resolved
  by the transmitted title.
- **Disposition:** the exact locus correction to 1.4–9 stands, and evidence
  for the initiatory interpretation was found. Because neither the Gifford
  delivery state nor a critical text is yet an exact repository source, the
  reader claim remains removed rather than being restored from a bare URL.

## Laodicea canon 26

- **Primary/translation family:** Percival's NPNF translation and public
  presentations of the Greek conciliar canons were searched by canon number
  and by the distinctive churches/private-houses restriction.
- **Critical family:** catalog searches were run for the Greek conciliar
  collections and modern critical discussion of the Laodicean corpus.
- **Specialist family:** histories of the exorcist office were searched to
  test whether canon 26 alone could bear a universal-office inference.
- **Bounded result:** the expected narrow translation was located in the
  public NPNF tradition, but this pass did not locate an already retained,
  hashed repository artifact or a registered critical Greek witness. The
  canon therefore remains a bibliographic lead. No claim of textual absence,
  universal reception, or universal office is made.

## Eusebius, *Historia ecclesiastica* 6.43.11

- **Primary/translation family:** McGiffert's NPNF translation family and
  public-domain complete scans were located.
- **Original-language/critical family:** the University of Fribourg BKV
  comparison page supplies the numbered Greek at 6.43.11. It explicitly
  coordinates exorcists, readers, and door-keepers under the single number
  fifty-two, confirming that no separate exorcist count can be extracted.
- **Specialist family:** specialist work on possession and exorcism in early
  Christianity was checked and independently observes the same numerical
  limit.
- **Disposition:** the historical claim is well evidenced, but no exact
  Eusebius artifact or passage is yet registered in the repository. It remains
  removed from reader prose until the Greek delivery or a lawful
  public-domain translation is normalized and bound.

## Church order conventionally called *Apostolic Tradition*

- **Primary/edition family:** Burton Scott Easton's public-domain 1934
  reconstruction was checked in Project Gutenberg. It preserves the relevant
  baptismal material but itself explains that the Greek is fragmentary and
  that its reconstruction draws on incomplete Latin and several dependent
  Oriental versions.
- **Critical family:** searches identified Dix, Botte, Bradshaw--Johnson--
  Phillips, and the newer reconstruction using the independently transmitted
  Ethiopic witness. The Alcuin Club describes the work as anonymous and
  untitled and explains the changed reconstruction prompted by the Ethiopic
  evidence.
- **Specialist family:** the modern commentary and textual-history literature
  were checked specifically for unity, authorship, Roman provenance, date, and
  chapter-number equivalence. Those matters remain contested, and editions do
  not expose one interchangeable text state.
- **Disposition:** the second pass confirms that a reader claim cannot be
  restored merely by binding Easton's reconstruction as though it were the
  securely recoverable text of Hippolytus at Rome around 215. No exact modern
  critical edition licensed for repository retention was found. The paragraph
  remains removed, and the lead must name its edition, versional basis, and
  chapter numbering before reuse.

## Conclusion

The second pass found positive textual support for the narrow Tertullian,
Origen, Jerusalem, Laodicea, and Eusebius descriptions, but did not close
their repository-level exact-artifact and passage-binding gate. They remain
removed for reproducibility, not because the historical evidence was
disconfirmed. The *Apostolic Tradition* pass instead reinforced the
edition-specific warning: the older reconstruction is searchable and useful,
but cannot silently stand for a unitary, securely attributed Roman text.

Only Justin, *Second Apology* 6, could be normalized from an already retained
exact public-domain artifact during this review. Its passage and binding remain
the sole restored apologetic claim in section 30.
