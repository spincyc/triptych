## Findings

The theological center is sound and proportionate. The study preserves divine
sovereignty, creaturely finitude, Christ's victory, human dignity, moral
responsibility, ordinary care, and the distinction between doctrine,
school-theological analysis, pastoral terminology, and case attribution. It
does not turn an unexplained event into a preternatural cause, a sacramental
into magic, or an allegation into a diagnosis. Its account of temptation,
imputability, sacramentals, and Thomistic limits on demonic knowledge and
influence is materially orthodox within the stated bounds.

The principal Latin canonical claims agree with CIC canon 1172: a major
exorcism over the possessed requires the local ordinary's particular and
express permission, and that permission is restricted to a suitably qualified
priest. The study correctly distinguishes universal Latin law, the ritual
book, dicastery acts, U.S. conference implementation, and diocesan process. It
also correctly refuses to transplant CIC canon 1172 into an Eastern Catholic
Church or to infer an Eastern rule from CCEO canons 1–2.

The pastoral and ecclesial synthesis is appropriately non-operational. It
directs readers toward accountable Church authority and ordinary pastoral,
medical, emergency, and protective care; it supplies no formula, sign list,
self-test, confrontation, or unauthorized ministry. The discussion of consent,
privacy, professional competence, and continuing care is congruent with the
identified U.S. guidance and does not convert that guidance into universal law.
