\section{\emph{Traditionis Custodes}: A New Legal Environment}

Francis issued \emph{Traditionis custodes} on 16 July 2021. It declared the liturgical books promulgated by Paul VI and John Paul II the unique expression of the Roman Rite's \emph{lex orandi}, placed authorization of the 1962 Missal under the diocesan bishop according to Apostolic See guidelines, limited new groups and personal parishes, and abrogated prior norms and customs not conforming to it. Article 6 placed institutes and societies erected by the Pontifical Commission \emph{Ecclesia Dei} under the competence of the Congregation for Institutes of Consecrated Life and Societies of Apostolic Life.

The FSSP responded on 20 July with surprise and sadness. Its communiqué appealed to constitution 8, professed adherence to the magisterium and fidelity to pope and bishops, denied that it recognized itself in the divisive tendencies described in Francis's accompanying letter, and asked to remain faithful to its charism. That response is an actor's position, not a legal interpretation binding the Holy See; it is nevertheless important evidence of how the society perceived the act.

The problem differed from 2000. Then the question concerned members' possible use of the reformed Missal and the authority of superiors. In 2021 the universal regime governing the older Missal itself changed, and the supervisory dicastery shifted. Rules aimed broadly at diocesan groups intersected with a society whose founding and constitutions had made use of several 1962 books corporate. Article 6 acknowledged the former \emph{Ecclesia Dei} institutes as a category but did not, on its face, print a complete special regime for them.

\subsection{Neither immediate suppression nor automatic exemption}

The FSSP remained a pontifical-right society. \emph{Traditionis custodes} did not say its constitutions were abolished, its clerics excardinated, or its houses suppressed. Conversely, continued juridical existence did not by itself answer how each liturgical provision applied. The months after July were therefore marked by real uncertainty rather than a simple choice between ``nothing changed'' and ``the Fraternity ended.''

The Congregation for Divine Worship's December 2021 \emph{Responsa ad dubia} further interpreted the general legislation, but the FSSP's particular situation still required treatment by competent authority. The society sought that treatment directly from Francis. An audience in February 2022 and a papal decree dated 11 February supplied the next controlling instrument.
