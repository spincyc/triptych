\section{Scope, Evidence, and Limits}

\subsection{Question, range, and terminal date}

This account asks how a group leaving the SSPX in July 1988 acquired pontifical-right status within three months; how its two seminaries and diocesan apostolates produced a transnational clerical body; how its proper law developed; why a dispute about liturgical freedom and government became acute in 1999--2000; and how the FSSP navigated the different regimes of \emph{Ecclesia Dei}, \emph{Summorum Pontificum}, and \emph{Traditionis custodes}. It ends with the Fraternity's structure, statistics, visitation, and papal contacts as publicly documented through 16 July 2026.

The geographic account concentrates on Switzerland, Germany, France and Belgium, the United States and Canada, Oceania, and representative missions elsewhere; it is not a national history of every apostolate. The evidence is principally English, French, German, Italian, Latin, and Spanish. Source-hosted English renderings are identified, legal substance is paraphrased, and no project translation, facsimile, or extended third-party passage is reproduced.

The detailed prehistory of Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre, the SSPX, the 1975--1976 conflict, the 5 May 1988 protocol, and the 30 June consecrations belongs to TPI-01. This volume restates only the branch point necessary to understand the FSSP, then tests the FSSP's own history against its separate declaration of intention, act of foundation, Roman declaration, liturgical decree, and erection decree.

The reader-facing label ``Traditional Priestly Institutes'' is bibliographic, not canonical. The FSSP is, at the cutoff, a clerical society of apostolic life of pontifical right. The SSPX does not share that status. The Institute of Christ the King Sovereign Priest has its own origins and juridical history, which are not reconstructed here.

\subsection{Evidence layers and negative results}

\begin{threestable}{Layer}{Representative evidence}{Control and limit}
Contemporary public act & Founding declaration, foundation act, papal and curial acts, erection decree, constitutions, chapter notices & Establishes what the actor did or enacted. FSSP-hosted translations are identified as such and are not silently treated as Vatican editions.\\
Institutional memory & Seminary histories, anniversary accounts, statistics, organization chart, and communiqués & Establishes the Fraternity's dated self-report and memory; it is not an independent census or neutral account of disputes.\\
Universal and particular law & Code of Canon Law, papal liturgical legislation, the 1988 and 2022 decrees & Establishes legal text at a date. Application and interaction can still require competent interpretation.\\
Modern reconstruction & Open-access canonical scholarship and contemporary reporting & Supplies analysis and source leads. An FSSP member writing canon law is a knowledgeable participant, not an external adjudicator.\\
\end{threestable}

Several absences shape the account. The checked set did not include the complete original constitutions, every intervening approval, the official file of the 2000 administrative recourse, a Holy See publication of the 2022 decree independent of the copy released by the FSSP, or a public final report from the apostolic visitation announced in 2024. The publication does not fill those gaps with rumor.

\subsection{A bounded canonical and liturgical module}

Current canonical statements use the Latin Church's 1983 Code and public special acts through the cutoff. Liturgical change is described historically under the law then in force. ``Pontifical right,'' ``proper law,'' ``faculty,'' ``diocesan consent,'' ``incardination,'' and ``personal parish'' retain distinct meanings. Nothing here determines a private dispute, advises a bishop or member, or rules on the legal effect of an unpublished act.

\evidenceframe{Code of Canon Law, canons 731--746; Pontifical Commission \emph{Ecclesia Dei}, Prot. No. 234/88, 18 October 1988.}{The FSSP is a clerical society of apostolic life of pontifical right; its members pursue a proper apostolic purpose in common life under constitutions, and clerics are ordinarily incardinated in the society.}{Pontifical right concerns relation to the Apostolic See and internal government. It does not remove episcopal authority over public worship, care of souls, and apostolic works, or eliminate the need for written consent to erect a house.}

\subsection{Publication boundary}

This source-audited historical study was checked through 16 July 2026. It distinguishes public law, institutional self-description, later reconstruction, and unresolved interpretation. It is not a canonical opinion, a pastoral directive, or an independent audit of membership and finance. Independent historical, theological, liturgical, and canonical review remains outstanding.
