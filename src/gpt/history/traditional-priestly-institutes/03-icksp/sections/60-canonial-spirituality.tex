\section{Canonial Form, Liturgy, and Salesian Spirituality}

The Institute's most distinctive term is ``canonial form.'' The 2008 decree says members live common life \emph{ad instar canonicorum}---on the model of canons. It connects that life with solemn liturgical worship and makes the sacrifice of Christ, sacramentally celebrated, the summit of common life and the source and end of pastoral care. This is stronger than a decorative title and narrower than a universal canonical classification as canons regular.

Current ICKSP usage styles its priests ``canons.'' That title expresses the society's received identity and common-life ideal. The checked sources do not show that each house is a chapter attached to a cathedral or collegiate church, that members belong to an ancient chapter, or that they profess the rule of a religious order of canons regular. The proper description keeps the Latin comparison in view rather than converting resemblance into identity.

\subsection{A spiritual architecture}

The decree and institutional presentations arrange the Institute's spirituality around a principal patron and three secondary patrons. The Immaculate Conception supplies Marian patronage. Saint Benedict represents ordered common life and priority of divine worship; Saint Thomas Aquinas represents theological formation; Saint Francis de Sales represents pastoral charity and measured speech. The Pauline motto \emph{Veritatem facientes in caritate} joins truth and charity as the desired style of common and apostolic life.

These associations are not four independent devotions placed beside one another. They form an institutional grammar: ordered worship, intellectual clarity, apostolic gentleness, and Roman-Marian loyalty. The seminary transmits that grammar, and the houses make it visible through schedule, choir, material culture, and forms of address.

Spiritual self-description is normative aspiration, not a finding that every member or superior embodies it. The 2023 public dispute is significant partly because reported concerns about governance, expenditure, formation, and ecclesial culture were measured against an institution that publicly emphasizes harmony, charity, and family life. Recording the ideal and the criticism together is more historically responsible than using either to erase the other.

\subsection{The liturgical center}

\emph{Saeculorum Rex} describes worship according to the extraordinary form of the Roman Rite in the vocabulary authorized under \emph{Summorum Pontificum}. It does not enumerate four specific 1962 books as the FSSP decrees of 1988 and 2022 do. Institution pages separately say the Institute uses the liturgical books conforming to the 1962 Vatican editions. The distinction prevents a later institutional description from being read back as the exact operative wording of N. 181/2008.

The liturgy gives the houses a common temporal and ceremonial pattern, but its public exercise remains ecclesial rather than private property. Ordination requires bishops; public apostolates require diocesan reception; universal liturgical law can change; proper law can require competent interpretation. The result is a stable patrimony whose legal environment has never been static.
