\section{The Opus Sacerdotale and the French Passage}

The least transparent stage lies between Roman formation and Gabon. ICKSP accounts say Wach worked with a priestly association in France and that the emerging community spent about two years there before moving into its African and Italian settings. A Polish institutional page identifies the Opus Sacerdotale. Later online narratives sometimes name Moissac, but no checked stable source in this dossier confirms that location; it therefore remains an unverified lead. The exact dates, residence agreement, formation responsibilities, location, and legal status of the group were not established by an original act in the checked set.

The Opus Sacerdotale was not created for the future ICKSP. Albert Jacquemin's historical study traces it to meetings around Canon Étienne Catta in 1963 and foundation in 1964. Its first purpose was mutual assistance in priestly sanctification during the transformations surrounding the Second Vatican Council. Jacquemin follows its growth and legacy through 1974, well before the ICKSP project took shape. A note near the end associates Wach with the Opus and records the ICKSP's later recognition of this inheritance.

That evidence supports connection, not identity. An older association of priests could provide contacts, houses, spiritual vocabulary, and practical help without becoming the same juridical person as a later society of apostolic life. The ICKSP's current self-presentation centers Wach and Mora as founders. The 2008 decree does the same. It does not name Catta or the Opus as a co-founder or predecessor juridic person.

\subsection{What the French passage can and cannot carry}

The French interval functions as a bridge: candidates gathered in France while a stable bishop, seminary, and canonical home were still being sought. It is reasonable to describe a French preparatory stage and association with the Opus Sacerdotale. It is not responsible to assign a particular town, a precise October 1988 opening, house population, curriculum, or transfer of property without the underlying record.

The bridge also prevents a misleading binary. The Institute was neither simply ``founded in Rome'' nor wholly created at Mouila without antecedents. Rome supplied formation and relationships; France supplied an intermediate community context; Gabon supplied the first reported diocesan erection and missionary field; Florence supplied the durable motherhouse and seminary. These are complementary stages, not rival origin myths.
