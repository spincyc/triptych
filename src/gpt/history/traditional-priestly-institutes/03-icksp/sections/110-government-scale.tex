\section{Government and Scale at the 2026 Cutoff}

The Institute's 2026 international calendar provides the clearest current public snapshot checked for this study. Its directory lists Gilles Wach as prior general at the general house and Philippe Mora as superior of the seminary. The descriptive pages call the Institute a pontifical-right society of apostolic life in canonial form and report more than 130 priests, about one hundred seminarians, and about twenty oblates. They report more than seventy Sister Adorers separately.

Those figures are rounded self-report, not an audited census. ``More than'' and ``about'' should not be converted to precise totals. The categories also matter: seminarians are not added to priests as if all were incorporated clerics; oblates retain their own status; the Sisters are a separate group whose last located controlling act erected a public association in 2008; diocesan priests affiliated spiritually and lay members are not male-society members merely because the calendar mentions them.

\subsection{Central, provincial, and local offices}

The general house coordinates assignments, formation, temporal administration, and relations with the Holy See. Provincial and other regional superiors organize houses and apostolates across broader territories. Local priors sustain common life; rectors, chaplains, and pastors exercise the office entrusted in the relevant diocesan arrangement. A title on an institutional directory does not by itself disclose every faculty or the ownership of a church.

The calendar illustrates real internationalization. It lists houses, clergy, oblates, churches, and Mass schedules across several languages and episcopal jurisdictions. Its directory value lies in a dated administrative snapshot. It is not a complete historical database: entries can change after publication, and absence from a printed directory does not prove absence throughout the entire year.

\subsection{The problem of stale web statistics}

An Italian page that remains online says the Institute is present in fifty-two dioceses and twelve countries with sixty-five priests and more than eighty seminarians ``in twenty-three years.'' The surrounding text dates it to an earlier institutional moment despite its current accessibility. The United States history supplies still other dated milestones. Current-looking design and live hosting do not make every number current.

The 2026 calendar therefore controls the present snapshot, while older pages become evidence of earlier self-presentation. This method preserves growth without manufacturing a continuous annual series. It also prevents a stale number from being used to challenge a current rounded count as if both described the same date and category.
