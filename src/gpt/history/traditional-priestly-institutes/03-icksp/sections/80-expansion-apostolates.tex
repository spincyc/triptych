\section{Expansion Through Episcopal Invitation}

The Institute expanded outward from Gricigliano and Gabon through diocesan reception. Institutional histories list apostolates across France, Italy, Belgium, Switzerland, Germany, Austria, Spain, Great Britain, Ireland, Sweden, the United States, and other settings. A country list shows reach, not institutional density. One resident house, a major shrine, a school, an occasional mission, and a group served from elsewhere are not equivalent units.

The United States history dates the first invitation to 1996, when Raymond Burke was Bishop of La Crosse. Subsequent bishops entrusted churches and communities, sometimes buildings threatened with closure or requiring extensive restoration. The Institute's model joined stable common life, a prominent liturgical schedule, catechesis, sacred music, and recovery of historic architecture. This produced visible apostolates in cities where one church could serve faithful drawn from a wide area.

The pattern was not simply rescue by private initiative. A bishop's invitation or consent, the society's assignment of members, the condition and ownership of property, local finances, and a community's pastoral needs all shaped a work. Restoration could strengthen local continuity, but it also created long projects dependent on benefactors and agreements. Public before-and-after narratives naturally emphasize success; this study does not independently audit property, expenditure, attendance, or parish registers.

\subsection{House, shrine, oratory, and parish}

Institutional publicity often uses ``apostolate'' as an umbrella. Canon law and diocesan records use more specific forms. An oratory is a place set aside for worship under law; a shrine has pilgrimage and devotional significance under ecclesiastical authority; a parish or personal parish carries defined pastoral governance; a society house sustains common life and requires episcopal consent. A location can combine some of these features without making the terms synonyms.

This vocabulary becomes important in the post-2021 record. Public Masses at the Institute's Chicago shrine were suspended, while diocesan acts later received ICKSP ministry at an oratory in Warren, a diocesan shrine in Oswego, and a shrine apostolate in Tampa. The differences are not contradictions in universal fact. They show that a pontifical-right society operates within particular churches whose bishops make legally and pastorally consequential decisions.

\subsection{Formation limits the rate of growth}

Every stable expansion requires personnel. The 2026 calendar reports more than 130 priests and about one hundred seminarians, but candidates require years of formation and not all become priests. Existing houses require superiors and common life; ministries need language and pastoral competence; African missions and schools require different capacities from European urban churches.

As a practical inference, the years required for formation and the finite number of formed members constrain the pace of expansion. The general government cannot answer every invitation merely because a building or group is available. The public location record consequently shows selective density: strong regional clusters, isolated houses, and places served through less permanent arrangements. Rounded international totals should not be turned into an exact map.
