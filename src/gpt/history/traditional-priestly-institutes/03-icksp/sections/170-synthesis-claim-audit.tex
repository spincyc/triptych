\section{Synthesis and Claim Audit}

The ICKSP's durable achievement is the conversion of a dispersed founding path into a reproducible institutional culture. Roman formation, a French preparatory setting, Gabonese diocesan reception, and a Florentine seminary could have remained unrelated episodes. Common government, proper law, a canonial ideal, and stable formation made them one history. Episcopal invitations then translated that culture into local missions, schools, shrines, churches, and pastoral works.

The same structure creates dependence at several levels. The Holy See erects, approves, visits, and can alter universal law. The prior general and proper law sustain identity and assign members. Bishops receive houses and govern public apostolate. Benefactors and faithful sustain places, while seminary formation limits how quickly they can be staffed. Pontifical right is not escape from these relationships; it organizes them.

The Institute's trajectory differs from TPI-01 and TPI-02. It did not emerge from the SSPX's 1988 internal branch point. Its 2008 decree was not the FSSP's special liturgical decree. Similar worship and former PCED supervision created a shared field, not one genealogy or interchangeable proper law. The history becomes clearer when comparison ends at the point where each institution's own acts begin.

\subsection{Claim audit}

\claimcheck{``The ICKSP is an SSPX or FSSP offshoot.''}{No checked founding record supports that genealogy. Its line runs through Wach and Mora's Roman formation, the Opus Sacerdotale connection, diocesan erection at Mouila, and Gricigliano. TPI-01 and TPI-02 supply comparison, not institutional ancestry.}

\claimcheck{``The Vatican's 1979 record names Wach and Mora among the ordinands.''}{The Vatican confirms John Paul II's ceremony and eighty-eight ordinands. The Institute identifies the future founders among them; the official homily and Angelus do not print a roster.}

\claimcheck{``The Opus Sacerdotale and ICKSP are one continuous juridical body.''}{Historical and institutional sources support association and inheritance, not a succession act. The Opus began in 1964; the 2008 decree names Wach and Mora as ICKSP founders.}

\claimcheck{``The Institute was first founded in Italy.''}{Gricigliano became the motherhouse and seminary, but institutional records date diocesan erection to Mouila, Gabon, on 1 September 1990. The original act remains a source gap.}

\claimcheck{``Canonial form means every priest belongs to a cathedral chapter or an order of canons regular.''}{N. 181/2008 says members live on the model of canons. The title, common life, and solemn worship are real; the comparison does not create those other canonical identities.}

\claimcheck{``Pontifical right lets the Institute operate without diocesan bishops.''}{Canons 733 and 738 preserve written episcopal consent for houses and local authority over public worship, care of souls, and apostolic works. The post-2021 location record makes that boundary visible.}

\claimcheck{``All clerical oblates are canonical clerics.''}{The Institute's title describes its non-priest members; N. 181/2008 calls the early group lay oblates. Under canon 266, clerical status begins with diaconate, not minor orders or proximity to priestly life.}

\claimcheck{``The Sister Adorers received the same 2008 status as the male society.''}{The male Institute became a pontifical-right society of apostolic life under N. 181/2008. N. 182/2008 separately erected the women as a public association of faithful for three years; no later status act was located.}

\claimcheck{``The 2014 visitation exonerated the Institute.''}{An institutional notice records an ordinary apostolic visitation. The mandate, report, recommendations, and result are not public in the checked set; the 2016 approval notice does not supply a retrospective verdict.}

\claimcheck{``The Institute has exactly 130 priests, 100 seminarians, 20 oblates, and 70 Sisters.''}{The 2026 calendar says more than 130, about 100, about 20, and more than 70. These are rounded self-reports in four distinct categories, not audited exact totals.}

\claimcheck{``The ICKSP has a published universal exemption from \emph{Traditionis custodes}.''}{No ICKSP-named universal faculty or exemption was located through the cutoff. The FSSP act in TPI-02 cannot be transferred by analogy, and the 2024 audience is not legislation.}

\claimcheck{``Chicago proves that the Institute was expelled or suppressed.''}{Its own page establishes suspension of public Masses from 1 August 2022. No checked decree establishes expulsion or universal suppression, and later dioceses received ICKSP ministry.}

\claimcheck{``The public allegations prove misconduct, or institutional denial disproves them.''}{The 2023 and 2026 reports establish attributed claims and responses. No checked public Holy See adjudication or completed independent report establishes either conclusion.}

\subsection{A mature institution with an incomplete public archive}

By 2026 the Institute reported substantial international scale, an enduring seminary, provincial organization, papal access, and more than three decades of apostolate. The central acts of 2008 and the 2016 approval notice establish recognized and supervised life. The missing early decrees, full constitutions, visitation reports, and outcomes of later concerns remain material limitations.

That combination resists both caricature and hagiography. Institutional durability is real. So are changing liturgical law, local dependence, concentrated founding leadership, and demands for accountable oversight. A source-first history can hold these facts together because it does not ask one act, one headline, or one liturgical preference to explain the whole society.
