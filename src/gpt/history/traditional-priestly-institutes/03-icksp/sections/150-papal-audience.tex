\section{The Papal Audience of 2024}

The Holy See Press Office records that Francis received Gilles Wach, prior general of the Institute, ``and entourage'' on 24 June 2024. The date was the forty-fifth anniversary of Wach's priestly ordination by John Paul II. The official bulletin establishes occurrence and the principal participant; it supplies no transcript, decision, or agenda.

The Institute's own account identifies Rudolf Michael Schmitz, vicar general, and Louis Valadier, provincial of France, as accompanying Wach. It says Wach thanked Francis for his apostolic letter on Saint Francis de Sales, described worldwide pastoral work, and received encouragement to continue service according to the Institute's charism in unity and communion. It also reports papal appreciation of the Sisters' prayer and apostolic work and the oblates' service.

The two sources perform different roles. The Press Office confirms access to the pope. The participant communiqué reports private content. Its account can be historically valuable without becoming an official transcript. No decree, rescript, liturgical faculty, visitation disposition, or constitutional amendment was published with the audience.

\subsection{A relationship expressed rather than law changed}

Private audiences are part of institutional history because they communicate recognition and allow superiors to present work and concerns. For an institute publicly defined by \emph{Romanità} and fidelity to the pope, the symbolism is substantial. The reported encouragement also complicates a simple narrative that liturgical restriction meant rejection of every former-PCED society.

Symbolism does not answer every legal question. A pope can encourage a charism while leaving universal and local norms in force. The Institute's report does not say Francis granted an ICKSP-specific exemption, and the official bulletin contains no such act. Nor does the audience resolve later allegations or substitute for visitation.

The anniversary joined the 1979 origin memory to the mature institution. A priest ordained in a large papal cohort returned as superior general of an international society. Yet the same evidentiary rule applies at both ends: the Vatican source establishes the public papal event, while the Institute identifies participants and private significance.
