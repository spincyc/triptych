## Irish Capuchin archival catalog

The Catholic Archives catalog is an official archival description system. Catalog descriptions and authority records were inspected; no claim treats an unseen file as though its full contents had been read.

1. **Ambrose Brunton authority record.** [“Brunton, Ambrose (1894–1974), Capuchin priest”](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/brunton-ambrose-1894-1974-capuchin-priest). Role: archive-controlled spelling, biography, and description of his 1923 appointment as the first Irish Capuchin pastor of Saint Anthony. “First Irish Capuchin pastor” is not expanded to “first priest ever.”
2. **Raphael Quinn authority record.** [“Quinn, Raphael (1888–1940), Capuchin priest”](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/quinn-raphael-1888-1940-capuchin-priest). Role: Ukiah pastor from 1922, work among Pomo communities, and archive attribution of responsibility for building Saint Anthony Parish Church. The attribution is reconciled with the 1921 church photograph by withholding a one-campaign building claim.
3. **Sebastian Brennan authority record.** [“Brennan, Sebastian (1861–1937), Capuchin priest”](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/brennan-sebastian-1861-1937-capuchin-priest). Role: Mendocino County arrival in 1903, initial Fort Bragg/northern assignment, Ukiah service, Irish transition in 1920, and Willits posting in 1928. It rejects the inference that his county arrival founded Saint Anthony in Willits.
4. **Saint Anthony church photograph.** Catholic Archives, ref. IE CA WA/4/3/17, “St. Anthony's Church, Willits, California,” dated 4 April 1921, [catalog record](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/st-anthonys-church-willits-california%3Bisad?sf_culture=en). Role: existence and exterior appearance of a church by that date. The image is not reproduced; catalog date and description do not prove dedication or continuity of every feature.
5. **Willits rectory photograph.** Catholic Archives, ref. IE CA WA/4/3/18, circa 1924, located in the [West Coast photograph browse](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/informationobject/browse?levels=236&page=2&sf_culture=en&sort=lastUpdated&sortDir=desc&topLod=0&view=table). Role: a rectory beside the church by the cataloged date. No image is reproduced.
6. **Joseph Fenelon correspondence concerning Willits.** Catholic Archives, ref. IE CA WA/1/4/10, June 1923 catalog description. Role: a Willits deputation's request to Archbishop Edward Hanna for a priest and Ambrose Brunton's assignment so Hopland and Willits could receive Sunday Mass. The archive's catalog description was used by narrow paraphrase; no manuscript transcription is presented as independently collated.
7. **Willits subseries.** Catholic Archives, ref. IE CA WA/7/14, [“Willits, Mendocino County, California”](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/willits-mendocino-county-california%3Bisad?sf_culture=en), 1929. Role: cataloged existence of a Brennan golden-jubilee item and a parish-status/financial-statement item. Their contents were not accessed and support no financial figure.
8. **Later priest authority records.** [Finbarr O'Callaghan](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/ocallaghan-finbarr-1879-1963-capuchin-priest), [Alban Cullen](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/cullen-alban-1898-1970-capuchin-priest), and [Isidore Kennedy](https://catholicarchives.ie/index.php/kennedy-isidore-1897-1973-capuchin-priest). 9. **Catholic Church of the Mendocino Coast, Elk parish history.** <https://coastcatholic.org/parish-history-elk>. Role: participant regional history stating that Irish Capuchin county service ended in 1968. It corroborates the 1968–1969 directory change but does not establish a Willits handoff act.

## Reconciliation and consequential negative searches

- **1903/1923:** The current official record conflicts. The 1903 directory has no Willits entry; 1906 lists an unnamed Willits mission; 1910 names Saint Anthony; 1923 still lists it under Ukiah; and 1924–1925 list it separately. Historical directories in 1941, 1960, 1963, 1968, and 1969 print “(1923).” The account interprets different thresholds without inventing a decree.
- **Building:** The cataloged 1921 church image precedes the resident transition. Quinn's authority record credits him with building the parish church, and a rectory is cataloged circa 1924. No checked source determines whether Quinn completed, rebuilt, enlarged, replaced, or regularized an earlier church, or how much of that fabric survives.
- **Brunton/Brenton:** The 1925 directory spelling and archive authority spelling remain separately attributed.
- **Indigenous stations:** Directory searches found administrative labels in 1910, 1923, and 1941. No Indigenous-authored parish account, sacramental register, attendance series, or oral-history collection was located. Silence in the checked public corpus is not assigned to Indigenous communities.
- **Capuchin handoff:** The 1968 and 1969 entries and regional history support a transition in personnel and county ministry. No appointment letter, diocesan decree, contract, or exact transfer date was located.
- **Pastoral succession:** Checked original pages, archival appointments, and current official records support the published officeholders and transition points. Search-text collation is reported only as an unverified alignment lead, not as original-page verification or a canonical appointment series. The 1950 and 1959 editions were unavailable, the 1997 entry was unusable, and later national-directory text was access-restricted. The 2001–17 March 2016 interval remains unresolved and is not characterized as a vacancy.
- **Kennedy/Cullen:** Kennedy appears in the checked 1949 entry before the archive's precise 1954 pastor appointment. Cullen's authority record describes 1940s ministry while checked 1938, 1941, and 1944 entries name Quinlan and Ahern in the leading office. The list preserves both conflicts and assigns no unsupported office.
- **Covelo:** The 1969 entry is the first checked Saint Anthony listing that names Queen of Peace. No original erection act, deed, building chronology, or uninterrupted administrative series was located.
- **Post-1969:** Checked transition pages yield the verified personnel skeleton through 1998, while intervening search text supplies only labeled alignment leads through 2000. No complete public parish chronicle, appointment-decree series, membership, sacramental, finance, school, or building ledger was found. Present-day activity remains dated and cutoff-bound.
