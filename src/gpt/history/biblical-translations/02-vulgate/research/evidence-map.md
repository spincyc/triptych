# Evidence map: The Vulgate

This map aligns the account's principal historical and interpretive claims with the evidence actually checked. It uses the profile's four classes:

- **A — Artifact / near-contemporary evidence:** manuscript, preface, correspondence, or material record at or near the stage described.
- **N — Ancient or medieval narrative witness:** a named author reporting, defending, or interpreting events at the author's own date.
- **R — Later reception / institutional memory:** liturgical, ecclesial, conciliar, papal, or editorial judgment that establishes its own period's status.
- **M — Modern reconstruction:** critical edition, text-critical argument, catalog analysis, or professional historical synthesis.

Confidence applies to the precisely worded claim. “High” does not remove a row's stated limit or make one type of evidence answer another type of question.

## Synthesis boundary

The concluding formulation that the Vulgate is a **successful composite tradition**—made by translators, anonymous revisers, scribes, worshiping communities, scholars, printers, councils, popes, and critical editors—is original editorial synthesis. It is grounded in the mapped evidence but is not attributed as a quotation or unique thesis of one modern scholar.

No row establishes a present doctrine, canonical obligation, or liturgical rule beyond what a named official act expressly says. No historical row authorizes a project translation. Independent patristic, Latin-biblical, medieval-manuscript, Reformation-history, and text-critical review remains outstanding.
