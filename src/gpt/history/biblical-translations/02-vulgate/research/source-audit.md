## Consequential negative results and rejected inferences

- No checked source supplies a Damasian whole-Bible commission; Jerome's Gospel preface names the four Gospels.
- No checked evidence securely assigns the Vulgate Acts, Pauline Epistles, Catholic Epistles, or Revelation to Jerome.
- No one pre-Jerome Latin Bible or uniform ancient edition called *Vetus Latina* was identified.
- No surviving Greek codex was identified as Jerome's one Gospel exemplar, and no Hebrew autograph or complete source manuscript survives from his Old Testament work.
- No checked evidence makes the Roman Psalter's Hieronymian authorship certain.
- No checked source supports treating about 390–405 as one continuous commission with a single completion date.
- No source supports importing Tobit's bilingual intermediary into Judith's translation procedure.
- No source supports calling Codex Amiatinus Jerome's autograph or a direct copy of one complete authorial Bible.
- No checked act promulgates Alcuin's text as the exclusive Vulgate of the Carolingian empire; Theodulf's distinct work and local mixtures remain material.
- No Paris Bible decree was identified; its standardizing influence is tied to school and book-trade mechanisms.
- Trent did not name the Clementine edition, which appeared in 1592, or declare the Vulgate critically superior to Hebrew and Greek.
- The motives for replacing the Sixtine printing are not reduced to either typographical error or politics alone.
- No critical edition is treated as a typical edition, and no typical edition is treated as an ancient-text reconstruction.
- No anonymous digital text labeled only “Vulgate” was used for an exact reading.

## Unused or unverified leads

- Specific Pelagian, Roman, Rufinian, or individual authorship proposals for the non-Gospel New Testament revision were not adopted because the checked evidence supports anonymity more securely.
- Precise dates for every one of Jerome's Hebrew-based biblical books were not used where the evidence gives only relative sequence or a broad range.
- A detailed reconstruction of Jerome's Jewish teachers and each lesson was not attempted; names and intellectual mediation remain unevenly documented.
- The claim that Charlemagne personally corrected a Bible from Greek and Syriac was not used as historical fact; Houghton treats the later narrative as hagiographical.
- Numerical counts of Alcuin's changes, copies produced, Sixtine–Clementine differences, or *Nova Vulgata* changes were avoided where they depend on definition and corpus.
- The popular claim that the Sixtine was withdrawn only because of thousands of typographical errors was rejected as overprecise and motive-exclusive.
- Modern online interlinear, devotional, and confessional “Vulgates” were not used as edition controls.
