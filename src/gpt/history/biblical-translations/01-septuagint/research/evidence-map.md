# Evidence map: The Septuagint

This map aligns the account's principal historical and interpretive claims with the evidence actually checked. It uses the profile's four classes:

- **A — Artifact / near-contemporary evidence:** papyrus, scroll, codex, preface, or material record.
- **N — Ancient narrative witness:** a named author reporting or interpreting the past at the author's own date.
- **R — Later reception or institutional memory:** theological, rabbinic, ecclesial, or editorial reception that establishes its own period's judgment.
- **M — Modern reconstruction:** critical edition, text-critical argument, or professional historical synthesis.

Confidence applies to the precisely worded claim, not to every associated detail. “High” may coexist with an explicit negative result about names, dates, motives, or mechanisms.

## Synthesis boundary

The final claim that the Septuagint has a real **historical unity**—a Greek scriptural culture initiated by the Torah and extended through production, revision, Jewish and Christian reception, manuscript collection, and modern reconstruction—is original editorial synthesis. It is grounded in the mapped evidence but is not attributed as a quotation or thesis unique to one modern scholar.

No row establishes inspiration, inerrancy, or a present ecclesial canon. No historical row authorizes a project translation. Independent specialist review remains outstanding.
