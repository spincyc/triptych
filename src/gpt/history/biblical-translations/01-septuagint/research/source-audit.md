## Consequential negative results and rejected inferences

- No contemporary Ptolemaic decree, library register, translator colophon, or Jerusalem dispatch was identified that independently verifies Aristeas's named commission and procedure.
- No checked source extends Aristeas's seventy-two translators beyond the Law to every later Septuagint book.
- No checked source establishes isolated cells and independently identical translations in Aristeas itself; that is a later reception form.
- No checked witness supplies a fixed Alexandrian Jewish table of contents identical with a modern printed Septuagint.
- No checked source establishes a Council of Jamnia decree universally rejecting the Septuagint.
- No great codex is treated as complete origin-era evidence, and no one codex is made the sole text of “the Septuagint.”
- No Greek–Hebrew difference is classified merely from its existence; each possible explanation remains book- and variant-specific.
- No single percentage for New Testament dependence on the Septuagint is used.
- No paywalled modern chapter is represented as fully read when only its publisher abstract and metadata were available.
- No claim is based on an unattributed web chronology, search-result snippet, or modern confessional summary.

## Unused or unverified leads

- Precise reconstructions of the number and identity of Pentateuchal translators were not used because no direct personnel record survives and the proposals exceed this account's checked evidence.
- A specific synagogue lectionary origin for the Greek Torah was not asserted; access and communal use are well supported, but the first exact ritual setting remains unrecoverable.
- Biographical identifications of Aquila, Symmachus, and Theodotion were not used as established facts where they depend on later reports or disputed synthesis.
- A unitary “Lucianic recension,” a complete Hexapla column diagram, and one corpus-wide kaige edition were not asserted beyond the checked evidence.
- Modern theological claims that a particular Greek or Hebrew corpus is inspired, inerrant, or ecclesially superior were outside scope and were not used as historical controls.
