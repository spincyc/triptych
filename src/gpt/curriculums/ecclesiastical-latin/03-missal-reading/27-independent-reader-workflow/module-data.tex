\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{What ``no facing translation'' does and does not mean}
    {Direct reading is visible analysis before summary, not a ban on English during correction.}
    {Using the first selected worksheet passage, make a no-aid frame map, then write a two-sentence sense only after every finite form and subject is accounted for.}
    {List three kinds of evidence that distinguish a parse from remembered translation.}
    {A complete model marks coordinators, finite verbs, subjects, governed forms, and any bracketed supply before giving sense. Accept morphology, clause attachment, and reference as the three evidence kinds; familiarity alone is not evidence.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Reconstruction without flattening}
    {A disposable analytical line must lead back to an observation about the source's printed order.}
    {Reconstruct the lesson's project-composed \Latin{Tibi, Domine, sacrificium laudis offerimus}; then state what initial \Latin{tibi} foregrounds.}
    {Name one element that reconstruction may move and one that it may never invent.}
    {Model: \Latin{Nos offerimus sacrificium laudis tibi, Domine}. The reconstruction groups verb, subject, object, genitive, and recipient; the return pass notes the foregrounded recipient. Words may move, but absent content may not be invented.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Dictionary discipline}
    {A strong lookup begins with a falsifiable grammatical proposal.}
    {For the lesson's \Latin{perfruamur}, write the four-line pre-lookup hypothesis, check it, and record whether the lexicon changed lemma, construction, or only sense.}
    {State the condition under which a second lookup of the same content word counts against the ceiling.}
    {The model proposes \Latin{perfruor}; first plural present subjunctive deponent; verbal center of its clause; enjoyment/possession governing an ablative. A repeated ledger word counts again when the learner failed to retain or recognize it.}
