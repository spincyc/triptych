\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{Advent: descent and germination}
    {Seasonal imagery becomes readable only when subjects, objects, and jussive or indicative forms are settled.}
    {On the lesson's Advent examples, make a morphology-first image map: form, function, literal action, and only then seasonal association.}
    {Identify one image that a capital letter or familiar theme could cause you to misparse.}
    {The model gives each decisive noun's case and each verb's full parse before describing descent or germination. Seasonal association without grammatical evidence does not pass.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Lent: practice directed toward action}
    {A correlative or means phrase states a grammatical relation that a broad penitential gloss can obscure.}
    {Trace the lesson's correlative members and means expression, naming governor, case, and clause function for each.}
    {State why the English word ``by'' does not prove an ablative of means.}
    {A passing answer identifies the Latin governor and unambiguously attaches each correlative form. English alone cannot distinguish means from agent, manner, or another relation.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Easter: the startling perfect}
    {A perfect form must first be read as morphology and narrative assertion before its rhetorical prominence is discussed.}
    {Place the lesson's Paschal finite forms on a tense line and explain what one perfect contributes relative to surrounding forms.}
    {Give the principal-part evidence for the selected perfect.}
    {The model derives the stem from the listed principal part, gives person and number, then explains completed-event force in its clause. It does not infer a doctrinal conclusion from tense alone.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Pentecost: a relative containing all things}
    {A dense relative clause is controlled by antecedent and internal case, not by the number of nearby nouns.}
    {Map the lesson's Pentecost relative from antecedent through finite verb and every internal dependent; then write a plain prose scaffold.}
    {State separately what determines the relative's gender/number and its case.}
    {The antecedent determines gender and number; the relative's own clause function determines case. The scaffold must retain every printed word and bracket any supply.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{The seasonal ledger}
    {Comparing the same lemma across seasons reveals construction and sense without turning recurrence into one fixed theme.}
    {Choose one recurring lemma and make four rows: exact heading, printed form, construction, and contextual sense. Record an honest absence where necessary.}
    {Explain why a missing seasonal occurrence is useful evidence.}
    {Every row cites actual course evidence; absence prevents a preselected pattern from being presented as universal. Contextual senses may overlap but must retain their distinct constructions.}
