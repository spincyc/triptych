\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{Requiem deixis and agreement}
    {Changing the intended deceased person changes a controlled chain of pronouns, nouns, adjectives, and sometimes titles.}
    {Transform one plural-intention line already printed in the lesson to one man and one woman, recording every changed and unchanged coordinate.}
    {State why mechanical replacement of one pronoun is insufficient.}
    {The model audits recipient, possessive or genitive, soul or servant wording, adjective gender, and finite number. Unchanged forms are explicitly justified rather than overlooked.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{The sequence as compressed judgment}
    {Short poetic lines still contain finite spines, witnesses, objects, and displaced modifiers.}
    {Take the lesson's printed Sequence line, restore a prose scaffold, and label future form, subject, object, prepositional phrase, and witness expression.}
    {Name one poetic effect retained only in the printed order.}
    {The scaffold accounts for every form and the witness expression's case; the return note cites repetition, rhyme, fronting, or compression without adding doctrine to the parse.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Holy Thursday and obligation}
    {An impersonal obligation construction must be separated from its accusative subject and infinitive.}
    {Diagram the Holy-Thursday example already printed in the lesson, naming impersonal governor, infinitive, accusative subject, and dependent phrase.}
    {Explain why the accusative is not an ordinary direct object.}
    {The model treats the accusative as the infinitive's subject within the construction and attaches the infinitive to the impersonal governor. A direct-object label cannot explain the whole clause.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Reproach and acclamation}
    {Rapid questions and responses shift divine speaker, addressed people, presenter, and assembly.}
    {Make a speaker/addressee table for the lesson's Reproach and Cross-acclamation excerpts, then classify every imperative, question, and hortatory form.}
    {Which printed forms prove the shift of addressee?}
    {The model cites vocatives, pronoun persons, verb persons, and command forms. Ceremonial roles may remain outside scope, but grammatical voices must be explicit.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{The Paschal proclamation}
    {Coordinated jussives may have different subjects while participating in one rising proclamation.}
    {On the lesson excerpt, pair each jussive with its subject and modifiers, then describe the coordination without merging those subjects.}
    {Give the person, number, tense, voice, and mood of each verbal center.}
    {Every jussive is parsed separately and paired by agreement with its subject. The synthesis may note parallel escalation, but no singular subject may be carried across a plural form.}
