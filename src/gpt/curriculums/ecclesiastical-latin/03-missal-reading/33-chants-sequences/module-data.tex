  \SubmoduleExtension{Psalmic parallelism}
    {Parallel members may repeat, develop, contrast, or complete one another while preserving distinct syntax.}
    {Make a two-column map of the lesson's first parallel pair: align corresponding heads, verbs, objects, and modifiers, then name the semantic relation.}
    {Identify one repeated feature and one grammatical difference.}
    {The model aligns forms only where morphology and syntax support the correspondence and labels the relation cautiously. Merely placing two translated lines side by side is insufficient.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{The sustained Tract}
    {A longer chant requires a speaker-and-addressee ledger across verse boundaries.}
    {On the lesson's Tract selection, mark every finite verb and pronoun, then record speaker, addressee, and antecedent for each verse unit.}
    {State when a verse mark may not be treated as a sentence boundary.}
    {The ledger follows grammatical persons and antecedents; a verse mark is not a sentence boundary when dependencies or a finite clause continue across it.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Sequence compression}
    {Rhyme and balanced short lines can separate words that remain bound by case, agreement, or government.}
    {Choose one stanza already printed in the lesson and present it in three forms: printed order, dependency groups, and neutral prose scaffold.}
    {Name one effect lost in the neutral scaffold.}
    {All three versions retain every printed form and bracket any supply. The return note may identify repetition, rhyme, suspense, or foregrounding, but must cite exact forms.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{A responsible ellipsis ledger}
    {A supply is a claim with an evidence class, not invisible editorial repair.}
    {Record three possible supplies from the selected worksheets, label each G, P, or I, and reject one tempting but unnecessary addition.}
    {Give the minimum evidence required for a grammatical supply.}
    {A grammatical supply is demanded by construction; a parallel supply comes from an adjacent repeated frame; an interpretive supply assists sense but is not forced. The rejected addition must be shown unnecessary to the parse.}
