\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{Exhortation built on time}
    {Temporal forms often create the premise from which an exhortation follows.}
    {On Worksheet III.17's reading, place every finite form on a past/present/future-or-response line and draw the connector that turns description into exhortation.}
    {Identify which mood, not an English helper verb, marks the communal response.}
    {The model separates completed circumstance, present condition, and hortatory subjunctives, then cites the inference or transition marker. A timeline without clause force is incomplete.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Tradition received and handed on}
    {A reported tradition contains nested speakers, actions, and time relations.}
    {Using the lesson's already printed institution narrative, make a three-level ledger for narrator, reported speaker, and quoted or enacted content.}
    {For one infinitive or relative clause, name its controller and relative time.}
    {A passing ledger never assigns a reported action to the narrator merely because the nouns are nearby; every level cites a finite governor, quotation boundary, or relative antecedent.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{A difficult indirect statement}
    {Accusative forms in indirect statement must be tested as subjects and predicates before they are called objects.}
    {Take the lesson's difficult example, bracket the infinitive construction, and label controller, accusative subject, predicate or complement, and relative time.}
    {State why converting every accusative into a direct object destroys the construction.}
    {The model attaches the full accusative-infinitive unit to its governing verb and distinguishes its internal subject from any object. It gives the infinitive's time relative to the controller.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Argument-map notation}
    {A compact notation is useful only when another reader can reconstruct the evidence behind every arrow.}
    {Turn one selected worksheet paragraph into six numbered propositions joined by labeled arrows; cite the Latin connector or mood beside every arrow.}
    {Mark one relation that context supplies without a dedicated conjunction.}
    {Each node states one proposition; arrows name relations such as reason, contrast, inference, or command. An implicit relation is accepted only when its sequence and clause force are explained rather than asserted.}
