\chapter{Scope and Qualifications}

\section{Reader and outcome}

This is a print-first, self-paced Latin course for an earnest English reader
beginning with no Latin grammar. It supplies the English-grammar bridge and
the orientation to Missal genres needed for its own work. Its principal
destination is direct reading of the 1962 typical edition of the
\emph{Missale Romanum}; its broader formation includes ordinary
project-composed Latin and progressively authentic classical, biblical,
patristic, ecclesiastical, theological, canonical, liturgical, and poetic
sources.

The intended outcome is broad grammatical and lexical independence, direct
reading across registers, defensible source-aware analysis, and composition
developed from controlled sentences through revised advanced prose. Poetry is
introduced in the common trunk and receives a separate eight-packet advanced
branch. Pronunciation, phonetics, singing, chant notation, ceremonial
instruction, and current authorization to use liturgical books remain outside
scope.

Knowledge of Latin improves access to sources but does not by itself settle
liturgical, theological, textual, or canonical questions or replace
competence in those disciplines.

\section{Self-contained instructional boundary}

The course bundle supplies its teaching explanations, paradigms, decision
procedures, vocabulary and grammar memory work, reading lexicon, assigned
source excerpts, ordinary practice Latin, worksheets, cumulative reviews,
answers, and composition models. The complete Missal, wider dictionaries, and
full source editions remain valuable for extension and independent transfer;
they are not hidden substitutes for material omitted from the instructional
sequence.

The common trunk comprises twenty-two substantial packets that preserve the
forty-six established stable lesson IDs. Each packet exposes all four
authoritative worksheet variants for its lessons and the corresponding
answers. The Memory Workbook supplies fuller detachable repetitions from the
same vocabulary and grammar registries. Four cumulative self-review companions
offer fresh work for qualitative reflection and later return.

\section{Text, orthography, and translation}

Received Missal wording is identified by liturgical location and checked
against the 1962 typical-edition page images named in the research records.
Biblical comparison uses the identified Vulgate witness unless a local label
names another edition. Classical, patristic, poetic, canonical, and other
received passages use the editions and exact loci recorded in the passage and
edition inventories. Searchable presentations, OCR, parsers, and dictionary
interfaces are finding aids rather than controlling texts.

Running Latin normally follows the course house typography: consonantal
\Latin{i}, the ligature \Latin{æ}, and no macrons. A source passage may be
normalized typographically without claiming a diplomatic transcription.
Textual-comparison exercises preserve or explicitly label witness spelling and
normalization. Uncited short drills, transformations, ordinary readings, and
model compositions are project-composed. Received passages are identified;
simplified, abridged, or reordered passages are explicitly labeled as
adaptations. English translations and glosses are project translations unless
an identified human translation is named locally.

\section{Sources, completeness, and review}

The Missal governs the final specialization but does not narrow the course to
memorized liturgical formulas. Early transparent prose uses ordinary objects
and actions; later reading moves through Scripture, Cicero, Augustine, other
excellent classical and Christian writers, theology, canon law, hymns, and
poetry before and alongside sustained Missal work. Advanced composition grows
from the writing practiced throughout the earlier packets rather than appearing
as an unrelated final exercise.

“Self-contained” describes the instructional sequence, not a claim to contain
every surviving Latin work, rare word, historical grammar, textual variant, or
specialist judgment. Uncommon independent reading will still benefit from
appropriate editions, lexica, and scholarly counsel.

The working manuscript was supplied for integration from a separate course
project whose generation provenance was not recorded there. Triptych's records
preserve that source identity, subsequent project-created revisions, content
map, corrections, and known limits without attributing donor wording to an
unverified author or model. Internal grammar, source, answer, and production
checks are recorded in adjacent research files. No independent Latinist,
liturgist, pedagogue, theologian, canonist, or ecclesiastical review is claimed
unless a named event is recorded.

\section{Rights and use}

The integration request identified and authorized work on the donor corpus,
but it did not independently establish authorship, ownership, or a general
distribution license for that corpus. Scripture, Missal wording and rubrics,
classical and historical texts, received hymns, official documents,
third-party translations, and reference works retain their own status. Their
inclusion does not promise that they may be extracted or redistributed
separately. Source records identify the known public-domain, license,
permission, or exception basis and any unresolved work-specific question. A
changed PDF receives no distribution status from an earlier exact-byte
authorization.
