\chapter{Common assessment protocol}

The Foundations, Core Grammar, and Advanced gates have two parallel forms. Take
Form A after the teaching volume and selected worksheets, and Form B only after
a delay, on a different day, as confirmation. At the Missal-reading stage,
Forms M-A and M-B are designated-formulary diagnostics: they test sustained
work but deliberately revisit material used in instruction. M-C is the
terminal transfer gate and requires two genuinely unfamiliar formularies.

Use ink and no solution manual. Unless a form says otherwise, use no parser,
facing translation, or dictionary. Correct every error, write its rule in the
ledger, and remediate by worksheet family.

For composition, the supplied answer is a model rather than the only possible
Latin. Score an alternate answer by morphology, government, intended sense,
and whether its construction can be defended from a grammar or credible
ecclesiastical model.

For a definition item, a memorized label without a deciding feature is
incomplete. A passing response states what the term includes, distinguishes
its nearest confusable neighbor where requested, and attaches the definition
to a defensible Latin example or reading decision.

For a percentage, establish the denominator before beginning. Each requested
form or parse feature is one point. Each connected-reading or analytical item
has four points: forms, syntax/evidence, sense, and completeness. Each
composition has four points: morphology/agreement, government/syntax,
propositional accuracy, and defensible idiom. Add earned and available points
within the named category; do not average an omitted item away or award one
indivisible point to a many-part answer. The scoring sheet remains with the
error ledger.
