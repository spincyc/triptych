  attach every dependent in \Latin{Tibi, Domine, sacrificium laudis
  offerimus}.}{Finite verb:
  \Latin{offerimus}; implicit subject \Latin{nos}; object \Latin{sacrificium};
  dependent genitive \Latin{laudis}; recipient \Latin{tibi}; vocative
  \Latin{Domine}.}

  \LearnerWorksheetBreak
  \Exercise{Reconstruct \Latin{Puris mereamur mentibus introire} in analytical
  order, then state what may not be omitted.}{Analytical order:
  \Latin{mereamur introire puris mentibus}. \Latin{mereamur} is first plural
  present subjunctive deponent and requires complementary infinitive
  \Latin{introire}; \Latin{puris mentibus} is ablative of attendant manner.}

  \Exercise{Classify the supply in \Latin{lex Dei eius in corde ipsius [est]}
  as grammatical, parallel, or interpretive and justify it.}{I: the printed
  text is a verbless nominal clause with a prepositional location predicate.
  \Latin{[est]} is an interpretive

  \Exercise{Explain why capital letters cannot decide the syntax of
  \Latin{Salvatorem germinet terra}.}{Case endings decide it: nominative
  \Latin{terra} is subject; accusative \Latin{Salvatorem} object; third-person
  singular subjunctive \Latin{germinet} agrees with \Latin{terra}. Capitals are

  \PassageExercise{Vias tuas, Domine, notas fac mihi: et semitas tuas edoce me.}
  {Make a clause map, prose-order line, and literal sense; no dictionary.}
  {Two coordinated imperatives: \Latin{fac} and \Latin{edoce}, implicit divine
  subject. Prose order: \Latin{Domine, fac vias tuas notas mihi et edoce me
  semitas tuas}. \Latin{notas} is predicate accusative; \Latin{mihi} dative;
  \Latin{me} object. Sense: make your ways known to me and teach me your paths.}

\begin{worksheet}{III.3}{C}{III.1}{Variant C}{Word-order reconstruction}{Give both analytical and printed-order observations.}

  {\Latin{eripi ab imminentibus periculis te protegente}. The ablative absolute
  is independent of the passive infinitive; \Latin{te} is not its object.}

  \Exercise{Reconstruct \Latin{Quam oblationem benedictam facere digneris}.}
  {\Latin{digneris facere quam oblationem benedictam}. Better dependency
  notation: \Latin{quam oblationem} is object of \Latin{facere},
  \Latin{benedictam} predicate accusative, and \Latin{facere} complement of
  deponent \Latin{digneris}.}

  \Exercise{Reconstruct \Latin{Agnus redemit oves: Christus innocens Patri
  reconciliavit peccatores}; name the parallelism.}{The printed order is already
  nearly analytical: subject--verb--object, then subject--dative--verb--object.
  \Latin{Agnus} and \Latin{Christus innocens} are co-referential parallel
  subjects in distinct clauses; \Latin{oves} and \Latin{peccatores} are the
  corresponding parallel objects. \Latin{Patri} belongs only to
  \Latin{reconciliavit}.}

  \PassageExercise{Omnipotens et misericors Deus, de cuius munere venit, ut tibi

  {Address: \Latin{Omnipotens et misericors Deus}. Relative frame:
  \Latin{de cuius munere venit [ut-clause]}. The entire substantive
  \Latin{ut}-clause is the subject/content of \Latin{venit}: that you are served
  worthily and laudably by your faithful comes from whose gift. Within it,
  \Latin{tibi} is dative and \Latin{a fidelibus tuis} personal agent.}
\end{worksheet}
