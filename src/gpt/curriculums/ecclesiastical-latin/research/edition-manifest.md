The Missal and Vulgate are coordinated witnesses, not interchangeable texts.
Where the Missal prints a biblical excerpt, its received liturgical wording is
the lesson object; the Vulgate witness supplies comparison only when the record
states it. Psalm numbers follow the Missal's Vulgate/Septuagint convention.

These works do not form an undifferentiated authority. Bennett and
Allen–Greenough describe a classical baseline. A Vulgate, late, medieval, or
stable liturgical use requires an appropriate source. The donor course did not
preserve exact grammar or lexicon loci for most explanations, so the internal
audit may record a correction or cross-check but does not call the whole
reference locus-audited.

## Substitutions and exclusions

No modern English hand missal, postconciliar Latin Missal, Nova Vulgata,
automated parser, generated dictionary entry, or donor PDF controls the text.
OCR extracted from the named scans was used transiently for search and
cross-checking and is not a tracked source. A searchable match is not recorded
as visual collation unless the corresponding page image was inspected.
