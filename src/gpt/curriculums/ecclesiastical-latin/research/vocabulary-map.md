# Vocabulary and Memory Map

## Controlling sources and method

The authoritative lexical rows are in `shared/memory/lexemes.tex`; the exact
new, R1, R2, R5, stage-return, grammar-fact, and prompt allocations are in
`shared/memory/sets.tex`. This map records the pedagogical decision in readable
form. It is not a second source to edit independently.

The controlled baseline has 598 lexemes: 550 in the twenty-two-packet trunk
(25 new per packet) and 48 in the eight-packet poetry branch (6 new per
packet). There are 574 productive and 24 principally receptive entries. Each
new item receives complete-entry study plus Latin-to-sense, sense-to-Latin,
morphology, and usage/government work in its first packet. The printed R1, R2,
R5, and S lists sample prior sets so a packet remains usable; they total 726
prior-item placements. A personally uncertain item returns more often whether
or not it is in the printed sample.

`N` means first study, `R1` the next packet, `R2` two packets later, `R5` five
packets later, and `S` a deliberately mixed stage return. These are study
events, not dates or deadlines. “Before continuing” descriptions are
qualitative abilities.

English senses are project glosses. Strand labels describe intended
instructional coverage and do not assert an occurrence in Cicero, Augustine,
Ambrose, the Vulgate, or the Missal. Exact author claims require passage ID,
token, edition, and locus. For the verified Ambrose passage used elsewhere in
the curriculum, the corrected ID is `AMB-KRAB1857` (Ambrose, *De officiis
ministrorum* I.11.38, Krabinger 1857, printed p. 44 / scan p. 61), not the
earlier candidate `AMB-CSEL32.1`; this correction does not by itself attest a
lexeme in the registry.

## Synchronization and unresolved work

The module renderers, workbook, answers, and complete Reading Lexicon all read
the same declarations. No packet may maintain a second vocabulary list. The
current 598-entry baseline supplies assigned work; wider dictionaries belong
to later semantic, source-specific, and independent research after an unaided
hypothesis.

Per-entry dictionary loci, corpus frequency, token-level author attestations,
systematic word families, and systematic collocations remain open lexical
audit work. No numerical corpus-coverage percentage or independent Latinist
review is claimed.
