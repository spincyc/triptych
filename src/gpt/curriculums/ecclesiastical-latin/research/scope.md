# Ecclesiastical Latin — Research Scope

## Identity

- **Provider:** OpenAI GPT, represented by the structured
  `generation-metadata.tex` record in every publication leaf.
- **Collection / genre:** *Triptych*; a print-first, self-paced Latin
  curriculum and edition-specific Roman liturgical study aid.
- **Canonical owner:** `curriculums/ecclesiastical-latin`, a non-publishable
  source and audit root.  The redesigned source edition owns three course
  companions, twenty-two substantial common-trunk packets, eight advanced
  poetry packets, and four cumulative-review companions.
- **Rite and principal destination:** the Roman Rite as printed in the 1962
  typical edition of the *Missale Romanum*.
- **Languages:** English instruction; Latin teaching texts, source excerpts,
  drills, readings, answers, and composition.
- **Jurisdiction:** the universal typical-edition corpus.  No local proper or
  later legal regime controls the language course.
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-07-22 for the redesigned source edition.  This date
  does not answer present authorization to celebrate with any liturgical book.

The Missal is the course's principal reading destination, not its only Latin.
The common trunk therefore begins with ordinary project-composed language and
progresses through connected narrative and exposition, classical prose,
Vulgate and patristic prose, liturgical genres, rhetoric, textual work, and
advanced composition.  Cicero is a principal classical prose model and
Augustine a principal Christian prose model in the later course.  Caesar,
Jerome, Ambrose, Aquinas, and other exact, edition-identified texts broaden the
register where their passages have actually been verified.

## Intended reader and reasonable terminal ability

The course addresses an earnest independent reader who reads English fluently.
It teaches the grammatical labels it uses and begins with no assumed Latin.
Familiarity with the order of Mass is helpful but is not needed to begin; the
course guide and Missal-reading packets supply the relevant textual and genre
orientation.  No pronunciation guide, phonetic system, or spoken assessment
is included in this edition.

Before moving from one packet to the next, a reader is told what it is
reasonable to be able to do with fresh material: recognize and produce the
forms in context, explain the syntax from evidence, retrieve the cumulative
memory set, read the supplied passage without leaning on its model, correct
recurring errors, and carry the construction into composition.  These are
descriptions of useful readiness.  A reader may repeat, pause, range sideways
through the readers, or continue when the stated work has become reasonably
secure.

The common trunk culminates in the ability to:

- read complete unfamiliar 1962 Missal formularies directly and continuously,
  without depending on a facing translation or memorized formula;
- approach unfamiliar ordinary, classical, biblical, patristic, scholastic,
  and ecclesiastical prose with the same morphological and syntactic method;
- distinguish printed source, normalization, editorial supply, translation,
  inference, and unresolved question;
- use the included lexicon, grammar, memory sheets, source notes, passages,
  exercises, and keys without needing a separate textbook or dictionary for
  ordinary course study;
- analyze register, argument, narrative, rhetoric, allusion, and bounded
  textual variants without making claims beyond the evidence; and
- plan, compose, revise, and document sustained original Latin prose.

The poetry branch adds page-based quantitative and rhythmic analysis,
scansion, commentary, imitation, and verse composition.  Poetry is introduced
in the common trunk through word order, ellipsis, parallelism, image, line, and
form; full prosody and verse composition deliberately branch only after the
advanced prose trunk.

The branch's present received-text ladder is limited to the passages actually
verified and printed here: Phaedrus, Virgil, Ovid, Horace, and the Pentecost
Sequence from the 1962 Missal.  Clearly labeled project-built hymn-style
stanzas supply controlled comparison and composition practice.  No additional
poet or hymn attribution is implied by that instructional comparison.

None of these abilities is a credential or authority to make liturgical,
doctrinal, canonical, historical, or textual judgments.

## Self-contained instructional boundary

Every ordinary learning and self-review task is designed to be completed from
the packet and its supplied course companions.  The publication set includes:

- a start-here guide and reusable study forms;
- an expanded reference grammar and controlled lexicon;
- a cumulative memory workbook with printable vocabulary, principal-part,
  government, word-family, paradigm, contrast, inflected-recognition,
  reconstruction, translation, and composition sheets, all with models;
- twenty-two common-trunk teaching packets containing the complete selected
  lessons, memory work, ordinary and authentic readings, integrated practice,
  all underlying worksheet variants, and terminal answers;
- eight advanced poetry packets with received texts, textual and metrical
  apparatus, exercises, and models; and
- four cumulative-review companions whose passages and necessary apparatus are
  printed within the course.

An included exercise may teach how to consult a full dictionary, inspect a
facsimile, or research a longer external text.  Such work is enrichment and
method training, not a hidden dependency for learning the grammar, acquiring
the core vocabulary, reading the course corpus, or checking an answer.  A
source scan or full Missal remains necessary for claims about a page beyond
the excerpt printed in the course and for independent research outside the
course's supplied corpus.

## Text and claim classes

- **P — project-composed Latin:** original instructional text, including the
  opening ordinary-world reader and uncited drills.  It is checked for the
  construction it teaches and never presented as received prayer, Scripture,
  patristic prose, or an author's words.
- **R — received text:** wording tied to an exact author, work, locus, edition,
  and verification state in the passage inventory.
- **N — normalized received text:** an R text with declared orthographic,
  typographic, punctuation, or lineation changes.  A diplomatic base remains
  identifiable when the distinction matters.
- **A — adaptation:** a source-based instructional alteration that names its
  source and every material change; it is not quoted as the author.
- **G — project gloss or translation:** original English teaching language,
  not an official or inherited translation.
- **U — unverified lead:** a possible passage, attribution, parallel, or claim
  retained in research but excluded from received teaching text.

Verification state is recorded separately from class: a corpus may be located,
textually compared, or visually collated to a page image without changing its
identity as R, N, A, or P.

## Orthography and translation

The ordinary house style uses consonantal `i`, vocalic and consonantal `u/v`
according to conventional lower-case Latin typography, and `æ/œ` where the
course's Missal transcription uses them.  Macrons and quantity marks appear in
memory or poetry apparatus when they teach quantity; they are annotations and
not silently attributed to a source.  Capitalization, punctuation, ligatures,
lineation, and abbreviation are preserved or normalized according to each
passage record.  Any change that can alter form, parsing, meaning, meter, or
source identity is declared locally and in the inventory.

English explanations, structural translations, natural translations, glosses,
prompts, and models are project-created unless explicitly attributed.  A
structural rendering may expose Latin grammar before a natural rendering, but
neither may hide a material ambiguity.  Open prose and verse compositions have
models and diagnostic criteria rather than one allegedly unique answer.

## Source hierarchy

1. an edition-identified page-image witness for every received text where
   visual collation is claimed;
2. the 1962 typical *Missale Romanum* for its exact wording, boundaries,
   headings, rubrics, and printed citations;
3. the named Hetzenauer 1914 Clementine Vulgate for biblical comparison;
4. exact public-domain scholarly editions for Cicero, Augustine, other
   classical and Christian authors, and poetry;
5. the exact grammar or lexicon editions named in the edition manifest for
   inherited morphology, syntax, and lexical range;
6. searchable presentations, OCR, corpora, and dictionary interfaces only as
   finding aids until checked to the controlling edition; and
7. internal consistency and automated checks, which may reveal an error but
   cannot establish a received reading by themselves.

## Exclusions

- pronunciation, phonetics, spoken accent training, chant performance, and
  singing;
- ceremonial instruction, calendar computation, selection of a proper, or a
  present-law judgment concerning the 1962 books;
- an official Latin text, critical edition, official translation, approved
  seminary program, credential, or replacement for any controlling source;
- a comprehensive edition of any classical, patristic, medieval, scholastic,
  legal, or liturgical author;
- a claim that school grammars alone establish every biblical, late,
  ecclesiastical, or liturgical development; and
- redistribution of source scans, full OCR corpora, dictionary databases,
  modern translations, or received texts beyond the focused excerpts and
  separately authorized installed PDFs.

## Completeness, review, and release state

The completed candidate redesign accounts for all forty-six stable teaching
lessons, 184 trunk and 32 poetry worksheet variants, the broad reader ladder,
twenty-two common-trunk packets, an eight-packet advanced-poetry branch, three
course companions, and four cumulative self-reviews. The controlled memory
system has 30 packet sets, 598 lexemes (550 trunk and 48 poetry; 574 active and
24 recognition-only), 81 grammar facts with 726 review placements, 30
reconstruction-and-composition model pairs, and 240 printable sheet families
rendered in 480 prompt-and-solution forms. The 37 installed PDFs total 2,068
pages. The lexicon inventory, curriculum map, passage inventory, answer audit,
and production manifest own the corresponding definitions and exact
production record.

The work has no imprimatur, nihil obstat, ecclesiastical approval, or
independent Latin, liturgical, pedagogical, theological, textual, canonical,
or rights review. Received-text rights, donor provenance, and page-image
collation remain qualified passage by passage. Source checks, automated
checks, builds, log review, and exact every-page raster review of the
redesigned PDFs are recorded without converting internal review into an
independent review. Installation of the redesigned set does not release-clear
it or resolve any recorded source, rights, or review limit.
