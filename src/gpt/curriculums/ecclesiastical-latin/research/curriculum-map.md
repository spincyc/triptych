# Ecclesiastical Latin — Curriculum Map

## Architectural decision

The course is a self-paced reading-and-composition curriculum whose principal
destination is the 1962 *Missale Romanum* and whose road is deliberately
broader than liturgical formula.  Forty-six inherited teaching units remain
authoritative, but they are now grouped into twenty-two substantial
common-trunk packets.  Eight further packets form an advanced poetry branch
after the common advanced prose and composition trunk.

A packet contains complete teaching, cumulative vocabulary and grammar memory
work, ordinary and authentic reading, composition, integrated transfer work,
all four worksheet variants for every included teaching unit, a qualitative
description of what should reasonably be secure before continuing, and
terminal answers and models.  The four cumulative reviews supply fresh work
and qualitative reflection with complete answers and models.

No calendar controls pacing.  Study events drive recurrence:

- **N:** encounter and understand a new item in context;
- **R0:** retrieve it before leaving the packet section;
- **R1:** retrieve it at the next study event;
- **R2:** retrieve it after another intervening event;
- **R5:** retrieve it after several intervening events;
- **S:** use it in a sentence or connected reconstruction;
- **X:** meet it in a different author, genre, or inflected form; and
- **P:** use it productively in composition.

The guide presents these as a repeatable loop.  The packet cover describes
reasonable ability before continuing, and the reader controls the timing and
number of returns.

## Entry and destination

The opening assumes fluent English reading but no Latin.  It teaches its own
grammar terminology.  The first connected Latin concerns animals, houses,
roads, towns, weather, and work; the opening model is the project-composed
`vulpes fusca est`, “the fox is brown.”  Received sacred text enters
progressively after the reader has enough form and syntax to see it as Latin.

By the end of the common trunk, one should reasonably be able to read a
complete unfamiliar Missal formulary directly, approach unfamiliar classical
and Christian prose with the same method, maintain exact source and uncertainty
records, and plan and revise sustained original Latin prose.  The branch then
adds formal prosody, major quantitative and rhythmic forms, commentary,
imitation, and verse composition.  Pronunciation is not taught in this edition.

## Teaching contract

Each packet moves through these six Parts:

1. **Teaching** — the complete selected grammar or poetry lessons and worked
   examples;
2. **Memory Work** — new and cumulative lexemes, forms, constructions,
   contrasts, cover-and-recall sheets, and reconstruction;
3. **Reading and Composition** — ordinary or received connected Latin with
   source class, local apparatus, translation or model, and a composition
   step;
4. **Cumulative Practice** — integrated transfer tasks, every underlying
   worksheet variant, and a terminal qualitative account of what one should
   reasonably be able to do with fresh material;
5. **Answers and Models** — the same authoritative prompts rendered with
   complete closed answers, acceptable alternatives, or diagnostic models; and
6. **Scope and Sources** — packet boundary, exact sources, claim classes,
   limitations, and metadata.

Definitions state deciding features; contrasts name the mistake that would
produce a different parse.  Earlier packets build composition continuously:
controlled transformation precedes connected description; description becomes
narrative and exposition; propositions become subordinate architecture;
source analysis becomes imitation; imitation becomes guided and free
composition.  Advanced prose composition therefore remains the common trunk's
culmination rather than appearing suddenly at the end.

## Reading and source progression

- **Project-composed ordinary Latin (P):** abundant and connected from M01,
  with controlled vocabulary and full models.
- **Biblical and Missal Latin:** introduced in bounded contrasts and expanded
  as grammar becomes available.
- **Clear classical narrative and exposition:** introduced in the core stage so
  unfamiliar non-liturgical prose becomes normal.
- **Cicero and Augustine:** present as named, exact models in later core,
  Missal-specialization contrasts, and the advanced rhetoric/composition spine.
- **Other prose:** Caesar, Jerome, Ambrose, Aquinas, and other authors appear
  only at verified loci suited to the construction and difficulty.
- **Poetry in the trunk:** word order, ellipsis, parallelism, imagery, basic
  line/form recognition, and enough verse reading for Missal chants and
  sequences.
- **Poetry branch:** textual quantity without pronunciation, scansion, major
  meters, Christian poetics, rhythmic hymnody, commentary, and composition.

Received text, normalization, adaptation, project Latin, and project
translation remain visibly distinct.  The edition manifest and passage
inventory, not an author's prestige or a generic website label, control every
quotation.

## Cumulative reviews

The four companions retain their stable form IDs for source and answer
traceability but render as self-guided cumulative reviews:

- **F-A / F-B:** nominal and verbal foundations, reference, connected ordinary
  reading, and early Missal transfer;
- **C-A / C-B:** complete core syntax across ordinary, classical, biblical, and
  short liturgical prose;
- **M-A / M-B / M-C:** supplied complete formularies and fresh fixed transfer
  texts, all printed in the course rather than selected by another person; and
- **A-A / A-B:** source-first advanced reading, rhetoric, collation, prose
  composition, revision, and research records.

Answers invite comparison, correction, a named next practice, and preservation
of any uncertainty a finite sample leaves unresolved.

## Completeness checks

The structural checker confirms that:

- the registry has twenty-two M rows and eight P rows;
- the forty-six inherited unit IDs occur exactly once across M packets;
- P1–P8 occur exactly once;
- each unit defines A, B, C, and D worksheets and its packet renders all four;
- worksheet and passage IDs are unique and stable;
- every publication leaf is registered, including the guide, reference,
  memory workbook, and four cumulative reviews;
- packet parts and terminal models appear in the declared order; and
- the declared self-paced, qualitative progression language remains consistent.

The lexicon inventory controls vocabulary ownership and recurrence.  The
answer-key audit controls closed answers and open models.  The authentic-source
ladder, edition manifest, passage inventory, and poetry map control exact
received text and source state.
