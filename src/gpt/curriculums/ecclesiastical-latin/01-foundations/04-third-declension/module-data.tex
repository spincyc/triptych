  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Stem first, then endings}
    {A fixed stem-recovery ladder prevents the nominative from being mistaken for the inflectional base.}
    {For each of the lesson's ten review nouns, write four lines: dictionary pair, genitive with \Latin{-is} divided off, recovered stem, and the consonant-stem or i-stem decision with evidence.}
    {Without notes, state the neuter i-stem plural and genitive-plural endings and the evidence required before using them.}
    {Each stem must come from the genitive rather than a guessed nominative. A complete i-stem card gives neuter nominative/accusative plural \Latin{-ia} and genitive plural \Latin{-ium}, while requiring the lesson's dictionary and stem-pattern evidence rather than semantics alone.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {A stem is mastered when it survives number, case, and clause-role changes.}
    {Perform the lesson's king/city transformations, marking the recovered stem under every form and labeling the syntactic reason for each ending. Then make a one-line i-stem card for one lesson noun.}
    {Explain why identical final letters in two forms do not guarantee identical case or number.}
    {The transformation must preserve the lexical stems and change only forms required by the new number or role. The i-stem card must state the diagnostic and relevant ending. The retrieval answer must appeal to the whole ending, declensional class, agreement, government, and clause context.}
