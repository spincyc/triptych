\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Exact paradigms}
    {A small high-frequency mini-lexicon links complete dictionary entries to the forms actually encountered in the Missal.}
    {Build a six-entry card from lesson nouns: two first-declension, two masculine second-declension, and two neuter second-declension entries. For each, write nominative, genitive, gender, stem, and one liturgically frequent oblique form already used in the lesson.}
    {Cover the card and state the neuter rule and the information supplied by a noun's genitive singular.}
    {A correct card preserves each lesson dictionary entry, derives the stem from the genitive, and labels the selected oblique form accurately. The retrieval statement must say that neuter nominative and accusative are identical and that neuter plurals end in \Latin{-a}; the genitive identifies the stem and declensional pattern.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {Changing number or clause role tests a whole paradigm more effectively than reciting endings in a fixed order.}
    {Apply the lesson's singular-to-plural and statement-to-address transformations. Underline the stem once and box the ending in every changed noun.}
    {Explain why a vocative transformation changes \Latin{Dominus} but does not turn an object into a vocative.}
    {The transformed forms must preserve their intended clause roles and use the correct number. A sound explanation distinguishes morphological vocative marking from syntactic role: only the person addressed becomes vocative; an object remains accusative even when another noun changes to direct address.}
