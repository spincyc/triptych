\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Conjugations and present endings}
    {A third-\Latin{-io}/fourth contrast card prevents similar present forms from erasing different dictionary identities.}
    {From the lesson entries for \Latin{capio} and \Latin{audio}, write infinitive, present stem, first singular, third plural, and singular imperative in parallel columns. Circle the evidence that assigns each conjugation.}
    {Name the principal part that distinguishes the two classes and state one present-form resemblance that does not decide the class.}
    {The infinitives \Latin{capere} and \Latin{audire} assign third-\Latin{-io} and fourth conjugation. Present \Latin{capio/audio} and third plurals \Latin{capiunt/audiunt} show resemblance, but principal parts control classification.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Imperfect and future}
    {The same English future can come from two Latin future systems, while imperfect forms share a broader \Latin{-ba-} marker.}
    {Make a contrast table for first/second-conjugation future and third/third-\Latin{-io}/fourth future. Add one imperfect form from each class and divide stem, tense sign, and ending.}
    {From memory, state which future uses \Latin{-bo/-bi-} and which begins first singular in \Latin{-am}.}
    {First and second conjugations use the \Latin{-bo, -bis, -bit} system; third, third-\Latin{-io}, and fourth use \Latin{-am, -es, -et}. Imperfect forms must preserve the class-specific stem connection before \Latin{-ba-} and the personal ending.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {A synopsis across person and tense reveals whether a learner is producing forms or merely reciting one row.}
    {Choose one lesson verb from each conjugation. Write first singular and third plural in present, imperfect, and future, then annotate stem, tense sign, and ending. Check only after all fifteen cells are complete.}
    {Explain why person and number must be identified before an English subject is supplied.}
    {Every cell must preserve the correct conjugation and tense system. The retrieval answer must state that the finite ending encodes person and number; English often requires an explicit pronoun that Latin need not print.}
