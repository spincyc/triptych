  \SubmoduleExtension
    {First/second-declension adjectives}
    {An agreement-chain diagram separates the controller noun from every adjective coordinate it determines.}
    {For three lesson noun phrases of different genders, place the noun in a center box and draw separate gender, number, and case arrows to its adjective. Transform each phrase to plural and update only the coordinates that change.}
    {Explain why a feminine noun does not require the noun and adjective to belong to the same declension.}
    {Every arrow must carry matching gender, number, and case before and after transformation. A correct explanation says that agreement coordinates are grammatical features; noun and adjective retain their own inflectional systems.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Third-declension adjectives}
    {Dictionary terminations tell how many nominative forms are displayed, not how many genders the adjective can modify.}
    {Turn the lesson's two-termination and one-termination adjective entries into mini-paradigm cards. On each, mark the genitive stem and the neuter nominative/accusative forms.}
    {State the difference between a one-termination dictionary entry and an indeclinable word.}
    {Each card must show a declining genitive and the relevant neuter forms. A one-termination adjective has one nominative form shared across genders but still declines; an indeclinable word would not change for case, number, or gender.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {The three adjective uses can share identical morphology, so clause structure must determine the label.}
    {For the lesson's attributive, predicate, and substantival examples, draw the controller chain where one is expressed and write the evidence that assigns each use. Then complete one lesson transformation across unlike declensions.}
    {Define the three uses without using only their English translations.}
    {Attributive adjectives modify an expressed noun inside its phrase; predicate adjectives complete a linking assertion about a subject; substantival adjectives stand in the noun slot without an expressed head noun. The transformation must preserve the controller's gender, number, and case across all agreeing words.}
