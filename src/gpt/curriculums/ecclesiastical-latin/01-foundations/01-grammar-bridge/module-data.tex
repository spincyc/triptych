\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {The English bridge}
    {The same visible word can fill different sentence jobs, so a neutral clause map protects the learner from importing English order into Latin.}
    {Make a four-column map for \Latin{Deum laudat Maria}: printed form, possible morphology, contextual function, and evidence. Then reorder the Latin twice without changing the roles.}
    {Without notes, define \emph{form} and \emph{function} and give one example in which first position does not mark the subject.}
    {A complete map identifies \Latin{Deum} as accusative object, \Latin{laudat} as third-person singular finite verb, and \Latin{Maria} as nominative subject. Accept any two grammatical reorderings that preserve those endings. The retrieval answer must make endings and clause government—not position—the deciding evidence.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Orthography without a pronunciation course}
    {Edition typography can change the page's appearance without changing the underlying form or syntax.}
    {Normalize \Latin{JESVS, EVANGELIVM, praeceptum} to the course house style, then annotate which changes are graphic and which, if any, affect grammatical analysis.}
    {State why capitalization, \Latin{i/j}, \Latin{u/v}, and \Latin{æ/ae} cannot by themselves assign subject or object.}
    {The course forms are \Latin{Iesus, Evangelium, præceptum}. All named changes are graphic here; none assigns a clause function. A sound answer says that endings, agreement, and government decide the parse.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {What a dictionary entry promises}
    {A headword is a compressed paradigm, not merely a translation label.}
    {Expand \Latin{rex, regis m.}, \Latin{omnis, omne}, and \Latin{sequor, sequi, secutus sum} into the minimum facts needed to recognize unfamiliar forms.}
    {For each part of speech, name the listed part that reveals the stem or inflectional system.}
    {For \Latin{rex}, the genitive reveals stem \Latin{reg-} and gender is masculine; \Latin{omnis, omne} identifies a two-termination third-declension adjective; \Latin{sequor} supplies a deponent present infinitive and perfect participial principal part. Merely giving English glosses does not satisfy the task.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {Copying becomes useful only when it forces retrieval, comparison, and correction rather than visual imitation.}
    {Copy one lesson clause, cover it, reconstruct it from a clause map, and mark every difference between the reconstruction and the source as form, order, spelling, or omitted content.}
    {Explain why a corrected full sentence is more diagnostic than copying one isolated ending.}
    {The reconstruction must preserve the original proposition and grammatical relations. A defensible explanation says that a full sentence retests agreement, government, and clause structure together, while an isolated ending may conceal the source of the error.}
