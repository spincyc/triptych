  \SubmoduleExtension
    {One stem, three tenses}
    {An irregular-stem retrieval card ties each perfect form to its actual third principal part rather than a guessed present stem.}
    {Using only verbs printed in the lesson, make cards with present headword on the front and third principal part, recovered perfect stem, third singular perfect, and first plural pluperfect on the back. Test the irregular cards twice.}
    {State how to recover the stem and why conjugation number alone cannot predict it.}
    {Each card removes final \Latin{-i} from the third principal part to recover the perfect stem and then adds the correct perfect-system material. A complete explanation notes that sound changes and suppletion make many perfect stems lexical facts learned from principal parts.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Handwritten study and controlled composition}
    {A reference-point timeline prevents perfect, pluperfect, future perfect, and imperfect from collapsing into an undifferentiated past.}
    {Place the lesson's perfect/imperfect contrast on a line, then add one lesson-derived pluperfect event before a past reference point and one future-perfect event before a future reference point. Label completion and reference point separately.}
    {Define each perfect-system tense by its relation to a reference point, not by an English auxiliary alone.}
    {Perfect presents completion relative to the present or as a bounded past event; pluperfect places completion before a past reference point; future perfect places it before a future point. Imperfect presents past action as ongoing, repeated, or incomplete. The Latin forms and proposed English must agree with those relations.}
