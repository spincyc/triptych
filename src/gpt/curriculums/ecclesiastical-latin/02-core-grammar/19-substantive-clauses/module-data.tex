  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Clause families}
    {A governor dependency diagram shows both the clause's noun slot and the construction licensed by its governor.}
    {For every family in the lesson, put the governing expression in a left box, the marker and mood in a center box, and the filled noun slot in a right box. Add a separate label for volitive, fact/result, factual, hindrance/doubt, or indirect question.}
    {Name the four noun slots a substantive clause can fill.}
    {The diagram must distinguish subject, object, apposition, and predicate complement. Each construction label must be supported by the governor and marker; the marker alone cannot substitute for the dependency.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {Near-identical surface clauses can fill different slots or express different dependencies.}
    {Choose four contrasting lesson examples. For each, bracket the whole clause, draw its syntactic link to the governor, label the noun slot, then state the narrowest family and the evidence.}
    {Explain why factual \Latin{quod/quia/quoniam} is not automatically interchangeable with fact/result \Latin{ut}.}
    {Each connective belongs to an attested construction and presents content differently. The answer must preserve the lesson's governor, mood, and dependency instead of treating English \English{that} as proof of interchangeability.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {Controlled composition checks whether governor, marker, mood, sequence, and noun slot work as one system.}
    {Complete the lesson's five models. Above each clause write the governor, slot, family, marker, and sequence; then make one model negative without changing its family.}
    {State why a grammatically correct subjunctive is still insufficient when the governor does not license the intended construction.}
    {All five models must use the specified lexical governors and appropriate markers and mood. The negative transformation must choose the family-specific marker. A complete rubric requires a valid dependency, not only a well-formed isolated verb.}
