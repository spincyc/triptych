\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Direct volition}
    {A speaker/addressee/person map selects imperative, jussive, or hortatory form before polarity is added.}
    {Make rows for second-person command, first-person plural exhortation, and third-person jussive. Add positive and ordinary negative columns and fill them from the lesson.}
    {State which persons an ordinary imperative addresses and which mood supplies first- and third-person volition.}
    {Imperatives ordinarily address second person; first-person plural and third-person volition use the subjunctive in hortatory or jussive use. Negative forms must follow the distinct prohibition patterns printed in the lesson rather than simply adding \Latin{non}.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {Wishes require both time and fulfillment status, while fear clauses require the feared proposition to be stated before translating the marker.}
    {For each lesson wish, label present/past/future orientation and fulfilled, possible, or unfulfilled status. For each fear clause, write the feared event in neutral English before deciding how \Latin{ne}, \Latin{ut}, or \Latin{ne non} maps it.}
    {Recite the ordinary fear-clause marker logic.}
    {The wish label must follow its tense and context. After a verb of fearing, \Latin{ne} introduces what is feared may happen; \Latin{ut} introduces what is feared may fail to happen; \Latin{ne non} can make the negative proposition explicit. The final English must not reverse it.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {A controlled set tests whether person, polarity, wish reality, and feared event are chosen independently.}
    {Complete the lesson's command, prohibition, wish, and fear prompts. Above each verb write speaker, addressee, person, mood, polarity, and time/reality where relevant.}
    {Explain why the same English \English{may} can represent more than one Latin use.}
    {Each form must match its intended person and construction; both fear versions must preserve the same feared proposition. English \English{may} can render a wish, jussive, possibility, or dependent subjunctive, so the Latin governor and clause relation decide the use.}
