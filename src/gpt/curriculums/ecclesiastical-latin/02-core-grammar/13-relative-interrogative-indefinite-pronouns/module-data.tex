\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {The working method}
    {Stage II clauses require morphology, governor, signal, clause type, and sense to be recorded in that order.}
    {Apply the five-step method to one relative example already analyzed in the lesson. Cover the lesson's answer, write each step on a separate line, and mark where antecedent evidence enters and where clause-internal government enters.}
    {Recite the five steps and explain why translation belongs after clause classification.}
    {A complete analysis names the form, its governor or clause role, the relative/interrogative signal, the resulting clause type, and only then its contextual sense. Antecedent gender/number and internal case must appear as separate decisions.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Exact forms and rules}
    {A two-axis card makes visible that antecedent agreement and clause case answer different questions.}
    {Draw a card with gender/number on the horizontal axis and internal case/function on the vertical axis. Place four lesson relative forms, including one neuter, and annotate the evidence on both axes.}
    {State the two-axis rule without looking at a paradigm.}
    {Every form must match its antecedent in gender and number and take its case from its own clause. The rule is complete only if neither axis is allowed to dictate the other.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {Question, ordinary relative, headless relative, and indefinite readings depend on clause architecture and discourse signals.}
    {Classify the lesson's contextual examples into those four families. For each, underline the signal and identify any expressed antecedent or explain why none is required.}
    {Give one diagnostic that separates interrogative \Latin{quid} from headless-relative \Latin{quod} in context.}
    {The classification must cite punctuation or interrogative force, an expressed antecedent, a self-contained noun slot, or an indefinite particle as appropriate. No single English gloss is sufficient; the clause's syntactic job is decisive.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {Controlled minimal changes test whether the learner can hold antecedent coordinates constant while changing internal case.}
    {Use the lesson's relative-clause composition pattern. Write two project-composed sentences with the same antecedent: make the relative subject in one and object in the other, then label both axes.}
    {Explain which relative-pronoun coordinates stayed fixed and which changed.}
    {Gender and number remain fixed because the antecedent is unchanged; case changes with the relative's new clause function. The sentences must be grammatical and the labels must make that independent change explicit.}
