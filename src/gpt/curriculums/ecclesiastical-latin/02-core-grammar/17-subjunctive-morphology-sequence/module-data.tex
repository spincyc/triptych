  \SubmoduleExtension
    {The four tenses}
    {A morphology-before-use flowchart prevents a familiar translation from assigning a construction prematurely.}
    {For twelve lesson forms, follow four boxes: identify stem and markers, state person/number/tense/voice/mood, list any formal ambiguity, and only then open the clause context.}
    {Recite the formation cue for each of the four ordinary subjunctive tenses.}
    {The forms must be generated by the lesson's present-subjunctive markers, present infinitive plus endings for imperfect, perfect-stem formation for perfect, and perfect infinitive plus endings for pluperfect, with passive formations preserved. No clause-use label belongs before morphology is complete.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Sequence of tenses}
    {A two-axis chart separates governing sequence from the dependent action's relative time.}
    {Place primary and historic governing verbs on one axis and same/subsequent and prior dependent time on the other. Fill the four lesson tense choices and add one project-composed English scenario to each cell without composing new Latin.}
    {State which dependent tenses ordinarily fill the four cells.}
    {Primary plus same/subsequent ordinarily uses present subjunctive; primary plus prior uses perfect; historic plus same/subsequent uses imperfect; historic plus prior uses pluperfect. The scenario must preserve those time relations.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {Only after morphology and sequence are fixed can governor and signal support a use label.}
    {For each subjunctive in one lesson reading, write a four-line dossier: full parse, governing word, connective or signal, and narrowest defensible use. Add any genuine ambiguity instead of forcing one label.}
    {Explain why \Latin{ut} plus subjunctive is not enough evidence by itself for purpose.}
    {The dossier must consider the governing expression, internal markers, negation, sequence, and discourse force. \Latin{ut} introduces several clause families, so subjunctive mood and connective alone do not distinguish them.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {A time diagram tests sequence while keeping syntactic use constant.}
    {Use the lesson's \Latin{rogat}/\Latin{rogavit} diagrams. Replace only the governing time and select the dependent tense that preserves same or prior relation; label the relation before checking the model.}
    {State what changed and what stayed constant in each pair.}
    {The governing sequence and dependent tense change together, while the intended relative-time relation and clause dependency remain constant. Correct pairs follow the four-cell sequence chart rather than an English tense imitation.}
