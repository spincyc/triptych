\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading what endings connect}
    {G/P/I labels separate grammatical necessity, strict parallel recovery, and interpretive help before a supply enters reconstruction.}
    {Apply G, P, or I to every omitted element proposed in the lesson's ellipsis examples. For each G or P, cite the ending, government, or strict parallel that licenses it; keep every I outside the reconstructed Latin.}
    {Define G, P, and I and state which labels may enter a clause reconstruction.}
    {G is grammatically required, such as an implicit subject contained in a finite verb; P is recoverable from a strict parallel member; I is interpretive help. Only justified G and P supplies enter reconstruction, and no label licenses an invented agent, cause, or theological proposition.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {A period diagram holds distant dependencies without mistaking punctuation or proximity for syntax.}
    {Choose one worked period already printed in the lesson. Place finite governors on the center line, attach subjects and complements, branch subordinate clauses from their governors, and put address/apposition material in separate frames. Number the printed order around the diagram.}
    {Explain how the diagram can preserve emphasis even after a prose-order line is written.}
    {Every printed word must appear exactly once in the diagram and attach by morphology or government. The numbered original positions preserve fronting and delay as rhetorical facts; the prose-order line exposes dependencies without pretending the original order was accidental.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {A five-version record prevents prose-order reconstruction and idiomatic translation from silently changing the source.}
    {Complete the lesson sequence: diplomatic copy, marked syntax, prose-order Latin, close English, and idiomatic English. Add a period diagram and a G/P/I ledger between marked syntax and prose order.}
    {State the invariant that all five versions must preserve.}
    {All printed words, grammatical relations, negation, agency, time, and propositional content must remain accounted for. Supplies require G/P/I labels; prose order may rearrange but not rewrite; idiomatic English may smooth expression but not reverse or omit a relation.}
