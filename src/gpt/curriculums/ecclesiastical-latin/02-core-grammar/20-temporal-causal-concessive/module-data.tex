\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Mood follows viewpoint}
    {A \Latin{cum} matrix separates temporal reference from circumstantial, causal, and concessive framing.}
    {Build rows for temporal, circumstantial, causal, and concessive \Latin{cum}; add columns for ordinary mood, time or viewpoint, contextual signal, and possible English rendering. Fill only claims supported by the lesson.}
    {Explain why subjunctive \Latin{cum} does not automatically mean the event is unreal.}
    {The matrix assigns indicative to straightforward temporal assertion where appropriate and subjunctive to circumstantial, causal, or concessive framing as taught. Subjunctive marks presentation or dependency here, not necessarily unreality.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Reading the forms in context}
    {Cause can be asserted by the writer or attributed to another viewpoint, and the mood helps preserve that stance.}
    {For the lesson examples, label whose explanation is being presented and whether it is asserted, circumstantial, reported, or conceded. Underline the conjunction and parse the finite verb before translating.}
    {State one piece of contextual evidence, besides mood, that supports a reported-cause reading.}
    {A defensible answer identifies an attribution, speaker shift, distancing context, or governing discourse that makes the cause someone else's allegation. Mood supports but does not alone create that stance.}
  \SubmoduleExtension
    {Writing laboratory}
    {Changing an asserted cause into a reported explanation tests whether mood and discourse stance change together.}
    {Perform the lesson's cause transformation. Make a before/after chart naming connective, mood, speaker commitment, and English rendering; preserve the underlying proposition.}
    {Explain what changes when stance changes and what must remain constant.}
    {The proposition and time relation remain; the presentation of the cause and ordinarily its mood change. The English should signal assertion versus attribution without adding certainty or unreality not present in the Latin construction.}
