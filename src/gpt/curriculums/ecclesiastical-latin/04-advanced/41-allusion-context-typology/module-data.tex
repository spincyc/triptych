\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{The allusion dossier}
    {A dossier makes the reasoning reproducible by placing wording, grammar, context, and counterevidence in separate fields.}
    {Using only the proposed relationship selected for Worksheet IV.10, fill six fields: liturgical form, source window, exact overlap, adapted morphology, evidence against, and final classification.}
    {Downgrade the claim if the overlap consists only of common vocabulary.}
    {A model copies only the text already selected in the worksheet, distinguishes exact from lemma-level overlap, records every formal change, names a closer alternative or negative search result, and permits parallel/formula or unresolved as successful outcomes.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Typology as contextual reading}
    {Typological reading asks what a verified source context contributes; it does not transform a two-word resemblance into a source claim.}
    {For the completed dossier, write two paragraphs: first the grammatical relationship, then the contextual contribution. If the link fails, write why the proposed context must not govern interpretation.}
    {State the stopping rule when dependence is unestablished.}
    {The first paragraph cites forms and classification; the second uses only verified source context. When the relation is formula, parallel, or rejected, the model stops short of treating that source as controlling.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Negative-result check}
    {Rejecting an attractive proposal is positive evidence of calibrated method.}
    {Test one two-word overlap from the dossier against commonness, a closer alternative, syntactic fit, and wider context.}
    {Choose quotation, adaptation, formula, echo, parallel, or unresolved and give one sentence of evidence.}
    {Common words without distinctive sequence, syntax, or contextual fit warrant formula or parallel at most. Unresolved is acceptable when the search record is incomplete; certainty is not rewarded.}
