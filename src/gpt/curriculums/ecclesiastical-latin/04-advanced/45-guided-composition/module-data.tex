\newcommand{\ModuleEnrichment}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{Proposition before sentence}
    {Planning relations first prevents vocabulary search from deciding the thought.}
    {Write six English proposition cards for one project: agent, action, object, ground, contrast or circumstance, and intended end. Join them with labeled arrows before choosing Latin.}
    {Remove one card and state which later clause would lose its governor or purpose.}
    {A passing plan contains complete propositions and explicit relations. The model identifies the dependent proposition whose role would become unclear, rather than merely shortening the eventual wording.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Four guided forms: choosing a form}
    {Genre choice should follow communicative purpose and evidence, not whichever model offers the most reusable wording.}
    {For the same proposition set, outline how Collect, narrative, exposition, and commentary would organize it; select one and state why.}
    {Name one structural demand unique to the selected form.}
    {The outlines differ in discourse job and architecture. A valid choice cites purpose and required structure, and explicitly rejects wholesale borrowing from a received model.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Collect-shaped exercise}
    {A Collect-shaped composition is an instructional architecture, not a newly authorized liturgical prayer.}
    {Using project content, draft address, ground, petition, and intended end in four separate lines before joining them.}
    {Label the governor of every subordinate clause.}
    {The model remains labeled project-composed, has one recoverable petition spine, and attaches ground and desired end unambiguously. Formulaic wording is documented if borrowed.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Narrative paragraph}
    {A narrative plan needs stable participants, ordered events, background, and a controlled tense system.}
    {Turn four project events into a participant/tense ledger, then draft a five-sentence Latin narrative without copying a Gospel sequence.}
    {For every pronoun, identify its antecedent from the ledger.}
    {A passing narrative preserves event order and participant reference, distinguishes background from completed action where claimed, and records any model idiom used.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Expository paragraph}
    {Exposition defines, distinguishes, and supports a claim rather than merely naming a theme.}
    {Write one project definition, one contrast, one example, and one conclusion in Latin; underline the connective making each relation explicit.}
    {State the deciding feature in the definition.}
    {The model's definition gives a genus or category and a differentiating feature; contrast and example genuinely test that feature. A string of synonyms is not exposition.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Commentary note}
    {Commentary moves from an exact form through syntax to a restrained interpretive observation.}
    {Select one form already printed in the lesson and write morphology, governor, clause role, contextual sense, and one limited observation in five sentences.}
    {Cross out any sentence not supported by the form or its immediate context.}
    {Every retained sentence traces to the printed lesson evidence. Broader theology, history, or source claims require their own checked evidence and may be omitted without penalty.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Three-pass revision}
    {Separate passes expose structural errors before surface polish hides them.}
    {Mark the actual first draft in three colors: skeleton for finite spine and clause attachment, concord for morphology/agreement/reference, and idiom for model-supported diction and order.}
    {Record one change from each pass and the rule behind it.}
    {The model preserves the marked first draft, gives a reason for every substantive change, and never treats stylistic preference as a substitute for correcting government or agreement.}
