\newcommand{\ModulePractice}{%
  \SubmoduleExtension{The imitation ladder}
    {Increasing freedom should change one controlled dimension at a time so the learner can locate a failure.}
    {Starting from the lesson model, list its invariant clause slots. Draft three project-composed stages: lexical substitution, changed subject/object with full cascade, then changed proposition with the same architecture.}
    {Circle the first stage at which a governing construction changes.}
    {A passing sequence labels every sentence project-composed, preserves the stated invariants at each stage, and marks all agreement/reference changes. If government changes unintentionally, the learner returns to the prior stage.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{The calque audit}
    {A calque diagnosis names the English-driven construction and replaces it from a checked Latin pattern.}
    {For the completed imitation, make three columns: suspect phrase, why English structure drove it, and revised Latin with model or grammar support.}
    {Name one revision that is stylistic and one required by government.}
    {The required revision must cite a case, complement, or clause construction; the stylistic revision may improve order or cohesion while preserving grammar. A bare claim that the revision ``sounds better'' does not pass.}
  \SubmoduleExtension{Worked invariant-and-change ledger}
    {A visible ledger separates legitimate imitation from disguised copying.}
    {Annotate one finished draft with S for preserved structure, P for changed proposition, M for morphological cascade, and B for borrowed phrase recorded in the source ledger.}
    {Explain why an unchanged long phrase requires stronger documentation than an unchanged clause type.}
    {The model records borrowed wording and its source, while structural labels describe abstract syntax. Unacknowledged extended wording is copying; a shared clause architecture is the declared object of imitation.}
