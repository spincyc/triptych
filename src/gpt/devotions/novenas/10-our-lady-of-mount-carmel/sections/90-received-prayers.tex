\section{The Complete \textit{Flos Carmeli}}

\subsection{Text, history, and variants}

The \textit{Flos Carmeli} is a medieval Carmelite sequence whose images join biblical Carmel, Marian virginity and maternity, protection, struggle, counsel, hope, and final glory. Traditional attribution to Simon Stock is not historically secure. Its reception belongs to the later medieval Carmelite liturgical and devotional history; this booklet does not project the complete text into 1251.

The short prayer in Section~6 consists of the first two stanzas. Two principal readings circulate in the last petition: \textit{esto propitia}, ``be gracious,'' and \textit{da privilegia}, ``grant favors or privileges.'' The short form uses \textit{da privilegia} because that is the Latin variant paired with Kenelm Digby Best's 1902 English verse. The complete Latin below uses the received \textit{esto propitia} form. Neither variant can mean exemption from the Gospel, judgment, repentance, or the sacramental life.

The Latin below is a received full form. No complete English witness with both adequate provenance and clear reuse rights was located, so no English translation is supplied. In an authorized celebration use the competent Carmelite book.

\begin{studybox}{How the images are governed}
``Armor'' and ``battles'' name spiritual struggle, not violence against persons. The Scapular's protection is Marian intercession within Christ's victory. ``Key and gate'' is poetic subordinate agency: Christ alone is the door in the primary saving sense (Jn 10:7--9), while Mary intercedes for pilgrims and exemplifies the Church already brought to glory. Every stanza must be read within that Christological grammar.
\end{studybox}

\begin{studybox}{Copyright and use note}
The medieval Latin is reproduced as the governing prayer witness. The familiar complete modern English version has an unidentified translator, and the attributable 1984 Carmelite version remains under copyright; neither is reproduced. Best's public-domain English covers only the short form.
\end{studybox}
