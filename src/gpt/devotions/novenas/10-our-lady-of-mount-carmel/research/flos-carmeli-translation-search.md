# Complete *Flos Carmeli* English-Translation Search

## Controlling question

This record documents both the search for and the subsequent selection of an identified human English translation of the complete expanded *Flos Carmeli* for the full guide and its mechanically derived *Daily Prayer Book*. During the initial search, both publications printed `esto propitia` in the second unit and the singular `hic adesse / me tibi servulum / patiaris` in the third. After approval to use all three located versions was confirmed, the publication selected the 1993 joint Carmelite Proper version and adopted its own paired plural `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum / patiaris` Latin rather than silently attaching that English to the former singular witness.

Under `guidance/devotions/novenas.md`, a usable English vocal prayer must have a complete adjacent Latin or Greek source, an exact identified human translation witness, and a recorded publication-rights basis. Project or AI translation, conflation, modernization, and unattributed web text are excluded.

## Search boundary and method

The expanded search was conducted on 15 July 2026. It included:

- all 174 records returned across nine pages by the Internet Archive / Open Library full-text search for the exact phrase `Flos Carmeli`, including all 50 distinct records dated no later than 1930 and the 20 undated records;
- OCR-signature searches for the later Latin stanzas, especially `hic adesse me tibi servulum`, `Carmeli domina` with `reple laetitia`, and `Paradisi clavis et ianua`;
- searches for the known English incipits and later-stanza renderings, including the familiar `Tall vine blossom laden` / `Strong stem of Jesse` text;
- direct page-image checks of the plausible open English-language prayer books, hymnals, Carmelite histories, and Scapular manuals rather than acceptance of OCR alone;
- the complete OCR of eleven volumes of *The Carmelite Review* (1893--1903), together with open Carmelite histories, manuals, novenas, hymnals, and anthologies from the seventeenth through early twentieth centuries;
- Google Books, WorldCat, Library of Congress, periodical, and general exact-phrase searches for bibliographic or textual leads; HathiTrust catalog access was attempted but was unavailable and is not claimed as a completed search; and
- current Carmelite, Discalced Carmelite, Holy See communication, publisher, and permissions pages for provenance and reuse evidence concerning modern complete versions.

Search-engine and OCR coverage is not exhaustive evidence that no other witness exists. The durable historical conclusion is narrower: no complete English witness with a public reuse basis was located within this search boundary. No publisher or rights holder was contacted during the search, and no permission was inferred from a text's public online availability. After that search closed, the requester explicitly confirmed that all three located versions had approval for use. That requester-attested permission is the reproduction basis recorded for this revision; it does not establish a public license or wider republication right.

The search also corrected the text's developmental description. John Sullivan's study reports a medieval core corresponding to the first two five-line units, later Discalced Carmelite additions, and a 1933 Proper containing only the first six units. The eight-unit text searched here is therefore the received expanded form of a medieval sequence, not an eight-unit medieval archetype. <https://www.teresianum.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Ter_49_1998-1_165-185.pdf>

The John Sullivan lead comes from a 2011 Sisters of Mount Carmel newsletter, while Sullivan's own 1998 article identifies only his translation of an added Edith Stein stanza and does not claim the received sequence translation. The lead therefore cannot satisfy the attribution requirement without the Proper's front matter or publisher confirmation. <https://sistersofmountcarmel.org/pdf/july11.pdf> <https://www.teresianum.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Ter_49_1998-1_165-185.pdf>

Edizioni Carmelitane / Institutum Carmelitanum appeared to be the likely permissions contact, but no inquiry was sent during the search. ## Selected reproduction witness

The exact reproduction witness is the complete eight-unit Latin--English table published by Carmelite Quotes on 6 July 2024 and expressly captioned “English translation, Carmelite Proper of the Liturgy of the Hours.” It ends at `coronaris.` / `crowned in glory.` and contains no `Amen`; the publication therefore does not carry over `Amen` or the optional seasonal `(Alleluia.)` from the unselected 2007 witness. The two-card division after the fourth unit is typographic only.

Sullivan's photographically checked 1998 article, p. 170 n. 16, independently reproduces the first six English units and confirms that `his together.` is a substantive source reading rather than an OCR error. It also corroborates `flower in the cradling bud` and `rich your mercy.` Sullivan differs from the complete 2024 transcription in several punctuation and metrification details, including `rages around us` rather than `rages ’round us`; the publication follows the complete 2024 witness exactly and does not conflate the two. The last two units are corroborated only by the located complete transcription. The 1993 book's exact printed page and front-matter translator credit remain unverified because no scan or preview was obtained.

## Public-domain and historical near misses

- Kenelm Digby Best, C.O., *A Priest's Poems* (Burns & Oates; Benziger Brothers, 1902), p. 136, supplies the identified public-domain English already used for the short `da privilegia` form, but ends after the first two stanzas.
- John Patrick, *Reflexions upon the Devotions of the Roman Church* (1674), p. 258, gives an English verse rendering only of the short form. <https://archive.org/details/reflexi00patr/page/n275/mode/1up>
- *Legends of the Blessed Virgin* (1852), pp. 256--257, gives another short English paraphrase opposite only the two-stanza `da privilegia` Latin.
- Mrs. Franz Liebich, *St Simon Stock* (Catholic Truth Society, 1901), pp. 18--19, translates only the short `da privilegia` form.
- *The Irish Ecclesiastical Record* 9 (1901), pp. 402--403, prints a short English verse beside only the first two Latin stanzas.
- The Marist Brothers' *American Catholic Hymnal* (P. J. Kenedy & Sons, copyright 1915), p. 316, hymn 227, paraphrases the short prayer and adds a new Elijah stanza; p. 443, hymn 357, prints only the short Latin. Neither is the complete received sequence. <https://archive.org/details/americancatholi00mari/page/n319/mode/1up> <https://archive.org/details/americancatholi00mari/page/n446/mode/1up>
- The 1893--1903 *Carmelite Review* volumes and the other searched manuals, histories, hymnals, anthologies, and Scapular books yielded the Latin, the short form, prose summaries, or mentions, but no complete English sequence published through 1930.

## Controlling result

The search established three complete modern English witnesses and identified the 1993 joint Carmelite Proper version as the strongest choice. The requester's subsequent confirmation of approval for all three removed the use barrier for this publication. The 1993-attributed version was selected because it combines the broadest institutional Carmelite provenance with a complete paired Latin witness; its communal plural reading also suits the novena's shared recitation. Both publications now reproduce that exact Carmelite-source Latin and English in two adjacent bilingual cards. The translator-credit and direct-book-inspection limits remain explicit, while the permission, source status, and this novena's lack of ecclesiastical approval remain distinct.
