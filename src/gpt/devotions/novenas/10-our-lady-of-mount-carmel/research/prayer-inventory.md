## Inventory controls

- The full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import every source-specific prayer above from the same canonical fragment under `prayers/`; the Sign of the Cross, Lord's Prayer, and Lesser Doxology remain singly owned by the collection's shared prayer source. The companion owns no duplicate prayer wording or translation.
- No prayer originating solely in a modern vernacular source is included.
- No current proprietary English Missal, Liturgy of the Hours, Carmelite proper, ritual, or Bible prayer is reproduced. The daily English collects are new project translations, not official translations.
- Every printed English vocal prayer has its complete Latin source text immediately adjacent. There is no English-only fixed offering, intention, response, or concluding formula.
- The spontaneous statement and surrender of the reader's intention is an instruction rather than a fixed vocal formula.
- The short *Flos Carmeli* is not represented as the full sequence; the complete form appears separately and is substituted rather than redundantly added on Day 9.
- “Armor,” “battle,” “Scapular protection,” “key,” and “gate” in the sequence are governed by the document's Christological, nonviolent, and subordinate-intercession explanation.
- The nine daily Latin collects are unadapted received texts visually collated against the printed 1987 *Collectio*. The abbreviated printed conclusions are expanded conventionally and visibly disclosed; no day-specific clause is spliced into a prayer.
- Approval belongs to each collect in the *Collectio*'s proper liturgical context. It does not approve the thematic selection, project translations, devotional order, or novena and does not authorize these pages for liturgical use.
- The *Collectio* Latin is excluded third-party liturgical material. The new English translations, selection, arrangement, and surrounding notes are project-created; the source facsimile and its OCR remain external.
- Before ecclesiastical submission, public parish adoption, or any claim of linguistic or translation maturity, the received Latin and project English still require independent line-by-line liturgical, Latin, and translation review.
