Every vocal prayer printed for recitation in the publication is inventoried here. Historical exposition may name or briefly discuss a prayer without assigning it for recitation. Stress accents in the daily collect bodies follow the printed 1987 *Collectio*; the completed conclusions reproduce the words of the governing Roman-Missal formula with the booklet's established stress accents. Accents in the medieval prayers are reading aids and do not assert an accented archetype.

## Inventory controls

- The full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import every source-specific prayer above from the same canonical fragment under `prayers/`; the Sign of the Cross, Lord's Prayer, and Lesser Doxology remain singly owned by the collection's shared prayer source. The companion owns no duplicate prayer wording or translation.
- No prayer originating solely in a modern vernacular source is included, and no project-authored or project-translated prayer remains.
- No current proprietary English Missal, ritual, or Bible prayer is reproduced. The approved 2012 English collects are source pointers only. The focused complete *Flos Carmeli* English attributed to the 1993 Carmelite Proper is the sole modern Proper text reproduced, under the requester-attested approval recorded above.
- Every printed English vocal prayer has its complete Latin source text immediately adjacent. There is no English-only fixed offering, intention, response, or concluding formula.
- The spontaneous statement and surrender of the reader's intention is an instruction rather than a fixed vocal formula.
- The short *Flos Carmeli* is not represented as the full sequence; the complete form appears separately and is substituted rather than redundantly added on Day 9.
- The complete *Flos Carmeli* uses the selected 1993-attributed English only beside its own `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum` Latin. It is not conflated with `hic adesse / me tibi servulum`; the odd source readings `his together` and `rich your mercy` are not silently emended; and `Amen` is not imported from the unselected 2007 witness. Public availability itself was not treated as permission: the separate user confirmation supplies the recorded use basis.
- “Armor,” “battle,” “Scapular protection,” “key,” and “gate” in the sequence are governed by the document's Christological, nonviolent, and subordinate-intercession explanation.
- The nine daily Latin collects are unadapted received texts visually collated against the printed 1987 *Collectio*. Its decree treats the collection as an appendix to the Roman Missal, and Praenotanda 22 says it changes neither the 1975 *Missale Romanum* nor its rubrics. The abbreviated endings are completed by the exact longer Roman-Missal formulas and `Amen` specified in GIRM 54; no day-specific clause is spliced into a prayer.
- Approval belongs to each collect in the *Collectio*'s proper liturgical context. It does not approve the thematic selection, devotional order, or novena and does not authorize these pages for liturgical use.
- The *Collectio* Latin is excluded third-party liturgical material. The selection, arrangement, source pointers, and surrounding notes are project-created; the source facsimile and its OCR remain external.
- ICEL's standing copyright policy requires written permission or a license for the approved English; none was obtained, so those texts are not reproduced. The USCCB's published 2025 successor guidelines, effective 29 November 2026 rather than on this inventory's as-of date, likewise require authentication and permission. Before ecclesiastical submission or public parish adoption, the received Latin still requires independent liturgical and Latin review.
