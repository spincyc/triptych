## Non-transfer rules

The following forms of reception must never be silently transferred to another object:

1. Approval of the feast or Marian title does not authenticate the 1251 event.
2. Approval of the Scapular and its rite does not authenticate every origin narrative, salvation formula, or Sabbatine claim.
3. Papal commendation of a devotion or description of a venerable tradition is not automatically a supernatural-fact decree.
4. Canonization, Carmelite membership, shrine use, a confraternity, an indulgence, or an approved prayer would not by itself establish an apparition's historicity.
5. The general doctrine of Mary's intercession does not establish a literal time-bound promise about purgatory.
6. A collect's approval in its source book does not approve this thematic selection or the novena; devotional reproduction here does not cancel the collect's received proper-context status. Approval of the modern English likewise does not grant republication rights.

## Publication wording

Permitted formulations include “Carmelite tradition associates,” “conventionally dated,” “venerable tradition,” “approved Scapular sacramental,” “received Latin collect approved for its proper context,” “approved English cited but not reproduced,” “late and disputed documentary provenance,” and “no positive event decree is claimed.” Prohibited formulations include “the Church proved the 1251 apparition,” “Mary guarantees salvation to anyone wearing the cloth,” “the Sabbatine privilege releases every wearer on the first Saturday,” “this novena enrolls the reader,” and “this is the official, liturgically approved, or indulgenced Carmelite novena.”

This status record is current through 2026-07-14. A later competent decree, revised approved rite, authentic interpretation, or changed discipline would require a fresh source audit rather than silent amendment of the conclusion.
