## Conciliar bibliography context

The rendered bibliography retains *Dei Verbum* 8–10 and 21–26 as broad context for Tradition, the deposit and teaching office, Scripture in the Church, theology, and devotional reading. No distinct claim-level row depends uniquely upon that range, so its central source-library binding is deliberately catalog-only rather than represented as verified claim support.

## Prayer-text checks

- Every source-specific prayer card was factored without verbal revision into a canonical file under `prayers/`; the full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import those same files, and their common formulas remain imported from `shared/common-prayers.tex`. The companion therefore has no independent prayer-text layer to drift.
- The Sign of the Cross, Lord's Prayer, and Lesser Doxology are imported from the collection's canonical shared prayer source. The Sign of the Cross reproduces Lefebvre's facing Latin and English at p. 35. The Lord's Prayer Latin was collated to the 1962 *Missale Romanum*, Good Friday order no. 960, printed p. 180, and the Lesser Doxology Latin to its *Ordo Missae*, printed p. 216; their historical English comes from Lefebvre's 1925 *Daily Missal*, pp. 4–5. The shared file and `prayer-inventory.md` record ownership and consumers.
- The Hail Mary Latin and English were transcribed from the facing columns of Dom Gaspar Lefebvre's 1925 *Daily Missal with Vespers*, p. 5, and directly checked against the page image.
- The former feast prayer `Adiuvet nos, quaesumus, Domine...` was retained only as a cited theological witness after its non-source English rendering was removed. The recited feast collect was replaced by the exact historical Latin and English printed in Dom Gaspar Lefebvre's 1925 *Daily Missal with Vespers*, pp. 1499–1500. The abbreviated `Qui vivis` was expanded only with the same missal's full Latin and English formula on pp. 31–32. Both loci were inspected directly from the page images; the edition bears a 1925 imprimatur and is public domain in the United States.
- The short *Flos Carmeli* reproduces the first two stanzas using `da privilegia`. Its Latin was re-collated against the *Proprium Missarum et Officiorum Ordinis Carmelitarum Discalceatorum* (1959), printed p. (214), and its English was transcribed from Kenelm Digby Best, C.O., *A Priest's Poems* (1902), p. 136. The complete expanded form reproduces all eight Latin and English units exactly from the located Carmelite-source transcription attributed to the 1993 joint Proper. It uses that witness's `esto propitia` and `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum` readings, preserves its wording and punctuation, and ends at `coronaris` / `crowned in glory.` without importing `Amen` or `(Alleluia.)` from the unselected 2007 witness. Two adjacent bilingual cards divide the sequence only for page fit.
- The complete-English search was expanded on 2026-07-15 and is reproducibly bounded in `research/flos-carmeli-translation-search.md`. The full 174-record Internet Archive / Open Library exact-phrase result set, including all 50 records dated no later than 1930 and all 20 undated records, later-stanza signatures, historical page images, major catalogs, and modern Carmelite witnesses were checked. No complete pre-1931 English was located. Three complete modern witnesses were distinguished: the familiar version printed with singular `hic adesse` Latin in the 2007 *Discalced Carmelite Proper Offices*; the separate 1984 *Carmen Laudis* version, whose named compiler is not thereby its verified translator; and the 1993 joint Carmelite Proper version with plural `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum` Latin. The original search located no independent reuse license for any; the requester then confirmed approval to use all three. This revision selects the 1993-attributed version for its strongest institutional provenance and coherent paired text. Sullivan's 1998 article independently corroborates the first six English units, including `his together`; the full Carmelite transcription supplies the exact reproduction witness and the last two units. The translator remains separately unnamed and the printed 1993 book was not directly inspected.
- On 2026-07-14 all nine daily collects were visually collated against the exact printed pages of the 1987 *Collectio* facsimile: formularies 10, 20, 22, 23, 25, 28, 38, 41, and 46. The scan's corrupt hidden OCR was used only for navigation and is not a textual witness. The reproduced bodies follow the printed Latin rather than the OCR; page lineation is not represented.
- The *Collectio* prints `Per Dominum.` for Days 1–8 and `Qui tecum vivit.` for Day 9. Its decree treats the volume as an appendix to the Roman Missal, while Praenotanda 22 says promulgation changes neither the 1975 *Missale Romanum* nor its rubrics. GIRM 54 prescribes the exact corresponding longer endings and the people's `Amen`; those received governing formulas complete the cards. Stress accents are typographic reading aids and do not change the words. The selection does not splice in Elijah, silence, the Scapular, or other day-specific language.
- The current approved English bodies and conclusions in the 2012 *Collection of Masses of the Blessed Virgin Mary*, vol. I, were independently compared with the current iBreviary witness for all nine formularies. ICEL's standing copyright policy requires written permission or a license, which was not obtained. The USCCB's published 2025 successor guidelines, effective 29 November 2026 rather than on this audit's as-of date, likewise require authentication and permission. Every card therefore cites its approved English source without reproducing the prayer and names the Latin source, proper-context approval, devotional reproduction, exact governing conclusion, and unapproved status of the novena.
- No fixed English intention or meditation is silently assigned as vocal prayer. Every recited English prayer has adjacent complete Latin.

## Rejected or bounded leads

| Lead | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Elijah founded an uninterrupted Christian Carmelite order on the biblical mountain. | Not established and excluded. Scripture and the earliest Rule do not make the claim. |
| The cloud in 1 Kings 18 literally predicts Mary and proves the Immaculate Conception. | Rejected as literal or probative exegesis. A qualified later spiritual typology is retained. |
| Elijah's execution of Baal's prophets authorizes Christian religious violence. | Rejected under the canonical and Christological reading. |
| Albert's Rule records the Simon Stock vision or makes the Scapular promise foundational. | False. The Rule predates and does not contain the narrative. |
| Universal liturgical reception of the title proves every origin story historically or supernaturally authentic. | Rejected. Feast, title, sacramental, shrine, and event judgment are distinct objects. |
| John Paul II's 2001 phrase “venerable tradition” is a positive supernatural-fact decree. | Rejected. The address receives Carmelite tradition and expounds the sign; it does not issue such a decree. |
| Anyone who dies wearing brown cloth is saved regardless of faith, repentance, or conduct. | Theologically rejected. No private promise repeals the Gospel or manipulates final perseverance. |
| The Sabbatine bull is authentic, and Mary must release every wearer on the first Saturday after death. | Historically and theologically rejected. The document retains only bounded Catholic confidence in intercession and prayer for the dead. |
| Buying or privately placing on the Scapular enrolls a person in a confraternity or Carmelite order. | Rejected. Blessing, imposition, enrollment, and juridic forms follow competent rites and authority. |
| An image printed on cloth is what constitutes the Scapular's sacramental meaning. | Rejected by current Carmelite catechesis; the reduced habit-sign and ecclesial blessing are controlling. |
| Completion of these nine project days carries an indulgence or guarantees the requested temporal grace. | No such grant or promise is claimed. |
| Simon Stock certainly composed the exact surviving *Flos Carmeli*. | Not established. Traditional attribution and historical uncertainty are both recorded. |
