## Included corpus and architecture

Included are the principal biblical Carmel passages; Elijah's decision, intercession, exhaustion, silence, and corrected zeal; the Rule of Saint Albert and its scriptural architecture; the hermits' Marian patronage; papal confirmation and adaptation of the Rule; migration, mendicant development, reform, and the wider Carmelite family; development of the Marian commemoration and its extension to the Latin Church; Mary as Mother, Patroness, Sister, Virgin, and contemplative disciple; the Brown Scapular as reduced habit and approved sacramental; the Simon Stock tradition and its documentary limit; the salvation-promise and Sabbatine boundaries; enrollment and daily life; a reproducible 7–15 July calendar; nine distinct days; nine received collects selected from the 1987 *Collectio Missarum de Beata Maria Virgine*; a complete common order using received English only where provenance and reuse rights permit; the short and full *Flos Carmeli*; and an annual-use appendix.

The sibling *Daily Prayer Book* is a mechanically derived recitation companion for readers who already know this exposition or use both publications together. This full guide remains the sole substantive owner: both publications import the same canonical prayer fragments, while the companion adds only the vocal sequence, navigation, and status rubrics and inherits this guide's provenance, tradition qualifications, and review limits.

The nine daily movements are developmental: undivided worship; truthful silence; allegiance to Christ; hearing and bearing the Word; maternal and fraternal care; baptismal clothing and the Scapular; purity of heart and speech; communion and the common good; and final perseverance toward Christ the holy mountain. Each day has its own Scripture, meditation, examination, concrete act, intention, and an unadapted received Latin collect beside a source pointer to its approved 2012 English text.

## Material qualifications

1. Mount Carmel is first a real biblical place. Later Carmelite use is ecclesial reception, not proof that Elijah founded a Christian religious order.
2. The small cloud of 1 Kings 18 has received Marian typology. That spiritual reading is not the literal sense and cannot establish a Marian dogma or historical continuity by itself.
3. Elijah's violence belongs to an Old Covenant judgment narrative and does not authorize religious violence. Luke 9 and the Cross govern Christian zeal.
4. Albert's *formula vitae* dates between 1206 and 1214; Honorius III confirmed it in 1226, and Innocent IV adapted it in 1247. These are distinct stages, not one undifferentiated foundation document.
5. Marian patronage and the Order's Marian title are historically earlier than the surviving developed Simon Stock vision account.
6. Benedict XIII extended the 16 July feast to the Latin Church in 1726. The title's liturgical reception does not authenticate every claimed origin event or promise.
7. In the General Roman Calendar 16 July is an optional memorial; its greater rank in Carmelite and some local calendars does not authorize a private booklet to transfer or alter liturgical observance.
8. The fixed 7–15 July count is this guide's transparent private-devotional rule. A missed day is not a broken legal or magical condition.
9. Mary's maternal intercession is real, wholly dependent on Christ, and never an independent intermediary or universal source of sanctifying grace. “Mother,” “Patroness,” and “Sister” name complementary ecclesial relations without erasing her unique grace or creaturehood. This is the current conciliar and DDF boundary, not merely an editorial preference.
10. The Brown Scapular is an approved sacramental and reduced Carmelite habit. Its efficacy is ecclesial and dispositional, not an inherent occult force in cloth.
11. The Simon Stock appearance, conventionally dated 16 July 1251 at Aylesford, has late and disputed documentation. John Paul II's reception of it as venerable tradition is not recast as a modern supernatural-fact decree.
12. No form of the salvation promise can override repentance, judgment, Christ's sacraments, or the gift of final perseverance. Accidental loss of cloth does not overpower divine mercy.
13. The bull traditionally attributed to John XXII and used for the Sabbatine privilege is historically inauthentic. The 1613 permission for bounded preaching is not evidence that the bull was genuine or that a first-Saturday timetable binds God.
14. The approved 1996 rite requires preparation and competent ministry. This booklet does not bless, impose, enroll, or confer juridic Carmelite membership.
15. The *Flos Carmeli* is a received Carmelite text whose medieval core was expanded over time. Simon Stock authorship, textual growth, exact earliest form, and the relationship among variants remain uncertain. The complete form printed here coherently pairs the 1993-attributed English with its own `esto propitia` and `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum` Latin. It is not silently imposed on the distinct `hic adesse / me tibi servulum` variant.
16. The nine daily Latin collects are received liturgical texts from the 1987 *Collectio*, not project compositions. John Paul II approved that collection and ordered its publication, but approval remains proper-context approval: it does not attach to this devotional selection or this unapproved novena. The approved 2012 English is cited, not reproduced.

The received Latin prayer texts are reproduced in focused devotional form. The nine daily collects come from the 1987 *Collectio Missarum de Beata Maria Virgine* and remain excluded third-party liturgical material; their thematic selection, arrangement, source pointers, and surrounding expression fall under the project license. ICEL's standing copyright policy requires written permission or a license to reproduce the approved 2012 English; none was obtained, so those English prayers are not printed. The USCCB's published 2025 successor guidelines, effective 29 November 2026 rather than on this record's as-of date, likewise require authentication and permission. The common Lord's Prayer and Lesser Doxology use Latin from the 1962 *Missale Romanum* and historical English from Dom Gaspar Lefebvre's 1925 *Daily Missal*; the Sign of the Cross uses Lefebvre's facing Latin and English on p. 35. The Hail Mary and feast collect use Lefebvre's historical Latin and English, while the short *Flos Carmeli* uses Kenelm Digby Best's 1902 verse. For the complete *Flos Carmeli*, the expanded search documented in `research/flos-carmeli-translation-search.md` located three modern witnesses; after the requester confirmed approval to use all three, this revision selected the 1993 Proper version for its joint institutional provenance and exact paired `nos ad esse / tecum in saeculum` Latin. Its Carmelite-source transcription is reproduced without verbal emendation on that user-attested basis and remains excluded third-party material. The *Collectio* facsimile and its corrupt hidden OCR remain external evidence and are not incorporated into the repository. No project-authored or project-translated vocal prayer remains.
