# N00 Source and Claim Audit

## Primary claim matrix

| Consequential claim | Governing source and locus | Source class and qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Ascension-to-Pentecost company persevered together in prayer with Mary. | Acts 1:3–15; Luke 24:49–53. | Public Revelation. Acts names the event and company but not a later booklet. |
| The Pentecost novena emerged from prayerful reflection on this event and should, where possible, consist of solemn Vespers. | CDWDS, *Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy* (2001), no. 155. | Authoritative pastoral directory. It calls the exercise a pious exercise and preserves liturgical priority. |
| “First novena” is warranted only as the saving-event archetype, not as the first documented fixed text. | Acts 1–2; Directory 155; Directory 32 for medieval novena forms. | Source-grounded editorial synthesis with an explicit negative boundary. |
| Thursday Ascension yields nine intervening days before Pentecost. | Acts 1:3; Lev 23:15–21; General Norms for the Liturgical Year 22–26; Leo, *Sermon* 75. | Scriptural and liturgical reckoning. The document distinguishes inclusive feast language from exclusive intervening-day count. |
| Ascension may be assigned to the Seventh Sunday of Easter where not observed as a holy day of obligation. | General Norms for the Liturgical Year and Calendar, no. 7 and Easter Time norms; CIC canon 1246 §2 for conference transfer/suppression competence. | Mutable territorial application. Reader must check the competent local calendar. |
| A nine-day private countdown can begin Friday even where Ascension is transferred. | Leo XIII, *Provida Matris* (nine continuous days before Pentecost); Pentecost's fixed relative placement; profile calendar rule. | Editorial devotional solution, not a liturgical transfer norm. It is labeled as such. |
| Medieval popular piety developed tridua, octaves, novenas, and devotional months. | Directory 32. | Broad official historical synthesis, not a critical history of every novena. |
| Leo XIII invited the novena in 1895 and decreed it annually in parish churches in 1897. | *Provida Matris* (5 May 1895); *Divinum illud munus* 12–14, especially 13. | Papal primary texts; historical quantified indulgences not treated as current. |
| The Spirit is divine; Pentecost is Christological, Trinitarian, ecclesial, sanctifying, and missionary. | Constantinople I Creed; Florence, *Laetentur caeli*; Jn 14–16; Acts 2; LG 4; AG 4; CCC 243–248, 683–747; *Dominum et vivificantem*. | Dogma and authoritative doctrine. The devotional synthesis does not create a new procession formula. |
| A renewed invocation does not imply that the Spirit is absent from the baptized or the Church. | Jn 14:16–17; Rom 8; 1 Cor 3:16; 6:19; Acts 4:31; CCC 733–741, 2670–2672. | Doctrinal synthesis distinguishing constitutive Pentecost from continuing grace. |
| Seven gifts, fruits, and charisms are related but distinct. | Isa 11:1–5; 1 Cor 12–14; Gal 5:22–23; ST I–II qq. 68–70; CCC 799–801, 1830–1832. | Catholic doctrine and theology; textual enumeration qualification retained. |
| Mary's Pentecostal presence is real and subordinate within Christ's unique mediation. | Acts 1:14; Luke 1:35; LG 60–62; CCC 965–970. | Scripture and authoritative doctrine. No independent Marian causality is inferred. |
| The Spirit's diverse action can be imaged by one water producing diverse effects. | Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechesis* 16.11–12, 16. | Patristic analogy, not a definition of divine essence. |
| Many tongues signify the catholic scope and unity of the Church. | Acts 2:5–11; Augustine, *Sermon* 267.4 and *Sermon* 269. | Scriptural-patristic interpretation. It does not claim each Christian speaks every language. |
| *Veni Creator* belongs to the ninth-century Carolingian setting; Rabanus authorship is uncertain. | F. J. E. Raby, *History of Christian-Latin Poetry*, 2nd ed.; manuscript/hymnological summaries. | Professional historical synthesis; received attribution reported but not asserted. |
| *Veni Sancte Spiritus* is an early-thirteenth-century sequence of disputed authorship. | John Julian, *Dictionary of Hymnology*; Cantus/DIAMM records; received Roman liturgical text. | Manuscript-informed historical synthesis; Innocent III / Stephen Langton attributions remain open. |
| Complete historical English verse versions of both hymns are available for devotional republication. | Edward Caswall, *Lyra Catholica* (London: James Burns, 1849), 103–104 and 234–236; edition scan, Internet Archive identifier `LyraCatholica1849`. | Primary printed translation witness. Caswall's wording is public domain; its historical and metrical character does not make it a current approved liturgical translation. |
| Current discipline grants a partial indulgence for devout participation in a publicly conducted novena and separately treats solemn public *Veni Creator* on Pentecost. | Fourth *Enchiridion Indulgentiarum*, concessions 22 and 26. | Current universal Latin indulgence text checked 2026-07-13. General norms and exact circumstances still govern. |

## Prayer-text checks

- The full *Veni Creator Spiritus* and *Veni Sancte Spiritus* were checked against the Holy See's *Compendium of the Catechism* appendix.
- Caswall's complete English *Veni Creator* was transcribed and visually checked against *Lyra Catholica* (1849), printed pp. 103–104; his complete English *Veni Sancte Spiritus* was transcribed and visually checked against printed pp. 234–236. The check used the edition scan identified above, not a later altered hymnal text.
- The short invocation `Veni, Sancte Spiritus, reple...` was checked in the fourth *Enchiridion*, concession 26 §2.
- `Emitte Spiritum tuum...` and `Deus, qui corda fidelium...` were checked in the official AAS 46 (1954), p. 105, Roman ritual witness.
- Common received formulas and all original daily collects are separately classified in `prayer-inventory.md`.
- The English hymns are historical poetic translations, not project renderings, critical editions, literal interlinears, or approved current liturgical translations. The governing Latin remains adjacent so Caswall's metrical compression and expansion remain visible.

## Rejected or bounded leads

| Lead | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Jesus explicitly commanded a nine-day “novena” in Acts. | Rejected. He commanded waiting for the promise; Acts gives the event and interval, not the later genre name or text. |
| The apostles recited the same surviving prayer for nine days. | Unverified and excluded. No apostolic prayer manual is claimed. |
| The Pentecost novena is historically the first documented use of every kind of nine-day Christian observance. | Not established. “First” is retained only as ecclesial archetype. |
| Pentecost must be repeated or the Spirit is absent until invoked. | Theologically false. The once-for-all saving event and continuing missions/graces are distinguished. |
| A local transfer of Ascension moves Pentecost or creates a six-day novena by law. | False. The document gives a private devotional reckoning and defers to the local liturgical calendar. |
| Leo XIII's years-and-quarantines indulgence measure remains the current formula. | Rejected as superseded discipline. Current claims use the fourth *Enchiridion*. |
| Private completion of this booklet automatically gains the public-novena indulgence. | Rejected. Concession 22's public participation condition is stated exactly. |
| *Veni Creator* is certainly by Rabanus, or *Veni Sancte Spiritus* certainly by Innocent III or Stephen Langton. | Not established. Authorship remains qualified. |
| A charism proves holiness, office, or freedom from correction. | Rejected by 1 Cor 12–14 and CCC 799–801. |
| A missed day nullifies prayer or requires anxious doubling. | Rejected as superstition; the resumption guidance is pastoral rather than legal. |

## Copyright, attribution, and review

- No modern Bible translation, Missal translation, Office, papal text, or scholarly chapter is reproduced in bulk.
- Ancient Latin prayers are reproduced for recitation; English prayer texts are either public-domain received forms, Caswall's public-domain 1849 hymn translations, or original project translations.
- Papal, conciliar, catechetical, patristic, and scholarly sources are paraphrased with exact loci.
- Internal source, theology, prayer-completeness, calendar, hymn-transcription, and copyright-status checks were completed before the revised build.
- The internal ordering key was removed from reader-facing source, public catalog display, PDF text, and PDF metadata while remaining available in internal audit and ordering records.
- The revised final 25-page build passed a clean-log scan, metadata and font inspection, extracted-text review, and visual inspection of every page, including full-size checks of the revised title and annual-use sheet; the installed PDF was checked for byte identity with the reviewed build and raster identity with the reviewed page images.
- Independent human historical, liturgical, theological, patristic, linguistic, pastoral, and ecclesiastical review remains outstanding.
