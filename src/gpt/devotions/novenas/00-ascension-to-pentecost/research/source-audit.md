## Conciliar bibliography context

The rendered bibliography retains *Dei Verbum* 7–10 as broad context for apostolic transmission, Scripture, Tradition, and the teaching office. No claim-level row in the primary matrix depends uniquely upon that range, so its central source-library binding is deliberately catalog-only rather than represented as a verified claim control.

## Prayer-text checks

- Every source-specific prayer card was factored without verbal revision into a canonical file under `prayers/`; the full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import those same files, and their common formulas remain imported from `shared/common-prayers.tex`. The companion therefore has no independent prayer-text layer to drift.
- The full *Veni Creator Spiritus* and *Veni Sancte Spiritus* were checked against the Holy See's *Compendium of the Catechism* appendix and re-collated on 2026-07-14. That pass restored the omitted conjunction in `teque utriusque Spiritum`, the source's `Per Te` capitalization, and three source punctuation readings across the two hymns.
- Caswall's complete English *Veni Creator* was transcribed and visually checked against *Lyra Catholica* (1849), printed pp. 103–104; his complete English *Veni Sancte Spiritus* was transcribed and visually checked against printed pp. 234–236. The check used the edition scan identified above, not a later altered hymnal text.
- The short invocation `Veni, Sancte Spiritus, reple...` was re-collated with the fourth *Enchiridion*, concession 26 §2, on 2026-07-14; the source has no comma before `et tui amoris`.
- `Emitte Spiritum tuum...` and `Deus, qui corda fidelium...` were checked in the official AAS 46 (1954), p. 105, Roman ritual witness.
- The shared Lord's Prayer follows the complete 1962 Missal formula at Good Friday order no. 960, printed p. 180 (facsimile PDF p. 261), omitting only its rubrical recitation marks. The shared Lesser Doxology follows the 1962 *Ordo Missae*, printed p. 216 (facsimile PDF p. 297), including the colon after `semper`.
- The nine daily collect bodies were visually checked against the page images at the exact loci above in the public 1962 typical-edition facsimile, SHA-256 `648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`. Searchable OCR served only as a locating aid and does not control publication text. The visual check corrected the OCR-derived Day 9 opening `Tuo nos igne` to the facsimile's `Illo nos igne`.
- The publication retains the witness's stress accents and ligature spelling in each received body. Where a source page abbreviates a conclusion, the publication uses the exact full same-Missal formula prescribed by *Rubricae generales* 115: 115(a) for the ordinary formula, 115(a)–(b) for `Per eundem`, 115(c) for the relative formula, and 115(e) for the same-Spirit variant. No collect body is spliced, paraphrased, or combined with another prayer.
- Every prose English prayer and the Sign of the Cross's Latin were visually checked against Lefebvre's 1925 edition, SHA-256 `28c9046c5acfa19297ab9aeaa14ac4f8d8fcffab1307b51215b94fa4ca6172a2`. The governing loci are the bilingual Sign of the Cross, p. 35; Lord's Prayer, p. 4; Lesser Doxology, p. 5; daily invocation and collect, p. 1873; and Days 1–9 at pp. 948, 987, 1310, 977, 895, 980, 180–181, 1827, and 992 respectively.
- The English conclusion joins are exhaustive and mechanical. Days 1–2, 4–5, and 7 use their printed ordinary or same-Spirit abbreviations with the edition's exact `Through our Lord Jesus Christ...` formula and `Amen` at pp. 8–9. Day 3's English abbreviation at p. 1310 reads `Through our Lord`, while the adjacent Latin reads `Per eundem Dominum`; the completed English therefore uses the edition's exact `Through the same Lord Jesus Christ Thy Son...` formula and response at p. 833. Days 6 and 9 use their printed `Who with Thee ... in the unity of the same` wording with the exact relative same-Spirit formula and response at p. 861. Day 8 uses its printed `Who with Thee` wording with the exact relative formula and response at p. 833. The invocation retains the `Collect` label and `Through our Lord Jesus Christ` conclusion from p. 1873 and adds only the edition's exact `Amen` response. No English word in a vocal prayer was introduced by the project.
- The 1925 title matter records Theodorus's abbatial *Imprimatur* of 21 March 1924, William Busch's *Nihil Obstat*, and Archbishop Austin Dowling's *Imprimatur* of 13 May 1925. These approvals govern that historical edition alone; they do not make its English a current approved liturgical translation or approve this novena.
- Common received formulas and the nine received daily collects are separately classified with exact loci, translation ownership, devotional use, and approval limits in `prayer-inventory.md`.
- The English hymns are historical poetic translations, not project renderings, critical editions, literal interlinears, or approved current liturgical translations. The governing Latin remains adjacent so Caswall's metrical compression and expansion remain visible.

## Rejected or bounded leads

| Lead | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Jesus explicitly commanded a nine-day “novena” in Acts. | Rejected. He commanded waiting for the promise; Acts gives the event and interval, not the later genre name or text. |
| The apostles recited the same surviving prayer for nine days. | Unverified and excluded. No apostolic prayer manual is claimed. |
| The Pentecost novena is historically the first documented use of every kind of nine-day Christian observance. | Not established. “First” is retained only as ecclesial archetype. |
| A local transfer of Ascension moves Pentecost or creates a six-day novena by law. | False. The document gives a private devotional reckoning and defers to the local liturgical calendar. |
| The Ascension Day collect `Concede, quaesumus... hodierna die...` is suitable as the fixed Day 1 prayer. | Rejected because its time-indexed “this day” is false on the guide's ordinary Friday-after-Ascension Day 1 and on Days 1–2 before a transferred Ascension. The non-deictic Sunday-after-Ascension collect was selected instead. |
| A collect approved in the 1962 Missal thereby approves this assembled novena or authorizes its present liturgical use. | Rejected. Historical source status remains attached to the original formulary and governing law; devotional reproduction does not transfer approval. |
| Leo XIII's years-and-quarantines indulgence measure remains the current formula. | Rejected as superseded discipline. Current claims use the fourth *Enchiridion*. |
| Private completion of this booklet automatically gains the public-novena indulgence. | Rejected. Concession 22's public participation condition is stated exactly. |
| *Veni Creator* is certainly by Rabanus, or *Veni Sancte Spiritus* certainly by Innocent III or Stephen Langton. | Not established. Authorship remains qualified. |
| A charism proves holiness, office, or freedom from correction. | Rejected by 1 Cor 12–14 and CCC 799–801. |
| A missed day nullifies prayer or requires anxious doubling. | Rejected as superstition; the resumption guidance is pastoral rather than legal. |
