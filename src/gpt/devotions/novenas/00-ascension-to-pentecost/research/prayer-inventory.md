## Inventory controls

- The full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import every non-shared prayer above from the same canonical fragment under `prayers/`; the Sign of the Cross, Lord's Prayer, and Lesser Doxology remain singly owned by the collection's shared prayer source. The companion owns no duplicate prayer wording or translation.
- The shared Lord's Prayer follows the 1962 Missal's complete Good Friday text at no. 960, printed p. 180, omitting only its rubrical recitation marks. The shared Lesser Doxology follows the 1962 *Ordo Missae*, printed p. 216. Neither Latin formula is reconstructed from memory or translated by the project.
- No prayer originating solely in a modern vernacular source is included.
- No proprietary current English Missal or Liturgy of the Hours prayer is reproduced.
- The two ancient hymns are complete rather than selectively excerpted. Their English texts are Caswall's received metrical renderings, not original literal cribs; the publication preserves his full 1849 wording, including poetic contractions, without silently importing a later revision.
- Caswall's versions are public domain in the United States by their 1849 publication date and in the United Kingdom under its ordinary authorial term; Caswall died in 1878. Public-domain status permits republication but does not make them current approved liturgical translations.
- The nine daily Latin bodies were visually collated against the page images of the 1962 Vatican typical-edition facsimile. Searchable OCR was used only to locate candidates and does not govern wording. The checked facsimile file has SHA-256 `648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`.
- Lefebvre's title matter bears an *Imprimatur* from Theodorus, abbot of St. Andrew's Abbey, dated 21 March 1924; a *Nihil Obstat* from William Busch of the St. Paul Seminary; and an *Imprimatur* from Austin Dowling, Archbishop of St. Paul, dated 13 May 1925. These are historical permissions for that edition, not present approval of its English as a liturgical translation or of this novena.
- The historical English was visually checked against the scan at the exact loci in the table. Its checked file has SHA-256 `28c9046c5acfa19297ab9aeaa14ac4f8d8fcffab1307b51215b94fa4ca6172a2`.
- Lefebvre abbreviates the conclusions at the daily loci. No new wording is supplied: Days 1–2, 4–5, and 7 join the exact full `Through our Lord Jesus Christ...` formula and `Amen` at pp. 8–9 to their printed ordinary or same-Spirit abbreviations. Day 3's English at p. 1310 prints `Through our Lord` although the adjacent Latin says `Per eundem Dominum`; its completion therefore follows the exact same-Lord formula and response at p. 833. Days 6 and 9 join the printed relative same-Spirit ending to the exact full wording and response at p. 861; Day 8 joins its printed relative ending to the exact full wording and response at p. 833. The daily invocation retains the `Collect` label and conclusion printed at p. 1873 and supplies only the exact `Amen` response witnessed at pp. 8–9. These are mechanical expansions of explicit abbreviations within one witness, not translations or compositions.
- Each Latin daily fragment expands its printed abbreviation only with the exact full formula prescribed in the same 1962 Missal, *Rubricae generales* 115: ordinary, 115(a); same-Lord, 115(a)–(b); relative, 115(c); and same-Spirit, 115(e). This mechanical same-witness expansion does not otherwise adapt or combine the received Latin body.
- The Missal's historical approval governs each collect only in its identified liturgical setting under the law then applicable. Reproducing and arranging those collects here does not approve this project-authored novena, establish a present liturgical use, or displace the competent current books and authority.
- The received Latin liturgical wording and received historical English prayer wording are excluded from the project's CC BY 4.0 grant. The 1925 edition is public domain in the United States. Its title page nevertheless says “All rights reserved,” and Lefebvre died in 1966; the status of authorial matter may therefore remain uncertain outside the United States in ordinary life-plus-70 jurisdictions through the end of 2036. No current proprietary English Missal translation is reproduced.
- The spontaneous statement of the reader's intention is an instruction, not a printed fixed prayer; no English-only vocal formula is assigned.
- Before any ecclesiastical submission, public parish adoption, or claim of textual maturity, the Latin and historical-English transcriptions and every joined conclusion require independent line-by-line review.
