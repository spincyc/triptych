## Inventory controls

- The full guide and its *Daily Prayer Book* import every non-shared prayer above from the same canonical fragment under `prayers/`; the Sign of the Cross, Lord's Prayer, and Lesser Doxology remain singly owned by the collection's shared prayer source. The companion owns no duplicate prayer wording or translation.
- No prayer originating solely in a modern vernacular source is included.
- No proprietary current English Missal or Liturgy of the Hours prayer is reproduced.
- The two ancient hymns are complete rather than selectively excerpted. Their English texts are Caswall's received metrical renderings, not project translations or literal cribs; the publication preserves his full 1849 wording, including poetic contractions, without silently importing a later revision.
- Caswall's versions are public domain in the United States by their 1849 publication date and in the United Kingdom under its ordinary authorial term; Caswall died in 1878. Public-domain status permits republication but does not make them current approved liturgical translations.
- The nine daily Latin bodies were visually collated against the page images of the 1962 Vatican typical-edition facsimile. Searchable OCR was used only to locate candidates and does not govern wording. The checked facsimile file has SHA-256 `648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`.
- Each daily fragment supplies the collect's conventional full conclusion so that the private recitation is complete. This disclosed expansion does not otherwise adapt or combine the received Latin body.
- The Missal's historical approval governs each collect only in its identified liturgical setting under the law then applicable. Reproducing and arranging those collects here does not approve this project-authored novena, establish a present liturgical use, or displace the competent current books and authority.
- The received Latin liturgical wording is third-party material excluded from the project's CC BY 4.0 grant. The adjacent English renderings are project translations covered by that grant; no current proprietary English Missal translation is reproduced.
- The spontaneous statement of the reader's intention is an instruction, not a printed fixed prayer; no English-only vocal formula is assigned.
- Before any ecclesiastical submission, public parish adoption, or claim of textual or translational maturity, the Latin collation and all nine project translations require independent line-by-line review.
