# Interpretive review: from equal dossiers to natural movements

## Review question

Does the exposition interpret each part of the 1962 Roman Order according to that part's own literary, ritual, sacramental, and historical character, or does an editorial template determine the result in advance?

The review was conducted on 2026-07-15 against the 51-page installed edition, its TeX sources, the 1962 Missal facsimile, the sequence inventory, and the claim matrix. It included a separate movement-by-movement literary and mystagogical audit, a source audit, and a build/layout audit.

## Baseline evidence

The superseded source contained 29,191 words in its section files. It used `\massunit` exactly thirty-three times and placed one `\sourcedossier` after every unit. Each dossier printed the same four labels---received boundary, biblical context, historical witness, and patristic/Thomistic/structural reading---for 132 visible field headings. The body after each dossier ordinarily returned to the same evidence under another recurring sequence headed by literal action, Scripture or history, doctrine or symbolism, and relation to adjacent units. Dozens of explicit numerical cross-references reinforced the editorial chain.

The result was not poorly researched. It was over-controlled by its research apparatus. Evidence gathered to test claims became the publication's rhetoric, and then much of it was stated twice. Equal typography and similar word budgets suggested that a variable proper slot, a short transition, the twofold consecratory center, and four historically layered endings were interpretively equivalent objects.

## Findings

1. **The number governed despite its disclaimer.** The text called the thirty-three divisions editorial and unequal, but titles, transitions, dossiers, and the structural diagram made the count the reader's primary map. McMahon's devotional antecedent had become a constraint rather than a provenance note.
2. **Unlike genres received the same method.** Psalms, hymn, creed, oration, dialogue, procession, gesture, proper slot, consecratory prayer, Communion, and dismissal all passed through the same questions in the same order. This obscured the feature most in need of interpretation: what kind of act each one is.
3. **Some boundaries were unnatural.** Former units 4, 10, 14, 23, 28, and 30--33 bundled distinct acts. Former units 25 and 26 split the two signs at the center of one sustained Canon. The bundle “dismissal, Placeat, blessing, and Last Gospel” hid four endings from different strata.
4. **History often displaced exegesis.** A first secure witness or terminus protects against legend, but a date does not by itself disclose how a psalm speaks, how a dialogue enacts assent, or how incense orders bodies in space.
5. **Backward-forward transitions became formulaic.** Nearly every unit was made to “prepare” the next. This produced a false single-file performance. Sung and Solemn Mass have concurrent tracks: Introit and altar prayers, schola and celebrant readings, Sanctus and quiet Canon, Communion chant and distribution.
6. **Proper slots were over-generalized.** Introit, Collect, lesson, interlection chants, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Communion, and Postcommunion cannot receive one content apart from the appointed proper. The exposition must explain the position and performance while leaving textual exegesis to the identified proper.
7. **Defensive qualifications accumulated.** Many cautions were sound, but repeated “not magic,” “not another consecration,” “not equal ministries,” and similar negations gave error prevention more space than contemplation of the received act.

## Strengths retained

- Exact 1962 edition and language boundaries, including stable, proper, conditional, seasonal, Requiem, and ministerial distinctions.
- Direct control by the typical-edition facsimile rather than OCR or a hand-missal transcription.
- Claim-level evidence, calibrated dates, rejection of unsupported papal authorship legends, and negative controls from the 1474 Missal.
- Distinction among received rite, biblical and doctrinal reality, historical stratum, received spiritual interpretation, and editorial synthesis.
- No project-authored English prayer wording or close translation.
- Doctrinal precision concerning sacrifice, real presence, concomitance, ordered priestly participation, and worthy reception.
- The Leonine appendix's distinction among the Missal, external law, encouragement, exception, intention, and suppression.

## Revised interpretive method

The four evidentiary questions remain mandatory in the research record, but they no longer appear as four visible fields. The received movement determines the published method and proportion. Its numbered heading uses the received incipit or standard ritual name; the editorial thesis is a subordinate heading, so interpretation illuminates rather than renames the part of Mass.

| Kind of material | Governing interpretive work |
| --- | --- |
| Psalm or biblical canticle | Speaker, conflict, literary arc, canonical setting, antiphonal framing, and the effect of ritual relocation |
| Hymn or creed | Rhetorical turns, accumulation, address, doctrinal sequence, bodily performance, and conditional omission |
| Oration | Addressee, syntax, petition, conclusion, presidential voice, audibility, and the appointed proper content |
| Dialogue or acclamation | Distinct speakers, summons, response, assent, repetition, sound, and ministerial relation |
| Procession, object, or gesture | Agent, direction, space, touch, matter, sequence, omission, and differentiated ministry |
| Proper slot | Liturgical function and performance, with no invented universal exegesis; the appointed text is interpreted in its own guide |
| Canon and Communion | The grammar of a sustained prayer, Christ's efficacious words, anamnesis, offering, intercession, sacramental sign, reception, and fruit |
| Composite ending | Historical stratigraphy and received theological rereading without false lexical or compositional claims |

History enters where it changes the reading: the ancient mixed cup and reassigned Christmas collect, the public gift procession under later private offertory prayers, the delayed ritual fraction after the narrated breaking, or four endings accumulated across different periods. Patristic and Thomistic texts are used where they actually illuminate the material, not as mandatory decorations.

## Movement-specific agenda

- Read Psalm 42 as the dramatic voice of exile moving through judgment, light, truth, altar, and hope; read the Confiteor through changed pronouns, reciprocal but asymmetric speech, witnesses, breast-striking, and deprecatory responses.
- Distinguish ascent, altar kiss, relics, and first incense; let omission after another public approach disclose ritual function.
- Read Kyrie as alternating Christological acclamation and intensified petition; Gloria as a rhetorical journey from angelic song through praise of the Father, address to the Lamb, supplication, and Trinitarian close.
- Distinguish greeting, invitation, and Collect; distinguish lesson, Gradual, Alleluia, Tract, and Sequence; make Gospel preparation a question of cleansing and commissioned proclamation; let the Creed's corporate singular and bodily genuflection traverse the economy confessed.
- Expose the Offertory's historical palimpsest; interpret the mixed chalice through Incarnation, deification, and ecclesial participation; let bow, raised hands, incense route, washing, bodily turn, pronouns, and sound govern the later offertory movements.
- Read the Preface dialogue as performative assent and the proper Preface as the day's reason for thanksgiving. Treat the Canon as one sustained prayer with internal movements: concrete communion and names; the household's offering under divine action; the twofold consecratory center; memory becoming offering; accepted biblical types and heavenly altar; dead, sinners, creation, and doxology.
- Let filial boldness govern the Lord's Prayer; distinguish fraction, commingling, Lamb, and peace; distinguish private preparation, priestly Communion, faithful Communion, purification, proper chant, and Postcommunion.
- Interpret dismissal, Placeat, blessing, and Johannine Prologue as four received endings. Mission is a theological synthesis of the whole, not a false derivation of *missa* or proof of one compositional plan.
