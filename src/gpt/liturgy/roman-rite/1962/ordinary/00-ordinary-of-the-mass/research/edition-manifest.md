# Edition manifest: The Ordinary and Order of the Mass

## Object and boundary

- **Rite and use:** Roman Rite, Order of Mass in the 1962 edition of the Roman Missal.
- **Primary edition:** *Missale Romanum ex decreto SS. Concilii Tridentini restitutum Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum*, editio typica, 1962, promulgated under Saint John XXIII.
- **Primary components used:** `Ritus servandus in celebratione Missae`; `Ordo Missae`; `Canon Missae`; the General Rubrics where they govern omission or substitution; and the Missal's notated ordinary where needed to identify audible or sung forms.
- **Public facsimile:** Church Music Association of America scan, <https://media.churchmusicassociation.org/pdf/missale62.pdf>, accessed 2026-07-12. The principal printed loci are `Ritus servandus`, pp. LII--LXIV, and `Ordo Missae` / `Canon Missae`, pp. 216--328.
- **Language:** the received liturgical text is Latin. The publication quotes only short incipits and supplies no project-authored English prayer wording or translation. Its English prose is third-person analysis; any future English liturgical wording requires an exact, identified historical or approved human witness and a recorded rights status.
- **Adjacent post-Mass law:** the Leonine-prayer appendix is controlled by the 1884, 1887, and 1960 Sacred Congregation of Rites acts; the Sacred Congregation of Indulgences and Sacred Relics acts of 1904 under Pius X; Pius XI's 1930 direction; and *Inter Oecumenici* (1964, effective 1965). These acts explain historical practice without importing the prayers into the Missal's *Ordo Missae*.
- **Territory:** the Latin typical edition rather than a territorial hand-missal translation.
- **Celebration forms:** the inventory follows the full Missal order and marks differences among Low, sung, and Solemn Mass where they materially affect speaker, audibility, procession, incense, or ministry. It is not a ceremonial manual.

The work treats the complete stable framework of Mass, not only the five sung Ordinary chants. It includes variable proper *slots* only where the fixed Order assigns their function; it does not import any day's proper wording. Conditional omissions, Requiem variants, seasonal forms, the homily, and Communion of the faithful are distinguished from invariant text.

## The thirty-three-unit segmentation and its antecedent

The 1962 Missal does not promulgate a doctrinally privileged list entitled “the thirty-three parts.” A documented catechetical antecedent is Msgr John T. McMahon's *Pray the Mass*, Part I, 13th revised edition (Sydney: Pellegrini, 1959; imprimatur, Archbishop Raymond Prendiville, June 1959). McMahon divides the Mass into thirty-three “steps,” explicitly associating them with the traditionally reckoned years of Christ's earthly life. His list is devotional and uneven in scale: it counts such practical actions as changing the Missal while gathering several distinct postcommunion actions under one step.

The present number is therefore a received catechetical prompt but the segmentation is an editorial reconstruction made for this collection. It preserves the useful Christological invitation while replacing McMahon's list with thirty-three comprehensive units drawn at textual, ritual, and theological joints in the 1962 order. The sequence inventory maps every unit back to the Missal, includes the complete Canon and Communion sequence, and marks proper slots and conditional elements. No historical or theological conclusion depends on the numerical correspondence.

Catalog identifier `O00` (“Ordinary 00”) places this stable framework before the numbered temporal propers for reader navigation. It does not convert the Ordinary into a proper or add a fictitious Sunday to the temporal cycle.

## Exclusions and adjacent material

- The Asperges and other pre-Mass rites and local devotional prayers remain outside the exposition. The Leonine prayers receive a final unnumbered appendix because they were juridically prescribed after Masses without chant and remained operative during 1962; they remain outside the Mass, outside the typical Missal's *Ordo Missae*, and outside the thirty-three units. The appendix is not unit 34.
- The sermon is discussed as a historically important and rubrically adjacent act after the Gospel, but it is not counted as a fixed text of the 1962 `Ordo Missae`.
- The proper of the day is represented only by its structural positions: Introit, Collect, Epistle, interlection chants, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Communion, and Postcommunion.
- Current canonical permissions for using the 1962 Missal are mutable and distinct from this textual-historical exposition. The work is not a permissions guide.
- Comparative claims about postconciliar editions are included only when needed to prevent anachronism or identify a later change; they do not create a synoptic comparative study.

## Source-control notes

The facsimile is the textual baseline. Searchable OCR and hand-missal transcriptions may locate material but do not control a Latin quotation or rubric. Short patristic translations are checked at their named loci and paraphrased unless exact wording is necessary. Later historical scholarship is used to date strata and to preserve uncertainty; a medieval symbolic interpretation is never presented as the original compositional intention of an earlier text merely because it became influential.

Every numbered unit now displays a source dossier identifying its received Latin boundary or variable proper status, a third-person account of ritual function where useful, Scripture in literary and canonical context, a calibrated historical witness, and its patristic, Thomistic, and structural relation. The functional prose neither closely translates the received clauses nor supplies recitation wording. *research/unit-source-matrix.md* records the audit level behind those dossiers. “Secure,” “probable,” “traditional,” and “unresolved” describe different evidentiary states and are not silently collapsed into one narrative certainty.

The McMahon source is used to document the modern thirty-three-step catechetical antecedent, not to establish rubrics or historical origins.
