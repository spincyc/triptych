## Medieval reception and modern historical synthesis

Amalarius, Innocent III, and Durandus control claims about influential medieval spiritual reception, not the first historical cause of every ceremony. Exact textual controls include Martène's printed ordines and *Missa Illyrica* loci; Silva-Tarouca's *Capitulare ecclesiastici ordinis*; Bernold's *Micrologus* in PL 151; Warner's edition of the Stowe Missal; Lippe's 1474 Missal; Botte's critical apparatus for the Roman Canon; and the conciliar collections and manuscript editions named in the unit matrix. Josef Jungmann, Eric Palazzo, John Baldovin, and Uwe Michael Lang are identified secondary syntheses for sacramentaries, stational worship, Frankish-Roman interaction, full Missals, and codification. Jungmann's printed volume and page range is recorded for each historical claim rather than cited as an undifferentiated authority. Where dating remains disputed, the publication uses ranges or calibrated language rather than a single scholar's reconstruction as fact.

## Thirty-three-step claim audit

John T. McMahon, *Pray the Mass*, Part I, 13th revised ed. (Pellegrini, 1959), is the documented catechetical antecedent. The title/imprimatur and the express thirty-three-step/year-of-Christ explanation were checked in the public scan at <https://www.ccwatershed.org/pdfs/pray-mass-booklet/download/>; publication context was checked at <https://www.ccwatershed.org/2014/09/13/pray-mass-booklet-and-cards/>.

The publication does **not** claim that McMahon's list is official, ancient, universal, or coextensive with the 1962 Missal. Its own thirty-three units are an editorial reconstruction recorded in `sequence-inventory.md`.

## Unit-level text, biblical-context, and chronology audit

The claim-level record is *unit-source-matrix.md*. It distinguishes literal quotation, canonical synthesis, typology, allusion, and analogy; records a first secure witness or safe terminus; marks secure, probable, traditional, and unresolved claims; and gives an exact checked locus for every unit's principal chronological claim. The publication's visible dossier for each unit was checked against that matrix. A generic reference to “1474/1570” is not accepted as proof: the 1474 pages were checked directly, and four absences materially control the account---no opening sign or complete received step complex at p. 198, no exact doubled *Confiteor* there, no *Per evangelica dicta* after the principal Gospel at p. 199, and no Last Gospel at pp. 210--212.

The expanded scriptural audit uses complete literary settings rather than isolated verbal parallels. Examples include Psalm 42 as an exile-and-vindication lament; Isaiah 6 as judgment, purification, and mission; Luke 2 as a Messianic announcement answered in praise; Nehemiah 8 as covenant renewal; Revelation 19 as judgment, reign, and nuptial Alleluia; Daniel 3 as exilic prayer without temple sacrifice; Matthew 6 as hidden filial prayer joined to forgiveness; 1 Corinthians 10–11 as communion judged by ecclesial unity; and John 1 as the complete creation-Incarnation Prologue. These contexts govern the publication's classifications and prevent a later Roman gesture from being presented as though a biblical verse directly prescribed it.

Additional dated controls used in the unit dossiers include Nicaea (325; DS 125) and Chalcedon (451; ACO II.1.2, p. 128) for the Creed, with the surviving 381 acts' textual limitation expressly preserved; Third Toledo canon 2 (589; Mansi IX, 993) for Western Eucharistic use before the Lord's Prayer; Berno's account of Roman adoption in 1014 (PL 142:1060--1061); the *Praecepta synodalia* under Odo of Sully, around 1200, canon 28 (Pontal I, p. 82), as an early catalyst for Host elevation rather than its universal invention; and the Sacred Congregation of Rites decree *Novis hisce temporibus* (13 November 1962; AAS 54 [1962], 873), effective 8 December 1962, for Saint Joseph in *Communicantes*.

For medieval private prayers, the audit names the earliest checked ancestor, distinguishes partial from exact form and different from received placement, and then gives a printed terminus where one has actually been checked. It does not assign an author where the manuscript record establishes only gradual Roman, Gallican, or Frankish reception. In particular, the priest's triple *Domine, non sum dignus* is not backdated to the 1474 Missal's single recitation, and the faithful's *Ecce Agnus* complex is kept distinct from the priest's medieval preparation. The traditional Celestine I Introit notice, Gregory I attribution for part of *Hanc igitur*, and Sergius I Agnus notice are preserved as retrospective evidence rather than treated as contemporary autograph decrees.

The appendix paraphrases the 1962-era form and quotes only short Latin incipits. It distinguishes the short 1886 Saint Michael prayer from the much longer 1890 exorcism formula and rejects the alleged post-Mass vision or dialogue attributed to Leo XIII as undocumented legend: no contemporary primary witness was found, popular dates conflict, and the story cannot carry a historical claim. The base law's phrase “Mass without chant” is retained instead of silently converting it into a claim about every possible Low Mass; the controlling 1960 exceptions are stated from AAS 52 (1960), 360.

## Known limits and review state

- This is not a diplomatic edition or exhaustive manuscript stemma.
- Selective Missal quotations preserve the collated words, capitalization, and punctuation, with omissions marked by ellipses. As an explicit house-style normalization, they expand printed ligatures to `ae`/`oe`, omit stress accents, and use `V.`/`R.` for the printed versicle and response signs.
- English explanations of Latin texts are original study paraphrases, not an approved liturgical translation.
- Exact historical dating of some Canon clauses, preparatory prayers, and cross-regional influence remains probabilistic.
- The central Roman locus for the 1886 Leonine revision remains outstanding; the authoritative 1962-horizon exception corpus is controlled by the 1960 decree.
- Medieval mystical readings are labeled as reception rather than silently converted into compositional history.
- Present canonical authorization for celebrations using the 1962 Missal is outside scope.
- On 2026-07-14 the corrected 51-page publication passed a settled two-pass build, clean-log, metadata, font, extraction, PDF-structure, and every-page visual checks. A local keep-with-next guard repaired the split section heading found during that review. The installed PDF is byte-identical to the reviewed build, SHA-256 `c924db267fca7723742283cea594a39a5fe8613d87067abfff7abf77826a1704`.
- Independent review by a liturgical historian, patristics specialist, and sacramental theologian remains outstanding.
