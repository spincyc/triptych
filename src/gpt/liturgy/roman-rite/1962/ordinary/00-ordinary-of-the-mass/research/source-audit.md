The work conventionally called the *Apostolic Tradition* is treated as a redactionally complex witness. No claim rests on an unqualified attribution to Hippolytus or on treating its reconstructed prayer as the Roman Canon.

## Medieval reception and modern historical synthesis

Amalarius, Innocent III, and Durandus control claims about influential medieval spiritual reception, not the first historical cause of every ceremony. Josef Jungmann, Eric Palazzo, John Baldovin, and Uwe Michael Lang are used as identified secondary syntheses for sacramentaries, stational worship, Frankish-Roman interaction, full Missals, and codification. Where dating remains disputed, the publication uses ranges or calibrated language rather than a single scholar's reconstruction as fact.

## Thirty-three-step claim audit

John T. McMahon, *Pray the Mass*, Part I, 13th revised ed. (Pellegrini, 1959), is the documented catechetical antecedent. The title/imprimatur and the express thirty-three-step/year-of-Christ explanation were checked in the public scan at <https://www.ccwatershed.org/pdfs/pray-mass-booklet/download/>; publication context was checked at <https://www.ccwatershed.org/2014/09/13/pray-mass-booklet-and-cards/>.

The publication does **not** claim that McMahon's list is official, ancient, universal, or coextensive with the 1962 Missal. Its own thirty-three units are an editorial reconstruction recorded in `sequence-inventory.md`.

## Known limits and review state

- This is not a diplomatic edition or exhaustive manuscript stemma.
- English explanations of Latin texts are original study paraphrases, not an approved liturgical translation.
- Exact historical dating of some Canon clauses, preparatory prayers, and cross-regional influence remains probabilistic.
- Medieval mystical readings are labeled as reception rather than silently converted into compositional history.
- Present canonical authorization for celebrations using the 1962 Missal is outside scope.
- Independent review by a liturgical historian, patristics specialist, and sacramental theologian remains outstanding.
