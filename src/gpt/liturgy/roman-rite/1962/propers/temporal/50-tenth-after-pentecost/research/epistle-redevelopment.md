# Epistle redevelopment record — 1 Corinthians 12:2–11

## Integration boundary

`sections/20-epistle-redevelopment.tex` is an integration-ready replacement
for the detailed Epistle block in `main.tex`, beginning with
`\sectionguard` immediately before `Charisms ordered to common benefit` and
ending after that block's `fourstable` table. It is intentionally not imported
while the Sunday-wide integrator serializes changes to the monolithic source.

The shorter Epistle paragraph in the two-page brief, under `One giver makes
difference useful rather than competitive`, should later be conformed to the
same Greek and canonical controls without reproducing the full treatment.

## Textual and canonical controls

- Appointed text: the visually checked 1962 Missal, printed pages 389–390,
  PDF pages 470–471, with complete English controlled by the registered
  Douay–Rheims/Challoner verse rows.
- Immediate heading: 1 Corinthians 12:1, `τῶν πνευματικῶν`, retained as a
  deliberately broad contextual phrase rather than silently equated with the
  modern technical use of “charisms.”
- Verse 2: `ἀπαγόμενοι` supports “being led” or “led away”; “subjection” was
  rejected as an overstatement.
- Verse 3: `ἀνάθεμα Ἰησοῦς` / `Κύριος Ἰησοῦς` supplies a necessary
  Christological criterion, not a sufficient standalone authentication
  protocol.
- Verses 4–6: `διαιρέσεις` governs the differentiated nouns `χαρισμάτων`,
  `διακονιῶν`, and `ἐνεργημάτων`; the repeated “same” clauses relate them to
  Spirit, Lord, and God.
- Verse 7: `πρὸς τὸ συμφέρον` gives the social direction, “toward benefit” or
  “the common good.”
- Verse 11: `ἰδίᾳ ἑκάστῳ καθὼς βούλεται` preserves individual distribution
  and the Spirit's agency and freedom.
- Canonical controls: 1 Corinthians 8:1–13 and 10:14–22 for the idol horizon;
  12:12–27 for the one body; 12:28–31 for differentiated divine appointments
  and the denial that all have every gift; chapter 13 for love; and chapter 14
  for intelligibility, testing, edification, and order.

## Reception controls and exact functions

- John Chrysostom, *Homily 29 on First Corinthians*: §§1–3 for pagan
  leading, manifestations, and confession; §4 for gift rather than debt, the
  same source, and common profit; §6 for the Spirit's distribution “as he
  wills.” His historical claims about baptismal manifestations and later
  obscurity are reception, not a universal cessation decree.
- Ambrosiaster, *Commentary on First Corinthians* 12:1–11, PL 17,
  cols. 150–152: grace excludes boasting; triadic unity and differentiated
  gifts serve ecclesial usefulness. No claim narrower than the checked
  chapter-and-column locus should be made without a new passage record.
- Thomas Aquinas, *Super I ad Corinthios* XII: use the chapter-level locus
  until the Corpus Thomisticum material for chapter 12 is installed and
  lecture numbering is independently checked. The currently registered
  edition contains only a passage record for 1 Corinthians 6:9–11 and does
  not yet support a verified chapter-12 binding.
- Basil, *De Spiritu Sancto* 16.37: doctrinal reuse of 1 Corinthians 12:4–6,
  11 concerning inseparable divine operation and the Spirit's agency; do not
  label it a running exegetical commentary.
- Vatican II, *Lumen gentium* 12 and *Apostolicam actuositatem* 3: later
  ecclesial reception joining diverse charisms, building, usefulness, and
  pastoral discernment.
- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* §§799–801, 951, 2003: later doctrinal
  synthesis on charisms, common good, gratitude, and discernment. These exact
  loci require source-library passage records before they are added to the
  shared binding file.
- CDF, *Iuvenescit Ecclesia* (2016), especially §§4–8, 10, 17–18, 22–24, is
  a valuable further reception witness on hierarchical and charismatic gifts.
  It was not added to the publication prose because no local source-library
  record presently controls it.

use belongs in the research scope and bibliography with explicit evidence
limits.

## Rejected inferences

- Former pagan identity does not make Gentile Christians lesser members.
- Confessing the correct formula does not independently validate a claimed
  manifestation.
- The triadic clauses do not divide divine action into three unrelated
  economies.
- Charism is not automatically identical with office, natural talent, or an
  extraordinary phenomenon.
- Paul does not promise every listed gift to every person.
- The Spirit's freedom does not abolish testing, stewardship, ecclesial
  judgment, intelligibility, love, or liturgical order.
- Chrysostom's historical explanation is not a magisterial settlement of
  universal cessation.
