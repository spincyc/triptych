# Introit Redevelopment Audit

## Evidence boundary

This record governs only the Introit of the Tenth Sunday after Pentecost in
the 1962 *Missale Romanum*. The exact Missal facsimile and the registered
Douay–Rheims/Challoner Psalms artifact control the appointed Latin identity
and historical English respectively. OCR remains a locating witness. The
Augustine check below is direct reception of the psalm; it is not evidence of
the Roman compiler's intention or of the psalm's original historical setting.

## Exact appointed text and phrase map

The controlling Missal page prints:

- marginal citation: `Ps. 54, 17, 18, 20 et 23`;
- antiphon: `Cum clamarem ... ab his qui appropinquant mihi ... iacta
  cogitatum tuum in Domino, et ipse te enutriet`;
- psalm verse: `Ps. ibid., 2 Exaudi, Deus, orationem meam ...`;
- doxology cue: `V. Gloria Patri`.

The printed citation is part of the verified Missal text and is not corrected
editorially. A separate verbal-source inventory discloses that `ab his qui
appropinquant mihi` is the end of Vulgate-numbered Psalm 54:19. The registered
Challoner witness likewise places “from them that draw near to me” in verse
19. Consequently:

| Liturgical wording | Verbal source |
| --- | --- |
| `Cum clamarem ... exaudivit vocem meam` | Ps 54:17–18 |
| `ab his qui appropinquant mihi` | Ps 54:19 |
| `et humiliavit eos qui est ante saecula` | Ps 54:20 |
| `iacta cogitatum ... enutriet` | Ps 54:23 |
| `Exaudi, Deus, orationem meam ...` | Ps 54:2 |

The English rendering must include the verse-19 clause corresponding to the
Latin without importing the unappointed beginning of that verse. The binding
for the registered Psalms artifact should therefore add `Ps.54.19` while
retaining the Missal's printed citation in the reader-facing heading.

## Canonical context

The complete psalm places the selected confidence within:

1. petition, enemy pressure, fear, and death-darkness (vv. 2–6);
2. desired dove-flight, wilderness, and waiting for rescue (vv. 7–9);
3. divided tongues, violence, and deceit in the city (vv. 10–12);
4. betrayal by an intimate worship companion (vv. 13–15);
5. imprecation (v. 16);
6. cry, prayer at the three hours, redemption amid those drawing near,
   divine hearing and judgment, and deceptive opponents (vv. 17–22);
7. care cast on the sustaining Lord, followed by judgment and final trust
   (vv. 23–24).

The received heading is Davidic but names no episode. Author, first audience,
place, date, city, and betrayer remain unrecoverable from the appointed text.
Later identifications belong to named reception witnesses, not to the literal
historical dossier.

## Augustine locus correction

Direct inspection of Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 54* (English
*Exposition on Psalm 55*) establishes:

- §4 treats the opening petition used as the psalm verse;
- §16 treats the cry to the Lord;
- §17 treats evening, morning, and noon Christologically through Passion,
  Resurrection, and Ascension;
- §18 treats redemption in peace from those who draw near;
- §19 treats divine hearing and the Eternal's humbling judgment;
- §23 directly treats casting care upon the Lord and divine nourishment; and
- §24 begins the following verse concerning descent into the pit.

The former shorthand “§§17–20, 24” was therefore misleading for the appointed
phrases. The direct appointed loci are §§4, 16–19, and 23; §24 may be named
only as adjacent context. Augustine's Donatist and ecclesial applications are
reception and must not be projected backward as the psalm's original horizon
or forward as labels for contemporary opponents.

## Held claims and source gaps

The existing work names Theodoret (PG 80, cols. 1277–1284), Cassiodorus
(unexplained local verse numbers), and Bellarmine (scan pp. 174–178). The
publication currently has no canonical source binding for any of them, and
the broad locators do not yet demonstrate a distinct, phrase-level
interpretive contribution. They should not control the redeveloped Introit
until the exact editions and lemmas are directly checked and bound.

The existing statement that Aquinas's Psalms commentary “ends at Ps 54:16”
also needs an edition-specific terminal-lemma check. Until that is complete,
the safe claim is narrower: no direct Aquinas witness for the appointed later
verses has been established in the currently checked material.

Within the presently bound repository source set, no witness comments on this
Roman Introit specifically as a liturgical cento. That is a bounded
source-set statement, not a universal negative.

## Serialized integration requirements

The whole-work integrator should:

1. import `sections/10-introit.tex` in place of the existing Introit half of
   “The heard cry and merciful omnipotence,” retaining the Collect in its own
   treatment;
2. add `Ps.54.19` to the registered Psalms artifact binding;
3. insert “from them that draw near to me” between the verse-18 and verse-20
   clauses in the appointed English, without importing the unappointed first
   half of verse 19;
4. distinguish the Missal's printed citation from the verbal-source inventory
   in `propers/verified.md`, the historical dossier, and the passage matrix;
5. replace the Augustine shorthand with §§4, 16–19, 23, naming §24 only if its
   adjacent context is actually discussed; and
6. either verify and bind Theodoret, Cassiodorus, Bellarmine, and the Aquinas
   endpoint or remove claims that depend upon them.
