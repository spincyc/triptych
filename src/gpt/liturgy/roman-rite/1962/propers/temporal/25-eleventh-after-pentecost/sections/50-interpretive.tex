\section*{The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities}

\noindent\emph{The following proposals are exploratory editorial synthesis,
not historical intent recovered from the formulary and not teaching
attributed to the cited witnesses. Each states its nearest checked precedent
or analogue and its controlling limit.}

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{The ear governs the tongue.\enspace\properrefs{Ep., All., Gosp.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} Paul hands on what he first
received, while the Corinthians receive, stand, and hold fast. Psalm~80 calls
Israel to hearing only in the wider psalm context, beyond the appointed Alleluia
verses. Jesus
opens ears before the tongue speaks rightly and immediately qualifies public
speech by commanding silence. This conjunction presents Christian confession
as received obedience rather than self-expression. Anthony §§13--14,
Augustine's \emph{De consensu} IV.4.5, and Honorius IV.68 approach the
relation without combining all three anchors. The proposal concerns the order
of faith and speech; physical deafness never signifies culpable unbelief.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Feared silence is answered by a groan and one word.\enspace\properrefs{Grad., Gosp.}}
\emph{Classification: not located in the checked corpus.} The Gradual fears
that God will be silent and depart; Christ looks to heaven, groans, and says
one effective \latin{Ephphetha}. The juxtaposition suggests that divine
answer is measured by presence and efficacy rather than verbal length, a
pastoral fruit for prayer that experiences silence. Direct commentators treat
each gesture or psalm separately, but the targeted corpus search found no
precedent joining them. The Mass does not promise an audible answer or
identify every silence with abandonment.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Grace becomes returnable possession.\enspace\properrefs{Coll., Ep., Sec., Comm.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located in an older formulary.} Mercy exceeds
merit and request; grace is not void in Paul; offered service seeks acceptance
and aid; substance and firstfruits honor the Lord. The multi-step mechanism
turns possession into something received and therefore returnable in witness,
 worship, and generosity. Honorius, \emph{Gemma animae} IV.65, joins the
Collect, Epistle, and Communion within an older Sunday set, though with
Luke~18 and a different Alleluia; it does not include this Secret. That partial
precedent does not prove the 1962 compiler's intention, and the
proposal may not import the unappointed remainder of 1~Corinthians~15:10.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{An enemy becomes a witness.\enspace\properrefs{Int., Ep.}}
\emph{Classification: not located in the checked corpus.} The Introit asks
that God's enemies scatter; Paul confesses that he persecuted the Church, yet
the risen Christ appeared to him and grace made him an apostle. Together they
open a conversion-shaped alternative to treating enemy language as a fixed
license for destruction: divine victory can include the adversary's
transformation into testimony. No checked witness ties this Introit clause to
Paul's former persecution. The connection does not deny judgment, excuse
violence, or predict every opponent's conversion.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Material signs minister to the whole person.\enspace\properrefs{Gosp., Sec., Postcomm.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} Fingers, saliva, ears, and
tongue are placed beside an offering that becomes help for fragility and a
sacrament asked to aid mind and body. The sequence resists both magical
materialism and disembodied religion: created acts serve personal healing
because Christ acts and the Church petitions. Ambrose's baptismal reception,
Anthony, and Schuster are the nearest analogues. The Gospel is a unique
healing, the Secret does not define sacramental causality, and present bodily
cure is not guaranteed.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{From unvoid grace to heavenly fullness.\enspace\properrefs{Coll., Ep., Comm., Postcomm.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} The formulary repeats an
abundance pattern: divine goodness surpasses request, grace is not empty,
barns and presses fill, and heavenly remedy has fullness. Read together, the
images frame abundance as grace reaching its Godward and eschatological fruit
rather than as accumulated surplus. Honorius, Bede on Proverbs, and Schuster
provide partial analogues, not the entire chain. The proposal is invalid if
used as prosperity teaching or as a claim that material poverty discloses
spiritual lack.
