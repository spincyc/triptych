The dates derive from the older reign; the first audience is unknown. NABRE Ps.~30:1 records a later Temple-dedication adaptation, with its 164~BC horizon in 1~Maccabees. \emph{Sources: 2~Sam.~5:11; 24:18--25; Aquinas, \emph{Super Ps.} 29; Patrizi, pp.~115--116; 1~Macc.~4:52--59; NABRE Ps.~30.}}\\[0.1em]

The date derives from David's Jerusalem reign; NABRE does not securely date the poem. \emph{Sources: Num.~10:35; 1~Chr.~15--16; Haydock at Ps.~67:1; Bellarmine; Catholic Encyclopedia; NABRE Ps.~68.}}\\[0.1em]

\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\multicolumn{4}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.92\linewidth}}{Bossuet records Jerome's attribution to Asaph and Ambrose's to David for Asaph to sing. Chronicles places Asaph before the Ark; Israel's festival assembly receives the song, though the feast is debated. NABRE secondarily allows later guild transmission. \emph{Sources: 1~Chr.~15--16; Bossuet on Asaphic psalms; NABRE Ps.~81.}}\\[0.1em]

\multicolumn{4}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.92\linewidth}}{Proverbs~1:1 associates the collection with Solomon; chapters~1--9 form a son in trust and the neighbor's due. The first addressee is unknown; Israel receives the wisdom. Later collections are named, and NABRE places final gathering postexilically or early in the Second-Temple period. \emph{Sources: Prov.~1:1; 3:1--35; 25:1; Catholic Encyclopedia; NABRE introduction.}}\\[0.1em]
