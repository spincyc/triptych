# Eleventh Sunday after Pentecost — appointed-text control

This record preserves the complete facsimile-collated formulary used by the
GPT canonical research edition and its mechanical synthesis companion. The
calendar registry is a locator only; it does not control the wording printed
in the publication.

## Edition and witness

  restitutum, Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum*, editio typica (Vatican

  `edition.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-1962`.

- **Artifact:** CMAA facsimile,

  `artifact.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-1962.cmaa-facsimile-pdf`,

  `648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`,
  82,815,941 bytes and 1,088 PDF pages.
- **Retrieval/access:** the exact facsimile was accessed and its bytes were
  hash-matched on 2026-08-20, before visual collation.
- **Method and status:** all three pages were inspected as page images at 300
  and 600 dpi; the Gospel opening was re-read at 1,600 dpi. Every heading,

## Complete Latin in printed order

### 1542 — Ant. ad Introitum — Ps. 67, 6-7 et 36; Ps. ibid., 2

> Deus in loco sancto suo: Deus qui inhabitáre facit unánimes in domo: ipse
> dabit virtútem et fortitúdinem plebi suæ. Ps. ibid., 2 Exsúrgat Deus, et
> dissipéntur inimíci eius: et fúgiant, qui odérunt eum, a fácie eius.
> ℣. Glória Patri.

The antiphon centonizes v. 6b, v. 7a, and v. 36b. Its `unánimes` is not the
Clementine `unius moris` rendered by the registered Douay English.

### 1543 — Oratio

> Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, qui, abundántia pietátis tuæ, et mérita
> súpplicum excédis et vota: effúnde super nos misericórdiam tuam; ut dimíttas
> quæ consciéntia métuit, et adícias quod orátio non præsúmit. Per Dóminum
> nostrum.

The contracted `adícias`, the comma pair around `abundántia pietátis tuæ, et
mérita`, and the printed conclusion were read directly.

### 1544 — Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Corínthios. — 1 Cor. 15, 1-10

> Fratres: Notum vobis fácio Evangélium, quod prædicávi vobis, quod et
> accepístis, in quo et statis, per quod et salvámini: qua ratióne
> prædicáverim vobis, si tenétis, nisi frustra credidístis. Trádidi enim vobis
> in primis, quod et accépi: quóniam Christus mórtuus est pro peccátis nostris
> secúndum Scriptúras: et quia sepúltus est, et quia resurréxit tértia die
> secúndum Scriptúras: et quia visus est Cephæ, et post hoc úndecim. Deínde
> visus est plus quam quingéntis frátribus simul, ex quibus multi manent usque
> adhuc, quidam autem dormiérunt. Deínde visus est Iacóbo, deínde Apóstolis
> ómnibus: novíssime autem ómnium tamquam abortívo, visus est et mihi. Ego
> enim sum mínimus Apostolórum, qui non sum dignus vocári Apóstolus, quóniam
> persecútus sum Ecclésiam Dei. Grátia autem Dei sum id quod sum, et grátia
> eius in me vácua non fuit.

The lesson moves `Fratres` to the liturgical address and ends within v. 10.
It does not appoint `sed abundántius … laborávi: non ego autem, sed grátia Dei
mecum` or v. 11. The heading ends with a printed period after `Corínthios.`

### 1545 — Graduale — Ps. 27, 7 et 1

> In Deo sperávit cor meum, et adiútus sum: et reflóruit caro mea, et ex
> voluntáte mea confitébor illi. ℣. Ad te, Dómine, clamávi: Deus meus, ne
> síleas, ne discédas a me.

The order is v. 7 then v. 1. The chant's `ne discédas a me` is absent from the
Clementine at Ps. 27:1 and matches Clementine Ps. 34:22, an inter-psalm textual
relation rather than a phrase translated by the Douay verse printed here.

### 1546 — Alleluia — Ps. 80, 2-3

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Ps. 80, 2-3 Exsultáte Deo adiutóri nostro, iubiláte
> Deo Iacob: súmite psalmum iucúndum cum cíthara. Allelúia.

The chant abbreviates v. 3, omitting `et date tympanum; psaltérium` and moving
`iucúndum` to `psalmum`.

### 1547 — ✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Marcum. — Marc. 7, 31-37

> In illo témpore: Exiens Iesus de finibus Tyri, venit per Sidónem ad mare
> Galilǽæ, inter médios fines Decapóleos. Et addúcunt ei surdum et mutum, et
> deprecabántur eum, ut impónat illi manum. Et apprehéndens eum de turba
> seórsum, misit dígitos suos in aurículas eius: et éxspuens, tétigit linguam
> eius: et suspíciens in cælum, ingémuit, et ait illi: Ephphetha, quod est
> adaperíre. Et statim apértæ sunt aures eius, et solútum est vínculum linguæ
> eius, et loquebátur recte. Et præcépit illis, ne cui dícerent. Quanto autem
> eis præcipiébat, tanto magis plus prædicábant: et eo ámplius admirabántur,
> dicéntes: Bene ómnia fecit: et surdos fecit audíre, et mutos loqui.

Immediately following: `Credo.` The heading ends with a printed period after
`Marcum.` The controlling image prints **`Exiens Iesus de finibus Tyri`**:
neither `Exiens` nor `finibus` bears an accent. The liturgical incipit supplies
Jesus' name and omits Mark's opening `Et iterum`; `Ephphetha` differs from the
registered Douay's `Ephpheta`.

### 1548 — Ant. ad Offertorium — Ps. 29, 2-3

> Exaltábo te, Dómine, quóniam suscepísti me, nec delectásti inimícos meos
> super me: Dómine, clamávi ad te, et sanásti me.

The chant drops the Clementine `Deus meus` from v. 3.

### 1549 — Secreta

> Réspice, Dómine, quǽsumus, nostram propítius servitútem: ut, quod
> offérimus, sit tibi munus accéptum, et sit nostræ fragilitátis subsídium.
> Per Dóminum.

Immediately following: `Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.` The printed `Ssma`
carries a contraction mark. The short conclusion `Per Dóminum.` is what the
typical edition prints.

### 1550 — Ant. ad Communionem — Prov. 3, 9-10

> Honóra Dóminum de tua substántia, et de primítiis frugum tuárum: et
> implebúntur hórrea tua saturitáte, et vino torculária redundábunt.

Against the Clementine the antiphon drops `ómnium`, the imperative `da ei`,
and the second `tua`.

### 1551 — Postcommunio

> Sentiámus, quǽsumus, Dómine, tui perceptióne sacraménti, subsídium mentis
> et córporis: ut, in utróque salváti, cæléstis remédii plenitúdine
> gloriémur. Per Dóminum.

The comma after `ut` is printed in the typical edition. This is the complete
final element before the next formulary.

## English witnesses

Scriptural English is the registered public-domain Douay–Rheims/Challoner.
The exact whole verses were inspected in the tracked Psalm, Proverbs, Mark,
and 1 Corinthians artifacts. Because the Introit, Gradual, Alleluia,
Offertory, and Communion materially shape or alter their verses, the
publication prints complete Douay verses and declares the gaps rather than
creating a new English antiphon. The Gospel incipit and Epistle ending receive
the same boundary treatment.

The Collect, Secret, and Postcommunion are quoted from *The Roman Missal
translated into the English language for the use of the laity*, first revised
edition (Philadelphia: Eugene Cummiskey, 1861), printed pp. 418 and 420,
artifact PDF pp. 427 and 429. The registered scan's SHA-256 is
`85034c90d5cbfe891f4359fb2faff907d217dbd11a30458b55e4c48eae028898`.
The 1861 text is a historical lay-hand-missal translation, not official 1962
English and not text for liturgical recitation. Its abbreviated `Thro'.` is
preserved. Its Collect makes the petitioners, rather than prayer, the subject
of not presuming; its Postcommunion reverses `mentis et corporis` and renders
`plenitudine gloriemur` more loosely as enjoying the remedy's full effect.
