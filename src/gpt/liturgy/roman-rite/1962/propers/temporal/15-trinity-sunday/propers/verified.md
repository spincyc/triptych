## 1. Introit

**References:** Tobit 12:6, with Psalm 8:2.

Benedícta sit sancta Trínitas atque indivísa Unitas: confitébimur ei, quia fecit nobíscum misericórdiam suam. Ps. 8, 2 Dómine Dóminus noster, quam admirábile est nomen tuum in univérsa terra! ℣. Glória Patri.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** in Tobit 12 Raphael has revealed his angelic identity after Tobiah’s return and the healing of Tobit. He commands father and son to bless God, confess his deeds before all the living, and write what has happened; the antiphon gives that thanksgiving an explicitly Trinitarian liturgical form. Psalm 8 frames human dignity within the majesty of the divine name throughout the earth. Together they make remembered mercy and blessing the congregation’s entrance voice.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline’s stopping point after the doxology is retained, with the secondary’s explicit repeat incipit documented above.

## 2. Collect

Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, qui dedísti fámulis tuis, in confessióne veræ fídei, ætérnæ Trinitátis glóriam agnóscere, et in poténtia maiestátis adoráre Unitátem: quǽsumus; ut, eiúsdem fídei firmitáte, ab ómnibus semper muniámur advérsis. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical oration asks the almighty eternal God that servants given true faith may acknowledge the glory of the eternal Trinity, adore unity in majestic power, and be defended by steadfastness in that faith. As the Collect, it gathers the assembly’s praise into confession, adoration, and perseverance before the readings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** Romans 11:33–36.

O altitúdo divitiárum sapiéntiæ et sciéntiæ Dei: quam incomprehensibília sunt iudícia eius, et investigábiles viæ eius! Quis enim cognóvit sensum Dómini? Aut quis consiliárius eius fuit? Aut quis prior dedit illi, et retribuétur ei? Quóniam ex ipso, et per ipsum, et in ipso sunt ómnia: ipsi glória in sǽcula. Amen.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Romans 9–11 was read as the governing context. Paul has considered Israel’s election and stumbling, Gentile inclusion, the warning against Gentile boasting, and God’s saving mercy before the appointed doxology acknowledges the unsearchable depth of divine wisdom. The Epistle therefore presents wonder as the conclusion of sustained attention to salvation history, not as an abstract refusal to inquire.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 4. Gradual

**Reference:** Daniel 3:55–56.

Benedíctus es, Dómine, qui intuéris abýssos, et sedes super Chérubim. ℣. Benedíctus es, Dómine, in firmaménto cæli, et laudábilis in sǽcula.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** these lines belong to the Greek addition containing the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three Young Men. Within the furnace, faithful Judeans under a pagan empire call all creation to bless the God who sees the depths, reigns above the cherubim, and is praised forever. In the guide’s explicitly editorial synthesis of the assembled formulary, the Gradual answers Paul’s unsearchable depth with revealed blessing rather than attempted mastery.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Daniel 3:52.

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Benedíctus es, Dómine, Deus patrum nostrórum, et laudábilis in sǽcula. Allelúia.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** the opening of the three young men’s hymn blesses the Lord as the God of Israel’s fathers before the song widens through sanctuary, throne, heavens, and the whole created order. In the guide’s explicitly editorial synthesis of the assembled formulary, the Alleluia carries inherited Israelite praise into the proclamation of the risen Christ’s universal command.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Matthew 28:18–20.

In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Data est mihi omnis potéstas in cælo, et in terra. Eúntes ergo docéte omnes gentes, baptizántes eos in nómine Patris, et Fílii, et Spíritus Sancti: docéntes eos serváre ómnia quæcúmque mandávi vobis. Et ecce ego vobíscum sum ómnibus diébus, usque ad consummatiónem sǽculi.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Matthew 28 was read in full. After the Resurrection, the eleven go to the appointed mountain in Galilee, worship the risen Jesus, and receive a mission grounded in his authority over heaven and earth: disciple all nations, baptize in the one name of Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, teach observance, and rely on his abiding presence. The Gospel proclaims baptismal confession as the form of universal mission and obedient life.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Tobit 12:6.

Benedíctus sit Deus Pater, unigenitúsque Dei Fílius, Sanctus quoque Spíritus: quia fecit nobíscum misericórdiam suam.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** the same angelic command to bless God for mercy is adapted into an explicit blessing of Father, only-begotten Son, and Holy Ghost. At the Offertory, the mercy confessed at the entrance becomes praise carried with the gifts toward the altar.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 8. Secret

Sanctífica, quǽsumus, Dómine Deus noster, per tui sancti nóminis invocatiónem, huius oblatiónis hóstiam: et per eam nosmetípsos tibi pérfice munus ætérnum. Per Dóminum.

**Rubric:** no commemoration of the Sunday is made; Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical prayer asks God to sanctify the victim of the oblation through invocation of his holy name and, through it, to perfect the worshippers themselves as an eternal gift to him. As the proper prayer over the gifts, it distinguishes the offered victim from the offerers while joining sacramental oblation to ecclesial self-offering.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Tobit 12:6.

Benedícimus Deum cæli, et coram ómnibus vivéntibus confitébimur ei: quia fecit nobíscum misericórdiam suam.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Raphael’s command joins blessing the God of heaven to public confession of his mercy. The Communion antiphon puts that testimony on the lips of those receiving the sacrament and turns private benefit outward before all the living.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Profíciat nobis ad salútem córporis et ánimæ, Dómine Deus noster, huius sacraménti suscéptio: et sempitérnæ sanctæ Trinitátis eiusdémque indivíduæ Unitátis conféssio. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** after Communion, the Church asks that reception of the sacrament and confession of the eternal Trinity and indivisible unity advance the salvation of body and soul. The Postcommunion therefore seeks the continuing fruit of both sacramental reception and confessed faith.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.
