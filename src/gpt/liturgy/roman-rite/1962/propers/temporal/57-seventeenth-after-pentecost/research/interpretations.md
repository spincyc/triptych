# Proposed coherent whole-formulary readings

Independently checked during the research stage on 17 September 2026 for the universal 1962 Seventeenth Sunday after Pentecost, 20 September 2026. This records completed bounded research, not an independent review verdict. Cold research review and reader-facing authoring remain outstanding. No postconciliar proper or study supplies evidence for this leaf.

Stable lane keys: `healed-charity`, `one-people`, `faithful-offering`. These name editorial arguments, not historical schools. Exact source URLs, languages, retention states and checked loci live in `scope.md`; textual identity and rights in `source-audit.md`. The principal arguments have been independently read in their retained witnesses; scope.md records the completed proportionate second search and excludes unverified optional leads.

These are editorial source-grounded architectures, not three historical patristic schools or claims that the Fathers composed this Sunday's formularies. Each lane crosses all ten elements and articulates all four senses; individual element comments support that argument rather than form ten disconnected mini-essays.

### A. God heals the heart that he commands to love

**Literal:** demands of law, Pauline conduct, confession, supplication, vows and the three prayers together expose the need for purification. **Allegorical:** David's Son is also Lord; the divine Word/Spirit establish God's people, and Christ's mysteries heal them. **Moral:** the whole heart becomes truthful mercy, patient forbearance and faithful action. **Anagogical:** love's healing reaches the eternal remedy and shared hope; judgment measures what love has become. Principal engine: Augustine on Ps 118 and Christian Doctrine, Chrysostom Mt71, Thomas III q.79. It is strongest as a remedy for voluntaristic preaching: the demanding Gospel stands inside repeated prayers for grace.

### B. The one Lord gathers a people who learn to bear one another

**Literal:** Israel's elected people, Daniel's community, Paul's body and Jesus' hearers remain historically distinct speakers. **Allegorical:** the Church is gathered by the Word and Spirit under the one Lord whom David worships. **Moral:** unity is sustained through humble patience and service, with a common confession rather than rivalry. **Anagogical:** the called share one inheritance and perfected peace beyond the sovereignty of earthly rulers. Principal engine: Chrysostom Eph9–11; Augustine Ps 32 and101; Thomas Eph 4. This lane makes the chants perform essential argumentative work, especially the single voice praying for the whole sanctuary people.

### C. Mercy teaches the worshipper to offer a faithful life

**Literal:** the Mass moves from admitting God's justice to asking mercy, hearing the law of love, interceding, presenting the holy action, fulfilling commitments and seeking lasting healing. **Allegorical:** Christ the Davidic Lord and saving Head makes this common approach to the Father possible; his sacramental action gives the petition its efficacy. **Moral:** vows become lived charity and confession becomes changed conduct, not merely intense religious speech. **Anagogical:** the Lord before whom rulers lose their breath grants his people eternal remedy and peace. Principal engine: Augustine Ps 75; Jerome Dan 9; Thomas III q.79. Best homily lane because its movement follows the worshippers' actual action while making Gospel identity and commands its centre.

The four senses must not become four names for one moral application. Each lane needs actual literal controls, Christ/church fulfillment, concrete moral action, and eschatological consummation. Full-formulary matrices are drafting controls; reader prose can remain connected and speakable.

## Argument development and controls for each lane

### `healed-charity`: God heals the heart that he commands to love

Controlling claim: the twofold command requires the whole person, and the whole Mass asks God to make that obedience possible. Augustine Ps 118 §123 reads mercy as the opposite of reliance on one's own righteousness; §136 keeps divine justice real; §§34–36,41 explain why the commanded heart needs God's enabling gift. His reasoning proceeds from the psalmist's own petitions: the same speaker promises to keep the law and requests the power to walk it. This is direct evidence for a grace-centered reading, not a retrospective patch over a harsh Gospel. Chrysostom Mt71 joins the two commands because one cannot claim love of God while refusing the neighbor, then interprets David's Son/Lord as disclosure of Christ's divinity. Thomas III q.79aa1–2,6 gives a sacramental account of growth in charity, preservation and final fruit, doctrinally illuminating the prayers without claiming to explain their composition.

The Introit's justice/mercy juxtaposition sets the problem; the Collect asks release from what divides allegiance. The Epistle describes healing in observable virtues rather than private feeling. The Gradual contributes the constituting Word and Spirit, not a generic nature image: its literal creation praise is the basis of Augustine's righteous-as-heavens reading, Ps 32§6. The Alleluia gives the needy heart a voice. In the Gospel love's sovereign object is identified as David's Lord, so the second half is necessary to the lane. Daniel's sanctuary and named people prevent the petition from shrinking into personal self-improvement. The Secret asks the holy action to bear on sins already committed and temptations ahead. Communion makes healing accountable through fulfilled commitments before the feared sovereign. Postcommunion states the intended result explicitly: cured vices and eternal remedy.

Four-sense distillation: **Literal**—real commands, distress, penitence, election and prayer remain distinct historical speech acts; no psalm is reduced to an anticipation of this Mass. **Allegorical**—the Davidic Lord, Word made flesh and Head of his people heals through his mysteries; Augustine Ps 118§41 explicitly connects Christ the righteousness of God with the neighbor. **Moral**—ask for help, then practice patience, truthful correction, generosity and fidelity; do not convert grace into an excuse for inertia. **Anagogical**—the shared hope and eternal remedy complete the heart's charity, while judgment remains serious.

Strongest difficulty: full-hearted love can sound like a demand for immediate emotional intensity or moral perfection, while sacramental remedy can sound automatic. The primary witnesses instead join commanded action with requested grace. Thomas III q.79a6 preserves the possibility of later free rejection. Cummiskey's future-offenses wording must therefore be explained, not improved under his edition's name. This lane's center is transformation of the person; `one-people` asks whose common life that transformation serves, and `faithful-offering` asks how worship enacts it.

### `one-people`: The one Lord gathers people who learn to bear one another

Controlling claim: the one Lord constitutes a common people through gifts they receive together, and their conduct must express that given unity. Chrysostom Eph9 grounds peace in the Spirit's power to join divided people; hom 10 names the one body across place and time; hom 11 distinguishes common salvation from differing ministries. Thomas Super Ephesios 4lect1 identifies the practices that destroy unity—pride, anger, impatience and disordered zeal—then distinguishes charity from negligent consent; lect2 shows one ruler, law, signs and end. Augustine Ps 32§12 names the heavenly city's elected people, and Ps 101§§1–3 supplies their shared praying voice through Head and members. These arguments are compatible without declaring every metaphor lexically identical.

The Introit's singular servant stands among those walking God's law; this move from singular to plural is in the appointed material. The Collect already names God's people and exclusive allegiance. Ephesians gives the lane its fullest grammar: body, Spirit, hope, Lord, faith, baptism, Father. The Gradual's people exists by election and the divine Word/Spirit, not collective self-promotion. The Alleluia's solitary cry becomes ecclesial through Augustine's explicitly argued Head-and-members reading, while the literal afflicted speaker is retained. The Gospel identifies the one Lord beyond merely human descent and gives the common life's law of love. Daniel intercedes for the sanctuary and named people rather than claiming private access. Secret and Postcommunion ask together for purification and healing. Communion surrounds the Lord with gift-bearers; Augustine Ps 75§12 sharpens this into refusal to privatize truth and build a faction, while §13 humbles proud sovereignty.

Four-sense distillation: **Literal**—Israel, Daniel's community, Paul's addressees and Jesus' interlocutors are distinct settings, and the psalm's heavens first belong to creation praise. **Allegorical**—the Church is the body of the Davidic Lord, constituted by Word and Spirit and praying with its Head. **Moral**—common baptism forbids contempt; different ministries call for mutual service, patient endurance and correction ordered to peace. **Anagogical**—one hope becomes shared inheritance and perfected peace under the Lord above earthly princes.

Strongest difficulty: unity might be mistaken for unanimity at any price, ethnic or national self-assertion, or erasure of roles. Chrysostom's alternative meanings of one spirit are shown as alternatives, while his common-saving-gifts argument preserves different ministries. Thomas distinguishes spiritual unity from cooperation in evil. Augustine's spiritual nation is not a modern state's mandate. Avoid importing his polemical ancient collective characterizations as contemporary fact. This lane centers the social body; `healed-charity` explains how its members change, and `faithful-offering` follows its common act of worship.

### `faithful-offering`: Mercy teaches worshippers to offer faithful lives

Controlling claim: the petitions and gifts of the assembly demand continuing fidelity, and the mysteries give the healing by which that fidelity can endure. Augustine Ps 75§3 relates confession to peace with God: the sinner ceases defending what God condemns. §§9–10 connect repentance, thankful remembrance, baptism and daily sacrifice; §11 distinguishes the fidelity all owe from particular vows and insists that God enables fulfillment; §§12–13 resist private possession of truth and proud domination. Jerome InDanII9:17–21 explains restoration petition, divine attention, alternative accounts of corporate confession, and evening sacrifice. Thomas III q.79aa1–2,6 explains charity, future preservation and eternal fruit. This is an editorial liturgical sequence grounded in these arguments, not a claim Augustine or Jerome expounded this precise Sunday.

The Introit begins with honest acknowledgment of judgment and dependence on mercy. The Collect asks for the renunciation necessary to follow God alone. The prisoner of the Epistle makes worthy living concrete; worship's peace must take the shape of patience. The Gradual establishes that the gift-bearing people first receives existence and election. The Alleluia models supplication. Both Gospel halves specify whom worshippers adore and what their Lord commands: knowing the correct command is insufficient if one remains resistant to its Lord. The Offertory gives the assembly Daniel's intercession, with the difference between canonical chapter and selected liturgical adaptation explicit. The Secret prays that what is enacted transform past and future conduct. Communion confronts the congregation with vows paid and gifts offered to the sovereign who judges princes. Postcommunion receives all this as healing that must bear eternal fruit.

Four-sense distillation: **Literal**—the several biblical speech acts and the actual order of this Missal's ten elements support an approach, petition, holy action and hoped-for fruit; this is not proof they were composed together. **Allegorical**—Christ, David's Lord and the body's Head, makes access and common prayer possible; Thomas's Eucharistic doctrine illuminates the holy action. **Moral**—religious words become fulfilled commitments, mercy toward a difficult neighbor and renunciation of self-justification. General baptismal fidelity must not be conflated with a newly imposed private vow. **Anagogical**—final judgment removes false sovereignty and the faithful receive the eternal remedy and peace for which they pray.

Strongest difficulty: this could become a sermon that worship merely reminds people to try harder. Augustine's insistence that the Lord enables payment and Thomas's account of sacramental efficacy keep divine action central. Daniel's communal confession neither licenses denial of personal wrong nor assigns equal culpability to everyone. The Communion's fear is not excised in favor of comforting language: Augustine joins trembling worship to humility and God's removal of pride. This lane centers enacted worship and subsequent fidelity; it can use `healed-charity`'s grace logic without collapsing `one-people`'s distinct ecclesial emphasis.

## Comparison and eventual derivative documents

The lanes share Christ's lordship, grace-enabled charity, communal prayer, morally serious judgment and eternal hope. Their governing questions differ: what heals the commanded heart; what makes many persons one people; and how the common act of worship bears fruit in faithful lives. Their overlap is substantive agreement, not evidence of historical consensus about this Sunday. Their real exegetical alternatives—questioner's motives, meanings of one spirit, literal heavens versus spiritual righteous, individual versus Head-and-members voice, personal versus representative confession, ordinary fidelity versus special vows—belong next to the relevant arguments, not in a detached objection gallery.

The eventual concise study should interleave these readings under questions such as how commanded love becomes possible, whose voice prays, and what offering requires tomorrow. It should preserve qualifications about motives, sacramental freedom and judgment. The proposed homily may follow `faithful-offering` using `healed-charity`'s grace logic: hearing the right answer; meeting the Lord who commands it; learning Paul's patient love; praying Daniel's intercession; receiving healing so promises become conduct. One practical response may be patient charity toward a real difficult person with correction at a fitting time. This is a planning recommendation, not spoken prose, a fictitious pastoral experience or a claim that the assembly has been consulted.

## Bellarmine supplement integrated into the proposals

The focused later saintly search adds the following usable comparisons to the existing arguments, with full source and reading limits in scope.md. For `healed-charity`, Bellarmine Ps 118ad124 interprets teaching as practical love, giving the same grace-centered claim a distinct epistemic consequence: knowing the command is not yet living it. Ps 101ad1 asks for the grace to pray and supplies a direct reading of the Alleluia as already dependent on divine help. For `one-people`, Ps 32ad6 keeps the creation sense first and explains Trinitarian action as inseparable; ad12 directs the chosen people's present faith, hope and charity to final vision. For `faithful-offering`, Ps 75ad11–12 insists on payment of vowed gifts and God's authority even over kings' lives. Place that concrete judgment beside Augustine's reading of proud spirits and bodily self-rule rather than combining them into a single alleged lexical meaning.

These additions materially distinguish literal and spiritual senses within each lane. Bellarmine's original psalm prefaces and philological arguments were omitted by O’Sullivan, so no claim that he surveyed or rejected all other readings is warranted from this English witness.
