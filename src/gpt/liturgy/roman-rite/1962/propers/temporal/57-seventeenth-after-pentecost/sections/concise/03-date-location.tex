\midrule
Offertory & Dan.~9:17--19, adapted & Babylon, first year of Darius the Mede
(9:1); prayer for the desolate sanctuary &
\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: the prophet Daniel, recording his own
prayer as the seventy years foretold by Jeremias ran out; the 1908
\work{Catholic Encyclopedia} article reports both the traditional date shown
and a rival attribution to an unknown later author, for which the admitted
evidence establishes no numerical modern critical date. The ``seventy years'' in the Date column is the
duration of the servitude within the prayer's setting, not a composition
date; the chant selects and rearranges the petitions.}
\midrule
Gospel & Matt.~22:34--46 & Written, in the received account, in Judea for
Jewish believers; the encounter narrated in the Temple at Jerusalem (21:23) &
\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: the last of the Lord's Temple controversies
(21:23--22:46), shortly before the Passion forecast at 26:1--2. No numerical
date for the encounter is established here.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St~Matthew the Apostle. The Date
column's alternatives are not of one standing: about A.D.~38--45 counts eight
or fifteen years from the Ascension with early writers; about A.D.~40--42 follows
the twelve-year apostolic dispersal, a tradition the 1911 \work{Catholic
Encyclopedia} article itself calls ``admittedly not too reliable''; A.D.~40--45
reports that article's survey of the Catholic critics of its day; about
A.D.~60--68 is conditional on a later proposed apostolic departure;
about A.D.~64--67 rests on a text of Irenaeus whose difficulties of
interpretation, the article says, prevent any positive conclusion; and about
A.D.~50 is Durand's date (1912) for the Aramaic original alone, the Greek
rendering left undated. These are composition alternatives; the Temple is the
place of the narrated controversy, not an asserted writing-place. No separate
event date is supplied. The generated comparison gives the NABRE's
post-A.D.~70 boundary for the Greek Gospel, probably at least a decade later,
without a precise endpoint; it remains separate from the traditional
alternatives.}
\midrule
Epistle & Eph.~4:1--6 & Paul, ``a prisoner in the Lord'' (4:1); to the saints
at Ephesus (1:1) & \chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St~Paul, in a letter of his captivity.
The 1909 \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} article assigns the captivity letters
the period shown and leaves Rome or Caesarea open; the encyclopedia's 1911
chronology of St~Paul gives Ephesians the single year shown. The 1909 article
reports the denial of Pauline authorship by most liberal critics since
Schleiermacher but supplies no numerical date for that position; no additional
modern critical date is established here. It holds ``at Ephesus'' (1:1) no
part of the primitive text and proposes a circular to churches in Asia Minor.
The address above belongs to the received biblical text, not a settled
identification of the first recipients.}
