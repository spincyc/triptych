\section*{Source and delivery note}

The spoken homily ends on the preceding page. Intended audience: an adult parish assembly. Its 1,283 spoken words suggest approximately 10.7--11.7 minutes at 110--120 words per minute, allowing an unhurried pace. This is a word-count estimate, not a timed human delivery. A silent editorial reading checked sentence sense, transitions, oral clarity, and the return to the opening image; no audible rehearsal is claimed.

The argument derives principally from the reviewed study's \emph{God heals the heart that he commands to love}, with compatible applications of shared baptism and continuing fidelity from its other two readings. It joins Matthew's commands and revelation of Christ's lordship, Paul's patient charity, and the Mass's petitions for grace and sacramental healing. The whole-Mass construction and practical applications are editorial; no Father is credited with this complete homily. Both the expansive and concise studies received independent workflow review before this derivation. This authored homily is not an official liturgical text or an assertion of ecclesiastical approval. The celebration is the universal 1962 Seventeenth Sunday after Pentecost, studied for 20 September 2026; no local calendar has been specified.

The prayer explanations follow the \emph{Missale Romanum} (1962), and biblical wording follows the historical Douay--Rheims study witness. Exact sources follow, entirely outside the speech.
