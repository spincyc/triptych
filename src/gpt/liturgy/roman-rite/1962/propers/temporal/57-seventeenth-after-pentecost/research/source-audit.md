The complete collated text is now in ../propers/verified.md; the following audit records the important text distinctions.

The following is a normalized research transcription from fresh 1962 image reading, with stress accents removed and ae/oe expanded. Preserve target I/J practice or disclose orthographic normalization when authoring. Prayer conclusions below follow the target's printed extent.

1. **Introit**, Ps 118:137,124; verse 118:1 (modern119): `Iustus es, Domine, et rectum iudicium tuum: fac cum servo tuo secundum misericordiam tuam. Ps. Beati immaculati in via: qui ambulant in lege Domini. V. Gloria Patri.` The juxtaposition of justice and mercy belongs to the actual antiphon, not a commentator's repair.
2. **Collect**: `Da, quaesumus, Domine, populo tuo diabolica vitare contagia: et te solum Deum pura mente sectari. Per Dominum nostrum.`
3. **Epistle**, Eph 4:1–6: begins `Fratres: Obsecro vos ego vinctus in Domino`; ends `Unus Deus, et Pater omnium, qui est super omnes, et per omnia, et in omnibus nobis. Qui est benedictus in saecula saeculorum. Amen.` The doxology is a liturgical ending beyond standard Eph 4:6, and the initial `Fratres` is liturgical. Do not silently replace the appointed form with a whole Bible paragraph. The main sequence has humility, meekness, patience, mutual bearing in charity, keeping spiritual unity in peace, then body/Spirit/hope/Lord/faith/baptism/God and Father.
4. **Gradual**, Ps 32:12,6 (modern33): `Beata gens, cuius est Dominus Deus eorum: populus, quem elegit Dominus in hereditatem sibi. V. Verbo Domini caeli firmati sunt: et spiritu oris eius omnis virtus eorum.`
5. **Alleluia**, Ps 101:2 (modern102:1): `Alleluia, alleluia. V. Domine, exaudi orationem meam, et clamor meus ad te perveniat. Alleluia.` Verse-number trap: modern Hebrew numbering excludes the psalm's superscription; cite the missal's 101:2 first.
6. **Gospel**, Mt 22:34–46: liturgical opening `In illo tempore: Accesserunt ad Iesum pharisaei: et interrogavit eum unus ex eis legis doctor, tentans eum`; then the twofold command, Christ's question about David's Son, Ps 109:1, and the opponents' inability to answer. The canonical34 opening about hearing of the Sadducees' silence is not the Missal's opening. The pericope includes BOTH love and Christ's identity; a love-only homily amputates its ending.
7. **Offertory**, Dan 9:17,18,19 (missal citation): `Oravi Deum meum ego Daniel, dicens: Exaudi, Domine, preces servi tui: illumina faciem tuam super sanctuarium tuum: et propitius intende populum istum, super quem invocatum est nomen tuum, Deus.` This is a liturgical adaptation, not a continuous Bible quotation: the Daniel self-introduction and rearrangement require explicit apparatus. Full Dan 9:1–27 context supports penitence, appeal to divine mercy, sanctuary desolation, and promised restoration; it does not establish that the medieval chant compiler intended every detail of a modern synthesis.
8. **Secret**: `Maiestatem tuam, Domine, suppliciter deprecamur: ut haec sancta, quae gerimus, et a praeteritis nos delictis exuant, et futuris. Per Dominum nostrum Iesum Christum, Filium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitate.`
9. **Communion**, Ps 75:12–13 (modern76:11–12): `Vovete et reddite Domino Deo vestro, omnes, qui in circuitu eius affertis munera: terribili, et ei qui aufert spiritum principum: terribili apud omnes reges terrae.` Do not omit the feared judgment upon princes to make the whole seem merely gentle.
10. **Postcommunion**: `Sanctificationibus tuis, omnipotens Deus, et vitia nostra curentur, et remedia nobis aeterna proveniant. Per Dominum nostrum.`
