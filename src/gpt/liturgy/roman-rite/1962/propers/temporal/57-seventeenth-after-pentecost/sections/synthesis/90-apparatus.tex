\runninghead{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}

This concise study derives from \emph{Love, Unity, and the Healing of Worship}, the expansive study of the universal 1962 Roman Missal's Seventeenth Sunday after Pentecost for 20 September 2026. It compares that study's three editorial whole-formulary readings; no Father is credited with the complete construction. No particular local calendar is specified. The full study supplies the appointed texts and larger arguments. This is a study aid, not an official liturgical book or an ecclesiastically approved commentary.

Psalm numbers follow the Missal: 32,75,101,118 correspond to common modern 33,76,102,119; the Alleluia's 101:2 corresponds to 102:1. Biblical contexts are read in the public-domain American 1899 Douay--Rheims study text. Cummiskey's 1861 English is historical human translation, not official 1962 English. Commentary here supplies no substitute text for ritual recitation. The inherited Latin has a separately collated public-domain 1862 antecedent; the modern Missal facsimile itself receives no blanket redistribution licence. Date cells on \emph{Scriptural Date and Location} are generated from \path{research/chronology.toml}, alternatives and unresolved states included. The admitted evidence establishes no numerical modern critical date for Daniel or Ephesians and no numerical narrated-event date for the Gospel. A separately attributed comparison gives the NABRE's post-A.D.~70 boundary for the Greek Gospel, without a precise endpoint or any union with the default traditional alternatives.

The source witnesses retain the full study's limits: NPNF's Augustine Psalms is abridged; Jerome's Latin and Bellarmine's historical English are retained transcriptions, not newly collated critical texts. Bellarmine's translator omitted philological discussions, variants, and original psalm prefaces. Their contributions are paraphrases. Thomas's Ephesians commentary is read from the 1857 Latin facsimile; the English \emph{Summa} electronic edition discloses editorial modifications. His sacramental doctrine illuminates the prayers; no direct ancient exposition of their exact wording was established. The research and expansive study received independent workflow review before this condensation; this does not assert completed review of the concise artifact. Detailed evidence and production records remain in \path{research/scope.md}, \path{research/interpretations.md}, and \path{research/production-review.md}.
