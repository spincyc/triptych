## Biblical contexts actually read

Complete contexts: Psalms 2, 28 and 71; Daniel 7; Colossians 1–2; John 18–19;
Apocalypse 1, 5 and 19, plus Psalm 88 for the inactive alternatives, in the
registered American-1899 verse texts. Acts 4:24–28 and Jeremiah 25:11–12 were
also read for the chronology evidence. Their functions:

- Apocalypse 1 joins witness, blood, kingdom, priesthood and patient tribulation;
  chapter 5 places the slain yet standing Lamb within heavenly worship and the
  opening of the sealed book. The Introit's royal praise must retain the slaughter.
- Psalm 71 places universal dominion amid justice for the poor, release from
  oppression and enduring peace. Verses 8 and 11 do not erase verses 12–14.
- Psalm 2 begins with resistance and ends with admonition and refuge. Its
  inheritance language is neither an authorization for the congregation to seize
  nations nor a promise that every ruler already obeys willingly.
- Psalm 28 moves through the divine voice, storm and worship toward permanent
  kingship and peace. The Communion selects the conclusion without claiming that
  the whole psalm describes quiet conditions.
- Daniel 7 opposes transient beastly kingdoms to the dominion received by the
  human figure; verses 18 and 27 extend possession of the kingdom to the saints.
  The vision's first year of Baltasar is its narrative setting, not automatically
  a date for the book's composition or a date for the heavenly kingdom.
- Colossians 1:21–23 applies reconciliation to former enemies and requires
  perseverance. Chapter 2's warning about angelic religion gives context for
  Chrysostom's rejection of angels as rival mediators. Universal scope does not
  by itself assert the salvation of every individual irrespective of response.
- John 18 includes Peter's sword and Jesus' refusal of it; John 19 develops the
  royal mockery, Caesar accusation, sentence and inscription. The appointed
  scene remains the interrogation inside the governor's hall, not the Crucifixion
  itself. The text identifies particular speakers and authorities; it does not
  authorize collective blame of Jewish people across time.
