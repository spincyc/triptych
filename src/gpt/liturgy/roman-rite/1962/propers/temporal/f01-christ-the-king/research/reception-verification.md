## Arguments and exact loci inspected

**Augustine, Tractate on John 115.1–5.** The complete tractate was read in
NPNF I.7, retained lines 34853–35057. Section 2 distinguishes the kingdom's
origin from its presence: believers remain in the world but become members
through regeneration. Augustine himself brings Colossians 1:13 into the argument.
Section 4 distinguishes creaturehood from grace-enabled obedient belief. The
direction of the truth/hearing relation matters: independent merit does not
cause belonging to truth. Section 5 extends into John 18:38–40 and therefore
supplies context beyond the appointment.

**Jerome, Commentary on Daniel VII, on 13–14, 17–18 and 25–28.** These loci
were read in retained Latin, electronic lines 38–44 with preceding discussion.
Jerome explains received dominion through incarnation and Philippians 2,
rejecting an inference of inferior divine nature. Endurance and scope exceed
a fulfillment exhausted by Maccabean victory. His treatment of the saints'
kingdom rejects an earthly millennium. These are exegetical positions, not
modern findings about composition. Generated paragraph prefixes are not native
citation numbers.

**Chrysostom, Homilies on Colossians II and III.** In NPNF I.13, the portion
of II expounding 1:12–15 and beginning its practical application was read at
lines 23905–24065. He distinguishes giving an inheritance from making recipients
fit, and rescue from darkness from transfer into the Son's kingdom. The transfer
discussion retains the recipient's participation. This inspection did not cover
all of II's exposition of 1:9–11 or its entire concluding address.

Homily III was read in full, including notes, at lines 24321–24817. Creation and
continuing dependence exclude the Son from the class of created powers.
Reconciliation in heaven concerns estrangement between angels and sinful
humanity, not sin in holy angels. Christ's self-giving supplies its means. The
extended application to the Church's exchange of peace criticizes feuds among
believers. NPNF's objections and variant discussions, especially at 1:18, belong
to the editor; they are neither Chrysostom's argument nor a replacement Epistle.

**Augustine, Enarrationes on Vulgate Psalms 2, 28 and 71.** The complete received
NPNF renditions were read: 2.1–11, 28.1–11 and 71.1–20. Printed ellipses in
the received Psalm 71 rendition mean this is not a claim to have read the
unabridged Latin exposition. NPNF I.8 heads the latter two as
English Psalms 29 and 72 and identifies their Latin numbering. At 2.7, the
Son's petition belongs to incarnate sacrifice and intercession. At 28.10–11,
preservation amid the world's flood precedes final reign; peace in Christ does
not promise freedom from adversity. At 71.1–2 Christ is the true Solomon because
the scope exceeds the historical king; 71.11,13–16 develops worldwide reception
and the beneficiaries' need for grace. Historical polemics and descriptions of
peoples are not the study's own descriptions of living communities.

**Bellarmine, Commentary on Psalms 2, 28 and 71, O'Sullivan English.** Psalm 2
was read in full, as were the complete received expositions of Psalms 28 and 71.
Bounded rereading on 20 October closed the earlier truncated-output gap:
retained lines 95–130, 981–1016 and 2527–2568 respectively. The following
claims rest on these actual passages, not an inventory entry.

Psalm 2, comments on verses 7–10, relates the Son's identity to rightful
inheritance and distinguishes corrective mercy from final judgment. Psalm 71's
opening permits a Davidic prayer for Solomon before moving beyond him. In this
delivery, numbered comment **10** expounds biblical **71:11**, and comments
11–13 expound the subsequent deliverance passage. Exposition numbers must not
become biblical verse numbers. Bellarmine distinguishes widespread present
conversion, final acknowledgment and authority over unwilling subjects. Psalm
28's conclusion preserves natural and ecclesial readings and several flood
interpretations. Present strength and final blessing sustain perseverance
without guaranteeing worldly security.

**Received Victorinus commentary, ANF VII.** Chapter 1's comments on verses
1 and 4–7, chapter 5 in full, and chapter 19's brief comment on verse 11 were
read. Chapter 1 identifies kingdom and priesthood with the believing Church;
chapter 5 develops the Lamb's death, victory and opening of the testament.
This supports Apocalypse 5:12 contextually, but supplies no separate verse-12
exposition resolving every term. The chapter-19 comment concerns verse 11,
not verse 16. Attribution and interpolation cautions remain; this audit does
not settle recension history or make this witness indispensable to either reading.

**Pius XI, Quas primas.** The official English delivery was authenticated against
its registered hash. Inspected sections 7–8, 12–25 and 28–34 connect kingship,
redemption, priesthood, public responsibility and an annual feast's pastoral
purpose. They establish institutional context, not each prayer's authorship or
first composition date. Pius XI is not counted among the canonized carriers of
the readings. No fresh uninterrupted reading of every paragraph is certified.

## Consequences and open work

Inspection sustains both proposals in `interpretations.md`. Augustine and Jerome
substantially carry regenerated membership and enduring inheritance; Chrysostom
and Bellarmine carry creative lordship, sacrificial reconciliation and incomplete
willing recognition. Augustine and Bellarmine differ in Psalm 71's immediate
exegetical scope despite shared Christological fulfillment. Coordination across
eleven elements remains editorial synthesis, not a recovered ancient exposition
of the complete feast.

The complete sourced text record and the current chronology projection are
supplied for fresh review. No newly authored translation, stitched liturgical
English or ancient whole-formulary attribution fills a source gap. Uninspected
later reception and uncertain historical dates remain explicitly limited;
additional authoring claims would require additional evidence. The final studies,
publication checks and independent acceptance belong to subsequent stages.
