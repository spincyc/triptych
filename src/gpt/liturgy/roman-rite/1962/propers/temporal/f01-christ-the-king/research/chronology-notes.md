## Attribution is not occasion or composition

The canonical binding added in this stage reaches only the exact F01 Psalm loci.
Psalm28's attribution comes from its title. Psalm2 is attributed to David by the
apostolic reception in Acts4:25 and by Bellarmine's exposition, although its own
inspected title has no name. Psalm71's title concerns Solomon; the attribution
to David is specifically Bellarmine's reading of the opening prayer. The title
must not be reported as if it explicitly named David as author. His reference era
does not place every attributed poem inside his reign.

No occasion-year was inserted. The tabernacle title does not prove the dedication
of Solomon's Temple, and the general storm imagery does not date a particular
weather event. Bellarmine's Solomon-appointment reading is preserved as reception,
not a settled reconstruction of composition. Augustine's Christological reading
is not used to date Psalm71 to the life of Jesus. Psalm2's apostolic Passion
reception of verses1–2 does not extend a narrated-event binding to the appointed
verse8. These distinctions are part of the appendix's explanation, not optional
fine print.

## Concise place descriptions and row boundaries

Use the generated orientation row identifiers, with concise place prose in their
place-description argument. Do not copy older composite element annotations into
a table row naming only one biblical component.

- Psalm attribution row: Israel/Judah in the received Davidic frame; the printed
  date concerns David's conventional reign. No particular place of composition
  has been established for the appointed verses.
- Psalm occasion row: Psalm71 concerns a king and king's son, Psalm28 a
  tabernacle-completion title and a storm world naming Libanus/Cades, Psalm2 Sion
  and the nations. These are textual settings; a shared precise composition
  location is not known. Keep the concise sentence short and retain detail here.
- Psalm composition row: Israel's Psalter; precise writing places unresolved.
- Daniel setting row: Babylonian royal setting in Daniel7:1; the heavenly court
  belongs to the vision. The seventy years is a duration, not a map coordinate
  or a single date for the vision.
- Daniel composition row: Babylon in the received authorial setting; distinguish
  it from the disputed historical circumstances of the book's formation.
- Gospel event row: the governor's hall at Jerusalem, John18:28,33. Do not place
  this row at Calvary or claim a precise modern archaeological site from these
  verses. The exact calendar year remains unresolved in the generated answer.
- Gospel composition row: Ephesus in the received tradition reported in the
  inspected encyclopedia's composition discussion; distinguish this from the
  narrated Jerusalem scene.
- Epistle composition row: Rome in the selected captivity chronology; addressed
  to Colossae, which is the recipient location, not the source's writing location.
- Apocalypse composition row: Patmos in the received seer's setting, with the
  heavenly throne-room as vision rather than an earthly writing location. The
  passage label must include both Apocalypse1:6 and5:12 and exclude the Introit's
  Psalm when giving this composition answer.

The generated record contains the fuller source identifiers and claims. The
appendix must retain those alternatives and the distinction between source
tradition, source uncertainty and editorial explanation. The concise page may
group identical Psalm subjects, as its generated rows do; it must not collapse
their attribution, occasion and composition into one date. Four composed proper
elements—Collect, Secret, Preface, Postcommunion—have no biblical composition
date. Their feast context is1925; that is neither a date of Scripture nor proof
of each prayer's first composition.
