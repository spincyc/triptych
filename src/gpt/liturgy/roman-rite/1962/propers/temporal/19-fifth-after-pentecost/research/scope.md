# Fifth Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

**Last updated:** 2026-07-10

This record preserves source roles, historical judgments, and claim boundaries compressed in the reader-facing guide. Complete appointed texts, checksums, facsimile locations, and witness differences remain in [the verified propers](../propers/verified.md).

## Scriptural and Liturgical Scope

- Appointed and immediate contexts studied: Psalms 15, 20, 26, and 83 in full; Matthew 4:12–5:48; and 1 Peter 1–5, with special attention to 3:8–17 and the letter's address and conclusion.
- Related texts actually used: 1 Chronicles 6:16–23; Psalm 33:13–17 as quoted in 1 Peter; Acts 2:25–32; and 1 Peter 5:12–13.
- The Collect, Secret, and Postcommunion were analyzed in their complete facsimile-collated Latin forms. The PDF retains only the clauses needed for argument because it accompanies a hand missal.
- The face–heart–peace–offering route is an editorial synthesis of the appointed formulary, not a historical claim about its compilers or any patristic author.

## Historical Coordinates of the Direct Scriptural Propers

The one-page orientation inventories six distinct directly appointed passages in Catholic canonical order: Psalm 15:7–8 (Offertory), Psalm 20:1 (Alleluia), Psalm 26:1, 4, 7, and 9 (Introit and Communion, including the Introit's appointed psalm verse), Psalm 83:9–10 (Gradual), Matthew 5:20–24 (Gospel), and 1 Peter 3:8–15 (Epistle). Psalm numbering follows the Vulgate; the common modern equivalents are Psalms 16, 21, 27, and 84. Composed orations and their loose biblical echoes were excluded. The Gospel alone receives a separate event row so that Jesus' Galilean setting is not confused with the Evangelist's traditional place of composition.

- **Psalms 15, 20, and 26:** the Greek/Latin titles assign all three to David. Psalm 15 and Psalm 20 name no life episode or writing site, so their `c. 1000 BC` entries are broad inherited Davidic orientations rather than autograph dates. Psalm 20/21 is a royal thanksgiving but does not establish whether David composed it for himself, was its royal subject, or inherited a court song. Psalm 26/27 adds “before he was anointed”; because Scripture records anointings in 1 Samuel 16 and 2 Samuel 2 and 5, `c. 1010 BC` and `Judah / Israel` remain deliberately broad. The *NABRE* does not reconstruct demonstrable authorial biographies for these poems.
- **Psalm 83:** the title assigns the song to the sons of Korah. Numbers 26:11 and 1 Chronicles 6 provide the corporate horizon of a preserved Levitical family that became sanctuary singers; no individual writer is named. `Jerusalem sanctuary` and `c. 1000 BC` describe the traditional Davidic cultic association, not a proven autograph place or date. The poem's pilgrimage form may reflect a later Temple horizon.
- **Matthew:** the traditional row follows ancient testimony that St. Matthew, apostle and former tax collector, wrote for Jewish believers in Palestine early in the apostolic mission, represented as `c. AD 40–45`. The *NABRE* records the modern majority view of an unknown final Greek author after AD 70, plausibly at Antioch. The narrated-event row cites Matthew 4:12–5:2 and 5:20–24 for an unnamed Galilean mountain in present-day northern Israel during the early public ministry, represented by the harmonized `c. AD 27`; neither the text nor tradition securely identifies the mountain or a civil year.
- **1 Peter:** Christian tradition identifies Peter as author, Rome as “Babylon,” Silvanus as his assistance or bearer, and the five provinces of Asia Minor as the audience. The `c. AD 63–64` entry follows the inherited placement shortly before Peter's Neronian martyrdom. The audience was largely Gentile and socially pressured rather than a single local congregation. The *NABRE* also presents a later Roman Petrine-school judgment, usually AD 70–90. The prose preserves both without collapsing them.

Geographic equivalents are broad: Davidic Israel and Judah belong to the southern Levant; the Korahite row's Jerusalem is a sanctuary association; Palestine and Galilee are distinct composition and event horizons; Rome is in present-day Italy; and the letter's first audience lived in Roman Asia Minor, chiefly present-day Turkey.

## Direct Reception Retained

- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 26* §§4, 7–9, direct exegesis of the appointed Introit and Communion verses: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801027.htm).
- The Venerable Bede, *Super epistolas Catholicas*, *In Primam Epistolam Petri* III at 1 Peter 3:8–17, direct Latin commentary; the variant lemma is disclosed: [text](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Super_epistolas_Catholicas).
- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 16 on Matthew* §§6, 9–12, direct exposition of the greater righteousness, anger, contempt, altar, and reconciliation: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/200116.htm).
- St. Augustine, *On the Sermon on the Mount* I.9.21–I.10.28, direct exposition of Matthew 5:20–24 and the present or absent brother: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/16011.htm).
- St. Jerome, *Letter 125 to Rusticus* §14, reception of Psalm 33's command to seek and pursue peace, quoted by 1 Peter: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001125.htm).

These witnesses expound biblical passages or their quoted psalm, not the later assembled Sunday formulary.

## Related Illumination Retained

- St. Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* I.22.20–I.29.30, on God as the unchangeable good, ordered love of self and neighbor, and particular service within universal charity: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/12021.htm).
- St. Cyprian, *On the Lord's Prayer* §8, on common Christian prayer and the concord of individual voices: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050704.htm).
- St. Augustine, *City of God* X.5–6, on true sacrifice and the whole redeemed city offered through Christ the High Priest: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120110.htm).

These sources illuminate the Collect and Secret but are not direct commentaries on the Roman orations.

## Controlling Distinctions

- Ordered love does not turn the neighbor into a disposable instrument or oppose love of God and neighbor as rival quantities.
- Matthew first directs the offerer toward a grievance another has against the offerer. The guide does not make culpability symmetrical or require unsafe contact.
- Peter's peace includes truth, departure from evil, positive good, and a clean conscience; it is not passive avoidance or moral vagueness.
- The Secret preserves distributive personal agency in *singuli* and asks for common fruit in *cunctis*; the guide does not infer that each gift has identical form or effect.
- Applying Psalm 26's one house to Eucharistic communion and joining the chants into one face–heart–peace–offering route remain editorial liturgical syntheses.

## Cultural-Reception Search

- Exact-phrase and same-psalm searches on 2026-07-10 tested Psalm 26's light, sought face, and one-house language; Psalm 83's sanctuary longing; Matthew's altar and reconciliation command; and 1 Peter's blessing and pursuit of peace across institutional mottos, literature, film, television, hymns, and classical and popular music.
- Two varied examples survived verification. The University of Oxford's arms bear *Dominus illuminatio mea*, the opening of Vulgate Psalm 26 and the Introit's appointed psalm verse; the [Bodleian University Archives](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/universityarchives/guides/university-arms) identifies the source and documents use by the later sixteenth century. Movement IV of Johannes Brahms's *Ein deutsches Requiem*, Op. 45 (1868), sets Psalm 83:1–2, 4 rather than the Gradual's verses 9–10; an [Indianapolis Symphony program note](https://www.indianapolissymphony.org/backstage/program-notes/brahms-a-german-requiem/) identifies the movement's biblical text.
- Modern devotional songs using “one thing have I desired” and generic uses of “seek peace” were reviewed but omitted because the Oxford motto and Brahms movement provide stronger primary identification and more distinct forms of reception. No verifiable ironic or humorous use of Matthew 5:23–24 survived the search.

## Survey and Material Limits

- Surveyed the public 1962 missal facsimile and focused OCR, USCCB/NABRE introductions and notes, New Advent biblical titles and *Catholic Encyclopedia* articles, accessible patristic collections in English and Latin, and the sources already supporting the legacy guide.
- No secure direct patristic commentary was located for the Collect, Secret, or Postcommunion; they are treated through verified grammar and explicitly labeled doctrinal illumination.
- Psalm authorship and dates, Matthew's composition city, the Sermon's mountain and civil year, and the precise mechanics of Silvanus' role remain uncertain.
- The survey does not exhaust manuscripts, untranslated works, subscription databases, or modern critical scholarship. No source-category quota was applied. All links were accessed 2026-07-10.
