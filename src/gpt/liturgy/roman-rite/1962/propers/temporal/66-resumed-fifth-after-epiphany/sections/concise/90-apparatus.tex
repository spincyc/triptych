\section{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}
This concise study compares the expansive study's two interpretations of
the resumed Fifth Sunday after the Epiphany in the Vatican typical edition
of the 1962 Roman Missal, General Roman Calendar, 8 November 2026. The
eleven-element appointment has no optional branches or additional
commemorative prayers here. Local calendars, the winter Fifth Sunday and
present permission to celebrate with this book are outside its scope.

The expansive study contains the collated appointed Latin, the identified
historical English study witnesses and the full historical appendix. This
companion presents their inventory and commentary. The interpretations
are editorial syntheses; each Father is credited with his own cited
exegesis. Chrysostom's permission of nonlethal suppression qualifies his
rejection of killing, and his heretics remain distinct from Augustine's
broader wicked Christians. These loci do not settle every question of
civil force or ecclesiastical discipline.

The research used historical English patristic translations, Jerome's
printed Latin and the retained Bede transcription. No new Greek collation
or Bede image collation is claimed. Bellarmine's English is abridged.
Aquinas's checked Colossians page covers 3:16--17; preceding missing
pages support no claim. The historical appendix retains traditional
attributions and qualified modern proposals. Exact evidence states,
rights and bounded research remain in \texttt{research/scope.md},
\texttt{research/interpretations.md}, \texttt{research/source-bindings.toml}
and \texttt{propers/verified.md}.

Received Latin and historical translations retain their recorded United
States public-domain bases and are excluded from the project's licence
for original commentary. The modern facsimile is a collation source;
its whole scan and modern material are not cleared for redistribution.
Historical English is a study witness, without approved proclaimed
status. Author proof checks and independent reviews are separate events;
this study claims no external or ecclesiastical approval.
