# Research boundary and source findings

Research inspected on 2 November 2026 for the General Roman Calendar Mass of
8 November 2026. The complete eleven-element appointment in context.md was
independently checked against the 1962 page images and rubrics. The permanent
identity is the resumed Fifth Sunday after Epiphany, not the winter Fifth
Sunday or the Twenty-third Sunday after Pentecost. The first supplies its
orations and lessons; the last supplies its chants by RGMR 298. The general
calendar has no additional commemorative prayers here. No local proper is

## Biblical contexts actually read

The exact Challoner/Gutenberg 1581 artifact was reopened at all of Jeremias29,
Psalms43,84,129 (Vulgate numbering), the entire Colossians letter, all of
Matthew13, and all of Mark11. The electronic witness is a composite historical
Challoner text, not an assertion about one printed edition. The appointed
English Scripture basis in verified.md is selected mechanically from that
witness. The Introit joins Jer29:11,12,14 to Ps84:2; verse13 is context, not an
appointment. Matthew13:36–43 supplies Jesus's own interpretation, without
extending the appointed Gospel past13:30. Mark11:25–26 supplies the forgiveness
condition omitted by the short Communion chant.

Jeremias29 addresses actual exiles, rejects rival promises of an immediate
return, commands their settled life and prayer for the city's peace, and
promises eventual restoration. Its good future is not an immediate exemption
from exile. Psalm43 recalls divine victories and then laments present defeat
despite fidelity: verses8–9 cannot erase the rest of the psalm. Psalm84's
opening thanksgiving still leads to petitions for restoration and to the
meeting of mercy and truth. Psalm129 is a penitential ascent from the depths
to waiting and Israel's redemption, not a victory cry detached from sin.
Colossians3:12–17 follows death to the old life and precedes concrete household
duties; charity, forbearance, peace, abundant instruction, song and thanksgiving
belong together. Matthew distinguishes the enemy's sowing from the master's
good seed and present restraint from final separation. Mark11 includes the
fig tree, the temple conflict, faith in God and forgiveness; the chant does
not promise automatic fulfilment of every unconverted desire.

### Corpus and extent controls

NPNF historical English was used for Chrysostom and Augustine, not a modern
New Advent revision. These translations are public domain in the United
States; their editorial footnotes and abridgments are not silently attributed
to the Fathers. Augustine's Psalm headings use the modern number while their
notes give the Vulgate equivalent. The accidentally inspected Enarratio128
(NPNF Psalm129, physical61955–62170) is an omitted analogue and remains in the
source-registration handoff rather than being counted as Ps129 exegesis.
Bellarmine's O'Sullivan1866 English is abridged historical reception; its
retained body was read, without a claim of new printed-page collation.

The uncapped index also returned Cassiodorus, Theodoret, Hilary, Athanasius,
Ambrose and medieval/postmedieval commentary families. Their uninspected
loci remain leads. A whole container or a title-level join supplies no
passage claim: in particular, Matthew's Catena lead joined to an in-Lucam
holding, and Colossians's broad Pauline title joined several non-Colossians
constituents. Those are not checked Matthew or Colossians sources here.
Origen's surviving Matthew commentary begins at13:36, outside the appointment;
the canonical13:36–43 context is not converted into an Origen witness at24–30.
Aquinas's Psalter extent ends at54, so a Ps84 lead cannot establish an extant
direct commentary. These are extent limits of the identified holdings, not
claims that the tradition lacks commentary.
