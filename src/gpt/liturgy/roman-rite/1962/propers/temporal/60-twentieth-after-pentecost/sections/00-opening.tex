Christ sends a frightened father home with a word. The child is elsewhere,
the father has not yet seen him healed, and the journey begins before the
confirmation arrives. The Twentieth Sunday after Pentecost places this act
of trust among words that are equally frank about need: confession in the
Introit, hunger in the Gradual, tears in the Offertory, and a plea for
heavenly medicine in the Secret. Faith does not make these needs unreal.
It receives them into a relation with the Lord who speaks, gives, heals,
and teaches his people how to live.

The Sunday also asks how thanksgiving can remain truthful in a wounded
world. Ephesians calls for song and continual thanks; the Offertory remembers
people who sit down and weep. The liturgy gives both voices to the same
assembly. Its answer cannot be perpetual cheerfulness. The promise of God
sustains a people whose present circumstances do not yet resemble their
promised home. Their praise has room for confession, lament, mutual service,
and the patient work of charity.

Two readings unfold these questions. \emph{The word heals and teaches faith}
follows the movement from the father's request to his departure, and from
the prayer for pardon to the prayer for obedient reception. Gregory explains
why the father's estimate of Christ's power must grow; Augustine explains
how a promise comforts those still under trial. \emph{Learning thanksgiving
in exile} begins with the tension between song and tears. Chrysostom's account
of thanksgiving and reciprocal service meets Augustine's account of the
pilgrim people who remember Zion beside Babylon's currents. Bellarmine's
open-handed generosity and Schuster's Eucharistic exposition deepen their
practical and sacramental consequences.

\section{The appointed movement}
\begin{maptable}
Introit & Dan.~3:31,29,35; Ps.~118:1 (119:1) & Confession appeals to mercy; the psalm verse sets the way of obedience before the people.\\
Collect & \emph{Largire} & Pardon and peace free the faithful to serve with a secure mind.\\
Maternity Collect & \emph{Deus, qui de beatae} & When commemorated, the Mother of the incarnate Word intercedes for believers.\\
Epistle & Eph.~5:15--21 & Wise walking leads through inward song and thanksgiving to mutual submission in Christ.\\
Gradual & Ps.~144:15--16 (145) & All look to the giver who opens his hand and feeds in due season.\\
Alleluia & Ps.~107:2 (108), adapted & A prepared heart answers God in praise.\\
Gospel & John~4:46b--53 & The father believes Christ's word, departs, learns of the healing, and believes with his household.\\
Offertory & Ps.~136:1 (137), adapted & Captives weep as they remember Zion.\\
Secret & \emph{Caelestem nobis} & The mysteries are asked to provide heavenly medicine and cleanse the heart of vice.\\
Maternity Secret & \emph{Tua, Domine} & When commemorated, the offering is joined to intercession for present and perpetual peace.\\
Trinity Preface & \emph{Qui cum unigenito} & Thanksgiving adores one divine essence, distinct Persons, and equal majesty.\\
Communion & Ps.~118:49--50 (119), selected & The servant seeks fulfilment of the word that gives hope and comfort in humiliation.\\
Postcommunion & \emph{Ut sacris} & The faithful ask for obedience that makes them worthy of the holy gifts.\\
Maternity Postcommunion & \emph{Haec nos communio} & When commemorated, communion cleanses and grants fellowship in heavenly remedy.\\
\end{maptable}

The three Maternity prayers follow their corresponding Sunday prayers at
Low or conventual Mass in this general-calendar occurrence. They are omitted
at a sung Mass that is not conventual. The Sunday retains the Trinity Preface
in either case. The commemoration adds no Marian lesson, Gospel, or chant.
The Gloria and Creed belong to the Mass; the customary Ordinary surrounds
the appointed elements listed here.\footnote{\work{Missale Romanum} (1962),
pp.~412--413, 685--686; \work{Rubricae generales}, nn.~91, 106--109, 111;
\work{Rubricae generales Missalis romani}, nn.~433--437, 480, 483, 494, 505.}
