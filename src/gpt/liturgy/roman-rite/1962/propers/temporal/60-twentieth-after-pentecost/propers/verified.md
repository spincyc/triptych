## Biblical wording and adaptation

The Latin transcription is the appointed Missal text. The historical English
Bible extracts in `retrieved.txt` are separately labeled. They do not silently
replace an adapted chant. Use the registered Challoner/Gutenberg 1581 witness
throughout; its number is an ebook identifier, not a sixteenth-century printing
date. It is not the American 1899 impression.

* The Introit’s printed reference is Daniel 3:31, 29 and 35. The antiphon also
  condenses ideas expressed in 3:30, 42–43; its plea to glorify the divine name
  is not verbatim verse 35. Never label a freshly translated composite as the
  received English text. Psalm 118:1 and the doxology are separate parts.
* The Epistle retains its liturgical address and starts with the injunction
  to walk wisely. The English Bible distributes the first two clauses across
  verses 15–16; this is not a different pericope.
* The Alleluia’s *tibi, gloria mea* differs from the Bible’s *with my glory*.
  Do not settle the referent merely by substituting one wording for the other.
* John’s narrative names Cana in the unappointed first part of 4:46 and
  Capharnaum in the appointed second part. The Missal begins *Erat quidam
  regulus*. A biblical context box may quote the whole verse; the appointed
  Gospel inventory must mark its partial beginning. Verse 54 is context only.
* The Offertory addresses Sion (*tui*) rather than simply naming it as an
  object of memory. Only verse 1 is appointed; the harps, coercion and violent
  closing verses are contextual evidence, never additions to the chant.
* The Communion omits the biblical clause explaining that the word gives
  life. That clause can illuminate the chant when explicitly labeled context;
  it must not be printed as part of the antiphon.

## English witnesses and conclusions

Cummiskey’s anonymous English (Philadelphia, 1861), printed 448–450, supplies
the three Sunday prayers. His Prefaces, xxviii–xxix, explicitly mark a common
opening and starred continuations; assembling those same-witness pieces is
not a newly written translation. The abbreviation “Holy, &c.” remains his.
The Preface itself ends before the Sanctus, which belongs to the Ordinary.

The three Marian prayers use Lasance and Walsh, *The New Roman Missal*, the
1945 revision in the registered later printing, pp. 1233, 1235–1236. This is
neither the 1937 first impression nor an approved English edition of the 1962
Missal. Preserve “Through the same.” Do not silently modernize it. The
Postcommunion’s identification of the heavenly remedy with Christ is the
translators’ interpretation; the Latin can also be discussed directly.

The full Latin conclusions are not expanded here. For a study quotation,
retaining the facsimile’s printed abbreviation is honest. The Maternity
Collect’s longer printed ending is likewise retained as printed. Introit
repetition and its full doxology are ritual performance governed by RGMR
427–429, not missing antiphon words reconstructed in this record.

class. RG 91’s precedence table puts the second-class Sunday ahead of this
