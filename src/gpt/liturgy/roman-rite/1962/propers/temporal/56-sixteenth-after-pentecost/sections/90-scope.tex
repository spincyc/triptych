\sectionguard\section*{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}
This study concerns the \textit{Missale Romanum}, Vatican typical edition of 1962, \latin{Dominica decima sexta post Pentecosten}, II class, printed pp.~397--398, nos.~1592--1601. The formulary orders the Creed and Trinity Preface; civil-date occurrence, commemorations and particular calendars belong to Mass assembly. No non-Eucharistic ritual-sacrament appendix applies.

The source audit of 5 September 2026 collated all ten Latin elements and their boundaries against the controlling page images, with the public-domain Pustet 1862 printing as antecedent. No unresolved Latin reading remains. The full edition reproduces the complete Latin at the source's abbreviated length and quotes Challoner Scripture and Cummiskey's 1861 orations as distinct historical English witnesses. Biblical English is not an exact rendering of each liturgical adaptation. Cummiskey is an anonymous pre-1955 lay-book translation, not an approved English text of the 1962 typica. The synthesis edition omits the full appointed texts and the element-by-element exposition and supplies an integrated commentary; both retain the complete synthesis, gallery and exploratory proposals.

The direct reception includes Latin and Greek patristic traditions, medieval Doctors, later saints and liturgical commentators at the cited loci. Augustine's NPNF Psalm volume contains abridgments; its English section numbers are retained. Cyril is read in Payne Smith's English from the Syriac; Theodoret's detailed reading follows the paired Latin with Greek cross-checks. Digital Latin editions of Ambrose, Aquinas and Augustine's Letter~140 are not claimed as new critical collations. The ancient sacramentaries supply an oration pair, triad and separate chant group, not a first-use date or named compiler for the complete formulary. No such compiler or complete-set first use was established within the checked Wilson editions, Schuster passages and \textit{Liturgical Year} continuation chapter.

Biblical dates come solely from the Scripture chronology corpus. It supplies no date for the Gospel event. Its Psalter-wide composition boundary does not securely date any individual psalm. Exact composition geography and a full modern authorship discussion of Luke and Ephesians are not supplied by the consulted material; page~2 states those bounds. The narrative settings, traditional composition alternatives and modern chapter-level textual qualifications have their own distinct sources.

The cultural gallery's five entries were checked at their cited texts and loci. Phrase searches for the other scriptural elements located no qualifying redirection within the recorded English searches, not throughout all literature. Proposal comparisons covered the specified English and Latin proper-study snapshot, Anthony's two sermons and the continuation's Sixteenth-Sunday chapter. Their findings establish bounded precedents or analogues, not universal novelty. The complete reception matrix, searches, discarded leads, citation qualifications and proposal and gallery audits remain in \texttt{research/scope.md}; appointed-text provenance is in \texttt{propers/verified.md}, with the uncorrected finding aid in \texttt{propers/retrieved.txt}.

The Latin's publication basis is its independently witnessed public-domain antecedent; neither the complete 1962 facsimile nor modern host presentation is declared public domain. Challoner and Cummiskey have separate United States public-domain bases. Short cultural quotations retain the named editions; modern commentary and host material retain their own rights. Source collation and research checks are recorded; they confer no ecclesiastical approval. This is a study companion, not an official liturgical book or substitute for the cited authorities.
