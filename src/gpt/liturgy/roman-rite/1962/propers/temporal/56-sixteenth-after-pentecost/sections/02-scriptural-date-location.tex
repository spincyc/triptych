\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}
\begin{dossiertable}
\dossierentry{Offertory}{}{Ps.~39 (40):14--15, selected}{An endangered petitioner amid public thanksgiving.}{\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}}{The whole psalm moves from rescue and obedience back to threatened life; no particular writing-place or enemy is identified. The USCCB Psalms introduction supplies a shared latest composition boundary, not a secure date for this psalm. \sourcecite{Ps.~39:1--18; USCCB, Psalms introduction.}}
\dossierseparator
\dossierentry{Communion}{}{Ps.~70 (71):16--18, selected}{An aging speaker; Davidic and captivity settings remain distinct.}{\chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}}{Youth, failing strength and future hearers frame the prayer. Bellarmine reads David's testimony; no exact composition place is established. The common critical boundary does not date the individual psalm; the alternate A.M. figure belongs to the superscription discussion, not the preferred historical setting. \sourcecite{Ps.~70:1--24; Bellarmine, pp.~212--213; chronology sources in References.}}
\dossierseparator
\dossierentry{Introit}{}{Ps.~85 (86):3,5; v.~1}{Davidic title; a needy servant threatened by attackers.}{\chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}}{The title supplies the received attribution; the psalm names no particular Davidic crisis or writing-place. Its audience of prayer extends toward the nations' worship. Individual composition remains insecure within the common critical boundary. \sourcecite{Ps.~85:1--17; USCCB, Psalms introduction.}}
\dossierseparator
\dossierentry{Alleluia}{}{Ps.~97 (98):1, opening}{Israel's salvation before the nations and creation.}{\chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}}{No particular rescue or composition place is named. The critical boundary is common to the Psalter. The two traditional alternatives concern the Nativity as prophetic referent, not the writing of the psalm. \sourcecite{Ps.~97:1--9; USCCB, Psalms introduction; \textit{Catholic Encyclopedia}, ``Christmas'' and ``Chronology of the Life of Jesus Christ.''}}
\dossierseparator
\dossierentry{Gradual}{}{Ps.~101 (102):16--17}{Sion/Jerusalem restored; peoples gathered for worship.}{\chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}}{An afflicted speaker contrasts his frailty with God's permanence. Jerusalem is the subject, not a proved writing-location. Neither the restoration nor the psalm has an individual date established here. \sourcecite{Ps.~101:1--29; USCCB, Psalms introduction.}}
\dossierseparator
\dossierevententry{Gospel}{}{Luke~14:1--11}{St Luke's Gospel; an unnamed leading Pharisee's house.}{\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}}{Event: Sabbath meal and cure in the journey-to-Jerusalem context (Luke~13:22; 14:1--11); town and host unnamed. The wedding is the seating parable's setting.}{Traditional composition alternatives are those of the \textit{Catholic Encyclopedia} and the Biblical Commission. Composition geography, detailed first-audience geography and a full modern authorship discussion are not established in the consulted material. The modern USCCB chapter note reports son/ox beside the ass/ox variant; the Missal retains ass/ox.}
\dossierseparator
\dossierentry{Epistle}{}{Eph.~3:13--21}{St Paul, imprisoned for his Gentile mission; saints sharing one inheritance.}{\chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}}{The apostle's affliction and the Gentile recipients frame his prayer (Eph.~2--4). The traditional alternatives come from the \textit{Catholic Encyclopedia}. Prison city, destination geography and a full modern authorship account are not established in the consulted introductions; the USCCB chapter notes treat the prayer's wording and interpretation.}
\end{dossiertable}
