## Complete appointed Latin

Psalm references give the Missal's Vulgate number first and the common modern
psalm number in parentheses. Verse numbers in the headings remain the
Missal's; see the reference table below for modern English verse offsets.

### 1. Introit — Ps. 85 (86):3 and 5; verse 1 — no. 1592

Miserére mihi, Dómine, quóniam ad te clamávi tota die: quia tu, Dómine,
suávis ac mitis es, et copiósus in misericórdia ómnibus invocántibus te.
Ps. ibid., 1 Inclína, Dómine, aurem tuam mihi, et exáudi me: quóniam inops
et pauper sum ego. ℣. Glória Patri.

### 2. Collect — no. 1593

Tua nos, quǽsumus, Dómine, grátia semper et prævéniat et sequátur: ac
bonis opéribus iúgiter præstet esse inténtos. Per Dóminum.

### 3. Epistle — Eph. 3:13–21 — no. 1594

Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Ephésios.

Fratres: Obsecro vos, ne deficiátis in tribulatiónibus meis pro vobis:
quæ est glória vestra. Huius rei grátia flecto génua mea ad Patrem Dómini
nostri Iesu Christi, ex quo omnis patérnitas in cælis et in terra nominátur,
ut det vobis secúndum divítias glóriæ suæ, virtúte corroborári per Spíritum
eius in interiórem hóminem, Christum habitáre per fidem in córdibus vestris:
in caritáte radicáti, et fundáti, ut possítis comprehéndere cum ómnibus
sanctis, quæ sit latitúdo, et longitúdo, et sublímitas, et profúndum: scire
étiam supereminéntem sciéntiæ caritátem Christi, ut impleámini in omnem
plenitúdinem Dei. Ei autem, qui potens est ómnia fácere superabundánter
quam pétimus, aut intellégimus, secúndum virtútem, quæ operátur in nobis:
ipsi glória in Ecclésia, et in Christo Iesu, in omnes generatiónes sǽculi
sæculórum. Amen.

### 4. Gradual — Ps. 101 (102):16–17 — no. 1595

Timébunt gentes nomen tuum, Dómine, et omnes reges terræ glóriam tuam.
℣. Quóniam ædificávit Dóminus Sion, et vidébitur in maiestáte sua.

### 5. Alleluia — Ps. 97 (98):1 — no. 1596

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Cantáte Dómino cánticum novum: quia mirabília fecit
Dóminus. Allelúia.

### 6. Gospel — Luke 14:1–11 — no. 1597

✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Lucam.

In illo témpore: Cum intráret Iesus in domum cuiúsdam príncipis
pharisæórum sábbato manducáre panem, et ipsi observábant eum. Et ecce
homo quidam hydrópicus erat ante illum. Et respóndens Iesus dixit ad
legisperítos et pharisǽos, dicens: Si licet sábbato curáre? At illi
tacuérunt. Ipse vero apprehénsum sanávit eum, ac dimísit. Et respóndens
ad illos, dixit: Cuius vestrum ásinus, aut bos in púteum cadet, et non
contínuo éxtrahet illum die sábbati? Et non póterant ad hæc respondére
illi. Dicébat autem et ad invitátos parábolam, inténdens quómodo primos
accúbitus elígerent, dicens ad illos: Cum invitátus fúeris ad núptias, non
discúmbas in primo loco, ne forte honorátior te sit invitátus ab illo, et
véniens is qui te et illum vocávit, dicat tibi: Da huic locum: et tunc
incípias cum rubóre novíssimum locum tenére. Sed cum vocátus fúeris,
vade, recúmbe in novíssimo loco: ut, cum vénerit qui te invitávit, dicat
tibi: Amíce, ascénde supérius. Tunc erit tibi glória coram simul
discumbéntibus: quia omnis, qui se exáltat, humiliábitur: et qui se
humíliat, exaltábitur.

Credo.

### 7. Offertory — Ps. 39 (40):14 and 15 — no. 1598

Dómine, in auxílium meum réspice: confundántur et revereántur, qui
quærunt ánimam meam, ut áuferant eam: Dómine, in auxílium meum réspice.

### 8. Secret — no. 1599

Munda nos, quǽsumus, Dómine, sacrifícii præséntis efféctu: et pérfice
miserátus in nobis; ut eius mereámur esse partícipes. Per Dóminum.

Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.

### 9. Communion — Ps. 70 (71):16–17 and 18 — no. 1600

Dómine, memorábor iustítiæ tuæ solíus: Deus, docuísti me a iuventúte mea:
et usque in senéctam et sénium, Deus, ne derelínquas me.

### 10. Postcommunion — no. 1601

Purífica, quǽsumus, Dómine, mentes nostras benígnus, et rénova cæléstibus
sacraméntis: ut consequénter et córporum præsens páriter et futúrum
capiámus auxílium. Per Dóminum.

## Textual differences and boundaries

The 1862 page images establish the same Latin words throughout the ten
elements, with older i/j and cœ-/cæ- spelling, accent placement,
capitalization, and punctuation. In particular, 1862 prints `intelligimus`
where the controlling 1962 Epistle prints `intellegimus`; its Secret uses
commas at the positions where 1962 prints a colon and semicolon. Its
pre-1960 second and third orations are separate apparatus and are not
appointed here. It does not print the explicit Trinity-Preface pointer
between this Secret and Communion. The 1962 page controls the transcription
and rubrical facts; 1862 supplies the antecedent wording, not the 1962
rubrical system.

All three proper orations end `Per Dóminum.` in the controlling formulary.
The source abbreviations `Glória Patri.`, `Credo.`, and the Trinity-Preface
pointer are preserved at their printed length. No expanded conclusion,
doxology, Creed, or Preface is supplied from memory.

The registered Cummiskey Collect passage's historical note gives CMAA
marginal no. 1575. The page image establishes no. **1593** for this Collect;
that older metadata locator must not be reused. Cummiskey's p. 431 running
head reads `XVII. SUNDAY AFTER PENTECOST` while continuing the Sixteenth
Sunday. The Gospel ending, Secret, Communion, Postcommunion, and following
Seventeenth Sunday heading fix the correspondence independently of that
running head. Neither discrepancy leaves the appointed text uncertain.

The selected biblical phrases are not always identical to the registered
Clementine Bible. These are liturgical readings, not OCR corrections:

| Element | Relation to the biblical locus |
| --- | --- |
| Introit | The Missal has `mihi` for the Clementine's `mei` in v. 3, recasts v. 5 as `quia tu ... suavis ac mitis es, et copiosus in misericordia`, and adds `mihi` after `aurem tuam` in v. 1. The psalm inscription is not appointed. |
| Epistle | `Fratres: Obsecro vos` takes the place of the Clementine's `Propter quod peto` at v. 13. The reading continues through the doxology and `Amen` in v. 21. |
| Gradual | The Missal omits the initial `Et` of v. 16 and has `Quoniam` and `in maiestate sua` where the Clementine has `quia` and `in gloria sua`. |
| Alleluia | The verse selects the opening sentence of Ps. 97:1 and explicitly adds `Dominus` after `fecit`; the remainder about God's right hand and holy arm is not appointed. The Alleluia repetitions are liturgical framing. |
| Gospel | The Missal replaces `Et factum est` with `In illo tempore`; it ends at v. 11. The address to the host and invitation of the poor in vv. 12–14 are subsequent context, not appointed Gospel. |
| Offertory | `Domine, in auxilium meum respice` corresponds to the second clause of v. 14, whose Clementine form is `Domine, ad adjuvandum me respice`. The chant selects v. 15 through `ut auferant eam`, omits `simul`, and repeats its opening appeal. It omits both `Complaceat tibi ...` and the remainder of v. 15. |
| Communion | The chant joins the final clause of v. 16, the opening clause of v. 17, and v. 18 through `ne derelinquas me`. The surrounding declarations of entering God's powers and proclaiming his works to the coming generation remain biblical context. |

For common modern English numbering, the source-library concordance maps
Vulgate Ps. 39:14–15 to Ps. 40:13–14 and Ps. 101:16–17 to Ps. 102:15–16.
The other selected psalms retain the same verse numbers: 70/71, 85/86,
and 97/98. A changed psalm number alone must not imply unchanged verse
numbering for Psalms 39 and 101.

## English source controls

Scriptural English is quoted unchanged from the registered
Douay–Rheims/Challoner edition `challoner-gutenberg-1581`; `1581` in that
identifier is the Gutenberg eBook number, not the Bible's publication year.
The registered Psalm, Ephesians, and Luke verse artifacts control these
quotations. A canonical English verse must not be relabeled as an exact
translation of a differing liturgical Latin form. English additions,
refrains, or rewritten wording have not been composed for the chants.

### Scriptural loci

The following quotations retain the source's words and punctuation; the
psalm inscriptions are omitted from the verse-1 excerpts. Bracketed verse
labels identify separate source quotations and are not part of their text.

- **Introit:** [85:3] “Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I have cried to thee
  all the day.” [85:5] “For thou, O Lord, art sweet and mild: and plenteous
  in mercy to all that call upon thee.” [85:1] “Incline thy ear, O Lord,
  and hear me: for I am needy and poor.”
- **Gradual:** [101:16] “And the Gentiles shall fear thy name, O Lord,
  and all the kings of the earth thy glory.” [101:17] “For the Lord hath
  built up Sion: and he shall be seen in his glory.”
- **Alleluia locus:** [97:1, opening sentence after the inscription]
  “Sing ye to the Lord a new canticle: because he hath done wonderful
  things.”
- **Offertory locus:** [39:14] “Be pleased, O Lord, to deliver me: look
  down, O Lord, to help me.” [39:15] “Let them be confounded and ashamed
  together, that seek after my soul to take it away. Let them be turned
  backward and be ashamed that desire evils to me.” These full verses are
  a comparison control; the chant's shorter selections are listed above.
- **Communion locus:** [70:16] “I will enter into the powers of the Lord:
  O Lord, I will be mindful of thy justice alone.” [70:17] “Thou hast
  taught me, O God, from my youth: and till now I will declare thy
  wonderful works.” [70:18] “And unto old age and grey hairs: O God,
  forsake me not, Until I shew forth thy arm to all the generation that
  is to come: Thy power,” The full verses are retained here as a comparison
  control, not as an expanded Communion antiphon.

The **Epistle** is controlled by Ephesians 3:13–21, physical TSV lines
59–67. Its exact opening is “Wherefore I pray you not to faint at my
tribulations for you, which is your glory.” Its closing verse is “To him
be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus, unto all generations, world
without end. Amen.” The whole nine-verse sequence was read and may be
quoted directly from the registered artifact; a new “Brethren” incipit
must not be attributed to this Scripture witness.

The **Gospel** is controlled by Luke 14:1–11, physical TSV lines 662–672.
Its exact opening is “And it came to pass, when Jesus went into the house
of one of the chief of the Pharisees, on the sabbath day, to eat bread,
that they watched him.” Its closing verse is “Because every one that
exalteth himself shall be humbled: and he that humbleth himself shall be
exalted.” The whole eleven-verse sequence was read and may be quoted
directly from the registered artifact. “At that time” belongs to the
Missal's frame and is not added to Challoner's text.

### Orations — Cummiskey's Sixteenth Sunday after Pentecost

The following English is that of the registered public-domain hand missal,
cited by its formulary. Its abbreviated conclusions are retained. These
are historical English translations for study, not an approved 1962
liturgical translation.

**Collect:** May thy grace, O Lord, always prevent and follow us; and make
us constantly zealous in the practice of good works. Thro’.

**Secret:** Cleanse us, O Lord, we beseech thee, by the efficacy of this
sacrifice: and by thy mercy make us worthy to partake thereof. Thro’.

**Postcommunion:** Mercifully, O Lord, we beseech thee, purify our souls,
and renew them by these holy mysteries; that we may receive help thereby,
both while we are in these mortal bodies and hereafter. Thro’.

The Challoner Scripture and Cummiskey 1861 orations have separate
registered United States public-domain bases. The Google front matter,

raw source extract, is excluded from Triptych's CC BY 4.0 grant; original

no unresolved Latin reading remains. English source selections follow the
repository's rules: Challoner for Scripture, the registered Cummiskey hand
