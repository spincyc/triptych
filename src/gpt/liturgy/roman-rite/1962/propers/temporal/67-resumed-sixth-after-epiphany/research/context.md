Its body begins with thanksgiving and ends at the conclusion of verse 10.

**1 Thess. 1, 2–10** and prints the same appointed body. The latter citation,
the body and the dated Ordo agree. Record the erroneous resumed heading in
`propers/verified.md`; do not reproduce it as the biblical boundary or invent
a longer lesson. The registered Douay–Rheims/Challoner verse artifact places
“Grace be to you and peace” at the start of 1:2 before the thanksgiving;
that greeting is absent from the appointed Latin. The English source plan
must distinguish the exact selected biblical words from a complete-verse
quotation and must not generate a replacement liturgical introduction.

The winter Collect on p. 48, no. 337 has an extended conclusion prefix;
the resumed Collect on p. 421, no. 1760 prints the short cue. The winter
Postcommunion on p. 49, no. 345 has *Per Dominum nostrum*; the resumed
Postcommunion prints *Per Dominum*. The existing winter-prayer source records
therefore cannot certify literal equality of the entire resumed unit,
including its ending, merely because the petitions agree. Research must
collate the resumed targets and identify any expansion from the governing
book's own conclusion rule and received formula.

The running head on p. 421 names the resumed Sixth Sunday, but the top-left
Gospel and top-right Postcommunion still finish the Fifth Sunday. Conversely,
the running head on p. 422 names the Last Sunday, while the Sixth Sunday's
texts occupy most of that page. Marginal nos. 1759–1768 and the actual headings,
not running heads alone, delimit the target.
