# Proposed interpretations: resumed Sixth Sunday after Epiphany

The appointment is the complete 1962 resumed formulary, eleven elements including the Trinity Preface, for 15 November 2026. These are two defensible readings for the study, not two theories of how the Missal was compiled. Attribution belongs to each checked native locus below; the connection across the Mass is editorial source-grounded synthesis. No Father's verse commentary is represented as a commentary on this complete formulary. Both readings must be developed in the expansive study; the concise study should compare their answers at the points of difficulty. The homily can follow reading A's one argument: receiving Christ's word in difficulty makes a community capable of passing it on.

## Reading A — The word received in trial becomes a witness to others

**Controlling claim.** The Gospel's small beginnings are not a defect to be repaired by abandoning contact with the world. Christ gives the word power to work through people who receive it in faith, charity and hope; the Thessalonians show what that can look like. The Mass forms such a people by promise, prayer, sacrifice, confession of God, and nourishment. Its confidence looks toward the risen Son's return and does not measure success by freedom from suffering.

**Principal compatible witnesses and argument.** Saint John Chrysostom, Matthew46.2–3, notices the discouraging narrative context of failed seed and hostile tares. He therefore takes mustard and leaven as Christ's answer to fear about a few weak disciples. Leaven cannot transform what it never touches. He then challenges listeners who admire apostolic miracles while refusing apostolic virtue: ordinary Christian conduct matters. Saint Thomas Aquinas, 1Thessalonians1 lectio1 [87862], joins both work and labor to faith, then treats the Thessalonians' abundant works of charity and the endurance sustained by hope. He grounds election in God's action rather than a claim of deserved privilege, and makes the converts' exemplary life the reason their faith is reported elsewhere. He closes with the two future expectations of resurrection and freedom from punishment. These are distinct substantive contributions: Chrysostom interprets the parable's mechanism and moral demand; Aquinas explains the Epistle's evidence of it and its future end. Chrysostom's Thessalonian homilies reinforce the compatibility. Blessed Ildefonso Schuster's actual Sixth-Sunday commentary, volumeI416–418, supplies a later liturgical reading of this Epistle/Gospel conjunction: divine action through unlikely means, thanksgiving for the spread of faith, and the sacrifice's cleansing and protection. His five-chant commentary comes from a different chapter, volumeIII186–189, and is used only where its chants agree.

| Trinity Preface | The living and true God served in the Epistle is confessed with the Son and Holy Spirit, one God in distinct persons, and thanked with the heavenly worshippers. Mission is ordered to worship. |
| Communion | Confidence in God's hearing nourishes action; Mark's accompanying command to forgive makes such confidence inseparable from charity. |
| Postcommunion | Nourishment should produce a lasting appetite for true life, sustaining faithful conduct beyond the moment of worship. |

**Four senses, internal to A.** Literal: Jesus describes the kingdom through growth and permeation; Paul thanks God for the community's actual reception, conduct and witness; the prayers ask God to act in worshippers. Allegorical: the Church grows by Christ's power, and her worship confesses the Father, Son and Spirit; the victory is the work of the risen Son, not the triumph of an autonomous organization. Moral: receive the word, let faith issue in work and charity in costly service, forgive, pray, and let ordinary conduct make the word credible to others. Anagogical: wait for the Son from heaven and the completion of deliverance; hope's future is resurrection and the praise that lasts for ever, not a promised chronology of earthly expansion.

**Limits and comparison.** “The whole” is not a declaration that every person will inevitably be saved: Matthew's same discourse contains judgment and the tares' separation. Nor does leaven authorize coercion. Political claims in SchusterI417–418 and unverified historical generalizations about the catacombs, Celsus or apostolic miracles are not premises here. Unlike B, A does not need a symbolic correspondence for each of the three measures. Its distinctive inference is from contact and disproportion to persevering communal witness.

## Reading B — The Gospel reforms the whole person and teaches desire

**Controlling claim.** Growth in the kingdom involves the reordering of the person who hears Christ: understanding, bodily action, anger and desire must be brought into agreement. The Mass's movement from meditation and crying for mercy through sacrifice to renewed hunger shows such a transformation being asked of God. The inward reading remains ecclesial and active because the Epistle presents transformed persons as a pattern for others.

**Principal compatible witnesses and argument.** Saint Jerome, *Commentary on Matthew*II on13:31–35, PL26 cols89–94, first reads the seed as the apparently slight preaching of a crucified God; when received in a believing soul it grows into life-giving understanding. At13:33 his leaven is the understanding of Scripture communicated through the Church. He gives spirit/soul/body as one account of three measures and reason/anger/desire as another: reason should attain prudence, anger turn against vice, desire pursue virtue. These are Jerome's offered interpretations, not a biological division asserted by the study. Saint Augustine, *Enarratio*129.1–8, contributes the penitential and eschatological direction. The voice from the depths is not a performance earning God's proximity; the prayer is already a sign of release from mute despair. Christ's propitiatory sacrifice makes hope possible, and the morning watch leads to Christ's resurrection and the believer's future resurrection. Their compatibility does not rest on matching the word “hidden”: Jerome describes the Gospel's comprehensive effect, Augustine specifies how sinful persons pray and what they rightly hope to receive. Saint Robert Bellarmine's Psalm129 exposition adds the balance between remorse and trust; his Psalm84 explanation1 distinguishes present partial release from its completion in resurrection. Bede's Mark commentary supplies the practical test of forgiving others.

**Contribution of every appointed element.**

| Element | Contribution to B |
| --- | --- |
| Introit, including Psalm84 verse | Gathering from captivity becomes, in the checked spiritual reception, release from bondage to sin; literal exilic restoration remains its historical foundation. God initiates peace and calls the whole heart to seek him. |
| Collect | The movement from pondering *rationabilia* to pleasing words and deeds directly resists a merely intellectual or devotional change. Jerome's reformed faculties illuminate this request without becoming its historical source. |
| Epistle | Conversion from idols to the living God tests whether understanding has changed allegiance. Faith, charity and hope transform conduct and its relation to neighbors; waiting for the Son prevents making the self the final object. |
| Gradual | Praise displaces trust in one's own strength. Its continuing-tribulation context stops interior renewal being mistaken for emotional invulnerability. |
| Alleluia | Prayer names the real depth of need. Augustine locates the worst depth in ceasing to seek God, so the cry itself has a hopeful direction. |
| Gospel | The Gospel's leaven penetrates spirit, soul and body, or reason, anger and desire, in Jerome's offered reading. The parables also summon the reader to inquire into Scripture. |
| Offertory | Repeating the cry while offering gifts makes humility part of the action. The gifts are God's own, and the giver remains in need of mercy. |
| Secret | The four requested effects—cleanse, renew, govern, protect—give liturgical concreteness to the comprehensive change the Gospel's image suggests. |
| Trinity Preface | The terminus of purified understanding is worship of the revealed God. Distinct persons and one essence must be confessed from the Preface's own words, not deduced from the dough. |
| Communion | Asking in faith includes forgiving. Bede's surrounding exposition and Mark's context connect confidence with charity and the petition for eternal life, not mere intensity of desire. |
| Postcommunion | Being fed leads to asking for an enduring appetite for the very gifts that give true life. Desire is educated by the gift received rather than exhausted by consumption. |

**Four senses, internal to B.** Literal: Jesus compares the kingdom to leaven working through meal; the Collect joins reflection to speech and action; the Secret names four effects; the Postcommunion asks those already fed to desire life-giving gifts continually. Allegorical: the Gospel teaches through the Church and Christ's sacrifice grounds the sinner's confidence; the Church offers praise to the one God in Trinity. Moral: let scriptural understanding govern action, turn anger against vice rather than the neighbor, cultivate virtuous desire, pray honestly and forgive. Anagogical: Augustine's morning watch and the Epistle's coming Son direct renewed desire to resurrection and complete redemption, while Bellarmine distinguishes partial present liberty from the final freedom of God's children.

**Limits and comparison.** This is not private self-improvement detached from the assembly, neighbors or sacrifice. Jerome's anthropological correspondences are offered spiritual readings, not the only literal meaning of *three*. His warning against using uncertain parables to establish dogma must survive the comparison with the Trinity Preface. Augustine's interpretation of the whole Psalm extends beyond the two words/verses appointed, and must be introduced as fuller Psalm context. Unlike A, B's distinctive inference runs from pervasive leaven to comprehensive human conversion and from repeated prayer to purified desire. The two readings can illuminate one another without collapsing: growth outward and transformation inward are related effects with different exegetical starting points.

## Drafting constraints and useful comparison points

Open the study with the concrete inventory and ritual movement rather than the commentators' names. Develop the authors' reasoning at the relevant textual questions. Compare the three measures, joy under affliction, the completed/pending rescue, and confident prayer before presenting the two whole-Mass readings. Keep the historic source of the resumed chants separate from the source of the own readings and orations; that verified liturgical arrangement warrants their joint reading but does not prove a compiler selected them to encode either interpretation.

Brief quotations can be selected from verified public-domain Chrysostom, Augustine, Jerome Latin and historical Bellarmine. Aquinas's modern Corpus Thomisticum delivery supports attributed paraphrase; no new English rendering is offered as a historical quotation. Schuster's1924 and1927 printed wording is public domain, but its claims about early authors and political history remain secondary leads unless their originals are independently checked. Neither a memorable line nor a repository catalogue entry substitutes for the argument established above.
