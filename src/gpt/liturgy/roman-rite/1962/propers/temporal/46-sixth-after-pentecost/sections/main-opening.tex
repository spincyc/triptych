\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Dominus fortitudo plebis suae} (Ps. 27:8--9; verse 1)}{Strength for God's people}{The worshipper cries to God not to be silent, then confesses him as the people's strength and saving protector.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Deus virtutum}: increase religion; nourish and guard the good}{Growth in grace}{The new life requires God both to implant love of his name and preserve what grace begins.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:3--11}{Buried with Christ; newness of life}{Baptism gives participation in Christ's death and resurrection, not advice for renovating the old man.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Convertere, Domine}; \latin{Domine, refugium factus es nobis} (Ps. 89:13, 1)}{The Lord's return and refuge}{Mortal servants ask the Lord to return and confess him as the refuge of every generation.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{In te, Domine, speravi} (Ps. 30:2--3)}{Trust and deliverance}{Threatened trust asks God to incline his ear and hasten deliverance.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Mark 8:1--9}{Christ feeds the four thousand}{Christ's compassion supplies a people who cannot sustain themselves on the way.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Perfice gressus meos} (Ps. 16:5--7)}{Steady steps in God's paths}{The psalmist asks God to steady his steps in the divine paths, hear him, and display saving mercy.}
\properrow{Secret}{\latin{Propitiare, Domine, supplicationibus nostris ... nullius sit irritum votum, nullius vacua postulatio ... quod fideliter petimus, efficaciter consequamur}}{Faithful prayer made effective}{God is asked to receive his people's oblations, let no vow or petition be empty, and bring faithful asking to effective fulfillment.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Circumibo, et immolabo} (Ps. 26:6)}{Sacrifice of jubilation}{The psalmist sacrifices jubilation in God's tabernacle, sings, and praises the Lord.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Repleti sumus, Domine, muneribus tuis}}{Cleansing and aid}{God's gifts are asked to cleanse by their effect and fortify by their help.}
\end{properstable}
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Romans 6 says that the baptized have been buried with Christ and must reckon themselves dead to sin; Mark 8 recounts Christ's compassionate feeding of a hungry multitude.\\
Allegorical & Baptism joins the Church to Christ's paschal death and life. Bede and Augustine read broken bread as opened Scripture, apostolic distribution, the Spirit's fullness, and the Church.\\
Moral & The Christian must refuse the old servitude, walk in the grace already given, confess dependence, and receive rather than pretend to be self-sustaining.\\
Anagogical & Romans joins present newness of life to future life with Christ, while the Postcommunion asks that received gifts fortify the pilgrim Church under the risen Lord, over whom death no longer has dominion.\\
\end{sensestable}
