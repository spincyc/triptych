# Secret redevelopment record

## Controlling text and syntax

The 1962 text is:

> *Tibi, Domine, sacrificia dicata reddantur: quae sic ad honorem nominis tui
> deferenda tribuisti, ut eadem remedia fieri nostra praestares. Per Dominum.*

The syntax places `sacrificia dicata` as the subject of `reddantur`, addresses
the offering to God with `tibi`, and makes God the subject of `tribuisti` and
the implied subject of `praestares`. It therefore supports divine
authorization of the offering and divine efficacy in making the gifts
remedies. It does not by itself state *ex opere operato*, automatic fruit,
the precise character of the remedy, remission or propitiation, the required
dispositions, or a complete theory of sacrifice. `Reddantur` means that the
sacrifices are rendered or offered; language of acceptance belongs to the
Communion's `acceptabis`, not to this prayer.

## Direct later reception

Nikolaus Gihr, *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass: Dogmatically, Liturgically,
and Ascetically Explained*, 2nd ed. (St. Louis: B. Herder, 1902), p. 134 n. 2,
quotes the complete Secret in section 17, “The Value of the Eucharistic
Sacrifice, as also the Reason and the Manner of its Efficacy.” Gihr's main text
distinguishes the Godward fruits of praise and thanksgiving from the graces
and blessings obtained through propitiation and petition. The note adduces
this prayer for its movement from the honor of God's name to remedy for the
offerers.

This is direct theological reception or use of the Secret and corrects the
former claim that no later theological reception was located. Gihr does not
parse the prayer, however, and his surrounding fourfold account cannot be
presented as four categories expressly stated by the Secret.

## Transmission

The Old Gelasian, Book III, section VI, “ITEM ALIA MISSA,” transmits this
Secret in the same set as the Collect and Postcommunion (H. A. Wilson, ed.,
*The Gelasian Sacramentary*, pp. 227–228). The Secret and Postcommunion are
materially exact apart from ordinary editorial orthography; the Collect has
`gratiam` where the 1962 Missal has `misericordiam`.

The later Gregorian supplement, SupG 1159–1161, transmits the same set. The
Gellone witness places it under “DOMINICA XI POST OCTABAS PENTECOSTEN,” an
historically offset heading that must not be equated with the modern Eleventh
Sunday after Pentecost. These witnesses establish transmission, not personal
authorship by Gelasius or Gregory, a composition date, or the cause of the
1962 placement.
