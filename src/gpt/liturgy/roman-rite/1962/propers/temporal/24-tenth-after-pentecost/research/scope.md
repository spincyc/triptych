# Tenth Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

## Identity, Question, and Evidence Boundary

This audit governs stable temporal ID `24`, *Dominica Decima post
Pentecosten*, II class, in the 1962 *Missale Romanum*. It asks how all ten
appointed elements work in their scriptural contexts and checked reception
without claiming an official interpretation, a recovered compiler's intent,
or exhaustive coverage of all witnesses.

The controlling Latin is the checksum-matched CMAA facsimile, printed pp.
389–390/PDF pages 470–471. Internet Archive OCR located the material but
controls no reading. Complete English uses registered public-domain
Douay–Rheims/Challoner verse rows and the visually checked 1861 Cummiskey
orations. The old English remains a historical witness, not official 1962
English.

Evidence classes remain separate:

- appointed wording and canonical context are textual observations;
- dates, transmission, and source geography are documented historical
  orientation;
- named exegesis and later liturgical or cultural uses are documented
  reception;
- cross-proper conclusions supported by those classes are source-grounded
  synthesis;
- the six connections in the interpretive section are expressly exploratory.

## Formulary and Transmission Audit

The page images establish marginal items 1532–1541 in the order Introit,
Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Alleluia, Gospel with Credo, Offertory, Secret with
Trinity Preface, Communion, and Postcommunion. OCR column flow is especially
unsafe at the p. 389 boundary because the Ninth Sunday's ending occupies the
same page. No seasonal substitution or additional proper is printed.

The Collect appears in the Old Gelasian, Book III, section VI, `Item alia
missa`, with `gratiam` rather than the 1962 `misericordiam`. It later appears
as SupG 1159–1161 under an offset post-octave Sunday heading. That numbering
is reported through checked transmission records; Deshusses's protected
edition was not directly inspected. `SupG` is a
supplement to the Gregorian/Hadrianum, not its core. These witnesses establish
transmission and variant wording; they do not establish personal authorship by
Gelasius or Gregory, composition of all ten elements as a set, or the reason
for the 1962 Sunday placement. The Trinity Preface has an old Gelasian ancestor
in a Pentecost-octave formulary; that fact likewise does not make its original
title “Trinity” or prove one author's hand.

## Corpora and Languages Searched

The search covered the Latin and Greek appointed forms; the complete biblical
contexts in Latin, public-domain English, and modern Catholic orientation;
New Advent/NPNF Fathers; Augustinus.it; PG/PL scans and indexed Latin
transcriptions; Aquinas's *Corpus Thomisticum* and Aquinas.cc; Theodoret,
Cassiodorus, Ambrosiaster, Basil, Chrysostom, Cyril, Tertullian, Anthony of
Padua, the Benedictine Rule, Byzantine Triodion materials, Vatican and
postconciliar acts, the Old Gelasian, Gregorian-supplement scholarship, and
targeted bibliographic searches for cultural afterlives. Catenae mapped leads
only; the publication cites underlying works actually checked.

The search was broad across the reasonably accessible Greek, Latin, medieval,
later saintly, Eastern, magisterial, liturgical, and cultural corpora relevant
to each appointed passage. It is not a claim to have found all witnesses.
Protected critical editions were used conservatively by locus and paraphrase;
public transcriptions were checked where possible. Close ancient, patristic,
or Doctoral commentary on the Secret remains a discoverable gap.

**Material negatives.** Devotional homilies, the Byzantine Sunday, the Rule of
Benedict, the indulgenced invocation, Bach's cantata, Bunyan, and Spurgeon are
important reception but do not qualify as register-changing afterlives under
the profile. Ruel W. Tyson Jr., James L. Peacock, and Daniel W. Patterson's
*Diversities of Gifts* was removed: it is a bare title, the earlier audit
omitted Tyson, its authorial context was not verified, and it duplicated
Green's wording. Charlotte Yonge's *The Heir of Redclyffe* supports a
scholarly parabolic comparison but contains no exact appointed wording in the
checked public-domain text. Charlotte Smith's *The Young Philosopher* (1798),
p.~185, quotes the Pharisee exactly but makes only a straightforward moral
application, with little change of register. “Word of Wisdom” and generic
“discernment of spirits” leads lacked verified dependence. The gallery was not
padded.

## Rejected or Bounded Claims

- No witness was made the author of the complete formulary or its cross-proper
  architecture.
- `SupG` is not core Gregorian; Gelasian and Gregorian labels do not prove
  personal authorship.
- The 1962 `misericordiam` and early `gratiam` were not conflated.
- Augustine *Sermon 115* and older English Sermon 65 are one work, not two.
- Chrysostom's exact slander locus is *On the Statues* 3.13; the fasting
  application is 3.8.
- Anthony's historical numbering mismatch and different Introit/Epistle bar
  use as whole-formulary reception.
- Gifts are not merit badges, every gift is not promised to every person,
  humility is not self-hatred, publican behavior is not wholly endorsed,
  mercy does not abolish judgment, and psalm enemies are not modern targets.
- No verified chain from Luke 18:13 to the historical origin of the Jesus
  Prayer was found; none is claimed.
