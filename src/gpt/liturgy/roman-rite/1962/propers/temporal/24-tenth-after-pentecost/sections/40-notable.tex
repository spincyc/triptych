\section*{The Propers: Notable and Quotable}

\begin{studybox}{``Diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit'' in statistics}
Peter J.~Green gave the exact Pauline phrase to a 2003 Royal Statistical
Society paper on the plurality and unity of statistical methods. Its abstract
seeks cohesion amid disciplinary fragmentation. A statement about charisms
becomes a title for intellectual specialization and common scientific purpose.
The verbal allusion is transparent; the accessible record does not prove a
documented statement of authorial dependence.
\end{studybox}

\begin{studybox}{``Diversities of gifts'' as ethnographic plurality}
James L.~Peacock and Daniel W.~Patterson titled their 1988 collection
\emph{Diversities of Gifts: Field Studies in Southern Religion}. The shortened
Pauline clause moves from manifestations within one Corinthian body to diverse
Quaker, folk-preaching, Pentecostal, and denominational practices studied by
anthropologists. The title is a strong biblical echo; accessible publisher
metadata does not document the editors' precise intention.
\end{studybox}

\begin{studybox}{The Pharisee and publican after Emmett Till}
Martin Luther King Jr.'s Dexter Avenue manuscript ``Pride Versus Humility: The
Parable of the Pharisee and the Publican,'' probably preached 25 September
1955, explicitly quotes the publican's plea and redirects the parable toward
imperialism, lynching, and the jury that acquitted Emmett Till's killers.
Temple self-comparison becomes a civil-rights indictment of ceremonial
Christianity severed from justice. The short appointed biblical phrase is
public domain; King's protected sermon is paraphrased from the Stanford
archival transcript.
\end{studybox}
