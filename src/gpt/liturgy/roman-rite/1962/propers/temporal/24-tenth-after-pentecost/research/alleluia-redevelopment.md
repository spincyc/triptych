# Alleluia Redevelopment Record

## Appointed text and canonical horizon

The 1962 Missal appoints Vulgate Psalm 64:2 (modern/Hebrew Psalm 65:2):
`Te decet hymnus, Deus, in Sion: et tibi reddetur votum in Ierusalem`.
The local collation is recorded in `propers/verified.md`.

The complete psalm moves from praise and a fulfilled Temple vow at Zion
(Vulgate v. 2), through heard prayer, the approach of all flesh, pardon of
overpowering sins, and the chosen worshiper filled with the goods of God's
house (vv. 3--5), to saving justice for the ends of the earth and a divinely
watered creation that sings (vv. 6--14). The USCCB's official NABRE note
classifies it as communal thanksgiving and identifies the vow as a promised
Temple sacrifice after heard prayer.

The Greek and Latin superscription adds Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the return from
exile; the Hebrew title behind modern Psalm 65 is shorter. The exile frame may
therefore be described as inherited textual and reception orientation, not as
secure proof of author, date, or compositional occasion.

## Direct and later reception

### Augustine

- Work and locus: *Enarratio in Psalmum 64* 3--5.
- Latin control:
  <https://www.augustinus.it/latino/esposizioni_salmi/esposizione_salmo_081_testo.htm>
- Public-domain English orientation:
  <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801065.htm>
- Section 3 reads Zion/Jerusalem as the heavenly homeland. The renewed
  Christian already sings there by desire, with hope cast ahead like an anchor
  during present storms.
- Section 4 makes the vow eschatological and bodily: perseverance reaches its
  complete payment when soul and incorruptible body are whole in resurrection.
  Augustine develops this as a total holocaust kindled by charity.
- Section 5 continues the Christological reading.
- Limit: this is spiritual and heavenly reception, not the psalm's only
  literal horizon. The anti-Jewish and supersessionist rhetoric in sections
  1--2 is adjacent to, but does not control, the retained reading; it must not
  be reproduced or endorsed.

### Cassiodorus

- Work and locus: *Expositio Psalmorum*, part II, *In Psalmum LXIV*,
  introduction/division and local vv. 1, 3, 5, and 14.
- Latin control:
  <https://www.monumenta.ch/latein/text.php?rumpfid=Cassiodorus%2C+Expositio+in+Psalterium%2C+2%2C+++64>
- Introduction sections 2--4 move from literal return from Babylon to
  spiritual conversion from captivity to Christ's Church.
- At local v. 1 (page sections 6--7), Zion is contemplation/right faith,
  praise belongs to God, Jerusalem is the vision of peace, and the vow is
  rendered in good works.
- At local v. 3 (section 8), pardon is read through Christ as priest and
  victim.
- At local v. 5 (section 10), the goods of God's house explicitly include
  Paul's gifts in 1 Corinthians 12:8--9: wisdom, knowledge, faith, healings,
  tongues, and interpretations. This is a direct reception bridge between the
  Alleluia's complete psalm and the appointed Epistle.
- At local v. 14 (page sections 21--23), the grain-filled humble valley is
  explicitly Luke's publican, who will not lift his eyes or presume upon
  merits, confesses his weakness, casts himself down, and is raised by the
  Lord. This is a direct reception bridge between the Alleluia's complete
  psalm and the appointed Gospel.
- Limit: these correspondences are Cassiodorus's reception of the complete
  psalm. They do not prove why the Roman chants were selected or arranged.

### Bellarmine

- Work and locus: Robert Bellarmine, *Commentary on Psalm 64*, explanation
  1--2; 1866 English scan, printed pp. 197--200.
- Scan:
  <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Commentaryonbook0000bell.pdf>
- Searchable transcription:
  <https://www.ecatholic2000.com/bellarmine/commentary-on-psalms.shtml#Psalm%2064>
- Explanation 1 begins with the historical/literal movement: the returning
  people rightly desire Jerusalem because praise and sacrificial vows belong
  at its Temple and altar, not in Babylon. Bellarmine then argues from that
  terrestrial return to the celestial Jerusalem, where citizens offer
  themselves as a holocaust in ardent love.
- Explanation 2 extends the prayer to all flesh through terrestrial
  Jerusalem, the Church, and celestial Jerusalem.
- Limit: this is later saintly reception, not evidence of composition or
  compiler intent. Its captivity frame follows the inherited title.

### Theodoret and Aquinas

The existing matrix records Theodoret's *Interpretatio in Psalmos*, PG 80,
cols. 1345--1349, as a checked Greek witness. No deeper claim is added here
because a reliable verse-level argument was not independently recovered in
this revision.

Aquinas's surviving Psalms commentary ends during Psalm 54 and does not reach
Psalm 64. The bounded negative is therefore only that this surviving
commentary supplies no direct witness; it is not a claim that Aquinas never
quotes Psalm 64:2 elsewhere.

## Publication use and limits

The reader-facing replacement compares:

1. the complete canonical movement of the psalm;
2. Augustine's heavenly citizenship and resurrection-totality reading;
3. Bellarmine's explicit literal-to-spiritual order; and
4. Cassiodorus's unusually close links to the appointed Epistle and Gospel.

The Cassiodorus links are labeled reception, not compiler intent. The final
connection between the Gradual's appeal to divine sight and the Alleluia's
God-directed praise is labeled editorial synthesis.

No protected modern translation is reproduced. The source fragment paraphrases
the public-domain or Latin witnesses and uses no extended quotation.
