## 1. Introit

**Reference:** Psalm 46:2–3 (modern 47:2–3).

Omnes gentes, pláudite mánibus: iubiláte Deo in voce exsultatiónis. Ps. ibid., 3 Quóniam Dóminus excélsus, terríbilis: Rex magnus super omnem terram. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 46 is a complete enthronement hymn that calls all peoples to acclaim God, depicts him ascending amid jubilation, and ends with the nations gathered under the God of Abraham. The Introit supplies the entrance chant: verse 2 is its antiphon, verse 3 states the royal ground for the acclamation, and the doxology precedes the repeated antiphon.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the reference, verse sign, doxology rubric, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 2. Collect

Deus, cuius providéntia in sui dispositióne non fállitur: te súpplices exorámus; ut nóxia cuncta submóveas, et ómnia nobis profutúra concédas. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed oration first confesses the unfailing order of divine providence and then petitions both negatively, for the removal of all harms, and positively, for everything profitable. As the Collect, it gathers the Church's prayer before the appointed readings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording or punctuation issue.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** Romans 6:19–23.

Fratres: Humánum dico, propter infirmitátem carnis vestræ: sicut enim exhibuístis membra vestra servíre immundítiæ et iniquitáti ad iniquitátem, ita nunc exhibéte membra vestra servíre iustítiæ in sanctificatiónem. Cum enim servi essétis peccáti, líberi fuístis iustítiæ. Quem ergo fructum habuístis tunc in illis, in quibus nunc erubéscitis? Nam finis illórum mors est. Nunc vero liberáti a peccáto, servi autem facti Deo, habétis fructum vestrum in sanctificatiónem, finem vero vitam ætérnam. Stipéndia enim peccáti, mors. Grátia autem Dei, vita ætérna, in Christo Iesu Dómino nostro.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Romans 6 begins from baptism into Christ's death and resurrection, commands the baptized not to let sin reign in the mortal body, and then contrasts two services, their fruits, and their ends. The appointed conclusion moves from the presentation of bodily members to sanctification and from sin's wages to eternal life as God's gift; it is the Mass's Epistle reading.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the pericope boundary, reference, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 4. Gradual

**Reference:** Psalm 33:12, 6 (modern 34:12, 6).

Veníte, fílii, audíte me: timórem Dómini docébo vos. ℣. Accédite ad eum, et illuminámini: et fácies vestræ non confundéntur.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 33 is an alphabetic thanksgiving and wisdom psalm whose superscription places David's praise after his escape from danger. The later portion summons children to learn fear of the Lord and immediately turns instruction toward guarded speech, departure from evil, and good action; verse 6 supplies the earlier invitation to approach God and be enlightened. The Gradual answers the Epistle and mediates the movement toward the Gospel.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the nonsequential verse order, reference, verse sign, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Psalm 46:2 (modern 47:2).

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Omnes gentes, pláudite mánibus: iubiláte Deo in voce exsultatiónis. Allelúia.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This verse returns to the universal acclamation of the Introit within Psalm 46's proclamation of God's kingship over all the earth. As the Alleluia, it is the festal acclamation immediately before the Gospel.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the repeated Alleluia, reference, verse sign, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Matthew 7:15–21.

In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Atténdite a falsis prophétis, qui véniunt ad vos in vestiméntis óvium, intrínsecus autem sunt lupi rapáces: a frúctibus eórum cognoscétis eos. Numquid cólligunt de spinis uvas, aut de tríbulis ficus? Sic omnis arbor bona fructus bonos facit: mala autem arbor malos fructus facit. Non potest arbor bona malos fructus fácere: neque arbor mala bonos fructus fácere. Omnis arbor, quæ non facit fructum bonum, excidétur, et in ignem mittétur. Igitur ex frúctibus eórum cognoscétis eos. Non omnis, qui dicit mihi, Dómine, Dómine, intrábit in regnum cælórum: sed qui facit voluntátem Patris mei, qui in cælis est, ipse intrábit in regnum cælórum.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Near the close of the Sermon on the Mount, the narrow and broad ways precede this warning; claims of prophecy and wonders and the contrast between hearing-and-doing and hearing-without-doing follow it. The pericope therefore tests prophetic appearance by fruit and confession by obedience without treating religious vocabulary or charisma as sufficient. It is the day's Gospel and is followed by the Credo.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the pericope boundary, reference, Gospel incipit, Credo rubric, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Daniel 3:40.

Sicut in holocáustis aríetum et taurórum, et sicut in mílibus agnórum pínguium: sic fiat sacrifícium nostrum in conspéctu tuo hódie, ut pláceat tibi: quia non est confúsio confidéntibus in te, Dómine.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** In the Prayer of Azariah from the furnace, Israel is described as lacking prince, prophet, sacrifice, and place of offering; a contrite soul and humble spirit are then offered, with this verse asking that the offering be accepted like abundant legal victims. Wholehearted following and an appeal to God's mercy immediately follow. The Offertory antiphon accompanies the presentation of the gifts.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the reference, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret

Deus, qui legálium differéntiam hostiárum uníus sacrifícii perfectióne sanxísti: áccipe sacrifícium a devótis tibi fámulis, et pari benedictióne, sicut múnera Abel, sanctífica; ut, quod sínguli obtulérunt ad maiestátis tuæ honórem, cunctis profíciat ad salútem. Per Dóminum.

**Rubric:** Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed prayer confesses that God has given the diverse legal victims their definitive order through the perfection of one sacrifice, petitions him to receive and sanctify the present offering like Abel's gifts, and asks that what individuals offered for his honor profit all unto salvation. As the Secret, it is the prayer over the offerings immediately before the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the complete oration, Preface rubric, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Psalm 30:3 (modern 31:3).

Inclína aurem tuam, accélera, ut erípias me.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 30 is a lament of trust that asks urgent rescue, entrusts the speaker's spirit to God, and finally summons the faithful to courage. The Communion antiphon retains the rescued worshipper's petition for God to bend near and hasten to deliver.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the reference, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Tua nos, Dómine, medicinális operátio, et a nostris perversitátibus cleménter expédiat, et ad ea quæ sunt recta, perdúcat. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed prayer names sacramental action as God's medicinal operation and asks for its twofold effect: release from the recipients' perversities and direction toward what is right. As the Postcommunion, it interprets and petitions for the fruit of the sacrament just received.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording or punctuation issue.
