### Textual detail and application

The decisive difficulty is the coexistence of promised refreshment in
Matthew 11:28 with persecution for justice in Matthew 5:10. Christ's
meekness and humility in the following yoke verses explain the
invitation; Chrysostom XXXVIII.3 explicitly recalls the beginning of
the Beatitudes, and XXXVIII.3–5 distinguishes obedience's present
freedom from sin's burdens and the future reward. This changes the
listener's understanding of rest from exemption from difficulty to
grace-enabled obedience within difficulty. The detail that persecution
immediately follows peacemaking, repeated in the Communion, also
qualifies the application: peace cannot require wrongdoing, and
opposition alone does not prove fidelity. The practical response is
to answer an insult without retaliation while still addressing the
wrong. Its outcome is obedience in the listener's own answer; no
automatic reconciliation or change in the other person is promised.
Replacing these appointments with unrelated passages would remove
both the homily's central difficulty and its particular resolution.
