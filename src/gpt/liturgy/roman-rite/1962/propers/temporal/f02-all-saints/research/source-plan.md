## Lawful-text controls

Read and follow `src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml` and `lasance-new-roman-missal-rights-v1.toml`, with `guidance/liturgical-text-publication-policy.md`. They are controlling rights evidence, not new conclusions made by this context stage. The 1962 facsimile is a collation witness with unresolved whole-artifact rights; neither the entire PDF nor its images or modern rubrical bodies are proposed for publication. The Latin route uses proved public-domain antecedents under the repository's recorded preexisting-material analysis. The Lasance route excludes both later lettered insert ranges, pp. 1298a–1298n and 1302a–1302d. Original historical words retain their source rights and are outside Triptych's project-content licence. Public reachability and an institutional Ordo do not grant redistribution rights.

The `complete` scope does not excuse an unverified text or a missing rights chain. Research must resolve any remaining element-specific limitation through the actual source evidence and report it honestly. No reference-only declaration, text-free exception or rights-limited finding is inferred from an operational difficulty.

## Complete-context and reception research

The initial passage matrix must cover Ps. 32:1 (modern 33:1), Ps. 33:10 and selected 11 (modern 34), Wisdom 3:1–3 as adapted in the Offertory, Matthew 11:28, Matthew 5:1–12a and its Communion reuse at 5:8–10, and Apocalypse 7:2–12. Read the complete psalms, Wisdom's surrounding argument, the whole Sermon on the Mount and Matthew 11, and Apocalypse's complete surrounding vision. Preserve the distinction between the appointed boundaries and wider contextual material. Composed prayers and Introit antiphon receive their own source/echo/reception treatment; the three Sunday orations are part of this study's dated prayer inventory.

Search the library and its containment and formulary-locus inventories before new acquisition. Preparatory leads include Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* I.2–4; Chrysostom, Matthew Homily 15; Apocalypse 7's ancient commentaries; Augustine's second exposition of Ps. 33 §§14–16 (translated numbering differs); Augustine on Ps. 87 §5; Gregory, *Moralia* XII.5.6; Bernard, All Saints Sermon V §§5–9; Schuster, *Sacramentary* V, pp. 208–212. None has been independently examined for interpretive claims by this context stage. Check original work, exact locus, native/translated numbering, attribution, standing, complete argument and rights before adoption. Search Greek and Latin direct exegesis, then medieval and later saintly reception; record meaningful negatives and access limits per passage. Use `commentary-work-index formulary` to test a liturgical commentator's actual texts rather than trusting a feast title. No lead supplies an editorial whole-Mass interpretation automatically.

Research must establish two to five distinct compatible whole-Mass interpretations, each supported substantively by at least two eligible authors, preserving serious alternatives and each interpretation's own four senses. No proposed interpretation is fixed here.
