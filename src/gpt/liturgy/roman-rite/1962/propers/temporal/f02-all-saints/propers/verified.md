## Actual comparison findings

All ten feast bodies match the Mame antecedent word sequence under stated typography; the target Postcommunion adds `nostrum` to the abbreviated conclusion. Mame writes `millia` where the target writes `milia`; this is orthography. The historical books' octave, votive, and older additional-oration directions are excluded. Pustet three petitions match, but its cues are shorter than the target's extended conclusion. Target extended conclusions are received wording, not newly composed text; the same Pustet edition's standard conclusion must be bound if an author expands them further. No expansion is needed for this study.

Gradual: Ps33:10 plus only `Inquirentes` clause of11; Gospel: Mt5:1–12a, ending before the prophets clause; Lesson: Apoc7:2–12 with liturgical incipit; Offertory: selected Wis3:1–3 with `malitiae`, omitting biblical clauses and initial `autem`. DR Wisdom's `torment of death` cannot silently replace the chant's `malitiae`. The received Lasance wording above supplies that adaptation; it is identified as historical hand-missal English. The other scriptural passages retain DR study quotations with the actual omission bounds. The complete received Lasance scriptural counterparts were image-inspected as control but are not silently presented as DR.

The existing English conclusion-table passage records the same witness's short `Through our Lord.` family. That short cue is retained literally; it does not claim word-for-word translation of the target extended Latin conclusion. Common Preface body collation finds the same words under typography; Cummiskey's asterisk is a printed insertion seam, not a liturgical spoken word. Its historic English is retained literally.
