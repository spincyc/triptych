# Research scope — Twelfth Sunday after Pentecost

## Identity and governing evidence

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; Roman Rite; 1962 temporal proper guides.
- **Canonical publication:** `liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/26-twelfth-after-pentecost`;
  its `-synthesis` companion is a mechanical selection from the same manifest.
- **Liturgical identity:** recurring `DOMINICA DUODECIMA post Pentecosten`, II
  class, Vatican typical Missal of 1962. Civil occurrence, precedence,
  commemorations, proper calendars, and current authorization are excluded.
- **Controlling Latin:** CMAA facsimile, printed pp. 392–393, artifact PDF pp.
  473–474, marginal nos. 1552–1561. Every printed form and boundary was read on
  images; OCR is a locating aid only. The Benziger 1962 edition is a comparison
  witness and never replaces the typical edition.
- **Comparison editions:** the Clementine eBible latVUC verse text was checked
  at Ps 69:2–4 and Ex 32:11–14; Brenton's 1851 Septuagint translation was
  checked at Ex 32:11–14; the exact a1b5ff1 Rahlfs-derived snapshot identifies
  the restricted Greek dataset and edition provenance but is not counted as a
  replayable textual witness. Augustine, *Quaestiones in Heptateuchum* II.143,
  independently transmits the Old-Latin closing construction.
- **English:** registered public-domain Douay–Rheims/Challoner for Scripture;
  registered anonymous Cummiskey 1861 for Collect, Secret, Postcommunion. No
  project translation or composite English is published.
- **Evidence classes:** verified source text; checked quotation/paraphrase;
  source-grounded synthesis; expressly exploratory proposal; unverified lead.
  A located reception does not establish the evangelist's, psalmist's,
  or formulary compiler's intent.

## Textual findings that constrain interpretation

1. Introit `inimici mei` and `cogitant mihi mala` differ from the Clementine
   and its Douay English. The gap remains visible.
2. The Missal begins 2 Cor 3:4 with `Fratres`, displacing Paul's `autem`, and
   stops at v. 9 before the comparison ends.
3. The Gospel's liturgical incipit displaces Luke's narrative frame at 10:23.
4. The Offertory is an Old-Latin/Septuagint-shaped adaptation of Ex 32:11–14,
   incorporating v. 12 matter although the rubric cites 11, 13, 14. It may not
   be treated as a verbatim Clementine or Douay extract.
5. The Communion selects v. 13b, omits v. 14a, adds a vocative, and continues
   through v. 15. The printed citation `13 et 14-15` is accurate to that path.
6. The typical edition's required fine readings include the Alleluia comma
   after `Domine`; Gospel comma after `iumentum suum`; `Domini Dei sui` without
   a comma; `Abraham, Isaac, et Iacob`; Secret `ut, nobis...` and `Per Dominum
   nostrum`; Postcommunion `tribuat, et munimen`.

## Search coverage and limits

The research searched the appointed Latin and complete biblical contexts in
the local source library; the CMAA and Benziger Missals; Douay–Rheims verse
files and numbering aids; PG/PL witnesses; New Advent/NPNF; Augustinus.it;
Corpus Thomisticum/Aquinas.cc; the Old Gelasian, Hadrianum Gregorian, and
Verona sacramentaries; Eusebius, Basil, Theodoret, Origen, Chrysostom,
Ambrosiaster, Augustine, Ambrose, Cassiodorus, Gregory, Bede, Albert,
Bonaventure, Aquinas, Cassian, Benedict, Bellarmine, Trent, Vatican II,
Catechism, John Paul II, Benedict XVI, and Francis; plus targeted legal, literary,
psychological, and musical searches for register-changing afterlives.

This is a broad, passage-specific search of the principal reasonably
accessible Greek, Latin, medieval, later saintly, liturgical, and Catholic
doctrinal families, not an exhaustive patrology. Protected translations and
critical editions were not reproduced. Catenae and secondary citations served
only as leads until the underlying work and locus were checked.

Material negatives and distinctions:

- no checked witness establishes one author's design for the ten-element
  formulary;
- no direct Greek history is expected for the three Latin orations, and no
  Father is assigned their authorship;
- the psalm titles preserve received attribution but do not establish modern
  dates or locations;
- straight patristic exegesis, musical settings, devotional reuse, and bare
  titles were excluded from *Notable and Quotable*;
- no universal novelty claim is made for an exploratory relation;
- no source permits “letter kills” to become a rejection of literal sense,
  Scripture, moral law, Israel, or Jewish persons;
- no source permits the Good Samaritan's allegory to displace bodily mercy.

## Scriptural date/location audit

The page-2 sheet inventories each direct passage once in its dossier order:
Ex 32; the Introit, Gradual, Alleluia, and Communion psalms; 2 Cor 3; Luke 10.
It separates
composition from narrated event. Exodus' mountain/date, each psalm's date,
Luke's place of composition, and the exact location of Jesus' speech remain
uncertain. The conventional modern judgment for Luke is c. AD 80–90; 2
Corinthians is placed in Macedonia, probably autumn c. AD 57 with chronology
acknowledged as variable. Jerusalem–Jericho is parable geography, not the
location of the discourse.

## Rights and attribution

The facsimile payload is not stored or republished. Its record and the shared
rights inventory govern the unresolved 1962 status. Focused pre-existing Latin
is used under the recorded §103(b) analysis, not on an age assumption. The
English witnesses are registered public-domain sources. Public availability
was never treated as a license. Protected scholarship and official modern
translations are paraphrased or cited at short locus; they are not reproduced
at substitute length. The repository license does not relicense Scripture,
liturgical wording, historical translations, or third-party quotations.
