\section*{Source-Grounded Synthesis}

\subsection*{Gift does not abolish action; it makes action Christian}

The Collect and Epistle locate the beginning in God: service is his gift and
sufficiency is from him. The Gospel then refuses to let received grace become
an alibi for non-action. Its imperatives are exact: do this and live; go and do
likewise. Augustine's grace-and-charity account, Aquinas's new-law account, and
Bede's refusal to let allegory dissolve mutual duty all support the same
distinction. Catholic cooperation is neither self-salvation nor inertia; it is
action made possible and ordered by grace.

\subsection*{The opposition is not law versus mercy but killing isolation versus vivifying fulfilment}

Paul honors the old ministry's glory while identifying its inability, as
external letter, to overcome sin. Jesus sends the lawyer to the written law,
accepts his answer, and exposes what self-justifying reading withholds. Moses
then speaks from within covenant and the Secret asks pardon. Across these
texts, mercy does not cancel command or judgment. It forgives, writes charity
within, and makes the command become neighborly deed.

\subsection*{Seeing becomes morally significant only when it becomes approach}

The disciples' eyes are blessed; priest, Levite, and Samaritan all see. Sight
alone therefore cannot be the criterion. The narrative differentiates vision
by the movement that follows it: passing by or drawing near. The Gradual's meek
hearers and the Alleluia's day-and-night speaker widen this into a liturgical
anthropology: eyes, ears, mouth, memory, and body are trained for mercy. No
located source makes this exact ten-proper argument, but the Gospel's grammar
and the checked reception ground the central distinction.

\subsection*{Intercession and pardon do not erase judgment}

Moses remains before God for a guilty people and invokes a covenant promise
that precedes their desert. Augustine and Aquinas protect divine immutability;
Gregory receives the prayer as self-offering; Theodoret preserves the narrative
sequence in which mercy leaves discipline and accountability real. The Secret
likewise asks pardon without treating the offerings as a price. Intercession
is a graced secondary cause within providence, not a correction of God.

\subsection*{Providence remains visible in sacramental reception}

Psalm 103 praises the Creator's bread, wine, and oil before later reception
reads the verses sacramentally. Theodoret guards the providential literal
sense; Augustine and Cassiodorus document that later sacramental horizon. The
Postcommunion asks participation to vivify and to grant expiation and
protection. Neither verb promises automatic fruit or encodes continuation.
Created provision and sacramental gift can therefore be confessed together
without being made interchangeable.
