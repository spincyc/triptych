This synthesis companion omits the complete appointed-text component and the
ten-part sweep. The five claims below integrate every appointed element from
the common research record.

\subsection*{Received before enacted}

The Mass begins with need, not mastery. Psalm 69 opens with the petition
\latin{Deus, in adiutórium}; Cassian and Benedict receive it as a confession
suited to every condition. The Collect then says that worthy
service itself proceeds from God's gift, and Paul excludes sufficiency
\latin{quasi ex nobis}. Augustine and Aquinas explain the doctrinal form: grace
does not compete with created agency but gives the inward charity by which
command becomes life. The Postcommunion completes this arc by asking holy
participation to vivify and guard. Received life is ordered toward enacted
service.

\subsection*{The written law read in the Spirit}

Paul's ``letter kills'' does not oppose the literal sense or Jewish Scripture.
He calls Moses' ministry glorious and contrasts external command under sin
with the Spirit's inward, justifying work. Jesus likewise asks the lawyer what
the law says and commends his answer. The failure lies in using a correct text
to contain obligation. The Samaritan interprets by doing: compassion becomes
approach, binding, transport, expense, and continuing care. The Secret's
request for pardon prevents this active reading from becoming another project
of self-justification.

\subsection*{Praise can pass through night}

The Gradual blesses God at all times and summons the meek to rejoice; the
Alleluia chooses the opening cry of the psalter's bleakest sustained lament.
Augustine's Passion reading and Theodoret's Israel-and-human-suffering reading
differ, yet both preserve address to the God of salvation. Together with the
Introit's haste and the Collect's running, these chants form movement without
triumphalism: dependence can be urgent, praising, and dark at once.

\subsection*{Covenant intercession and pardon retain judgment}

Moses appeals to the patriarchal promise for a people that cannot plead
innocence. Augustine and Gregory guard the scene from imagining changeable
passion in God; Theodoret insists that mercy preserves a future without
removing discipline and accountability. The Secret asks favorable regard and
pardon at the altar without making human goods a price for forgiveness. Both
texts keep judgment, intercession, divine initiative, and real creaturely
action together.

\subsection*{Providence remains visible at Communion}

Psalm 103 sings oil, wine, and bread as fruits of providence. Theodoret keeps
their created goodness in view; Eusebius develops spiritual senses; Augustine
and Cassiodorus document sacramental reception. The Postcommunion asks that
participation vivify and grant expiation and protection. Its verbs state
requested fruits, not an encoded duration or automatic efficacy. Creation and
sacrament remain gifts ordered to one Giver without becoming interchangeable.
