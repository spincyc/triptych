\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Exod.~32:11--14 (adapted; cf.~v.~12) & Sinai wilderness; mountain
within the narrative & Wilderness generation; exact historical date disputed\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: Moses intercedes during the golden-calf crisis while Israel remains at the mountain after the exodus and covenant.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Exodus traditionally belongs to the Mosaic books. The text does
not identify the mountain in modern coordinates, and neither the narrated
event nor the book's long formation admits an exact date from this passage.
The chant is a received Old-Latin adaptation, not a continuous quotation.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps.~69:2--4 (modern 70:1--3) & Unspecified place of danger;
received Davidic title & Undated\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The received title associates the psalm with David and
remembrance; the short poem substantially repeats Ps.~39:14--18. Neither fact
proves a composition place or date. Its first verse later became the ordinary
opening cry of the Roman Hours.}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps.~33:2--3 (modern 34:1--2) & Title recalls David before a
Philistine ruler; setting disputed & Undated alphabetic acrostic\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The received title links David's feigned madness with the ruler
it calls Abimelech, while 1~Sam.~21 names Achish. Patristic solutions vary;
the alphabetic wisdom form makes the title reception evidence, not proof of
authorship or event date.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps.~87:2 (modern 88:1) & Prayer before God; Korahite/Heman
heading & Undated\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The heading names Heman the Ezrahite and the sons of Korah. That
Heman is not securely identical with the Levitical singer of Chronicles.
Jewish-calamity, Passion, and universal-lament readings belong to reception;
none supplies the poem's lost historical setting.}
\midrule
Communion & Ps.~103:13--15 (modern 104:13--15) & Cosmic creation hymn;
no narrated human site & Undated\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Latin psalter transmits a Davidic heading absent from the
Hebrew. The verses praise providence through rain, cultivation, bread, wine,
and oil. A sacramental reading is ancient reception and is intensified by
their liturgical placement; it is not the psalm's only literal horizon.}
\midrule
Epistle & 2~Cor.~3:4--9 & Paul writing from Macedonia to Corinth, Achaia &
Probably autumn c.~AD 57; chronologies vary\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Paul addresses a community with which relations had been
strained. After calling the Corinthians a Spirit-written letter, he defends a
God-given ministry of the new covenant. The verses presuppose Exod.~34 and
continue through a comparison that reaches beyond the appointed ending.}
\midrule
Gospel & Luke 10:23--37 & Composition place uncertain; Greek-speaking
Christian audience & Commonly c.~AD 80--90\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated teaching: during Jesus' journey toward Jerusalem,
approximately AD 28--30; his speaking place is unnamed. Jerusalem--Jericho is
the parable's road, not proof of where Jesus told it.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Tradition identifies the evangelist as Luke, Paul's companion;
modern Catholic introductions distinguish that attribution from judgments
about composition. The pericope joins sayings to the disciples, a lawyer's
question, and the Samaritan parable for an audience being taught how revealed
sight becomes merciful action.}
\end{dossiertable}
