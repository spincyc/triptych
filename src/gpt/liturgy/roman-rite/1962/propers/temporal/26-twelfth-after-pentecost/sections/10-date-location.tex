\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Exod.~32:11--14 (adapted; cf.~v.~12) & Sinai/Horeb, the covenant
mountain in the wilderness & 2014 Loreto/Feeney edition's uninitialled
apparatus: AM 2518; its Exodus frame also prints 1491 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: the golden-calf crisis after Moses' first forty
days on the mountain. A compiled note drawing on Jerome records a fast on the
seventeenth day of the fourth month as a memorial of the shattered tablets; it
does not demonstrate the event's date.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Catholic tradition receives Moses as the Pentateuch's principal
inspired author. The 1906 Pontifical Biblical Commission allows pre-Mosaic
sources, secretarial aid, inspired additions, and later linguistic updating
while maintaining substantial Mosaic authenticity. The 2014 Loreto/Feeney
edition's uninitialled chronological apparatus places the calf chapter in
AM~2518 and elsewhere prints 1491 BC for this Exodus frame; the old edition's
two systems are reported without harmonizing them. The 1908
\work{Catholic Encyclopedia} instead concluded for about 1277 BC. Current
chronologies dispute both date and the mountain's modern coordinates.}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps.~33:2--3 (modern 34:1--2) & The court of Achis at Geth and David's
escape & Traditional Achis episode: AM 2944 / c.~1060 BC; before accession\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The traditional title connects the psalm with David's feigned
madness and escape during Saul's persecution (1~Sam.~21). It calls the ruler
Abimelech where Samuel names Achis; Basil, Augustine, and other Catholic
commentators preserve solutions to the name. The alphabetic form orders praise
from beginning to end; modern authorship and dating remain debated.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps.~69:2--4 (modern 70:1--3) & David in danger; traditionally during
Absalom's revolt & Traditional Absalom setting: c.~1024--1023 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The received title assigns the psalm to David ``in
remembrance.'' Theodoret and the Haydock tradition place its urgent prayer
during Absalom's rebellion; no exact day or site is supplied. The poem repeats
Ps.~39:14--18, and modern composition judgments remain unsettled.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps.~87:2 (modern 88:1) & Korahite maskil of Heman/Eman the
Ezrahite, or David speaking in his name; no exact site & Traditional
Davidic--early Solomonic horizon, c.~1055--975 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Haydock's preferred identification makes Heman young under David
and renowned for wisdom under Solomon; other received commentators allow David
to speak in his name. The ancient heading supplies attribution rather than an
episode. The psalm is also received as the voice of Christ in his Passion,
death, and burial; modern dating remains open.}
\midrule
Communion & Ps.~103:13--15 (modern 104:13--15) & The created world under God's
providence; no single human site & Davidic attribution: traditionally
1085--1015 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Latin psalter's heading, ``For David himself,'' places the
hymn within the conventional Davidic lifetime without naming an occasion. The
Hebrew lacks that heading, and modern dating is undetermined. Bread, wine, and
oil first praise created providence; their liturgical and sacramental fullness
belongs to the Church's received spiritual reading.}
\midrule
Epistle & 2~Cor.~3:4--9 & St Paul at Philippi in Macedonia, addressing
Corinth in Achaia & Summer or autumn AD 57; some schemes shift about one year\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Catholic tradition and the letter itself identify the Apostle.
After meeting Titus in Macedonia, Paul writes a few months after First
Corinthians to prepare the community for his coming. The appointed verses
belong to his defense of a God-given new-covenant ministry and presuppose
Exod.~34.}
\midrule
Gospel & Luke 10:23--37 & Traditional provenance: Achaia; Aherne reports many
writers: Caesarea; Jesus' speaking place unnamed & Composition c.~AD 58--64;
Lapide: Oct.--Nov. AD 33; Maas: Sept.--Dec. AD 28\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated teaching: Cornelius a Lapide's expressly probable
harmony places the return of the Seventy-two, the lawyer, and the Samaritan
between Tabernacles and the November visit to Martha and Mary in AD~33. A.~J.
Maas gives a distinct Catholic harmony: the Seventh Journey, September--December
AD~28, the Seventy-two probably returning in lower Peraea, and the discourse in
the vicinity of Jericho. The two chronologies remain unmerged. Luke leaves the
speaking place unnamed: Jerusalem--Jericho is the parable's road, not by itself
a location notice for the discourse.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The ancient Church receives Luke the physician and companion of
Paul as author, writing according to Paul's preaching. An early
\work{Argumentum} names Achaia, and Aherne reports that many writers placed
composition during Paul's Caesarean imprisonment. The 1912 Pontifical Biblical
Commission requires Luke to precede Acts and the end of Paul's Roman captivity;
Howlett's \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} chronology places that terminus at about
AD~64. Modern Catholic introductions commonly offer c.~AD 80--90 as an
alternative critical horizon.}
\end{dossiertable}
