# Research scope — Eleventh Sunday after Pentecost

## Work identity and promised scope

- **Provider / collection / identity:** GPT; 1962 Roman Rite temporal proper;
  stable ID `25`; `Dominica Undecima post Pentecosten`, II class.
- **Canonical owner:**
  `liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/51-eleventh-after-pentecost`.
- **Outputs:** one full research edition, one mechanical synthesis companion,
  and the canonical web edition derived from the full source.
- **Requested deliverable:** a full scholarly, Catholic-faithful synthesis with
  patristic, medieval, saintly, liturgical, doctrinal, historical, and cultural
  reception; all ten propers; source-first records; and reusable corpus
  enrichment where independently warranted.
- **Liturgical boundary:** the recurring 1962 typical-Missal formulary, not a
  civil-year Mass-assembly sheet. Occurrence, commemorations, local calendars,
  and current authorization are excluded.

## Evidence hierarchy and research method

1. The CMAA page images of the Vatican 1962 typical Missal control the Latin,
   printed identity, rank, references, rubrics, and boundaries. The Benziger
   OCR is retained only as a locating artifact.
2. The registered Douay–Rheims/Challoner artifacts control complete English
   Scripture; the registered 1861 Cummiskey scan controls English orations.
   No English in the publication was translated, modernized, or synthesized.
3. Complete biblical contexts were read around every appointed locus.
   Traditional attribution, modern historical judgment, composition, narrated
   event, and later reception are kept separate.
4. Direct commentary and homily were sought before broader doctrinal or
   liturgical reuse. The search sampled Greek and Latin ancient traditions,
   medieval Doctors and compilers, later saints, liturgical historians,
   official Catholic teaching, and Catholic scholarship. A catena supplied
   leads only; every retained attribution was checked at the named locus.
5. Source-grounded synthesis is restricted to textual observation, documented
   history, documented reception, and restrained conclusions from them.
   Six cross-proper proposals are marked exploratory and separately audited.

Research was conducted in Latin, Greek where a direct text was available,
and English historical translations or modern summaries where rights and
source status allowed. Protected modern Scripture and scholarship are
summarized, not reproduced.

The passage rows below also delimit the language corpora actually checked.
For the four psalms, the Latin corpus is the named Hilary, Augustine, Jerome,
Cassiodorus, Lombard (PL 191:277–282, 601–604, 617–618), Aquinas, and
Bellarmine works, with Greek Eusebius and Theodoret at the identified psalms
and Origen, *Homilia II in Psalmum 67*, GCS NF 19, pp. 200–224, especially
222–224. For the orations,
the corpus is the named Latin sacramentaries, Missals, Aquinas loci, and
Schuster. For 1 Corinthians 15, Greek Chrysostom and Theodoret were compared
with Irenaeus in his received Latin version and
with Latin Tertullian, Ambrosiaster, Augustine, Gregory, Orange, Aquinas, and
Pius XII. For Mark 7, the checked
Greek corpus is the named catena and Theophylact at PG 123:565–568; the
Latin corpus is Gregory, Augustine, Pseudo-Jerome, Bede, Anthony, Lawrence,
Bellarmine, and the named later homilists. For Proverbs 3, the Greek corpus is
the catena fragment transmitted under Origen's name, Pseudo-Basil, and
Pseudo-Chrysostom; the early church-order corpus includes *Apostolic
Constitutions* IV.9.2; and the Latin corpus is Bede, Salvian, Aquinas, and
Lapide, Naples 1854, pp. 59–60. The PL 111 Bede duplicate was excluded as an
independent Rabanus witness. Evidence boundaries below state what the cited
loci can sustain; no universal negative is inferred from an unversioned corpus.

## Gospel attribution and reception controls

- Gregory's Mark 7 interpretation is not absent: it stands at *Homiliae in
  Hiezechihelem* I.10.20, PL 76:893–894 (Maurist pagination 1270–1271), and is the third Matins lesson for
  this Sunday under the 1960 Roman Office. Divinum Officium was used only as
  transcription corroboration, not as the controlling official edition.
- Bede's continuous Mark commentary and Gospel homily were kept distinct. The
  critical homily locator is II.6; older transmitted numbering is II.19.
- The `Chrysostom` material in the *Catena aurea* is anonymous Greek Mark-catena
  material transmitted through Victor of Antioch, not secure John Chrysostom.
  The `Jerome` material is Pseudo-Jerome.
- Theophylact is medieval Byzantine reception, not a patristic-period witness;
  his base commentary was separated from manuscript scholia.
- Saint Anthony's `Dominica XII` sermon directly treats Mark 7 and the Secret
  `Respice`, but its Epistle is 2 Corinthians 3 and its ordinal/set are not the
  exact 1962 formulary. Lawrence of Brindisi's Sunday-XI sermons are likewise
  later received liturgical interpretation, not source history.
- Physical deafness and speech impairment are bodily conditions Christ heals,
  not evidence of personal guilt; that disability-ethics rule arises from the
  Gospel's concrete dignity and Catholic moral reasoning.
- *Nostra aetate* 4 and CCC 839–840 separately govern Jewish relations. They
  exclude adopting Lawrence of Brindisi's inherited deaf-man/Israel allegory
  as this guide's theology; they are not disability texts.

## Liturgical transmission controls

- The named Veronese loci hold early forms of Secret and Postcommunion in
  separate Masses. This bounded comparison makes no claim about a Veronese
  locus for the Collect.
- Old Gelasian Book III.VII is the earliest checked witness joining all three
  orations; it does not yet fix them to this numbered Sunday.
- The Hadrianum Gregorian places the set at Sunday XII. Its marginal chant
  cues show a different relation of chants and orations than the 1962 one.
- Honorius, *Gemma animae* IV.65, supplies a genuine cross-proper precedent for
  six present elements in an older set with another Gospel and Alleluia.
  IV.68 treats Mark 7 within yet another combination. This proves historical
  synthetic reading, not intent behind the final 1962 pairing.

## Scriptural date/location audit

The page-2 order is Psalms 27, 29, 67, and 80; Proverbs 3; Mark 7; and 1
Corinthians 15. Each summary row now foregrounds sourced received Catholic
attribution and chronology. Psalm 27 is placed in the traditional flight from
Saul before the older 1055 BC accession; Psalm 29 gives the palace and
census-altar alternatives within David's 1055–1015 BC reign; Psalm 67 gives
the Ark-to-Zion setting soon after the older c. 1047 BC Jerusalem horizon;
Psalm 80 gives Asaph, or David for Asaph, in c. 1047–1015 BC sanctuary service.
These are declared traditional settings and broad chronological deductions,
not recovered autograph dates. Proverbs receives its Solomonic tenth-century
horizon (older Catholic chronology c. 1015–975 BC), with postexilic final
collection secondarily reported. Mark receives the Petrine/Roman account and
traditional AD 50–67 range; the Biblical Commission's pre-70 response; and the
unmerged Maas AD 28 and a Lapide AD 33 event harmonies, followed by NABRE's
around-70 Rome/Syria comparison. First Corinthians foregrounds the older Easter
AD 57 chronology and reports the current Catholic c. AD 56 estimate second.

## Rights, translation, and quotation

- The Vatican 1962 facsimile is used for verification; its artifact rights are
  governed by the central source record. Pre-1929 Roman Missals establish that
  the underlying appointed Latin materially predates 1962, but no broader
  relicensing claim is made.
- The registered Douay–Rheims/Challoner and 1861 Cummiskey texts are public
  domain in the United States. Cummiskey is a historical lay hand missal, not
  approved 1962 liturgical English. Its `Thro'.` is not expanded.
- Patristic quotations are kept short and drawn from public-domain editions;
  where modern translations are protected, the publication paraphrases the
  checked Latin or Greek and cites the original locus.
- Current Vatican material and modern scholarship are cited and summarized,
  not reproduced at substitutive length.
- Project-created prose remains CC BY 4.0. None of the Scriptural, liturgical,
  historical translation, or third-party quoted wording is relicensed.
