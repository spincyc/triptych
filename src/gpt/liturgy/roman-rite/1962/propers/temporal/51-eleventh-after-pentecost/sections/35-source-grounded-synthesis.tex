\section*{Source-Grounded Synthesis Across the Propers}

\subsection*{Historical layers and the authority of received unity}

The formulary reaches 1962 through several historical layers. The Veronese
collection holds early forms of the Secret and Postcommunion in separate
Masses; the Old Gelasian first supplies the checked three-oration set; the
Hadrianum Gregorian places that set at its Twelfth Sunday and associates it
with another chant field. These layers prevent attribution of the complete
arrangement to one ancient compiler. They do not make the Church's received
Roman arrangement theologically inert. Once the Church has joined these texts
in a stable Sunday formulary, their liturgical sequence is itself a legitimate
object of Catholic contemplation according to the literal, allegorical, moral,
and anagogical senses.

Medieval expositions demonstrate the practice while preserving the history.
Honorius, \emph{Gemma animae} IV.65, reads a Sunday sharing the current
Introit, Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Offertory, and Communion but having Luke~18
and another Alleluia. At IV.68 he interprets Mark~7 with a different Epistle
and chant field. Saint Anthony's direct Mark sermon belongs to another numbered
Sunday and formulary. They are not witnesses to the precise 1962 arrangement;
they are genuine precedents for receiving a liturgical sequence as a
theological whole.

\subsection*{The traditional senses disclose one paschal economy}

Literally, the psalms praise the God of Israel who shelters the vulnerable,
restores Davidic vigor, summons festival worship, and heals the one who cries.
Paul transmits a received Resurrection Gospel; Christ heals a particular man;
Proverbs orders real property and firstfruits; the orations ask real pardon,
acceptance, and sacramental aid. These historical and bodily goods are the
foundation of the spiritual senses.

Allegorically, the Fathers see the risen Christ arise in Psalm~67 and make the
Church his concordant house. The Gospel received by Paul tells this house whom
it confesses. Gregory, Bede, Pseudo-Jerome, Theophylact, Anthony, and Lawrence
then receive Mark's signs within Christ's work in the Church: the Spirit opens,
Wisdom touches the tongue, catechesis forms confession, and the Creator renews
his work. Augustine and Cassiodorus read flowering flesh and the dedication of
the house through Christ's Resurrection. The Mass therefore moves within one
Christological economy: the risen Lord gathers, opens, heals, and consecrates
his members.

Morally, concord becomes single-hearted virtue; the apostolic word must be
held fast; hearing precedes speech; confession is confirmed by conduct; healing
returns as praise; offered service awaits God's acceptance; firstfruits pass
through justice and mercy. Augustine and Cassiodorus's instrument exegesis,
Gregory's action-before-teaching, Bede's honest goods and hidden alms, Salvian's
justice, and Aquinas's lawfully offered firstfruits all converge here. Grace is
not opposed to action. Grace makes action truthful by giving it a received
source, a Godward end, and an ecclesial fruit.

Anagogically, the strength given to God's people is incorruption, the flesh
flowers in resurrection, Christ's house reaches its dedication, the barns
become heavenly satiety, and the sacramental remedy reaches fullness in mind
and body. The Postcommunion names this end most directly, but the whole
formulary approaches it. The Epistle's appearances establish the risen body;
Mark's healing honors the body; the Offertory gives thanks for healing; and
Communion nourishes the embodied person toward glory.

\subsection*{The cross-proper verbal architecture}

Several exact verbal relations carry this economy. The Introit says God gives
strength; the Gradual says the trusting heart is helped and flesh flowers
again. The Gradual cries \latin{Ad te, Domine, clamavi}; the Offertory answers
\latin{Domine, clamavi ad te, et sanasti me}. Between them Christ looks to
heaven, groans, and speaks one efficacious \latin{Ephphetha}. The Secret asks
that what the Church offers be both accepted \latin{munus} and
\latin{subsidium} for fragility; the Postcommunion asks that sacramental
reception be \latin{subsidium mentis et corporis}. Altar and Communion thus
answer the earlier cry not by abolishing weakness but by making divine help
sacramentally present within it.

The abundance vocabulary is equally exact. The Collect's
\latin{abundantia pietatis} exceeds merit and prayer; grace in Paul is not
\latin{vacua}; the Communion's barns fill and presses overflow; the final
petition seeks \latin{plenitudo caelestis remedii}. This is a theology of fruit,
not accumulation. Divine abundance produces apostolic witness, accepted
service, just firstfruits, and heavenly fullness. Every intermediate good
remains gift even when it becomes genuinely the creature's act.

\subsection*{A Catholic anthropology of sacramental help}

The day's material signs are not secondary decoration. Fingers, saliva, ears,
tongue, flesh, offerings, agricultural produce, bread and wine, mind and body
belong to the one created order assumed and redeemed by Christ. Ambrose's
mystagogy and the Roman \emph{Ephpheta} rite receive Mark's gestures because
the Church trusts that bodily signs can minister grace. Irenaeus receives the
Eucharist as pledge of the flesh's resurrection. Schuster reads the Secret's
service and the Postcommunion's remedy within the same sacramental realism.

Catholic sacramental anthropology holds the modes together without confusion.
Christ's bodily miracle manifests compassion for concrete suffering;
liturgical signs address embodied persons; Eucharistic remedy strengthens the
whole person through God's providence and reaches completion in resurrection.
Physical disability is not personal guilt, sacramental fruit is not an
automatic promise of present cure, and medicine remains a work of providential
care. Those distinctions protect rather than weaken the positive confession:
the Word became flesh, rose in the flesh, touches the creature through signs,
and saves mind and body in one heavenly fullness.

\subsection*{The holy household returns what it has received}

The final synthesis is a movement of gift into communion. God makes the house;
mercy exceeds its members' merit; the Church receives the Resurrection Gospel;
Christ opens hearing and speech; healed flesh gives praise; the weak offer
service; substance becomes firstfruits; Communion gives remedy. None of these
movements closes grace into an individual possession. The house becomes holy
as received mercy is handed on in confession, enacted in just worship and
mercy, and perfected in the common hope of resurrection.
