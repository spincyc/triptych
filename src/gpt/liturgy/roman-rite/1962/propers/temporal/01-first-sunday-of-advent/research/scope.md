# Research scope: the First Sunday of Advent

Research carried out on 22 November 2026 for the 1962 Roman First Sunday of Advent, celebrated 29 November in 2026. The single temporal identity, annual lections, rank and Sunday/weekday distinctions were rechecked against the governing facsimile and the two dated institutional notices. The French ICRSP notice includes a papal prayer in its own calendar. The universal calendar has no such extra prayer here. RG 109 and 111(a) and the heading of the 29 November commemoration account for the omission of St Saturninus. The older Pustet and Lasance witnesses also print additional prayers and their own rules, none applicable to this first-class Sunday. The Missal's Sunday station is St Mary Major in Rome; it is distinct from the place of Jesus' teaching in Luke.

## Chronology, attribution and place

The generated record and orientation-v3 table cover the scriptural appointments in this family's registry. In the sung texts only the second part of Ps 24:4 is used; the epistle ends inside Rom 13:14. The table identifies their containing verses and does not date a paraphrase, chant or prayer. Ps 24's heading names David in the Clementine and D, with no episode or place of composition. The reused Davidic regnal estimate (Hebron, then Jerusalem, 2 Kings 5:5) concerns the attributed figure. Corbett, *David, King*, gives the usual 1055–1015 B.C. and notes chronologies thirty to fifty years later; neither dates this psalm. The biblical introduction's before-c.165 B.C. bound applies to the dating discussion of the Psalter, not a precise year for this prayer. Ps 84's heading names the sons of Core; its recollection of Jacob's restored fortunes does not identify which deliverance, campaign, sanctuary, or year. The Catholic Encyclopedia, *Psalms*, places a Korahite collection between the time of Isaias and the return from exile (the generated estimate c.740–536 B.C.); it does not date that recollection and should not be reassigned to David.

Paul addresses Christians at Rome, without dating the day of their salvation. The two historical entries assign the writing of Romans to 57 (*St Paul*) or 58 (*Epistles*); the corresponding table prints the span. Luke situates the discourse among Jesus' last days of teaching in Jerusalem. In chapter 21 Jesus teaches by day in the temple and goes to Olivet at night (vv.37–38); Luke does not specify a mountainside for these verses. The speaking, the city's future distress, and the future manifestation of the Son of Man are distinct. The chapter yields no absolute date for this particular saying. For composition, the historical *Synoptics* article reports an estimate around A.D. 70, while the 1912 Pontifical Biblical Commission, AAS 4, p. 464, responsa VI–VII, rejects dating Luke at the siege and requires its priority to Acts and the conclusion of Paul's Roman imprisonment. The latter gives a relative bound only. Both positions are printed in the generated table. No year of the Parousia or date of a prophet's festival follows. The place and bounds of the speaking and the alternatives for composition were checked separately.
