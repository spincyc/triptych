## Homily, derive-homily iteration 0

“When You Look Up” addresses an adult parish assembly at the 1962 Mass on 29 November 2026. Its 1,170 spoken words (TeXcount on the body alone) give an estimated 10.2–11.1 minutes at 105–115 words per minute. The rehearsal was a silent read for sense, pronoun changes and transitions. The application was compared with the preceding three Sunday homilies. Here the listener who enlisted a friend's sympathy in a quarrel can acknowledge the part he left out of his account.

The homily follows “The Judge whom the faithful long to see.” In Luke 21:25–28 Jesus passes from describing other people's fear of what is to come to addressing his disciples about *their* redemption precisely as these things begin. Their reason to look up comes from recognizing the one they will see. The Collect asks that same Lord to come to people who acknowledge their part in the danger they face. That changes the wish to be vindicated by a Judge who knows the truth about a quarrel into a willingness to have one's own conduct known and forgiven. The Gradual asks to be taught God's ways, and the Secret and Communion allow the hearer to ask for a change even in what he enjoys. The terminal note identifies each liturgical and scriptural passage and the particular contributions of Gregory (*Homiliae in Evangelia* I.1.1–5), Chrysostom (*Homilies on Romans* 24 on Rom 13:11–14) and Augustine (*Enarrationes*, English Psalm LXXXV §§10, 12).
