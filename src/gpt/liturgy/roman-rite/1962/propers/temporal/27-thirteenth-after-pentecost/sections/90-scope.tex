\section*{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}
\begin{studyframe}
\textbf{Edition and use.} This companion studies the recurring
\latin{Dominica Decima Tertia post Pentecosten}, II class, in the 1962 Vatican
typical Roman Missal. It is not an official liturgical book, homily, civil-date
assembly sheet, or ruling on present authorization. Calendar occurrence,
precedence, commemorations, and particular calendars are outside this reusable
formulary.

\textbf{Text and translation.} All ten Latin elements, headings, boundaries,
references, rank, Credo, and Trinity Preface were visually collated against
the identified facsimile. Complete Scripture English is the registered
public-domain Douay--Rheims/Challoner; oration English is Cummiskey's anonymous
1861 witness. Witness-boundary notes expose places where those versions do not
translate the appointed Latin form.

\textbf{Research and rights.} Biblical contexts and the named Greek, Latin,
medieval, saintly, official, transmission, and cultural witnesses were checked
at the editions and loci recorded in \texttt{research/scope.md}. That
named-witness matrix is deliberately wide and claim-driven but non-exhaustive.
Protected modern works are paraphrased
or quoted only in short, necessary compass; the repository does not relicense
third-party texts.

\textbf{Global limits.} The nine are neither identified as Jews nor described
after they leave; \emph{Nostra aetate}~4 excludes collective-rejection
readings. Biblical \latin{lepra} supplies no diagnosis, judgment on illness,
or label for another person. No witness retained in the bounded matrix proves
a single compiler's cross-proper design, and no universal novelty is claimed
for an editorial proposal.

\textbf{Review.} The source leaf records redevelopment audits. Acceptance
requires current mechanical and page-by-page visual gates; installation and
publication are separate. Records:
\nolinkurl{propers/verified.md} and \nolinkurl{research/scope.md}.
\end{studyframe}
