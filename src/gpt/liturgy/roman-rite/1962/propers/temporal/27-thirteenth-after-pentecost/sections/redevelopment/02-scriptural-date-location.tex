\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps.~30:15--16 (modern 31:15--16; or 31:14--15) & Israel; exact place unknown &
Undated; Davidic heading\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The heading attributes this individual lament to David but names
no episode, life stage, first hearers, or writing site. Modern historical
judgment cannot recover its author or date. The Missal follows an ancient Latin
``times'' reading at v.~16, corresponding in sense to modern Catholic Psalm
31:16, rather than the Clementine's ``lots.'' The modern span is 31:15--16
when the title is verse~1 (NABRE), or 31:14--15 when it is unnumbered (psalm
heading; NABRE Ps.~31).}
\midrule
Introit; Gradual & Ps.~73 (modern 74) & Jerusalem; ruined sanctuary & Likely
587~BC catastrophe horizon\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Asaphic title is inherited attribution, not proof of personal
authorship. The communal lament addresses a devastated sanctuary community;
Christian interpreters have proposed Babylonian, Maccabean, and Roman
horizons. Modern Catholic orientation most readily associates the poem with
Jerusalem's destruction in 587~BC while leaving final authorship and date
uncertain (psalm heading; NABRE Ps.~74 introduction and note).}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps.~89:1 (modern 90:1) & Israel; exact place unknown & Undated;
Mosaic heading\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The heading calls this a prayer of Moses, the man of God, and
thus gives its received Mosaic horizon. It does not recover the writer's desk,
audience, or moment in Moses' life; ancient Christian interpreters themselves
disagreed about authorship. The community's refuge across generations is the
poem's temporal claim, not evidence for an exact date (psalm heading; Augustine,
\emph{Enarratio in Ps.~89} 1--3).}
\midrule
Communion & Wis.~16:20 & Alexandria, Egypt & About 100~BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Wisdom adopts Solomon's voice but is a Greek work of Alexandrian
Judaism. Its Exodus meditation addresses Jews negotiating fidelity amid
Hellenistic culture and religious pressure. Its approximate date, place, and
audience are historical judgments rather than data stated by this verse; the
Christian Eucharistic sense belongs to later canonical reception (NABRE,
Introduction to Wisdom and note on 16:20--21).}
\midrule
Gospel & Luke 17:11--19 & Achaia in older tradition; modern site unknown,
outside Palestine & Traditionally before c.~AD~62; commonly c.~AD~80--90\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: during Jesus' final journey toward Jerusalem,
through or between Samaria and Galilee, approaching an unnamed village; the
pericope yields no recoverable calendar year.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Tradition identifies Luke as Paul's physician-companion and an
older Catholic chronology places composition in Achaia before Paul's first
Roman imprisonment ended. Modern Catholic orientation commonly proposes a
Greek-speaking, largely Gentile audience outside Palestine about AD~80--90.
Neither approach turns the journey notice into an exact route, village, or
date (Luke 9:51; 17:11; NABRE Introduction to Luke).}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal.~3:16--22 & Ephesus to north Galatia; or Antioch to south
Galatia & c.~AD~54--55; or c.~AD~48--50\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Paul writes to baptized Gentile communities being urged toward
circumcision and Mosaic observance. On the northern hypothesis he writes from
Ephesus during his mature Aegean mission; on the southern hypothesis, from
Antioch before the Jerusalem council. Pauline authorship is the ordinary
historical judgment, but destination and chronology remain disputed (Gal.
1:2; 3:1--4:7; NABRE Introduction to Galatians).}
\end{dossiertable}
