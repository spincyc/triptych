# Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

## Complete appointed Latin

### 1. Introit — Ps. 73:20, 19 et 23; psalm verse 73:1 — no. 1562

Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum, et ánimas páuperum tuórum ne
derelínquas in finem: exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam, et ne
obliviscáris voces quæréntium te. Ps. ibid., 1 Ut quid, Deus, repulísti in
finem: irátus est furor tuus super oves páscuæ tuæ? ℣. Glória Patri.

The antiphon combines 73:20a, 19b, 22a, and 23a; the printed reference omits
v. 22. Roman-Psalter 73:23 has `voces quaerentium te`; the Gallican/Clementine
has enemies. Neither Roman nor Gallican supplies the Introit's `derelinquas`
or its `Exsurge, Domine`.

### 2. Collect — no. 1563

Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, da nobis fidei, spei et caritátis augméntum: et,
ut mereámur ássequi quod promíttis, fac nos amáre quod prǽcipis. Per Dóminum.

`fidei` carries no printed accent in the typical edition.

### 3. Epistle — Gal. 3:16–22 — no. 1564

Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Gálatas.

Fratres: Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes, et sémini eius. Non dicit: Et
semínibus, quasi in multis; sed quasi in uno: Et sémini tuo, qui est Christus.
Hoc autem dico: testaméntum confirmátum a Deo, quæ post quadringéntos et
trigínta annos facta est lex, non írritum facit ad evacuándam promissiónem.
Nam si ex lege heréditas, iam non ex promissióne. Abrahæ autem per
repromissiónem donávit Deus. Quid ígitur lex? Propter transgressiónes pósita
est, donec veníret semen, cui promíserat, ordináta per Angelos in manu
mediatóris. Mediátor autem uníus non est: Deus autem unus est. Lex ergo
advérsus promíssa Dei? Absit. Si enim data esset lex, quæ posset vivificáre,
vere ex lege esset iustítia. Sed conclúsit Scriptúra ómnia sub peccáto, ut
promíssio ex fide Iesu Christi darétur credéntibus.

The liturgical `Fratres` comes from v. 15, outside the appointed range. The
lesson ends before the pedagogue image of vv. 23–25.

### 4. Gradual — Ps. 73:20, 19 et 22 — no. 1565

Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum: et ánimas páuperum tuórum ne
obliviscáris in finem. ℣. Exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam: memor esto
oppróbrii servórum tuórum.

The last clause is verbally Psalm 88:51a, not Psalm 73:22b, although the
Missal prints the Psalm 73 reference. It is retained as an inter-psalm
liturgical conflation.

### 5. Alleluia — Ps. 89:1 — no. 1566

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Ps. 89, 1 Dómine, refúgium factus es nobis a
generatióne et progénie. Allelúia.

`a generatione et progenie` is the Roman-Psalter form; the Gallican and
Clementine read `a generatione in generationem`.

### 6. Gospel — Luke 17:11–19 — no. 1567

✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Lucam.

In illo témpore: Dum iret Iesus in Ierúsalem, transíbat per médiam Samaríam
et Galilǽam. Et cum ingrederétur quoddam castéllum, occurrérunt ei decem viri
leprósi, qui stetérunt a longe; et levavérunt vocem dicéntes: Iesu præcéptor,
miserére nostri. Quos ut vidit, dixit: Ite, osténdite vos sacerdótibus. Et
factum est, dum irent, mundáti sunt. Unus autem ex illis, ut vidit quia
mundátus est, regréssus est, cum magna voce magníficans Deum, et cécidit in
fáciem ante pedes eius, grátias agens: et hic erat Samaritánus. Respóndens
autem Iesus, dixit: Nonne decem mundáti sunt? et novem ubi sunt? Non est
invéntus qui redíret, et daret glóriam Deo, nisi hic alienígena. Et ait illi:
Surge, vade; quia fides tua te salvum fecit.

Credo.

The Missal supplies the incipit and subject `Iesus`; the
Clementine verse carries the subject from the preceding narrative.

### 7. Offertory — Ps. 30:15–16 — no. 1568

In te sperávi, Dómine; dixi: Tu es Deus meus, in mánibus tuis témpora mea.

The modern Catholic locus is Psalm 31:15–16 where the title is numbered as
verse 1, as in the NABRE, or Psalm 31:14–15 where the title is unnumbered.
Except for dropping the opening `Ego vero`, the antiphon follows the Roman
Psalter, including `tempora mea`; the Gallican/Clementine has `sortes meae`.

### 8. Secret — no. 1569

Propitiáre, Dómine, pópulo tuo, propitiáre munéribus: ut, hac oblatióne
placátus, et indulgéntiam nobis tríbuas, et postuláta concédas. Per Dóminum
nostrum.

Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.

The abbreviation expands to the
Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

### 9. Communion — Wis. 16:20 — no. 1570

Panem de cælo dedísti nobis, Dómine, habéntem omne delectaméntum, et omnem
sapórem suavitátis.

This is an adaptation: person, pronoun, verb, address, participial position,
and final genitive construction differ from the Clementine verse. The full
Douay verse, not an editorial chant translation, appears in the full edition.

### 10. Postcommunion — no. 1571

Sumptis, Dómine, cæléstibus sacraméntis: ad redemptiónis ætérnæ, quæsumus,
proficiámus augméntum. Per Dóminum.

The communicants are the subject of `proficiamus`; `augmentum` repeats the
Collect's governing noun.

## Independent-edition differences

The Vatican typical edition controls every published form. Benziger adds an
Introit repetition cue, adds or removes several commas, accents `fidei`,
misprints `misirére`, expands the Secret's conclusion, and uses fuller heading
styles. None creates an unresolved reading. They are edition differences and
are not silently harmonized.

## Historical English and rights boundary

Scriptural English is the registered public-domain Douay–Rheims/Challoner.
The three orations are the anonymous English of the Cummiskey *Roman Missal
… for the use of the laity* (Philadelphia, 1861), pp. 423–425. Its Collect
compresses `assequi`; its Secret paraphrases the second `propitiare`; its
Postcommunion changes the grammatical subject and omits `augmentum`. The Latin
therefore controls every argument resting on those words.

The complete formulary is preexisting liturgical material found in
public-domain pre-1962 Missals; the project reproduces no claim to the 1962
edition's typography or editorial contribution. The source-library rights
record for the 1962 facsimile remains controlling. This is a repository
rights assessment, not legal advice.
