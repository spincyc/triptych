# Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

## Identity and question

This audit governs stable temporal ID `27`, *Dominica Decima Tertia post
Pentecosten*, II class, in the 1962 *Missale Romanum*. It asks how the ten
appointed elements work in their scriptural contexts and in checked Catholic
reception. It does not recover a compiler's intention, make any commentary
official, or claim an exhaustive search of every manuscript and author.

The canonical full edition prints all ten Latin elements and their identified
historical English. The mechanical synthesis edition excludes the complete
appointed-text component and the ten-element sweep, then substitutes one
integrated cross-proper commentary. Both modes share the proper map, four
senses, exact page-2 dossier, brief synthesis, source-grounded relations,
four audited cultural afterlives, five exploratory proposals, scope, and
references.

- The image-collated appointed words and their canonical biblical context are

- Dates, places, psalm horizons, oration mobility, and witness relationships

- A relation supported by explicit liturgical reception or convergent direct
  witnesses is **source-grounded synthesis**.
- The five final connections are **exploratory proposals** attributed to no

No model-generated rendering is printed as source text. Protected modern
translations were not quoted. Quotations in the publication are short and

## Formulary and text control

The controlling Latin is the checksum-matched CMAA image of the Vatican 1962
typical edition, printed pp. 393–394/PDF pp. 474–475. Every element, heading,
reference, accent, punctuation mark, marginal number 1562–1571, `Credo`, and
Trinity Preface rubric was checked on 2026-08-19. Three letters touched by ink
loss in the Gospel were resolved at 600 dpi and against the independent
Benziger 1962 images. The Benziger edition's `misirére` is its own apparent
misprint; `miserére` in the typical edition controls. Its DjVu OCR is only the
unedited locating aid retained in `propers/retrieved.txt`.

The complete scriptural English is the registered public-domain
Douay–Rheims/Challoner. Because that English translates the Clementine rather
than every old Roman chant form, the full edition declares the gaps at the
Introit, Gradual, Alleluia, Offertory, and Communion. Oration English is the
public-domain 1861 Cummiskey witness, pp. 423–425, but its compressed or
recast grammar never controls an argument.

Direct Psalter collation corrected several earlier hypotheses:

- Roman Psalm 73:23 has `voces quaerentium te`; Gallican/Clementine has
  `voces inimicorum tuorum`.
- Roman Psalm 89:1 has `a generatione et progenie`, the Alleluia exactly.
- Roman Psalm 30:15–16 has `Tu es Deus meus ... tempora mea`; the Offertory
  drops only the opening `Ego vero` from that span.
- `ne derelinquas` and `Exsurge Domine` are found in neither checked Roman nor
  Gallican Psalm 73 form.
- The Gradual's `memor esto opprobrii servorum tuorum` is Psalm 88:51a even
  though the Missal cites Psalm 73. It is an inter-psalm conflation, not a
  Clementine translation issue.

## Historical orientation

The exact page-2 dossier distinguishes title, attribution, composition, and
narrated event. Vulgate Psalm 30 is modern Psalm 31; 73 is 74; 89 is 90. The
David, Asaph, and Moses titles remain traditional canonical orientation but do
not yield exact dates. Modern Catholic orientation associates Psalm 73
especially with the Jerusalem sanctuary catastrophe in 587 BC. Wisdom is a
Greek work of Alexandrian Judaism commonly dated about a century before
Christ. Competing Catholic judgments put Galatians in the north from Ephesus
about AD 54–55 or in the south from Antioch about AD 48–50. Luke's narrated
scene is in the Samaria–Galilee region during the final Jerusalem journey, at
an unnamed village and an unrecoverable date. No claim of “months before the
Passion” is retained.

## Oration-transmission audit and correction

The Verona sacramentary has the Collect alone in a July series without Sunday
assignment. The Old Gelasian has Collect and Postcommunion together in one
unnumbered Mass, an expanded Collect among prayers for charity, and the
Secret elsewhere; its appendix later assembles the incipits. The Gregorian
supplement has all three received orations at Sunday XIV. A Gellone witness
also abbreviates their incipits at Sunday XIV; a fourteenth-century Savona
fragment has the three under Sunday XIII and an extra `et` in the
Postcommunion transcription.

A direct page-image check overturned a material claim in an earlier parallel
publication: Wilson p. 172 n. b records Ottobonianus 313's marginal incipits
`Respice`, `Respice`, `In te speravi`, and `Panem de caelo` at Gregorian
Sunday XII, not with the Sunday XIV oration set on p. 173. They are chant
incipits, not full antiphons. This GPT guide therefore makes no claim that a
ninth-century witness already co-locates the whole 1962 formulary.

## Doctrinal controls

- Trent VI.10 excerpts `Da nobis, Domine, fidei, spei et caritatis
  augmentum`; it changes the prayer's full address and does not quote the
  entire Collect. Chapters 10 and 16 and canon 32 together control grace,
  increase, and merit.
- `Nostra aetate` 4 governs every ancient, medieval, and later claim about
  the nine as Jews. The Gospel itself does not identify them. No Jew is
  represented as collectively rejected, accursed, or incapable of gratitude.
- Biblical `lepra` is not equated with modern Hansen's disease. Patristic
  leprosy images are reception for moral self-examination, never diagnosis,
  proof of sin, or a label for outsiders.
- The priestly-showing tradition distinguishes God's cleansing from priestly
  judgment and sacramental ministry. It does not treat the ordained priest as
  the efficient cause of physical cure.
- Wisdom's manna remains Israel's received gift. The Communion's `nobis` is
  ecclesial thanksgiving within Israel's Scripture, not replacement of
  Israel.
- Bonaventure's sacramental sweetness is devotional and theological
  reception. The sacrament's reality and fruit are not measured by felt
  delight; dryness does not signify absence of grace.

## Notable-and-quotable audit

Four cases passed: Leo X's *Exsurge Domine* through Hutten's satire and
Luther's burning; Joyce's Benediction form of `Panem de caelo`; Otmar
Nestroy's bilingual genealogical title using the Alleluia's rare Roman form;
and Werfel's 1935 `Tempora mea in manibus tuis`. Each published entry names
the actual route of dependence, rights condition, register change, and limit.

Picart's engraved Inquisition standard was excluded because the examined
object is an eighteenth-century Protestant-published representation rather
than a surviving banner, and the stronger *Exsurge* dossier already covers
the juridical turn. Browning and Watts depend on English psalm reception, not
the Latin proper. A large search for a secular or political “Where are the
nine?” afterlife returned sermons and devotional matter only.

## Material negatives and attribution hazards

- No genuine Chrysostom homily on Luke 17:11–19 was located; Gregory the
  Great's forty Gospel homilies do not include it; Ambrose has only a
  transitional clause.
- Cyril's integral Syriac sermon at the pericope is lost; the surviving
  material is catena fragments.
- Marius Victorinus's Galatians comments on 3:11–19 are lost.
- Jerome has no direct Psalm 30 exposition and only a brief Psalm 73 fragment.
- `Synopsis Scripturae` PG 56 is pseudo-Chrysostom; `De decem leprosis` PL 175
  is Richard/pseudo-Hugh; `De vera et falsa poenitentia` is pseudo-Augustine;
  the PL 91/92 manna texts are pseudo-Bede; the PL 26 Psalms breviary is
  pseudo-Jerome. None is cited as authentic.
- No ancient witness located joins the two consecutive Samaritan Sundays in
  the 1962 arrangement. Guéranger's Rupert-based association concerns an
  older assignment and is labeled accordingly.
