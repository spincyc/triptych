\subsection*{The Gospel's penitential reception: real continuity, real boundaries}

The movement from command to priestly showing became a major line in Western
penitential theology. The anonymous \emph{De vera et falsa poenitentia}, long
attributed to Augustine, uses the episode for personal confession and for the
priority of the contrite heart; it entered Gratian and Lombard. Bruno of Segni
states that God may remit before the penitent reaches the priest, while the
priest judges the cleansing. Albert the Great and Bonaventure continue the
distinction: God cleanses, ordained ministry discerns and reconciles, and the
intention of completing confession matters before the external act is
finished. Their theology is received application. It should not be projected
back as the sole literal meaning of the Levitical instruction.

The attribution record matters. \emph{De vera et falsa poenitentia} is not
Augustine. The sermon \emph{De decem leprosis} at PL~175, col.~823 is Richard
of St.~Victor or pseudo-Hugh, not authentic Hugh of St.~Victor. A responsible
catena is improved by excluding false saints as much as by adding genuine
ones.

\subsection*{Modern papal reception: gift before response}

Benedict~XVI distinguishes bodily cleansing from definitive salvation and
makes faith and gratitude the hinge; in a second Angelus he reads Naaman and
the Samaritan as signs of a salvation universally offered through Israel and
fulfilled in Christ. Francis repeatedly stresses that the ten obey before
they are cured, that walking in faith and returning in gratitude belong
together, and that Eucharist means thanksgiving rather than possession of a
gift apart from the giver. Leo~XIV, preaching the lection on 12 October 2025,
emphasizes that Jesus heals freely without coercing thanks, that grace can
touch a person without eliciting a mature response, and that outsiders must
not be labeled ``lepers.'' These official receptions reinforce the strongest
literal distinction: gift is prior and free; salvation draws the recipient
into relation, worship, and gratitude.

\subsection*{Bounded negatives and attribution controls}

No genuine Chrysostom homily on the ten lepers was located, and Gregory's
forty Gospel homilies do not include the pericope. Ambrose offers only a
transitional sentence. Cyril's surviving matter is fragmentary because the
corresponding Syriac sermons are lost. Marius Victorinus's Galatians comments
on 3:11--19 are lost. Jerome has no direct Psalm~30 exposition and only
fragmentary comments on Psalm~73. These are evidence about the checked corpora,
not universal absence claims.

The \emph{Synopsis Scripturae} at PG~56 is pseudo-Chrysostom. The Pentateuch
material at PL~91 and a Wisdom repetition at PL~92 are not Bede. The
\emph{Breviarium in Psalmos} at PL~26 is pseudo-Jerome. None is silently
promoted into patristic authority. Protected modern translations were used,
where necessary, only for orientation and were not reproduced.
