\section*{The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities}

\noindent\emph{These proposals arise from AI-assisted editorial comparison.
They are attributed to none of the cited witnesses and make no claim about a
compiler's intention. Each gives its evidence class and controlling limit.}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Unexplained desolation entrusted across time.\enspace\properrefs{Int., Ep., All., Off.}}
\emph{Classification: precedent located.} Psalm~73 asks why rejection
continues and appeals to covenant; Paul marks 430 years without allowing time
to void promise; Psalm~89 names refuge across generations; Psalm~30 entrusts
``my times'' to God. Schuster traverses the same four anchors as apparent
abandonment under patriarchal promise, an interval unable to annul promise,
divine constancy through generations, and times held by providence
(\emph{Sacramentary} III, pp.~132--134). This precedent proves no compiler
intent. The synthesis must not turn
waiting into a chronology of the world's end or treat distress as illusory.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{A petitioning voice becomes a thankful voice.\enspace\properrefs{Int., Gosp.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} The Introit asks
that God not forget the voices of those seeking him; the ten raise one voice
for mercy; the one who sees his cleansing returns with a great voice to
glorify God. The verbal movement can train prayer to return as thanksgiving.
Theophylact moves from the lepers' raised supplication to the returned
Samaritan: the Catena's Latin has plural \latin{voces benignas}, ``kindly
voices'' (CLC17d5.11), while the Greek column speaks in the singular of a
``voice of gratitude'' (PG~123, 989C4--8). Albert moves from petition to the
Samaritan's loud praise. Neither checked
Luke locus joins Psalm~73's seekers to the Gospel voices;
that cross-proper identification remains editorial. Gratitude is not a price
paid for cure.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Manna becomes first-person thanksgiving.\enspace\properrefs{Gosp., Comm.}}
\emph{Classification: precedent located.} Wisdom speaks of bread given
to Israel as ``them''; the antiphon addresses God with \latin{dedísti nobis,
Dómine}. Heard after the stranger returns to give thanks, the grammatical
adaptation can become the assembly's answer: a people receives Israel's manna
memory as gift rather than possession. Guéranger moves directly from the
Samaritan Gentile's thanks to the Gentile faithful singing this Communion,
contrasting the new-Testament Bread with manna (XI, pp.~324--325). He does not
analyze the exact \latin{illis}--\latin{nobis} adaptation; that sharpening
remains editorial. The move may never
erase Israel, appropriate its history against the Jewish people, or imply
that only Christians can give thanks.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Obedience precedes sight and remains unfinished.\enspace\properrefs{Coll., Gosp., Postcomm.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} The Collect asks love of what
God commands; the lepers are cleansed while carrying out Jesus' command; the
Postcommunion asks continuing advance after sacramental reception. Guéranger
expounds all three within this Sunday; Schuster treats the same formulary,
Bonaventure connects the Gospel's saving word with growing faith, hope, and
charity, and Francis emphasizes walking before the cure appears. None states
this three-stage interpretation. Obedience is not a technique that compels a miracle,
and illness or delayed healing cannot be blamed upon insufficient faith.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{The Mediator's hand and the worshipper's times.\enspace\properrefs{Ep., Off.}}
\emph{Classification: near analogue located.} The law is ordered
\latin{in manu mediatóris}; at the altar the singer confesses that his times
are \latin{in mánibus tuis}. Aquinas's \emph{Super Hebraeos} 8, lect.~2,
places Psalm~30's \latin{sortes meae} in God's hands beside Christ's divinely
disposed ministry and reciprocal mediation. It neither cites Galatians~3 nor
uses the Offertory's \latin{témpora}. The relation neither identifies every
contingency with God's positive will nor removes human responsibility.
