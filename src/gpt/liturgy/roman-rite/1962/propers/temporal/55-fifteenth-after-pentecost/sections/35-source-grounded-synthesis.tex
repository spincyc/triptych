\section*{Source-Grounded Synthesis Across the Propers}

\subsection*{Divine mercy elicits rather than merely rewards response}

The Introit asks for hearing; the Gospel shows compassion preceding request;
the Offertory confesses hearing after patient waiting; the Communion gives
Christ himself. Psalm~85 grounds petition in God's mercy, Luke makes Christ the
initiator at Nain, Psalm~39 makes the new song a gift, and John~6 names life as
Christ's self-giving. Human response is therefore elicited and sustained by
mercy. The speakers and scenes remain distinct, and temporal relief is no
simple measure of divine favor.

\subsection*{Grace and responsibility exclude autonomy and quietism}

The Collect's inability, the Epistle's imperatives, and the Postcommunion's
priority of divine operation describe one agency under grace. Chrysostom's
loving service, Augustine's reconciliation of shared burdens with final
account, and Aquinas's practical treatment of gentle restoration show that
grace neither cancels duties nor leaves them self-sufficient. The Secret's
protection belongs inside that same field. Sacramental defense cannot make a
talisman of the rite, and moral effort cannot make the agent the source of
his own stability.

\subsection*{The registers of life are analogically joined}

Galatians' Spirit-led life and everlasting harvest, Luke's bodily restoration,
and John's life-giving flesh converge upon life received from God but remain
distinct in kind. Cyril supplies the strongest bridge: the incarnate Word gives life in
the Gospel miracle and in the Johannine gift, while Nain pledges the general
resurrection. Augustine, Chrysostom, and Aquinas deepen the ecclesial and
sacramental dimensions. The relation is analogical and eschatological, not a
claim that resuscitation is final resurrection or that Paul's and John's
\latin{caro} are one technical term.

\subsection*{Personal dependence flowers as ecclesial praise}

The scale moves from servant to Church and household, then into crowds,
universal kingship, and the world. Augustine's whole-Christ reading of Psalm~85 and one-bread reading of
John~6 place personal prayer within Christ's body. Ambrose's widow-as-Church
interpretation receives the restored son through maternal intercession, while
Luke's literal widow and son remain primary. The Gradual and Alleluia give
temporal and spatial extent to praise. Universal scope does not erase Paul's
special duty toward the household of faith and does not imply automatic
universal salvation.

\subsection*{The sacraments defend by forming the whole person}

The orations' grammar moves from continuing mercy, through defense, to the
heavenly gift's operation upon mind and body. Schuster reads this later
liturgical action as patient petition, sacramental defense, nourishment, and
grace-governed embodiment (\emph{The Sacramentary}, III, pp.~139--142). The
Epistle supplies the corresponding moral field, and John~6 supplies the
christological gift. The orations couple protection from diabolical assault
with transformation of mind and body; they neither ascribe every fault to
demons nor portray divine possession as the destruction of reason and agency.

\subsection*{The stable formulary carries a layered reception history}

The three orations occur together in the Old Gelasian Sacramentary and in the
Gregorian Hadrianum supplement under varying Sunday numbers. The complete ten
element sequence is securely identifiable in the 1570 Venice Missal and is
materially continuous in the 1922 Mame and 1962 Vatican Missals. Schuster
places the Sunday at the hinge between Roman groups reckoned after St~Lawrence
and St~Cyprian and records several Lenten reuses as part of his historical
reconstruction.

The prayer cluster belongs to an earlier historical horizon than the later
complete sequence. Patristic witnesses illuminate its scriptural readings,
and the Church receives the stable formulary as one liturgical action.
