\weektitle{Fifteenth Sunday after Pentecost}{DOMINICA DECIMA QUINTA post Pentecosten}{1962 Roman Missal propers: mercy heard, Spirit-led charity, restored life, ecclesial praise, sacramental protection, and the heavenly gift acting upon the whole person}

\begin{synopsistable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Inclína, Dómine, aurem tuam ad me} (Ps.~85:1--4)}{Cry, mercy, lifted soul}{The trusting servant cries all day; divine hearing prepares Christ's compassion and the Offertory's answered waiting.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Ecclésiam tuam, Dómine, miserátio continuáta}}{Continuing mercy governs}{The Church asks to be cleansed, fortified, and governed because she cannot stand safely without God.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Gal.~5:25--6:10}{Walking, bearing, sowing}{Life by the Spirit takes form in gentle restoration, reciprocal burdens, personal responsibility, perseverance, and good to all.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Bonum est confitéri Dómino} (Ps.~91:2--3)}{Mercy and truth}{Morning and night become the full horizon of praise amid prosperity, adversity, providence, and final rest.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{Deus magnus Dóminus} (Ps.~94:3)}{\latin{Super omnem terram}}{The Missal's adapted Latin acclamation widens the field before the Gospel's recognition of divine visitation.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Luke~7:11--16; Credo}{Compassion, touch, command}{Christ halts the bier, raises the widow's only son, gives him to his mother, and moves the crowds to fear and praise.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Exspéctans exspectávi Dóminum} (Ps.~39:2--4)}{Waiting heard; new song}{Patient appeal becomes divine regard, answered prayer, and a canticle placed in the mouth.}
\properrow{Secret}{\latin{Tua nos, Dómine, sacraménta custódiant}}{Sacramental defense}{The Church asks that the sacraments guard her continually against diabolical assaults.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Panis, quem ego dédero} (John~6:52)}{Flesh for the world's life}{Christ's life-giving self-gift brings bodily restoration into the sacramental and eschatological horizon of John~6.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Mentes nostras et córpora possídeat}}{Mind and body possessed by gift}{The heavenly gift is asked to act upon the whole person and to precede merely self-directed judgment.}
\end{synopsistable}
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & A servant appeals for mercy; the Spirit-led bear burdens and sow toward life; Christ bodily restores a widow's son; the Church asks sacramental guarding and effective gift.\\
Allegorical & Augustine reads the widow as Mother Church and the restored son as a penitent (Sermon~98, §§4--5); Cyril joins Naim's touch to Eucharistic participation on a following verse (John~IV.2, pp.~418--419, Pusey's English).\\
Moral & Humility becomes gentle correction, self-examination, reciprocal aid, personal accountability, generosity, perseverance, and good done while opportunity remains.\\
Anagogical & Eternal harvest and Christ's life-giving flesh promise fulfillment beyond present mortal restoration; Augustine distinguishes present union and future bodily resurrection (John~26.15--18).\\
\end{sensestable}
