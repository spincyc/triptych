## Complete appointed Latin

### 1. Introit — Ps. 85:1 and 2–3; 4 — no. 1582

Inclína, Dómine, aurem tuam ad me, et exáudi me: salvum fac servum tuum,
Deus meus, sperántem in te: miserére mihi, Dómine, quóniam ad te clamávi
tota die. Ps. ibid., 4 Lætífica ánimam servi tui: quia ad te, Dómine, ánimam
meam levávi. ℣. Glória Patri.

The antiphon joins selected clauses from vv. 1–3 and omits the psalm's
descriptions of the petitioner as needy, poor, and holy. The psalm verse is
v. 4; the published English must preserve these appointed boundaries.

### 2. Collect — no. 1583

Ecclésiam tuam, Dómine, miserátio continuáta mundet et múniat: et quia sine
te non potest salva consístere; tuo semper múnere gubernétur. Per Dóminum.

### 3. Epistle — Gal. 5:25–26; 6:1–10 — no. 1584

Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Gálatas.

Fratres: Si spíritu vívimus, spíritu et ambulémus. Non efficiámur inánis
glóriæ cúpidi, ínvicem provocántes, ínvicem invidéntes. Fratres, et si
præoccupátus fúerit homo in áliquo delícto, vos, qui spirituáles estis,
huiúsmodi instrúite in spíritu lenitátis, consíderans teípsum, ne et tu
tentéris. Alter altérius ónera portáte, et sic adimplébitis legem Christi.
Nam si quis exístimat se áliquid esse, cum nihil sit, ipse se sedúcit. Opus
autem suum probet unusquísque, et sic in semetípso tantum glóriam habébit,
et non in áltero. Unusquísque enim onus suum portábit. Commúnicet autem is
qui catechizátur verbo, ei qui se catechízat, in ómnibus bonis. Nolíte
erráre: Deus non irridétur. Quæ enim semináverit homo, hæc et metet. Quóniam
qui séminat in carne sua, de carne et metet corruptiónem: qui autem séminat
in spíritu, de spíritu metet vitam ætérnam. Bonum autem faciéntes, non
deficiámus: témpore enim suo metémus, non deficiéntes. Ergo dum tempus
habémus, operémur bonum ad omnes, máxime autem ad domésticos fídei.

### 4. Gradual — Ps. 91:2–3 — no. 1585

Bonum est confitéri Dómino: et psállere nómini tuo, Altíssime. ℣. Ad
annuntiándum mane misericórdiam tuam et veritátem tuam per noctem.

### 5. Alleluia — Ps. 94:3 — no. 1586

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Quóniam Deus magnus Dóminus, et Rex magnus super
omnem terram. Allelúia.

The Missal's final `terram` differs from the Vulgate and
Douay–Rheims/Challoner Psalm 94:3, which end “all gods.” This is an appointed
liturgical adaptation, not a reading to be silently repaired or represented
as verbatim Challoner.

### 6. Gospel — Luke 7:11–16 — no. 1587

✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Lucam.

In illo témpore: Ibat Iesus in civitátem, quæ vocátur Naim: et ibant cum eo
discípuli eius, et turba copiósa. Cum autem appropinquáret portæ civitátis,
ecce defúnctus efferebátur fílius únicus matris suæ: et hæc vídua erat: et
turba civitátis multa cum illa. Quam cum vidísset Dóminus, misericórdia motus
super eam, dixit illi: Noli flere. Et accéssit, et tétigit lóculum. (Hi
autem, qui portábant, stetérunt). Et ait: Aduléscens, tibi dico, surge. Et
resédit qui erat mórtuus, et cœpit loqui. Et dedit illum matri suæ. Accépit
autem omnes timor: et magnificábant Deum, dicéntes: Quia prophéta magnus
surréxit in nobis: et quia Deus visitávit plebem suam.

Credo.

The Missal replaces the Vulgate opening `Et factum est: deinceps` with the
liturgical frame `In illo tempore`; the Scripture translation begins with
Luke's appointed narrative. The Creed belongs to the Ordinary.

### 7. Offertory — Ps. 39:2, 3 and 4 — no. 1588

Exspéctans exspectávi Dóminum, et respéxit me: et exaudívit deprecatiónem
meam: et immísit in os meum cánticum novum, hymnum Deo nostro.

The antiphon joins opening clauses from vv. 2, 3, and 4; the remainder of
vv. 3–4, including the pit, mire, steadfast feet, and many witnesses, is not
appointed.

### 8. Secret — no. 1589

Tua nos, Dómine, sacraménta custódiant: et contra diabólicos semper tueántur
incúrsus. Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit
et regnat in unitáte.

Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.

### 9. Communion — John 6:52 — no. 1590

Panis, quem ego dédero, caro mea est pro sæculi vita.

The antiphon appoints only the final clause of the cited verse.

### 10. Postcommunion — no. 1591

Mentes nostras et córpora possídeat, quǽsumus, Dómine, doni cæléstis
operátio: ut non noster sensus in nobis, sed iúgiter eius prævéniat efféctus.
Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in
unitáte.

## Independent-edition differences

The Benziger edition abbreviates the Secret's special conclusion to `Per
Dóminum.`, while the controlling Vatican page prints the longer conclusion
through `in unitáte.` Benziger also inserts a comma after `Mentes nostras` in
the Postcommunion, where the Vatican edition has none, and uses its own
citation-abbreviation style. These are resolved edition differences, not
unresolved readings; the Vatican typical edition controls.

## English and rights boundary

Scriptural English is the registered public-domain Douay–Rheims/Challoner,
with the Missal's selected extents and liturgical adaptations identified
rather than silently attributed to Challoner. The orations are the anonymous
English in Cummiskey's 1861 *Roman Missal*: all three occur on printed pp.
428–429 (artifact pp. 437–438; Internet Archive leaves n436–n437). The p. 429
running head says “XVI. SUNDAY AFTER PENTECOST” although it continues the
Fifteenth Sunday's formulary; incipits, sequence, and the following boundary
show that this is a source running-head error. Cummiskey's historical
translations are public domain in the United States and are published for
study, not represented as approved liturgical translations.

No protected modern liturgical translation or chant translation is retained.
The appointed Latin is preexisting material witnessed in the registered
public-domain 1922 Mame edition. Its printed pp. 446–448 carry all ten
elements in the same order and at the same scriptural references. Mame's
differences are older i/j orthography, punctuation, abbreviated conclusions,
and pre-1960 additional-oration apparatus; those differences are recorded
and not blended into the controlling 1962 reading. The source-library
artifact is public domain in the United States because the book was published
in 1922; the project relies on that antecedent for republication and not on
any rights in the 1962 facsimile.

Each historical English source remains edition-identified. Research notes
and synthesis are original editorial material and do not become source text.
