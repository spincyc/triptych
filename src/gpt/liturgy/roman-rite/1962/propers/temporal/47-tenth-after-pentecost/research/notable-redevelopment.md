# Notable-and-Quotable Redevelopment Handoff

Date checked: 2026-07-28

This handoff records the evidence decisions behind `sections/40-notable.tex`.
It does not replace the compact audit in `scope.md`.

## Retained entries

1. **Peter J. Green, statistics.** The exact appointed phrase appears in the
   title of Green's Royal Statistical Society presidential address. The
   published summary supplies the contextual turn: diversity within statistics
   is considered alongside essential unity and the danger of disciplinary
   fragmentation. The publication is protected; retain only the title and
   paraphrase the summary. Checked locus: *Journal of the Royal Statistical
   Society: Series D (The Statistician)* 52.4 (December 2003), 423–438, DOI
   `10.1046/j.1467-9884.2003.02060.x`; address delivered 18 June 2003.

2. **Robert Burns, satire.** “Holy Willie's Prayer” gives the Pharisee's
   self-congratulating prayer a satirical Scots voice. This qualifies because
   the poem redirects the parabolic posture into an ironic dramatic monologue,
   not because it quotes the appointed English exactly. State that limitation
   explicitly. Checked text: Project Gutenberg ebook 18500, web lines
   3383–3479, especially the stanzas beginning “I bless and praise thy
   matchless might” and “Yet I am here a chosen sample.” Patrick Scott's
   publication-history note supports first book publication in Thomas Stewart,
   *Poems Ascribed to Robert Burns* (Glasgow, 1801); do not repeat the
   conflicting claim of an anonymous 1789 printing.

3. **Martin Luther King Jr., civil-rights indictment.** The Stanford archival
   transcript explicitly uses the parable and the publican's plea, while
   paragraphs 111–118 move through imperialism, lynching, the Emmett Till jury,
   and ceremonial Christianity, and paragraphs 136–139 return to the plea and
   carry the manuscript-folder note. Preserve the archive's uncertainty about
   date and place. King's sermon is protected; paraphrase it and quote only the
   short public-domain biblical wording.

## Rejected and fallback entries

- Ruel W. Tyson Jr., James L. Peacock, and Daniel W. Patterson, eds.,
  *Diversities of Gifts* (1988), is excluded. The former gallery omitted Tyson,
  supplied no checked authorial-intent or contextual locus, duplicated Green's
  wording, and offered only a bare title.
- Charlotte Smith, *The Young Philosopher* (1798), p. 185, reproduces the
  Pharisee's wording exactly, but the narrator's use is a straightforward
  moral application rather than a substantial change of register. It is a
  verified exact-phrase fallback, not a preferred gallery entry.
- Devotional homilies, the Byzantine Sunday, the Rule of Benedict, Bach,
  Bunyan, Spurgeon, bare artworks, mottos, and musical settings remain outside
  the profile category unless a later audit verifies a genuine redirection of
  the appointed wording.
