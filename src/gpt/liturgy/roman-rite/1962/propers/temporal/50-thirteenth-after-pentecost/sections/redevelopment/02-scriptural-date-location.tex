\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps.~30:15--16 (modern 31:15--16; or 31:14--15) & Flight from
Jerusalem under Absalom; exact Psalm site unrecorded; Maon alternative &
Davidic: Maon c.~1059~BC; Absalom 1024--1023~BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The titles assign the psalm to David. Theodoret and Bellarmine
place it during Absalom's revolt; Boylan records Saul's pursuit near Maon.
The received sources do not settle the occasion; the checked NABRE note
supplies no composition place or date.
The Missal maps to Catholic 31:15--16 where the title is v.~1, or
31:14--15 where unnumbered
(Theodoret, PG~80:1077--1080; Bellarmine, p.~78; Boylan, I, p.~106;
NABRE Ps.~31).}
\midrule
Introit; Gradual & Ps.~73 (modern 74) & Zion/Jerusalem; sanctuary in ruins &
Asaphic/Davidic horizon c.~1055--1015~BC; later referents chiefly 587~BC,
167--164~BC, and AD~70\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title recalls David's singer/seer and the Asaphite house
(1~Chr.~25:1--2; 2~Chr.~29:30). The afflicted sanctuary community is the
literal voice. Lyra hears Babylon; Bellarmine, Antiochus~IV (167--164~BC);
Augustine, Cassiodorus, and Theodoret, prophecy of AD~70. The NABRE calls it
especially apt to the destruction of 587~BC without fixing composition there.
The numerical Davidic range comes from an older Catholic reign chronology and
dates the received Asaphic ministry horizon, not a demonstrated autograph
(Corbett, ``King David,'' \emph{Catholic Encyclopedia} IV).}
\midrule
Gradual close & Ps.~88:51a (modern 89:51a) & No site for Davidic alternative;
Babylon in captive reading & Received Ethan attribution; Davidic horizon
c.~1055--1015~BC; Babylonian alternative A.M.~3416\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The received superscription, \emph{Intellectus Ethan
Ezrahitae}, names Ethan but no composition site or regnal year. Haydock
preserves both David writing in Ethan's (or Idithun's) person and a captive in
Babylon under Sedecias lamenting Juda's ruin. Thus c.~1055--1015~BC dates only
the received Davidic horizon; a separate Usher-attributed chronology places
Jerusalem's ruin and Sedecias's capture at A.M.~3416. Neither is an autograph
date. The checked NABRE identifies a communal lament but supplies no king,
event, composition site, or date (Haydock, Pss.~70:1; 88:1; Corbett, ``King
David,'' \emph{Catholic Encyclopedia} IV; NABRE, Ps.~89).}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps.~89:1 (modern 90:1) & Israel; no site named & Mosaic/Exodus
horizon, c.~1491~BC (older Catholic chronology)\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Jerome and Lyra receive Mosaic attribution positively; no camp
or first audience is named. Augustine regards the Mosaic title as deliberate
and reads Moses as Old-Covenant minister and New-Covenant prophet, while
declining to require Moses' direct composition; Theodoret reports both
authorship traditions. The numerical date marks that chronology's Exodus
epoch, not a datable Psalm episode; the checked NABRE note supplies no
composition place or date
(Jerome, \emph{Ep.~140} 2--5; Augustine, \emph{Enarr. in Ps.~89} 1--3;
NABRE Ps.~90).}
\midrule
Communion & Wis.~16:20 & Royal Jerusalem in Solomonic persona; probable
composition in Alexandria & Solomonic horizon, tenth century BC; NABRE
c.~50~BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Augustine receives the book as Scripture without making its Greek
wording Solomon's own (\emph{Civ. Dei} XVII.20). The NABRE places an anonymous
Greek-speaking Jewish author in Alexandria about 50~BC, addressing Jews under
Hellenistic pressure. Manna is Israel's Exodus
gift and a type of Christ and the Eucharist (NABRE Wisdom introduction and
16:20).}
\midrule
Gospel & Luke 17:11--19 & Achaia and Boeotia in Jerome; modern place disputed &
PBC 1912: before
c.~AD~63; NABRE c.~AD~80--90; Gadenz: prevailing 70s or 80s\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: an older Catholic harmony places the unnamed
village on the ninth/final journey, through or between Samaria and Galilee,
February--Passover AUC~782 (AD~29); Luke gives no exact route or date.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Church receives Luke, Paul's physician-companion, as author.
Jerome's prologue names Achaia and Boeotia. The PBC's 1912 chronology puts
Luke before Acts and the end of
Paul's first Roman captivity, conventionally before c.~AD~63. The NABRE
proposes c.~AD~80--90; Gadenz separately reports prevailing dates in the 70s
or 80s and a minority pre-70 case. Modern place remains disputed (PBC,
responses VI--VII; Jerome, prologue, PL~26:18; Gadenz, pp.~16--19, esp.
p.~19 n.~20; NABRE Luke introduction).}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal.~3:16--22 & Rome in ancient subscriptions; Ephesus or another
Greek city in the contrary tradition; to Galatia & Baronius/a Lapide
chronology AD~58; modern north c.~AD~54--55, south perhaps AD~48--50\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Paul addresses Gentile converts pressed toward circumcision and
Mosaic observance. A Lapide records Syriac and Greek endings and Jerome,
Athanasius, and Theodoret for Rome, but also Chrysostom and Baronius for
Ephesus or another Greek city and leaves place and time uncertain; his
Baronius-based chronology gives AD~58. The checked NABRE introduction instead
gives a northern hypothesis---ethnic Galatia near Ancyra, from Ephesus
c.~AD~54--55---and a southern hypothesis in Pisidia and Lycaonia, perhaps
AD~48--50, with writing place unsettled (Gal.~1:1; 6:11; a Lapide,
\emph{Argumentum} and proemium; NABRE Galatians introduction).}
\end{dossiertable}
