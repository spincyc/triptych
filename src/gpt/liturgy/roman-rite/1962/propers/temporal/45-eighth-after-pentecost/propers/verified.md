## 1. Introit

**Reference:** Psalm 47:10–11, with verse 2 (modern 48:10–11, 2).

Suscépimus, Deus, misericórdiam tuam in médio templi tui: secúndum nomen tuum, Deus, ita et laus tua in fines terræ: iustítia plena est déxtera tua. Ps. ibid., 2 Magnus Dóminus, et laudábilis nimis: in civitáte Dei nostri, in monte sancto eius. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 47 is a Zion hymn that celebrates God's city, recalls the defeat of hostile kings, receives divine mercy within the Temple, declares God's right hand full of justice, and carries his praise to the ends of the earth. The Introit takes its antiphon from the sanctuary-centered thanksgiving, adds the psalm's opening praise of God's city and holy mountain, and concludes with the doxology before the antiphon is repeated in performance.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the reference, verse sign, accents, punctuation, and absence of a printed repeat incipit after the doxology have no unresolved issue.

## 2. Collect

Largíre nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine, semper spíritum cogitándi quæ recta sunt, propítius et agéndi: ut, qui sine te esse non póssumus, secúndum te vívere valeámus. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed oration asks God mercifully to grant a constant spirit of thinking and doing what is right, then confesses the creature's dependence upon him so that life may be lived according to him. As the Collect, it supplies the petition that governs the readings' account of life under the Spirit and responsible action.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording or punctuation issue.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** Romans 8:12–17.

Fratres: Debitóres sumus non carni, ut secúndum carnem vivámus. Si enim secúndum carnem vixéritis, moriémini: si autem spíritu facta carnis mortificavéritis, vivétis. Quicúmque enim spíritu Dei agúntur, ii sunt fílii Dei. Non enim accepístis spíritum servitútis íterum in timóre, sed accepístis spíritum adoptiónis filiórum, in quo clamámus: Abba (Pater). Ipse enim Spíritus testimónium reddit spirítui nostro, quod sumus fílii Dei. Si autem fílii, et herédes: herédes quidem Dei, coherédes autem Christi.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Romans 8 contrasts life according to the flesh with life in the Spirit after proclaiming freedom from condemnation in Christ and the Spirit's indwelling. The appointed passage states a real death–life alternative, then moves through divine leading and adopted sonship to the Spirit's witness and coheirship with Christ. The printed pericope stops within verse 17 after *coheredes autem Christi*; the unappointed second half of verse 17 adds suffering with Christ unto glorification, while verse 18 begins Paul's comparison of present suffering with future glory. It is the Mass's Epistle.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the printed pericope boundary after the first half of Romans 8:17, reference, accents, capitalization of “Spíritus”, and punctuation have no unresolved issue. The complete biblical verse division was separately checked in the Holy See's *Nova Vulgata*; that witness controls the canonical context, not the 1962 liturgical wording.

## 4. Gradual

**References:** Psalm 30:3 and Psalm 70:1 (modern 31:3 and 71:1).

Esto mihi in Deum protectórem, et in locum refúgii, ut salvum me fácias. ℣. Ps. 70, 1 Deus, in te sperávi: Dómine, non confúndar in ætérnum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 30 is a threatened speaker's prayer of trust and deliverance; Psalm 70 is an old-age lament that asks God not to abandon one whose lifelong hope rests in him. The Gradual joins refuge with hope and answers the Epistle's summons to life in the Spirit by placing the adopted heirs in continuing dependence upon God.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the two-psalm construction, references, verse sign, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Psalm 47:2 (modern 48:2).

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Ps. 47, 2 Magnus Dóminus, et laudábilis valde: in civitáte Dei nostri, in monte sancto eius. Allelúia.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This verse returns to the Zion hymn used at the Introit and praises the Lord within his city and on his holy mountain. As the Alleluia, it renews the sanctuary and city setting immediately before Christ's parable of a household steward and eternal dwellings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the repeated Alleluia, reference, verse sign, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Luke 16:1–9.

In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis parábolam hanc: Homo quidam erat dives, qui habébat víllicum: et hic diffamátus est apud illum, quasi dissipásset bona ipsíus. Et vocávit illum, et ait illi: Quid hoc áudio de te? redde ratiónem villicatiónis tuæ: iam enim non póteris villicáre. Ait autem víllicus intra se: Quid fáciam, quia dóminus meus aufert a me villicatiónem? fódere non váleo, mendicáre erubésco. Scio quid fáciam, ut, cum amótus fúero a villicatióne, recípiant me in domos suas. Convocátis ítaque síngulis debitóribus dómini sui, dicébat primo: Quantum debes dómino meo? At ille dixit: Centum cados ólei. Dixítque illi: Accipe cautiónem tuam: et sede cito, scribe quinquagínta. Deínde álii dixit: Tu vero quantum debes? Qui ait: Centum coros trítici. Ait illi: Accipe lítteras tuas, et scribe octogínta. Et laudávit dóminus víllicum iniquitátis, quia prudénter fecísset: quia fílii huius sǽculi prudentióres fíliis lucis in generatióne sua sunt. Et ego vobis dico: fácite vobis amícos de mammóna iniquitátis: ut, cum defecéritis, recípiant vos in ætérna tabernácula.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Jesus addresses his disciples after the three parables of loss and recovery in Luke 15. The appointed parable names the steward unjust yet commends his foresight under an approaching account, rebukes the children of light through the greater resolve of the children of this age, and commands an eternal use of “mammon of iniquity.” The immediately following sayings require fidelity and prohibit serving both God and mammon; the Pharisees are then identified as lovers of money, and the chapter concludes with the rich man, neglected Lazarus, death, and irreversible judgment. This context does not commend dishonesty or make mammon a neutral master. It is the day's Gospel and is followed by the Credo.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the pericope boundary, reference, Gospel incipit, Credo rubric, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Psalm 17:28, 32 (modern 18:28, 32).

Pópulum húmilem salvum fácies, Dómine, et óculos superbórum humiliábis: quóniam quis Deus præter te, Dómine?

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 17 is a royal thanksgiving for deliverance. The appointed verses declare God's action: he saves the humble people, humbles proud eyes, and alone is God. As the Offertory antiphon, they answer the Gospel's crisis of stewardship with divine judgment upon pride and salvation of the lowly before the one true owner and deliverer.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the two-verse reference, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret

Súscipe, quǽsumus, Dómine, múnera, quæ tibi de tua largitáte deférimus: ut hæc sacrosáncta mystéria, grátiæ tuæ operánte virtúte, et præséntis vitæ nos conversatióne sanctíficent, et ad gáudia sempitérna perdúcant. Per Dóminum.

**Rubric:** Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed prayer asks God to receive gifts brought to him from his own bounty. It then petitions that the sacred mysteries, by the operative power of grace, sanctify the recipients in the conduct of the present life and lead them to everlasting joy. As the Secret, it orders stewardship, present conduct, and the final end within sacramental offering.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the complete oration, Preface rubric, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Psalm 33:9 (modern 34:9).

Gustáte et vidéte, quóniam suávis est Dóminus: beátus vir, qui sperat in eo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 33 moves from thanksgiving for deliverance to an invitation that the humble taste and see the Lord's goodness, take refuge in him, and learn the fear of the Lord through truthful and peaceable conduct. The Communion antiphon appoints the invitation and blessing during sacramental reception.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the reference, accents, and punctuation have no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Sit nobis, Dómine, reparátio mentis et córporis cæléste mystérium: ut, cuius exséquimur cultum, sentiámus efféctum. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** This composed prayer asks that the heavenly mystery restore mind and body so that those who perform its worship may experience its effect. As the Postcommunion, it asks that sacramental reception produce real renewal without reducing the mystery's efficacy to a passing sensation.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording or punctuation issue.
