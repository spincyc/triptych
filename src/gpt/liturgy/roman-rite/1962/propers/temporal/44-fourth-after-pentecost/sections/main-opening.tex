\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Dominus illuminatio mea}; \latin{si consistant adversum me castra, non timebit cor meum} (Ps. 26:1--3)}{Light, salvation, protection, courage}{The Lord is named as light, salvation, and protector; enemies fall, and even an encamped host need not make the heart fear.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{mundi cursus pacifice nobis tuo ordine dirigatur}; \latin{Ecclesia tua tranquilla devotione laetetur}}{Ordered course and tranquil devotion}{The Church asks God peacefully to direct the world's course and let the Church rejoice in tranquil devotion.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 8:18--23}{Suffering, creation, firstfruits, body}{Creation and believers groan in hope: present suffering opens toward glory, freedom from corruption, and redemption of the body.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Propitius esto, Domine, peccatis nostris}; \latin{Adiuva nos} (Ps. 78:9--10)}{Pardon, help, deliverance, God's name}{The endangered people ask forgiveness and rescue for the honor of God's name, lest the nations ask where their God is.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{Deus, qui sedes super thronum, et iudicas aequitatem}; \latin{refugium pauperum} (Ps. 9:5, 10)}{Equitable judgment and refuge}{The enthroned equitable Judge is acclaimed as the poor person's refuge in tribulation.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Luke 5:1--11}{Teaching, command, catch, confession, call}{Christ teaches, commands a cast after an empty night, gives abundance, answers Peter's confession and fear, and calls him to catch people.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte} (Ps. 12:4--5)}{Interior light against death}{The offerer asks God to illuminate the eyes, lest sleep in death let the enemy claim victory.}
\properrow{Secret}{\latin{Oblationibus nostris ... susceptis}; \latin{ad te nostras etiam rebelles compelle propitius voluntates}}{Accepted gifts and resistant wills}{God is asked to receive the oblations and mercifully compel even rebellious wills toward himself.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Dominus firmamentum meum, et refugium meum, et liberator meus} (Ps. 17:3)}{Strength, refuge, deliverance, help}{The communicant names the Lord, rather than the self, as strength, refuge, deliverer, and helper.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Mysteria ... sumpta purificent: et suo munere tueantur}}{Purification and protection}{The mysteries already received are asked both to purify their recipients and to protect them by their gift.}
\end{properstable}
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Romans describes creation and believers groaning for bodily redemption; Luke narrates Christ teaching from Simon's boat, commanding another cast, granting an overwhelming catch, and calling Peter after his confession.\\
Allegorical & Cyril and Ambrose read the catch as apostolic gathering under Christ's word; Augustine distinguishes Luke's burdened present catch from the unbroken net at the risen Christ's shore in John 21.\\
Moral & The disciple tells the truth about failure, obeys Christ's word before seeing a result, confesses sin amid abundance, and asks for illuminated sight and the merciful compelling of a rebellious will toward God.\\
Anagogical & Creation's travail tends toward freedom from corruption, the body toward glory, and the present mixed catch toward the final communion Augustine sees figured by John 21.\\
\end{sensestable}
