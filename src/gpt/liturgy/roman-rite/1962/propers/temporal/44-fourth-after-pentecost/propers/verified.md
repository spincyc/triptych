## 1. Introit

**Reference:** Psalm 26:1–2, with psalm verse 26:3 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 27 in most modern Bibles).

Dóminus illuminátio mea, et salus mea, quem timébo? Dóminus defénsor vitæ meæ, a quo trepidábo? qui tríbulant me inimíci mei, ipsi infirmáti sunt, et cecidérunt. Ps. ibid., 3 Si consístant advérsum me castra: non timébit cor meum. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 26 moves from confidence in the Lord as light, salvation, and defender amid attacking enemies to the desire to dwell with him and wait courageously for him. Verse 3 intensifies the danger to an encamped army without surrendering the heart to fear. The Introit makes that confident petition the congregation's entrance voice.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's stopping point after the doxology is retained, with the secondary's explicit repeat incipit documented above.

## 2. Collect

Da nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine: ut et mundi cursus pacífice nobis tuo órdine dirigátur; et Ecclésia tua tranquílla devotióne lætétur. Per Dóminum nostrum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical oration asks for two coordinated effects of divine order: the world's course directed peacefully for the faithful and the Church rejoicing in tranquil devotion. As the Collect, it gathers the assembly's prayer before the readings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's longer conclusion is retained. See the witness difference under `Unresolved discrepancies`.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** Romans 8:18–23.

Fratres: Exístimo quod non sunt condígnæ passiónes huius témporis ad futúram glóriam, quæ revelábitur in nobis. Nam exspectátio creatúræ revelatiónem filiórum Dei exspéctat. Vanitáti enim creatúra subiécta est non volens, sed propter eum, qui subiécit eam in spe: quia et ipsa creatúra liberábitur a servitúte corruptiónis, in libertátem glóriæ filiórum Dei. Scimus enim quod omnis creatúra ingemíscit, et párturit usque adhuc. Non solum autem illa, sed et nos ipsi primítias spíritus habéntes: et ipsi intra nos gémimus, adoptiónem filiórum Dei exspectántes, redemptiónem córporis nostri: in Christo Iesu Dómino nostro.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Romans 8 was read as a whole. Verses 14–17 identify those led by the Spirit as adopted children and heirs who suffer with Christ before being glorified with him. The appointed passage widens that expectation to creation's bondage, labor, and hope; verses 24–30 continue with unseen hope, the Spirit's intercession, and God's ordering of all things toward conformity to his Son. The Epistle therefore places present suffering within bodily and cosmic expectation.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 4. Gradual

**Reference:** Psalm 78:9–10 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 79 in most modern Bibles).

Propítius esto, Dómine, peccátis nostris: ne quando dicant gentes: Ubi est Deus eórum? ℣. Adiuva nos, Deus salutáris noster: et propter honórem nóminis tui, Dómine, líbera nos.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 78 is a communal lament after the nations have devastated Jerusalem and profaned the temple. These verses ask pardon and deliverance for the glory of God's name while hostile nations mock his apparent absence. The Gradual answers the Epistle's groaning with penitential dependence rather than self-vindication.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Psalm 9:5, 10 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering).

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Deus, qui sedes super thronum, et iúdicas æquitátem: esto refúgium páuperum in tribulatióne. Allelúia.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 9 praises God for judging the psalmist's cause, rebuking oppressive nations, and remaining an enduring refuge for the poor and afflicted. The Alleluia acclaims the equitable Judge as refuge immediately before Christ teaches from the boat and commands the catch.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Luke 5:1–11.

In illo témpore: Cum turbæ irrúerent in Iesum, ut audírent verbum Dei, et ipse stabat secus stagnum Genésareth. Et vidit duas naves stantes secus stagnum: piscatóres autem descénderant, et lavábant rétia. Ascéndens autem in unam navim, quæ erat Simónis, rogávit eum a terra redúcere pusíllum. Et sedens docébat de navícula turbas. Ut cessávit autem loqui, dixit ad Simónem: Duc in altum, et laxáte rétia vestra in captúram. Et respóndens Simon, dixit illi: Præcéptor, per totam noctem laborántes, nihil cépimus: in verbo autem tuo laxábo rete. Et cum hoc fecíssent, conclusérunt píscium multitúdinem copiósam: rumpebátur autem rete eórum. Et annuérunt sóciis, qui erant in ália navi, ut venírent, et adiuvárent eos. Et venérunt, et implevérunt ambas navículas, ita ut pæne mergeréntur. Quod cum vidéret Simon Petrus, prócidit ad génua Iesu, dicens: Exi a me, quia homo peccátor sum, Dómine. Stupor enim circumdéderat eum, et omnes qui cum illo erant, in captúra píscium, quam céperant: simíliter autem Iacóbum et Ioánnem, fílios Zebedǽi, qui erant sócii Simónis. Et ait ad Simónem Iesus: Noli timére: ex hoc iam hómines eris cápiens. Et subdúctis ad terram návibus, relíctis ómnibus, secúti sunt eum.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Luke 5 and the transition from 4:42 through 5:16 were read in full. Jesus has just declared that he was sent to preach God's kingdom; at the lake, the crowd presses to hear God's word, teaching from Simon's boat precedes the command, the superabundant catch produces Peter's confession, and grace answers fear with vocation. The following cleansing of the leper continues the disclosure of Jesus' authority. The Gospel proclaims this word, sign, confession, and apostolic call as the Sunday's central narrative.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline spelling `pæne` is retained, and both images establish Luke 5 despite the raw OCR's false `Luc. 6`.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Psalm 12:4–5 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 13 in most modern Bibles).

Illúmina óculos meos, ne unquam obdórmiam in morte: ne quando dicat inimícus meus: Præválui advérsus eum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 12 begins with repeated cries of “how long,” asks God to look, hear, and enlighten the eyes before death or enemy triumph, and ends in trust and praise for mercy received. At the Offertory its plea for interior sight accompanies the presentation of the gifts.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 8. Secret

Oblatiónibus nostris, quǽsumus, Dómine, placáre suscéptis: et ad te nostras étiam rebélles compélle propítius voluntátes. Per Dóminum.

**Rubric:** Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** the Secret asks God, once he has received the Church's oblations, to be appeased and mercifully to compel even rebellious wills toward himself. It is the priest's proper prayer over the offered gifts and distinguishes the received oblations from the wills whose conversion is also sought.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Psalm 17:3 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 18 in most modern Bibles).

Dóminus firmaméntum meum, et refúgium meum, et liberátor meus: Deus meus, adiútor meus.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 17 is David's thanksgiving after deliverance from his enemies and Saul; its opening names the Lord as strength, refuge, liberator, and help before recounting rescue from mortal danger. The Communion antiphon puts those titles on the lips of those approaching or having received the sacrament.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Mystéria nos, Dómine, quǽsumus, sumpta puríficent: et suo múnere tueántur. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** after Communion, the Church asks that the mysteries already received purify the communicants and protect them by their own gift. The Postcommunion petitions for the sacrament's continuing effect rather than treating reception as self-completing.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.
