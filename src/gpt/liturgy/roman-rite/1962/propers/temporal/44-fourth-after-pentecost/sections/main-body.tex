\section*{The Propers: Themes and Movement}

\begin{massbox}{Divine action restores creaturely action}
The propers neither leave creation, Church, or will to save themselves nor reduce them to inert instruments. God illumines, orders, forgives, commands, receives, mercifully compels, purifies, and protects; creatures groan, hope, pray, obey, confess, follow, offer, and take refuge. Christ's word brings exhausted labor into apostolic service, while Romans keeps that service inside the larger hope of bodily and cosmic liberation.
\end{massbox}

\subsection*{Illumined life moves under received order\enspace\properrefs{Int., Coll., Ep.}}

\begin{threestable}{Appointed claim}{Decisive witness}{Precise sense}
\textbf{Light amid conflict} & Augustine, \emph{Psalm 26}, 1--3, joins divine illumination and salvation to courage under assault. & Confidence rests in God; the psalm does not call danger imaginary.\\
\textbf{A course directed} & The Collect's subject is \latin{mundi cursus}, a world in motion; God's order is the requested source of peace. Augustine, \emph{City of God} XIX.13, calls the peace of all things the tranquility of order. & His definition illuminates the prayer, though he does not comment on it; peace is not simply comfort or political quiet.\\
\textbf{Travail under hope} & Chrysostom, \emph{Romans} 14, reads creation's groaning as travail tending toward newness and the body's irreversible freedom from corruption. & Travail is not yet birth; firstfruits are not final possession.\\
\end{threestable}

\subsection*{Mercy, judgment, and command meet truthful need\enspace\properrefs{Grad., All., Gosp.}}

\begin{threestable}{Movement}{Text and inherited witness}{Precise sense}
Need speaks & The Gradual asks help, deliverance, and mercy toward sins for God's name. Augustine, \emph{Psalm 78}, 12--13, calls God the healer: help neither denies grace nor removes the action of the one helped. & The prayer seeks God's honor, not the worshipper's vindication.\\
Judgment shelters & The Alleluia names God as equitable Judge and refuge for the poor. Augustine, \emph{Psalm 9}, 6 and 10, distinguishes God's knowledge of hearts from human judgment and locates refuge in him. & Refuge does not cancel judgment; judgment does not make refuge unsafe.\\
The word gives and calls & Teaching precedes command; truthful failure and obedience precede the catch; confession, “fear not,” vocation, and following come after. Cyril keeps the result Christ's; Ambrose joins doctrine, humility, and office. & No promise makes every faithful undertaking measurably successful; abundance certifies Christ's power, not Peter's perfection.\\
\end{threestable}

\subsection*{Illumined eyes and rebellious wills are brought to God\enspace\properrefs{Off., Sec.}}

\begin{threestable}{Textual hinge}{What is established}{Witness and precise sense}
\latin{Illumina oculos meos} & Sight is requested as a gift against death and the enemy. & Augustine, \emph{Psalm 12}, 3--6, reads the heart's eyes, sin's eclipse, and steadfastness as mercy; illumination is not mere information.\\
\latin{Oblationibus ... susceptis} & An ablative absolute: the oblations have been received; acceptance remains God's act. & The offerers act truly, but this clause does not identify their wills as the oblations.\\
\latin{ad te ... voluntates} & The wills are the object of \latin{compelle}, and God himself is their personal end. & Conversion is more than improved performance; the syntax does not identify these wills with the oblations.\\
\latin{etiam rebelles compelle propitius} & Resistance is real, efficacious action is requested, and \latin{propitius} governs its merciful mode. & Augustine, \emph{Grace and Free Will} 31--33, and Chrysostom on Phil. 2:12--13 preserve gifted and real willing; the prayer warrants no human coercion.\\
\end{threestable}

\subsection*{The receiver rests in another's strength and still needs effect\enspace\properrefs{Comm., Postcomm.}}

\begin{threestable}{Proper}{Dependence named}{Decisive reading}
Communion & The Lord is strength, refuge, deliverer, God, and helper. & Augustine, \emph{Psalm 17}, 2--3, speaks in the voice of whole Christ: strength and hope begin in God's prior help, not proud reliance on self.\\
Postcommunion & Reception is complete (\latin{sumpta}), yet purification and protection remain petitions. & The sacramental gift is not treated as a trophy of visible success or an excuse to stop asking for grace.\\
\end{threestable}

\begin{studybox}{Hope, body, gift, and grace}
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=0.25em,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Hope tells the truth about pain.} Travail has a promised end; suffering is neither denied nor made saving by itself.
\item \textbf{Salvation remains embodied.} Romans awaits redemption of \emph{our body}; Irenaeus identifies the mortal body as the body God vivifies.
\item \textbf{Gift does not certify the recipient.} The catch brings Peter to confession and discipleship, not self-congratulation.
\item \textbf{Grace does not abolish agency.} God commands, compels mercifully, purifies, and protects; the person obeys, follows, offers, and asks.
\end{itemize}
\end{studybox}

\begin{massbox}{Three related divine works}
The world's \latin{cursus} is asked to be \emph{directed} by God's order; rebellious wills are the object of his merciful \emph{compelling}; the received mysteries are the subject of \emph{purifying} and \emph{protecting}. Governance, conversion, and sacramental effect are therefore related divine works, not interchangeable descriptions. The boat and net belong to Luke's narrative; any link between them and these works is exploratory, and neither image translates the orations.
\end{massbox}
