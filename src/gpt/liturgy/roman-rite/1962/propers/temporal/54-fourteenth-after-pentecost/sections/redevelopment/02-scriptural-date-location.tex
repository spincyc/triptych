\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

\begin{dossiertable}
\dossierrow{Offertory}{Ps.~33:8--9a (34)}{David's flight to Geth in the title's setting}{\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Douay title names Achimelech; 1~Kings~21:10--15 names Achis, king of Geth. The title-setting figure is reported-excluded in the corpus and does not date composition. Thanksgiving becomes instruction for those fearing God; the critical boundary applies to the Psalter, without securely dating this poem (Ps.~33; NABRE Psalms introduction; Haydock, 1~Kings~21).}
\midrule
\dossierrow{Introit}{Ps.~83:2--3a, 10--11a (84)}{Sons of Core; worship and ascent toward Sion}{\chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title supplies the Korahite attribution. Courts, altars and Sion locate the desired worship; neither an individual writer, composition site nor earthly royal addressee is named. The received range concerns the Korahite collection (Ps.~83; \emph{Catholic Encyclopedia}, ``Psalms'').}
\midrule
\dossierrow{Alleluia}{Ps.~94:1 (95)}{Israel summoned to its Creator and shepherd}{\chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Hebrews~4:7 receives the warning ``in David.'' The complete psalm joins praise to wilderness disobedience and refused rest; it names no first performance or writing place. The modern critical boundary is general, not an individual composition date (Ps.~94; Heb.~3--4; NABRE Psalms introduction).}
\midrule
\dossierrow{Gradual}{Ps.~117:8--9 (118)}{Israel, Aaron's house; gates and the Lord's altar}{\chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Personal deliverance becomes communal thanksgiving. Neither the speaker nor a particular historical procession is identified. Bellarmine uses received Davidic examples; the critical boundary dates no specific occasion (Ps.~117; Bellarmine, p.~370; NABRE Psalms introduction).}
\midrule
\dossierrow{Gospel}{Matt.~6:24--33}{Galilean teaching; disciples and crowds}{\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: Jesus ascends an unnamed mountain after teaching in Galilee; disciples approach and crowds hear (Matt.~4:23--5:2; 7:28--29).}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{St~Matthew is the received author. Among the disputed composition alternatives above, the \emph{Catholic Encyclopedia}'s ``Gospel of St.~Matthew'' (vol.~10) supplies all except ``about the year 50,'' supplied by ``The New Testament'' (vol.~14); both report ``about A.D. 38--45.'' Neither witness supplies a composition place, particular hill, or independent modern-critical date.}
\dossierrow{Communion}{Matt.~6:33, adapted}{Same Gospel; the kingdom command at reception}{\chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The antiphon abbreviates the Gospel's final verse (Matt.~6:31--34), with no separate biblical author, audience or composition place. Its disputed dates concern Matthew: both Encyclopedia articles report ``about A.D. 38--45''; vol.~14 also gives ``about the year 50,'' and vol.~10 supplies the other four alternatives. They do not date the chant.}
\midrule
\dossierrow{Epistle}{Gal.~5:16--24}{Paul to the churches of Galatia}{\chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Paul defends freedom, Abrahamic promise and sonship against compulsory circumcision among converts formerly serving non-gods; the letter fixes no writing site or north/south identification (Gal.~1--6). The preferred ``anno Christi 58'' is reported in a Lapide's proemium and \emph{Argumentum}. The Encyclopedia's ``St.~Paul'' (vol.~11) supplies ``(1 and 2 Corinthians; Galatians), 56''; ``Epistle to the Galatians'' (vol.~6) supplies the alternatives ``A.D. 49-50,'' ``about A.D. 53, 54,'' and ``A.D. 57 or 58.''}
\end{dossiertable}
