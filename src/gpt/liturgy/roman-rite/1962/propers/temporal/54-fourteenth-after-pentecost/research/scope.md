# Research scope and synthesis brief

## Work identity and governing bounds

This brief governs the GPT guide to the Fourteenth Sunday after Pentecost in
the 1962 Roman Missal. The controlling formulary is *Dominica Decima Quarta
post Pentecosten*, printed pp. 394--396, marginal nos. 1572--1581, in the
Vatican typical edition. The guide is a hand-missal companion, not an official
liturgical book, critical edition, or claim about the intention of an unknown
compiler.

The appointed Latin and the historical English used by the publication have
complete edition-identified controls and a recorded rights basis. The 1962
facsimile controls the Latin wording and boundaries; the 1922 Mame Missal is
the checked United States public-domain Latin antecedent; the 1861 Cummiskey
hand missal controls the English orations; and the registered
Douay--Rheims/Challoner artifacts control the English scriptural passages.
Historical English remains the wording of its own witness and is not described
as an approved 1962 translation. These controls are reported by COV-001.

This brief integrates the seven workflow lanes and does no research beyond
their joined findings. Evidence classes below use the proper-guide profile's
distinctions: textual observation, documented historical orientation,
documented reception, source-grounded synthesis, exploratory proposal, and
unverified lead. Missing source-library registration is recorded as provenance
maintenance, never mistaken for missing evidence where a lane checked the
work, edition, and locus.

## Organized brief

### Appointed scriptural contexts and boundaries

The formulary appoints six distinct biblical contexts: Psalms 33, 83, 94, and
117; Galatians 5:16--24; and Matthew 6:24--33. Matthew 6:33 returns in a
materially adapted Communion antiphon. The Collect, Secret, and
Postcommunion are composed orations rather than additional direct scriptural
appointments. (SCR-001)

- **Introit -- Psalm 83:10--11, 2--3.** The whole psalm moves from longing for
  the Lord's courts through dwelling, ascent, and vision in Sion to prayer for
  the protector and anointed one, then to grace, glory, innocence, and trust.
  The Introit reverses the whole psalm's sequence by placing vv. 10--11 before
  vv. 2--3, stops v. 11 before the contrast with sinners' tents, and stops v. 3
  before the heart-and-flesh clause. The appointed cut therefore foregrounds
  protector, anointed face, surpassing courts, and longing. (SCR-002)
- **Epistle -- Galatians 5:16--24.** The pericope is the central contrast in
  Paul's appeal to use Christian freedom for charity. Flesh and Spirit oppose
  one another; works and fruit disclose their direction; and those who belong
  to Christ crucify the flesh with its vices and desires. The cut presupposes
  vv. 13--15 on liberty, charity, and communal strife, and ends before vv.
  25--26 renew the command to walk in the Spirit. Paul's morally charged
  “flesh” must not be equated with bodily nature. (SCR-003, SCR-010)
- **Gradual -- Psalm 117:8--9.** The appointed preference for trust in the
  Lord over man and princes belongs to a communal thanksgiving moving from
  distress and encircling enemies through salvation, gates of justice, the
  rejected cornerstone, the day made by the Lord, and festal approach to the
  altar. The cornerstone and altar occur in the complete psalm, not in the
  appointed two verses; Matthew 21:42, Acts 4:8--12, and 1 Peter 2:4--8 supply
  the explicit canonical christological relation. (SCR-004)
- **Alleluia -- Psalm 94:1.** The joyful summons opens a psalm that confesses
  the Lord as creator, king, shepherd, and savior before turning to present
  obedience and warning against the wilderness generation's hardness. Hebrews
  3:7--4:11 explicitly develops the psalm's “today” and “rest”; 1 Corinthians
  10:1--13 supplies a related typological warning about the wilderness
  generation. (SCR-005)
- **Gospel -- Matthew 6:24--33.** The passage completes a movement from
  earthly and heavenly treasure through the single or evil eye to indivisible
  service. Freedom from anxiety rests on the Father's knowledge and care, and
  the unit culminates in seeking first his kingdom and justice. Verse 25's
  “therefore” makes the treatment of anxiety depend on the demand for
  undivided service; the liturgical cut ends at the positive summit in v. 33
  and leaves v. 34 outside it. The broader Sermon supplies the Father's prior
  knowledge, the prayer for kingdom and daily bread, hunger for justice, and
  confident asking. (SCR-006)
- **Offertory -- Psalm 33:8--9a.** The angel's encampment and the invitation
  to taste divine sweetness belong to David's thanksgiving for deliverance
  and its instruction in fear of the Lord, righteous speech and action,
  contrition, and trust in redemption. The superscription names Achimelech,
  while 1 Kings 21 names Achimelech as priest at Nobe and Achis as the king of
  Geth before whom David changes his behavior; the mismatch remains explicit.
  The Offertory ends before the blessing on the one who hopes in the Lord,
  while the next verses promise no lack to those who fear and seek him.
  (SCR-007)
- **Communion -- adapted Matthew 6:33.** Against the Gospel's *Quaerite ergo
  primum regnum Dei, et iustitiam eius: et haec omnia adiicientur vobis*, the
  Communion omits *ergo* and *et iustitiam eius*, contracts *haec omnia* to
  *omnia*, and adds *dicit Dominus*. The adaptation preserves kingdom priority
  and promised addition but does not independently carry the Gospel's justice
  clause. (SCR-008)

For Matthew's historical orientation, Papias as transmitted by Eusebius says
that Matthew arranged the *logia* in a Hebrew dialect or language and that
each interpreted them as able; Irenaeus says that Matthew issued a written
Gospel among the Hebrews in their dialect while Peter and Paul evangelized
and founded the Church at Rome. These are verified reports made by the extant
patristic witnesses, not direct evidence that settles the relation of the
*logia* to canonical Greek Matthew, Hebrew versus Aramaic, a translator,
literary dependence, audience, composition place, or an absolute date.
Papias's statement survives indirectly through Eusebius; Irenaeus is
late-second-century testimony, preserved here in the ancient Latin tradition
and in the Greek clause quoted by Eusebius. (SCR-014, PAT-020--PAT-021,
COV-011)

First Peter 2:3--7 supplies a canonical bridge between the Offertory and the
Gradual's complete psalm: v. 3 reuses Psalm 33:9, and vv. 4--7 immediately
apply Psalm 117:22 to Christ the living cornerstone and extend the stone image
to believers as a spiritual house and holy priesthood. This is 1 Peter's own
sequence, not evidence that the Gradual appoints Psalm 117:22 or that the
formulary compiler intended the bridge. (SCR-009)

## Strongest cross-proper argument

The strongest available argument is that the formulary orders desire,
freedom, bodily life, agency, provision, and sacramental reception under God
and his kingdom as their final end. It should be redistilled into the
following five functional units rather than narrated element by element.

### 1. Desire and allegiance receive one order

The Introit longs for God's courts; the Gradual prefers refuge in the Lord to
confidence in men or princes; the Gospel excludes divided mastery and commands
the kingdom and God's justice to be sought first; and the adapted Communion
repeats kingdom priority at reception while omitting the justice phrase.
Augustine and Bellarmine read Psalm 83's better day within pilgrimage toward
God, while Chrysostom and Aquinas read Matthew's contrast through rival mastery
and final ends.

Evidence and bounds: Missale Romanum nos. 1572, 1575, 1577, 1580; Augustine,
*Enarratio in Psalmum 83* §§3--7, 13--15; Bellarmine, *Explanatio in
Psalmos* at Ps. 83:1--3, 9--10; Chrysostom, Homilies 21--22 on Matthew;
Aquinas, *Super Evangelium S. Matthaei lectura* 6, lect. 5, §[87342]. This
is source-grounded synthesis (THE-001). Psalm 117 states comparative final
trust, not rejection of every human or political mediation, and justice may
not be silently restored to the contracted Communion wording.

### 2. Freedom becomes filial and charitable service

Galatians places adoption, liberty, Spirit-led walking, and service through
charity in one Pauline argument; the appointed Epistle gives its flesh/Spirit
conflict, while the Gospel's two masters exclude masterlessness as well as
divided allegiance. Chrysostom treats love as the governing form of the
Spirit's fruit; Augustine describes delighted justice rather than legal
compulsion; Aquinas distinguishes freedom from coercion while retaining the
law's obligation.

Evidence and bounds: Gal. 4:1--7; 5:1, 13--24; Matt. 6:24--33; Missale
Romanum nos. 1574 and 1577; Chrysostom, Homily 5 on Galatians; Augustine,
*Expositio Epistolae ad Galatas* §§46--53; Aquinas, *Super Galatas* 5,
lect. 5--7, §§[87778]--[87780]. This is source-grounded synthesis
(THE-002). Paul's sonship/bondage, Spirit/flesh, and freedom/misuse contrasts
remain distinct from Matthew's mastery image; the relation is doctrinal
convergence, not literary dependence or compiler intent.

### 3. Bodily life is not Pauline flesh

The Collect names mortal human weakness without condemning creaturehood; the
Epistle identifies flesh through a morally diverse list that includes
idolatry, rivalry, anger, dissension, and envy; the Gospel values bodily life
above food and clothing and places its needs under the Father's care; and the
Offertory's tasting gives embodied language a positive spiritual use.
Chrysostom explicitly identifies Paul's flesh with depraved moral purpose
rather than bodily nature, while Augustine and Theodoret receive Psalm 33's
tasting christologically and sacramentally.

Evidence and bounds: Missale Romanum nos. 1573--1574, 1577--1578;
Chrysostom, Homily 5 on Galatians; Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 33*,
sermo I, §§10--11; Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos* at Ps. 33:8--11,
PG 80:1103--1106. This is source-grounded synthesis (THE-003). It rules out
a soul/body reduction but does not supply an exhaustive anthropology or make
Psalm 33's Eucharistic reception its exclusive literal sense.

### 4. Dependent agency acts within conflict

The Collect asks for continual help and direction; the Epistle commands
walking and crucifying vice; the Gradual orders reliance toward God; the
Gospel commands seeking without anxious self-torment; the Offertory places
angelic protection around those who fear the Lord; and the Postcommunion asks
for continuing purification and strengthening. Augustine, Theodoret, and
Bellarmine subordinate created help to God without erasing it, while
Chrysostom and Aquinas preserve honest work and moral exertion under divine
providence.

Evidence and bounds: Missale Romanum nos. 1573--1575, 1577--1578, 1581;
Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 117* §4; Theodoret, *Interpretatio in
Psalmos* at Ps. 117:8--9, PG 80:1811--1812; Bellarmine, *Explanatio in
Psalmos* at Ps. 117:8--9; Chrysostom, Homilies 21--22 on Matthew; Aquinas,
*Super Evangelium S. Matthaei lectura* 6, lect. 5, §[87342]. This is
source-grounded synthesis (THE-004, THE-006). It promises neither immunity
from hardship nor prosperity, and it supports neither passive providence nor
effort as an independent source of salvation.

### 5. Saving direction reaches sacramental action and final effect

The Collect asks to be directed toward saving things; the Alleluia praises
God as Savior; the Secret names the saving Victim offered for cleansing and
propitiation; the Gospel and Communion put the kingdom first; and the
Postcommunion asks that the sacraments purify, strengthen, and lead to the
lasting effect of salvation. Theodoret's Psalm 94 reading moves from historical
victory to Christ's saving victory, Augustine reads Psalm 33's tasting through
reception of Christ, and Schuster reads this proper's later action through
protection, struggle, providence, angelic deliverance, and kingdom priority.

Evidence and bounds: Missale Romanum nos. 1573, 1576--1581; Cummiskey,
Sunday XIV after Pentecost, Collect p. 425 and Secret/Postcommunion p. 427;
Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos* at Ps. 94:1, PG 80:1639--1642;
Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 33*, sermo I, §§10--11; Schuster,
*The Sacramentary*, vol. III (London, 1927), pp. 136--138. This is source-grounded
synthesis (THE-005, LIT-007). The shared salvation word-group does not prove
an identical technical sense at every occurrence or automatic sacramental
fruit.

## Passage-by-passage reception matrix

### Psalm 83:10--11, 2--3 -- Proper 54, Introit

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 83*
  §§3--7, 13--15 (Latin work; NPNF I.8 English witness), on the Church under
  pressure, purified desire, eternity, and Catholic humility (PAT-001).
  Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 83:2--3, 10--11, PG
  80:1537--1546 (Greek with Latin in the PG scan), first preserves Babylonian
  return and Jewish worship, then extends the psalm to Christian altar and
  resurrection hope (PAT-002).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Bellarmine,
  *Explanatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 83:1--3, 9--10 (Latin Doctor; O'Sullivan's 1866
  abridged English witness), reads the pilgrim soul's longing for heaven, the
  earthly altar as temporary nest and pledge, Christ's merits as protection,
  and the heavenly day above temporal good (PAT-011).
- **Use:** compare Augustine's ecclesial humility, Theodoret's historical and
  christological stages, and Bellarmine's pilgrim/altar horizon. Do not treat
  the abridged Bellarmine translation as preserving material its preface says
  it omits.
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** direct Latin and Greek psalm
  expositions and the later Latin Doctoral witness were checked at the loci
  above. The current evidence supplies no first-compiler or compositional-plan
  claim for the Introit cut.

### Galatians 5:16--24 -- Proper 54, Epistle

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Chrysostom, Homily 5 on Galatians at
  Gal. 5:16--24 (Greek homiletic commentary through NPNF I.13), rejects an
  anti-body reading and makes love the root and governing form of the
  Spirit's fruit (PAT-003). Augustine, *Expositio Epistolae ad Galatas*
  §§46--53, PL 35:2138--2143 (Latin), locates the conflict in grace-enabled
  combat, distinguishes feeling concupiscence from consenting to it, and
  contrasts delighted justice with legal compulsion (PAT-004).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Aquinas, *Super
  Epistolam B. Pauli ad Galatas*, cap. 5, lect. 5--7, §§[87778]--[87780]
  (medieval Latin, Corpus Thomisticum), reads Spirit-governed freedom from
  coercion, grace-produced virtues as sweet fruit, and crucifixion of the
  flesh as discipline of vice rather than destruction of nature (PAT-012).
- **Use:** preserve Chrysostom's, Augustine's, and Aquinas's distinct accounts
  of body, consent, law, love, grace, and pastoral list-making. SBLGNT v1.2
  and RP2018 at Gal. 5:22--23 support the nine-term Greek list against the
  twelve-term Latin/Vulgate list; the difference supports neither numerology
  nor a judgment of theological adequacy (COV-009).
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** Greek and Latin direct commentary,
  medieval Latin commentary, the Vulgate/Greek textual witnesses, and the
  registered USCCB/NABRE Introduction to Galatians were checked at the named
  loci. The complete composition-place, audience, life-stage, and modern
  critical dossier remains incomplete (COV-008).

### Psalm 117:8--9 -- Proper 54, Gradual

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 117*
  §4 (Latin work; NPNF I.8 English witness), makes good human or angelic aid
  an instrument of God without making it the final refuge (PAT-005).
  Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 117:8--9, PG 80:1811--1812
  (Greek with Latin in PG), contrasts God's immutable power and beneficence
  with mutable, mortal, sometimes unwilling or unable human agents (PAT-006).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Bellarmine,
  *Explanatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 117:8--9 (Latin Doctor; O'Sullivan's abridged
  English witness), keeps subordinate trust in angels or pious people only
  when referred through them to God (PAT-013).
- **Use:** preserve Augustine's instrumental account, Theodoret's sharper
  contrast, and Bellarmine's explicit qualification as distinct accents.
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** direct Greek and Latin psalm
  exposition and later Doctoral reception were checked at the named loci.
  Hugh of Saint-Cher's *Postilla super Psalterium* at Ps. 117:8--9 is an
  additional medieval Latin citation reported by COV-004, but no
  source-library record presently covers it (COV-004); that absence is
  provenance maintenance, not a claim-support defect.

### Psalm 94:1 -- Proper 54, Alleluia

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 94*
  §§2--3 (Latin work; NPNF I.8 English witness), reads approach as restored
  likeness and good life and jubilation as joy in the Lord beyond articulate
  speech (PAT-007). Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 94:1, PG
  80:1639--1642 (Greek with Latin in PG), distinguishes a Josianic victory
  setting from christological and ecclesial appropriation by apostles,
  martyrs, and imitators (PAT-008).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Bellarmine,
  *Explanatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 94:1 (Latin Doctor; O'Sullivan's abridged
  English witness), orders interior joy and admiration before voiced praise
  (PAT-014).
- **Use:** compare Augustine's inward approach, Theodoret's historical and
  christological stages, and Bellarmine's heart-and-lips ordering.
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** direct Greek and Latin psalm
  exposition and a later Doctoral witness were checked at the named loci.
  The cultural-afterlife lane found no qualifying redirected reuse for the
  Alleluia in its bounded English web/book-indexing sweep (CUL-010).

### Matthew 6:24--33 -- Proper 54, Gospel

- **Historical testimony checked:** Papias's notice, preserved by Eusebius,
  *Historia ecclesiastica* III.39.16, says Matthew arranged the *logia* in a
  Hebrew dialect or language and that each interpreted them as able
  (SCR-014, PAT-020). Irenaeus, *Adversus haereses* III.1.1, with its Greek
  clause also preserved by Eusebius at *Historia ecclesiastica* V.8.2, says
  Matthew issued a written Gospel among the Hebrews in their dialect while
  Peter and Paul evangelized and founded the Church at Rome (SCR-014,
  PAT-021). These witnesses support only those attributed reports: they do
  not identify the *logia* with the complete extant Greek Gospel, decide
  Hebrew versus Aramaic, name a translator, or settle authorship, dependence,
  audience, place, or absolute date (COV-011).

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Chrysostom, Homilies 21--22 on
  Matthew at Matt. 6:24--33 (Greek homiletic commentary through NPNF I.10),
  treats wealth as a rival master while preserving honest work and grounding
  confidence in the Father (PAT-016). Augustine, *De sermone Domini in
  monte* II.14--17 §§47--58 (Latin continuous commentary through NPNF I.6),
  distinguishes necessities used from the kingdom sought as final good and
  rejects both idleness and a prosperity guarantee (PAT-017).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Aquinas, *Super
  Evangelium S. Matthaei lectura*, cap. 6, lect. 5, §[87342] (medieval Latin,
  Corpus Thomisticum), reads rival mastery by final ends, limits forbidden
  solicitude to excessive or despairing attachment, preserves necessary
  labor, and interprets kingdom and justice under divine rule (PAT-018).
- **Use:** develop the Fathers' and Aquinas's positive account of undivided
  service, providence, work, and final ends. Keep permitted stewardship,
  medicinal deprivation, and ordinary labor beside the claims they qualify.
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** Greek and Latin direct commentary
  and medieval Latin commentary were checked across the whole pericope. The
  chronology corpus gives composition claims only and no narrated-event
  assertion for the Sermon (SCR-006, SCR-013). The complete composition-place,
  first-audience, authorial-life-stage, and modern-critical dossier remains
  incomplete (COV-008).

### Psalm 33:8--9a -- Proper 54, Offertory

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Augustine, *Enarratio in Psalmum 33*,
  sermo I, §§10--11 (Latin work; NPNF I.8 English witness), identifies the
  encamping angel christologically and reads tasting through John 6 as
  reception of Christ's body and blood (PAT-009). Theodoret,
  *Interpretatio in Psalmos*, Ps. 33:8--11, PG 80:1103--1106 (Greek with
  Latin in PG), moves from literal angelic guardianship to experiential
  knowledge and a baptismal/Eucharistic mystery while joining fear to love
  (PAT-010).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Aquinas, *In psalmos
  Davidis expositio*, Ps. 33 nn. 8--9, §§[87112]--[87113] (medieval Latin,
  Corpus Thomisticum), permits angelic, christological, and ecclesial-prelate
  senses and treats tasting as inward experiential knowledge preceding sight
  (PAT-015).
- **Use:** retain the literal angelic sense and compare Augustine's explicit
  Eucharistic emphasis, Theodoret's baptismal/Eucharistic movement, and
  Aquinas's experiential account rather than harmonizing them into one
  exclusive reading.
- **Search/corpus record and negatives:** direct Greek and Latin exposition
  and medieval Latin commentary were checked at the named loci. The Psalm
  superscription/1 Kings mismatch and the omitted hope clause are material
  textual controls (SCR-007), and the Offertory promises neither a personal
  guardian angel nor physical immunity.

### Adapted Matthew 6:33 -- Proper 54, Communion

- **Direct ancient exegesis checked:** Chrysostom, Homilies 21--22 on
  Matthew, and Augustine, *De sermone Domini in monte* II.14--17 §§47--58,
  interpret Matthew 6:33 inside the Sermon sequence, not as the contracted
  Communion formula (PAT-019).
- **Medieval, Doctoral, or later reception checked:** Aquinas, *Super
  Evangelium S. Matthaei lectura*, cap. 6, lect. 5, §[87342], likewise
  interprets the Gospel verse in context. A bounded search for independent
  treatment of the exact contracted formula checked Aquinas's medieval
  Matthew commentary and Catena, indexed PL leads, the identified Greek and
  Latin direct commentaries, web-indexed Latin reception, and Cantus Database
  ID 004377; exact-form hits were liturgical chant witnesses rather than
  independent exegesis (PAT-019).
- **Use:** source-grounded prose may compare the Gospel and Communion's exact
  verbal operations. Independent doctrinal content comes from the Gospel and
  its contextual reception. PRE-008 bars a proposal founded on repetition
  alone unless sacramental placement, grammatical recasting, appetite,
  allegiance, or another further mechanism performs actual interpretive work.
- **Material bounded negative:** no checked patristic, medieval, Doctoral, or
  later saintly witness treats *Primum quaerite regnum Dei, et omnia
  adiicientur vobis, dicit Dominus* as an independent scriptural unit. The
  search covered the named Greek, Latin, medieval Latin, indexed PL, web-Latin,
  and chant loci; it remains correctable by an unindexed sermon or commentary
  on the chant formula. (PAT-019)

## Liturgical development and competing historical judgments

1. **Ninth-century Gregorian supplement -- the orations at Sunday XV.**
   Vatican Reg. lat. 337 and Ottob. lat. 313 transmit the Collect, Secret,
   and Postcommunion consecutively at *Dominica XV post Pentecosten*; Wilson
   dates both manuscripts to the ninth century and considers Ottoboni, and
   possibly Reginensis, earlier than 850. Gellone II (1000--1025) and
   Vilabertran (third quarter of the twelfth century) retain the same
   Sunday-XV prayer placement. Cambrai 164 lacks the supplement and therefore
   does not witness the set. Ottoboni's marginal chant cues form a different
   constellation, so these early prayers do not establish the later complete
   formulary. (LIT-002)
2. **Early antiphoners -- a nucleus without a uniform Alleluia.** At Hesbert
   no. 186, Blandin, Corbie, and Senlis agree on *Protector noster*,
   *Immittet angelus Domini*, and *Panis*; only Corbie and Senlis have
   *Bonum est confidere*, while Blandin supplies other Graduals. Corbie and
   Blandin have no Alleluia, Senlis has *Jubilate Deo*, and Hesbert's
   Compiègne column reconstructs *Confitemini Domino* from the Maurist
   edition because the relevant leaves are lost. Monza is partial and Rheinau
   has a lacuna. The surviving evidence establishes a non-uniform early
   state, not contrary full formularies in unavailable leaves. (LIT-009--
   LIT-010)
3. **Late ninth or early tenth century -- Laon MS 239.** The diplomatic
   transcription gives Sunday XIV *Protector noster*, *Bonum est confidere*,
   *Confitemini Domino*, *Immittet angelus Domini*, and *Panis quem ego*;
   *Venite exsultemus* and *Primum quaerite* occur at Sundays XII and IX.
   The source metadata gives a ninth-century/c. 875--900 structured date but
   also a prose suggestion of about 930; the inconsistency remains open. The
   database transcription, not the inaccessible interactive folio, controls
   this entry. (LIT-003)
4. **1299 -- Valkenburg Gradual.** All five later Roman chants appear together
   and in the same order for Sunday XIV in the checked Cologne Franciscan
   facsimile and database collation. This is a lower bound in one witness, not
   an origin date or proof of universality. (LIT-004)
5. **1474 onward -- assembled Roman-Curia formulary.** The 1474 Milan
   Roman-Curia Missal contains the complete core constellation of Introit,
   three orations, Epistle, intervening chants, Gospel, Offertory, and
   Gospel-derived Communion. Lippe's pre-1570 collation, the 1570 Roman
   Missal, the 1922 Mame book, and the 1962 typical edition show substantive
   continuity. The checked 1474 evidence is Lippe's critical reprint and its
   apparatus, not a claim that every local use or earlier witness shared the
   assembly. (LIT-001)
6. **Competing schemes and later labels.** Hesbert's six early antiphoner
   traditions organize the post-Pentecost Sundays as continuous numbered
   series and do not use the Lawrence--Cyprian--Michael headings. Guéranger
   reports the
   Western nickname “Sunday of the two masters.” Schuster's *Quarta post
   Sancti Laurentii* reflects older Roman seasonal numbering by the feast
   milestones of Lawrence, Cyprian, and Michael rather than a dedication to
   those saints. The six antiphoners do not disprove the milestone system in
   other sources, but the two schemes may not be collapsed. Schuster also
   interprets the late action as angelic
   guardianship, saving sacrifice, ordered seeking, and sacramental
   purification and strengthening; that sequence remains his attributed
   exposition, not compiler testimony. (LIT-005, LIT-007, LIT-011)
7. **Temporal-cycle reuse.** The 1962 Missal reuses *Immittet Angelus* on
   Thursday after Lent I and *Bonum est confidere* on Friday after Lent IV.
   Recurrence does not establish origin or transfer direction. (LIT-006)

The checked evidence does not identify the first compiler, determine the
redactional stages or motive behind Sunday XV/Sunday XIV placement, or date
the exact stages by which *Panis quem ego* and variable Alleluias yielded the
later *Primum quaerite* and *Venite exsultemus* assignments. The current
Hesbert check narrows the early chant state but does not supply a universal
stemmata or one change date. Deshusses's modern critical Gregorian edition,
Tomasi's underlying apparatus, and further ninth- through thirteenth-century
local series remain named uninspected leads; Guéranger's report about Tomasi
is not manuscript evidence by itself. (LIT-008--LIT-010, COV-007)

## Interpretive-proposal audit

The following six proposals are selected from the precedent-search lane. Each
is exploratory and must remain in `The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities`,

### P1. Created help beneath undivided mastery

- **Anchors:** Gradual, Ps. 117:8--9 (men and princes); Gospel, Matt. 6:24
  (two masters); Offertory, Ps. 33:8--9 (encircling angel).
- **Mechanism and fruit:** distinguish dependent created mediation from the
  exclusive mastery and final trust owed to God. The conjunction lets the
  reader hold human authority and angelic ministry positively without making
  either an ultimate refuge.
- **Nearest precedent/analogue:** the same-identity Claude P5 places Gradual
  and Offertory in an unresolved tension; Claude's Eleventh-Sunday guide
  supplies the method of preserving real disagreement.
- **Search boundary:** both providers' 1962 proper interpretive sections and
  scope records were searched for princes, angel, two masters, mediation, and
  mastery. No guide outside the target made the exact three-element relation.
- **Classification:** `near analogue located` (PRE-002).
- **Strongest limit:** the Gospel anchor is not cited by Augustine at Psalm
  117; no compiler intent follows; legitimate authority, expertise, and
  angelic ministry must not be disparaged.

### P2. Courts, encircling camp, and anxious enclosure

- **Nearest precedent/analogue:** GPT's Fifteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time,
