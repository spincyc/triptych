\subsection{The Collect of the Third Sunday}
\label{sec:advent-gaudete-collect}

When it is hard to see your way, return to the petition the priest makes
at Gaudete Mass. This English text is the Marquess of Bute's rendering
of the same prayer at Lauds, the morning Office, in his \emph{Roman
Breviary}, Winter, p. 243.

O LORD, we beseech Thee, mercifully incline Thine ears unto our prayers, and lighten the darkness of our minds by the grace of Thy heavenly visitation; Who livest and reignest with God the Father, in the unity of the Holy Ghost, one God, world without end. Amen.

\subsection{Words for prayer during the four weeks}

These prayers come from the Douay--Rheims Bible. Start with the verse
in today's reading. If you are praying with someone, you can read
St Paul's blessing aloud for each other.

\subsubsection*{Lifting the soul to God, Psalm 24:1--2}

To thee, O Lord, have I lifted up my soul. In thee, O my God, I put my trust; let me not be ashamed.

\subsubsection*{Asking to be taught, Psalm 24:4b--5}

Shew, O Lord, thy ways to me, and teach me thy paths. Direct me in thy truth, and teach me; for thou art God my Saviour; and on thee have I waited all the day long.

\subsubsection*{A clean heart, Psalm 50:12}

Create a clean heart in me, O God: and renew a right spirit within my bowels.

\subsubsection*{For help now, Psalm 69:2}

O God, come to my assistance; O Lord, make haste to help me.

\subsubsection*{With one mouth, Romans 15:5--6}

Now the God of patience and of comfort grant you to be of one mind, one towards another, according to Jesus Christ: That with one mind and with one mouth you may glorify God and the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.

\subsubsection*{For someone who waits, Romans 15:13}

Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing: that you may abound in hope and in the power of the Holy Ghost.

\subsubsection*{The petition for dew, Isaiah 45:8}

Drop down dew, ye heavens, from above, and let the clouds rain the just: let the earth be opened, and bud forth a saviour: and let justice spring up together: I the Lord have created him.

\subsubsection*{Mary's answer, Luke 1:38}

Behold the handmaid of the Lord: be it done to me according to thy word.

\subsubsection*{Thomas's confession, John 20:28}

My Lord and my God.

\subsubsection*{At the crib on Christmas night, Luke 2:14}

Glory to God in the highest: and on earth peace to men of good will.

\subsection{The Magnificat and the seven O Antiphons}
\label{sec:advent-evenings}

You can pray Mary's song by itself any evening. From December 17 to 23,
say the antiphon for the date, pray the canticle, then say the antiphon
again. The canticle here is Luke 1:46--55 in the Douay--Rheims. The
antiphons are from the Marquess of Bute's English Roman Breviary.
The short Scripture passages beside them are readings for home. If you
are praying around a table, one person can read the passage and another
can start the antiphon.

\subsubsection*{Mary's song}

My soul doth magnify the Lord.

And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour.

Because he hath regarded the humility of his handmaid: for behold from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed.

Because he that is mighty hath done great things to me: and holy is his name.

And his mercy is from generation unto generations, to them that fear him.

He hath shewed might in his arm: he hath scattered the proud in the conceit of their heart.

He hath put down the mighty from their seat and hath exalted the humble.

He hath filled the hungry with good things: and the rich he hath sent empty away.

He hath received Israel his servant, being mindful of his mercy.

As he spoke to our fathers: to Abraham and to his seed for ever.

\subsubsection*{December 17. O Wisdom}

Read Wisdom 8:1. Bring to mind a decision for which you need prudence.

O WISDOM, That comest out of the mouth of the Most High, That reachest from one end to another, and dost mightily and sweetly order all things: come, to teach us the way of prudence!

\subsubsection*{December 18. O Adonai}

Read Exodus 3:1--10. Hear what the Lord already knows about the people whose cry has reached him.

O ADONAI, and Ruler of the house of Israël, Who didst appear unto Moses in the burning bush, and gavest him the law in Sinai: come, to redeem us with an outstretched arm!

\subsubsection*{December 19. O Root of Jesse}

Read Isaiah 11:1--10. Pray for one of the peoples looking to the son of David for justice.

O ROOT of Jesse, Which standest for an ensign of the people, at Whom the kings shall shut their mouths, unto Whom the Gentiles shall seek: come, to deliver us, make no tarrying!

\subsubsection*{December 20. O Key of David}

Read Isaiah 22:22 and 42:7. Remember a prisoner by name before asking Christ to open the door.

O KEY of David, and Sceptre of the house of Israël; That openest, and no man shutteth; and shuttest and no man openeth: come, to bring out the prisoners from the prison, and them that sit in darkness, and in the shadow of death!

\subsubsection*{December 21. O Dayspring}

Read Luke 1:76--79. Bring to prayer a person keeping watch in the dark.

O DAY-SPRING, Brightness of the everlasting Light, Sun of Righteousness, come, to give light to them that sit in darkness, and in the shadow of death!

\subsubsection*{December 22. O King of the Gentiles}

Read Ephesians 2:14--20. Listen for the two peoples Christ makes one and the place he has made for strangers.

O KING of the Gentiles, yea, and Desire thereof, O Corner-stone that makest of twain one: come, to save man, whom Thou hast made of the dust of the earth!

\subsubsection*{December 23. O Emmanuel}

Read Matthew 1:22--25. Stay for a moment with the name that means God with us.

O EMMANUEL, our King and our Law-giver, Longing of the Gentiles, yea, and Salvation thereof: come to save us, O Lord our God!

\subsection{Alma Redemptoris Mater}

You can close your evening prayer with this antiphon to Our Lady.
The English is from Bute's Roman Breviary.

Maiden! Mother of Him Who redeemed us, thou that abidest Heaven’s open gate, and the Star of the Sea, come, succour the fallen! Fallen indeed we are, but fain would rise by thy succour. Thou that beyond nature’s course hast borne in time the Eternal; Thou that a Virgin before, and after that childbirth remainest, From the Archangel’s lips the quickening message receiving, Mother of JESUS and us, turn thine eyes of mercy on sinners.
