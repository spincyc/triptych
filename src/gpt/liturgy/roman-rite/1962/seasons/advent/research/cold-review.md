# Reading and accuracy reviews

On November 20, 2026, a fresh writer used the section plan and collated
source records to draft the six body files. The writer did not consult
the Claude edition's manuscript. Two reviewers then read the draft
independently.

## Reader review

The reader received only the ten reader-facing files and no research
records or prior Advent edition. The reading identified the bread in
Bernard, the winter stores in Leo, Paul's thanksgiving in prison, and
Cyril's manger-table as passages to retain. It proposed five changes.

| Finding | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| The opening assumed a calendar day and the five-minute rule had no starting passage for a reader arriving early. The fast lacked a frequency. | Start with Psalm 24 after supper, offer one action for that evening and make the suggested fast weekly. |
| Definitions interrupted the movement from Luke and Isaiah to John and Bernard. The vacant-Sunday heading introduced a problem the chapter did not teach. | Put the book definitions in About this book, explain the Hours at their first use, remove the unneeded vacant heading and tighten Bernard's paragraph. |
| The Sunday advice often came in an indirect phrase. Introductory Mass wording and the Fourth Sunday's valley metaphor needed precision. | Name the entrance chant, put Gregory's meditation on the dying life-giver next to his words, give the Second Sunday's action directly and let John's conduct illustrate the valley. |
| The journey from Luke to Nazianzus gave every burial detail the same weight. The last words needed their setting in Jerusalem. | Retain one sentence about the arrest and the two scenes of wrapping, then go to the Resurrection and the altar. Identify when the crowd first spoke the words of the Sanctus. |
| Some weekly patristic readings were difficult to locate. The law occurred twice and the references did not match two Fathers actually quoted. | Link directly to the readings and the Bute pages, direct readers to the Vigil on December 24, give the law at length once, and correct Leo and Augustine. |

## Expanded edition

The maintainer asked for an expansive main study that could subsequently
supply synopsis editions. The expansion added four independent teaching
lanes in the same sequence and used the existing evidence where possible.
The complete Gibson Conferences were acquired. The additions were checked
against the exact loci in the expanded source audit. A reader who had
seen only the initial manuscript reviewed all five expanded sections
without the source or dispatch records.

The reader asked to move the Baptist's reed back into the discussion of
the Gospel before Paul's Epistle. That order, a single practical
application, and the chronological order of the O Antiphons are restored.
The history keeps Nathan's two houses and the difference in dismissal
between the 1570 and 1962 books, and drops incidental punctuation about a
vacant Sunday. The daily rule applies after a missed day as well as a
missed evening, and the confession section now speaks of absolution.
The Marian opening starts at her consent and does not recount the
Visitation again. The closing returns to Titus's instruction for how to
live. The arrest remains in one sentence, at the maintainer's request,
while Luke's swaddling and burial and Nazianzen's use of them carry the
argument.

## Formation revision

A fresh reader read the complete study and both practical appendices in book
order. A second reviewer checked attributions, the calendar and Scripture
against the witnesses. The following revisions resulted.

| What a reader or source check found | Revision |
| --- | --- |
| The opening left the last evenings ambiguous. Instructions about restarting after a missed day or the Immaculate Conception could send a reader to the wrong week. | Give the O dates at the door, and resume with the row for the day actually reached. |
| The first uses of the Missal, Breviary and Magnificat preceded their explanations. Calendar arithmetic and the dismissal delayed the historical story. | Identify the books and canticle where the reader encounters them. Shorten the counting and dismissal and name the three Gelasian prayers as petitions of one Mass. |
| Zachary blesses God before addressing John. His canticle was too easily mistaken for a Sunday Mass reading. Augustine was Proba's correspondent. Francis de Sales compares two occupations with religious lives. | Correct the order and speakers and keep the family's Gospel at home. Restore the comparisons to their named sources. |
| The movement from sorrow for sin to bereavement on Gaudete Sunday needed room. Ember Wednesday withheld Mary's answer. Paul wrote to Rome. | Let Paul's thanksgiving and prayer for peace speak to grief, give Mary's consent in Luke, and identify Isaiah's words in Paul's letter. |
| The Gospel at the crib gives the shepherds news before a sign. Several works cited in the body lacked their own references. | Restore “born to you,” remove the repeated explanation of the sign, and identify the two new historical books, Ambrose, Pius XII and Leo's December sermon in the references. |
| December 21 can coincide with two Ember Days. The Office can commemorate an Advent feria at a feast's Vespers. A Douay–Rheims reading stopped one verse before the promised flesh. | Cover Friday and Saturday, the Mass at which ordination takes place, and the Sunday exception. Qualify the Office appointment. Read John 6 through verse 52 and give an unambiguous Exodus reference for Sinai. |

The final accuracy read also distinguished the deacon's dismissal at Solemn
Mass, directed the reader to Bute p. 230 for the reed, and kept the Sinai
passage and the verse numbering of John explicit.
