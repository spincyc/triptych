# Advent source audit

The three research lanes read the history and rubrics, the four Sunday
formularies and their Fathers, and the sources on prayer and the Nativity.
Collation resolved their loci in the shared library before the fresh draft.
The source bindings pin those records to the claims below. A passage-level
record marks the extent verified there. Whole-text bindings identify the
particular portions inspected for this book.

| Claim key | What was checked | Limit or editorial decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| coming-jerusalem | Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechesis 15.1–3, describes the first coming and the second, with contrasts in clothing, glory and judgment. Gifford's historical translation and the whole volume were checked. | Cyril speaks of two; Bernard develops the intermediate coming. |
| coming-bernard | Bernard, Advent 5.1–3, gives the three comings and John 14:23. Checked the English Mount Melleray printing, pp. 40–43, and Latin PL 183, cols. 50–51. | Bernard is medieval. The present coming is hidden and spiritual, with conduct as the fruit of keeping the word. |
| history-rome | Mommsen, MGH Auct. ant. 9, p. 71, first entry records the Nativity in Bethlehem on the eighth day before the Kalends of January. Date by inclusive Roman reckoning. The entire Depositio Martirum, pp. 71–72, was acquired in print and its primary text retained in the shared corpus. | This entry establishes the Nativity in a fourth-century Roman calendar. It establishes no four-week preparation or founding date. The Pearse HTML omits material present on the printed pages. |
| history-zaragoza | Mansi III, cols. 633–635, first Zaragoza, c. 4, describes meeting in church from Dec. 17 through Epiphany and names alternatives to assembling there. | Local attendance, with no implication of a general fast or an origin for the O Antiphons. |
| history-tours | Gregory of Tours, Histories 10.31, §VI, records Perpetuus's calendar and the three weekly fasts from St Martin to the Nativity. Checked Latin in Arndt pp. 444–445 and Dalton II p. 471. | Reported local practice. Textual variants do not justify an exact year for the institution. |
| history-macon | First Mâcon c. 9, Maassen p. 157, names Monday, Wednesday and Friday and requires the Lenten order of celebration. | Late sixth century; datings of the council differ. |
| history-leo | Leo, Sermon 19.1–2, opens with Luke 21:34 and sets the December fast among four seasonal observances. The Sunday Gospel ends at Luke 21:33. | A connection between texts, without a claim that Leo preached on a modern First Sunday or observed a four-week Advent. |
| history-roman-books | Wilson's Hadrianum, pp. 113–116, has the recognizable first two opening prayers and places Ember formularies among the Advent material. Amalarius, Liber officialis 3.40, distinguishes five- and four-week forms. | The surviving order neither ascribes the modern form to a proved founder nor duplicates it word for word. |
| keeping-prayer | Augustine to Proba, Letter 130, §§18–20, 31, was checked in Cunningham's NPNF I.1, including the fixed times to renew desire, brevity and attention. | Suggestions about times, brevity and returning after a missed day are applications for the household or individual. |
| keeping-fast | Leo, Sermon 16.2, connects abstinence after the harvest with compassion for people in need and with prayer. | The suggested household practice is counsel. Its historical, juridical and theological sources have distinct roles. |
| calendar-and-mass | Read the four Sunday stations, Epistles, Gospels and opening chants on the exact 1962 Vatican typical edition facsimile, especially printed pp. 1–5 and 14–15. Checked the four Collects, Ember formularies on pp. 5–14, and Vigil and Christmas on pp. 15–20. Rubricae generales nos. 20, 30, 71–72, 131 and Rubricae generales Missalis no. 299 govern boundaries, rose and the Ember Masses. | Advent Sunday formularies retain Alleluia. The Vigil replaces Advent IV if they coincide. In the first Sunday's Epistle the Missal stops at Romans 13:14a. |
| sundays-gregory | Gregory's entire homily series is retained in Latin. Homilies 1.1, 3; 6.1–2; 7.1–3; and 20.2–4 supply the lifting of the head, John's prison inquiry, the voice and the Word, and the mountains and valleys. Bute pp. 216, 229 and 241 supply checked English excerpts. | The preaching headings differ from the present cycle. Bute misnumbers Homily 6 in the Second Sunday reading. Gregory's reading of John in prison is his interpretation. |
| prayer-antiphons | Bute's Roman Breviary, Winter, pp. 243–244, provides the seven dated English texts. All seven were compared word for word with the page-checked transcription. Their Latin order and use at the Magnificat were checked against the older Breviary and 1961 Liber Usualis, pp. 340–342. | The domestic form avoids Bute's pre-1960 rules of precedence. Reverse initials are observable without inferring the intention behind them. |
| prayer-alma | Bute, p. 210, complete received English for the Marian antiphon, compared with the page-checked transcription. | The antiphon is given without its seasonal versicles and collect. |
| prayer-magnificat | Luke 1:46b–55 in the registered Douay–Rheims, Challoner revision. | The appendix begins with the words spoken by Mary. |
| vigil-and-christmas | 1962 Missal pp. 15–20, including Vigil Collect's Redeemer and Judge and the Mass in the Night with Tit. 2:11–15 and Luke 2:1–14. | Christmas begins at First Vespers. The Advent Vigil and the Christmas Mass in the Night have different formularies. |
| bethlehem-bread | Gregory the Great, Homily 8.1, PL 76 cols. 1103–1104, joins Bethlehem, John 6 and the manger. Cyril of Alexandria, Sermon 1 on Luke 2:7, Payne Smith p. 12, calls the manger Christ's table and his body the life-giving bread. | Cyril's main text supplies this. The separately transmitted Bethlehem gloss noted by Payne Smith is unnecessary. |
| bethlehem-swaddling | Gregory of Nazianzus, Oration 29.19, NPNF II.7 p. 308, expressly pairs the swaddling and casting off the grave cloths. Luke 2:7 and 23:53 describe parallel acts by Mary and Joseph of Arimathea with different Greek verbs. John 18:4, 12 records Christ going out to meet his captors and their binding him. The Easter comparison comes from Gregory. | The arrest, burial and empty tomb are three episodes. The typology is theological meditation, with Gregory assigned the comparison he makes. |
| bethlehem-exchange | Leo, Sermon 26.2, 5, places baptismal birth in Christ and reads the angelic peace with Ephesians 2. | Participation by grace, without loss of created humanity. |
| discipline-history | 1917 Code c. 1252 §§2, 4 and Paul VI, Paenitemini, norm II. | They govern successive historical phases of Latin Church discipline. |
| discipline-current | 1983 Code cc. 1249–1253 and the United States bishops' statement, especially §§4–7, 17, 22–25, with the U.S. complementary norm. Official sources re-opened November 20, 2026. | Universal norm and United States disposition are identified separately. The personal rule proposes no general legal obligation of an Advent fast. |
| scripture-course | Checked Scripture wording against the held Challoner witness and the chapter and verse bounds against the registered Bible. | Weekday choices are a domestic course, with December 24 superseding any remaining weekday choices. |
| scripture-in-the-body | Compared quotations to the held Douay–Rheims, including Luke 21:28, Matthew 11:3 and 6, John 1:26 and 14:23, and Luke 2:12. The body loci are in the whole-Bible binding. | Opening words omitted in an excerpt are outside the quotation. |

## Evidence for the expanded edition

| Claim key | Checked source and use |

| coming-scripture-expanded | Douay–Rheims 2 Kings 7 and 3 Kings 8 supply Nathan's promise and Solomon's Temple, addressed by modern book name in the prose. Isaiah 9 and 11, Luke 1, Malachi 3, Psalms 103, 1 Corinthians 3 and 15, Acts 1 and Matthew 25 give the latter parts of the argument. |
| coming-cyril-expanded | Cyril of Jerusalem, Lecture 15.1–2, 4 and 26. Swaddling and light, Christ judged and coming to judge, the refiner and builder, the time of the coming, and practising mercy while keeping the lamp alight were read in Gifford's NPNF II.7. The line about things fairer than the present was checked at the passage for §4, p. 105. |
| history-gregory-heading | The heading of Gregory's Homily 1 in the complete Latin artifact, physical lines 10–14, places the preaching on the second Sunday at St Peter's. The heading is evidence of a different pairing, without inferring a date of reform. |
| history-venice | Checked the first and fourth Sundays in the 1570 Venice edition and the intermediate Sunday headings. The 1962 comparison uses the first and fourth Sunday propers and the general Mass rubrics, AAS 52 pp. 671 and 680–681, nos. 434 and 507. The 1570 Advent I instructions give extra prayers and the change of dismissal on omission of the Gloria. The 1962 general rule permits certain additions; the prose says what the ordinary Sunday Mass does. |
| keeping-cassian | Cassian, Conferences 10.10, read directly in the complete Gibson English held in the corpus. His exhausted reader, recollected conversations and warning against elation support the counsel. The recited words are Psalm 69:2 in Douay–Rheims, not the wording of Gibson's English Psalm. |
| keeping-scripture | Psalm 24 provides the example of sustained reading. Isaiah 58 places penitence beside justice and bread for the hungry. Psalm 50 and Zacchaeus supply the examination and restitution. John 20:22–23 supports the Lord giving his apostles the ministry of forgiveness. |
| keeping-penance | The 1829 Donovan Roman Catechism, Part II, Penance, names prayer, fasting and alms as forms of satisfaction. The chapter applies this to a present household and makes no new law. |
| advent-sunday-i-chrysostom | Chrysostom, Romans 24, NPNF I.11 lines 46756–46846, interprets the swallow, rising before daybreak, and putting on Christ himself. |
| advent-sunday-i-augustine | Augustine, Confessions 8.12.28–29, NPNF I.1 lines 11828–11873, tells of tears under the fig tree and Paul's words in Romans. “Take up and read” is at line 11852 of the held volume. |
| advent-sunday-ii-chrysostom | Chrysostom, Matthew 36.2–3, NPNF I.10 lines 22615–22709, reads the mission in terms of the disciples' difficulty and Christ's deeds. Gregory's descent-to-the-dead interpretation remains his own reading. |
| advent-sunday-iii-chrysostom | Chrysostom, Philippians 14, NPNF I.13 lines 22275–22365, joins mourning for sin, forbearance and joy in the Lord. The Beatitude citation uses Douay–Rheims verse numbering. |
| advent-sunday-iv-chrysostom | Chrysostom, First Corinthians 11.1, 5, NPNF I.12 lines 5172–5206 and 5358–5404, considers judgment of hidden conduct and motives at Corinth. |
| sundays-ember-and-communion | The 1962 Missal's numbered propers give the Third Sunday's Isaiah 35:4 Communion and the Fourth Sunday's Marian Offertory. Ember lessons on pp. 5–14, including the longer rite's introductory rubric and prayer for ordinations, supply the additional detail. The relation between Isaiah's dew and the fire is the book's meditation on successive lessons. |
| sundays-scripture-expanded | New Sunday and weekday citations read against the whole Douay–Rheims witness, including Ephesians 3, Romans 15, Isaiah's promises, Exodus' deliverance and Sinai, and Zachary's prophecy in Luke 1. |
| christmas-mary-consent | Bernard, Fourth Sermon on the Virgin Mother, §8, Mount Melleray English pp. 124–126 and PL 183 cols. 83–84. “Say the word and receive the Word” is the checked historical English. |
| christmas-mary-eve | Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.22.4, Roberts–Rambaut ANF 1, lines 57537–57601, on Eve and Mary. Used in paraphrase. |
| christmas-immaculate-feast | The 1962 Missal p. 437 no. 1873 and AAS 52 p. 598 no. 15 give the Collect and precedence of the feast on December 8. |
| christmas-immaculate-definition | Pius IX, Ineffabilis Deus, definition, 1855 Murphy/Dolman pp. 95, lines 2556–2566 of the OCR, corroborated in the Holy See text. Used in paraphrase. |

| christmas-vigil-collect | 1962 Missal p. 15 no. 99 independently located in the facsimile, and Lasance 1945 pp. 133–135. The earlier passage ID addresses the other Vigil orations; the precise Collect binding here replaces it. |
| christmas-cyril-life | Cyril of Alexandria, Commentary on John 4.2, Pusey pp. 408–425, on John 6:51–58, especially 6:56 in the commentary. His explanation and wax comparison are paraphrased. |
| christmas-wonderful-exchange | Athanasius, On the Incarnation 54.2–3, Robertson NPNF II.4 p. 65. The checked translation names the exchange. The surrounding prose distinguishes grace from the nature of the eternal Son. |
