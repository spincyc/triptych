\begin{minipage}[t]{0.47\linewidth}

  method before acting.
  \Boundary{HANDLE ONLY AT THE APPOINTED CUE}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.47\linewidth}
  \ObjectHeading{Recognizes only}{What the server identifies but leaves alone}{KEY-R}

  \Boundary{DO NOT TOUCH UNLESS TRAINED AND DIRECTED}
\end{minipage}

\vspace{0.28in}
\begin{studybox}{The twelve objects in this alpha}
\textbf{Handles:} altar bells; altar Missal and its support when assigned. \quad
\textbf{Varies by ceremony:} altar candle and candlestick. \quad
\textbf{Recognizes only:} sanctuary lamp and prepared book markers. \quad
\textbf{Must not handle in this Low-Mass model:} chalice and paten. \quad
\textbf{Solemn-Mass recognition:} Epistle book and Gospel book.
\end{studybox}

\section*{\LitDisplay{Three confusions to prevent}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \textbf{candle} is the wax light; the \textbf{candlestick} is its
    support. An assembled drawing contains both.
  \item The \textbf{chalice} is the priest's sacred vessel; it is not an altar
    cruet.
  \item The priest's \textbf{paten} is not the Communion plate held beneath a
    communicant's chin.
  \item A low altar \textbf{Missal stand} is not a freestanding lectern. A
    \textbf{cushion} replaces the stand; the two supports are never used
    together beneath the book.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{\LitDisplay{Scope and qualifications}}
This alpha renders twelve source-audited canonical records from the
altar-server queue. Ten belong to the bounded Low-Mass core:
altar bells, altar candle, altar candlestick, sanctuary lamp, altar Missal,
Missal stand, Missal cushion, book markers, chalice, and paten. The Epistle
and Gospel books begin the Solemn-Mass recognition branch;
their generic diagram teaches only the exact minister/use relationships
established by \emph{Ritus servandus} VI.4--5. The lectern appears only as a
source-qualified comparison. This alpha does not yet cover the server's
complete route, the complete altar
composition, cruets and Lavabo objects, Communion plate, vesture, sung or
solemn branches, the full Holy Week inventory, pontifical ceremonies, or
related ceremonies. It therefore makes no claim of dictionary completeness.

The official books control the printed liturgical claims. The generated
drawings are project-produced teaching aids, not evidence. Where an image's
particular morphology is not established by the cited locus, the page says so.
The earlier ambiguous paten candidate remains held. Its replacement is a
dimensioned catalog exemplar with a Missal-controlled prepared inset; the
Communion plate is distinguished textually because its period morphology
remains unresolved.
