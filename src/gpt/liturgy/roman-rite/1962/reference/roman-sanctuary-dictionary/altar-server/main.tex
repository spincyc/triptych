\vspace{0.28in}
\begin{studybox}{The twenty-four objects in this alpha}
\textbf{Handles:} altar bells; paired altar cruets; altar Missal and its support when assigned. \quad
\textbf{Varies by ceremony:} altar candle and candlestick. \quad
\textbf{Recognizes only:} sanctuary lamp and prepared book markers. \quad
\textbf{Must not handle in this Low-Mass model:} chalice and paten. \quad
\textbf{Solemn-Mass recognition:} Epistle book and Gospel book.
\textbf{Solemn-Mass carrying:} acolyte candlestick, elevation torch, and the
optional local processional cross. \quad
\textbf{Solemn-Mass incense service:} incense boat, spoon, and thurible.
\textbf{Sanctuary furnishings:} credence table and sedilia, with local
placement and furniture variation retained in the entries. \quad
\textbf{Sacristy recognition:} the locally appointed cross or suitable image
and a local departure signal bell, kept distinct from the altar bells. \quad
\textbf{Holy Week recognition:} freestanding lectern.
\end{studybox}

\section*{\LitDisplay{Three confusions to prevent}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \textbf{candle} is the wax light; the \textbf{candlestick} is its
    support. An assembled drawing contains both.
  \item The \textbf{chalice} is the priest's sacred vessel; it is not an altar
    cruet.
  \item The altar \textbf{cruets} are a small working pair for wine and water;
    their markings, form, and arrangement vary locally.
  \item The priest's \textbf{paten} is not the Communion plate held beneath a
    communicant's chin.
  \item A low altar \textbf{Missal stand} is not a freestanding lectern. A
    \textbf{cushion} replaces the stand; the two supports are never used
    together beneath the book.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{\LitDisplay{Scope and qualifications}}
This alpha renders twenty-five source-audited canonical records from the
altar-server queue. Ten belong to the bounded Low-Mass core:
altar bells, altar candle, altar candlestick, sanctuary lamp, altar Missal,
Missal stand, Missal cushion, book markers, chalice, and paten. The paired
altar-cruet record adds the next directly handled Low-Mass object. The Epistle
and Gospel books begin the Solemn-Mass recognition branch;
their generic diagram teaches only the exact minister/use relationships
established by \emph{Ritus servandus} VI.4--5. The freestanding lectern enters
only through its exact Palm Sunday, Good Friday, and Easter Vigil branches;
its illustrated pedestal form is not a universal norm. The carried-light
branch now renders and
distinguishes the footed acolyte candlestick from the footless,
non-self-standing elevation torch. It also includes
the optional local processional cross without presenting that cross as a
universal or Missal-required feature of a priest's High or Solemn Mass. The
torch entry stays within Fortescue's exact morphological and ceremonial
ceiling; no
universal local number, arrangement, or handling method is asserted. The
thurible and separately identified incense
boat and spoon begin the Solemn-Mass incense-service branch; loose incense remains a
consumable material rather than a peer vessel. The credence and sedilia
entries add two sanctuary-furnishing families without
turning the selected Epistle-side teaching model, furniture construction,
covering, or local placement into a universal rule. The sedilia page separates
the sacred ministers' station from local server seating; neither furnishing
creates handling permission by its appearance.
This alpha does not yet cover
the server's complete route, the complete altar
composition, the remaining Lavabo objects, Communion plate, vesture, sung or
solemn branches, the remaining Holy Week inventory, pontifical ceremonies, or
related ceremonies. It therefore makes no claim of dictionary completeness.

The official books control the printed liturgical claims. The generated
drawings are project-produced teaching aids, not evidence. The boat-and-spoon
image was edited to remove unsupported granular contents; the thurible figure
teaches only source-bounded component recognition. Where an image's
particular morphology is not established by the cited locus, the page says so.
The candle tool is practical/local equipment rather than a prescribed
liturgical furnishing; its plate omits the exact patent mechanism and teaches
no flame procedure.
The earlier ambiguous paten candidate remains held. Its replacement is a
dimensioned catalog exemplar with a Missal-controlled prepared inset; the
Communion plate is distinguished textually because its period morphology
remains unresolved.
