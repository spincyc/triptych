# MC and trainer edition guide map

Status: **held structural prototype; not approved for publication**
Audit date: 2026-07-27
Edition selection: `ed-mc-trainer`

## Reader and purpose

This use-derived edition is for masters of ceremonies and trainers. Its
eventual audience layer emphasizes verified roles, giver and receiver,
destination, branches, preparation and checking points, common confusions,
safe handling boundaries, and recovery.

## Current generated selection

The present `main.tex` is a publication-capable shell that consumes only the
generator-admitted `ed-mc-trainer` selection under `build/`. That generated
selection is empty because there are no publication-ready canonical objects
or verified ceremonial sequences. The shell therefore renders a held notice,
not role assignments, rubrical claims, or permission to teach a sequence.

When populated, `ed-mc-trainer` selects only `publication-ready` canonical
records whose `audience_relevance.mc_trainer` value is `required` or `useful`.
Records with unresolved status are excluded.
Selection requires recorded MC/trainer relevance; a proposed comparison or
training convenience cannot itself create inclusion.

## Priestly-review selection boundary

The priestly-review edition may expose only explicitly manifested canonical
records proposed for `ed-mc-trainer`, with workflow and evidence state
visible. Proposed roles, stations, transfers, branches, cues, and recovery
notes are review questions until their claim-level evidence is accepted. The
packet must never function as authority to teach an unverified sequence.

## Visible omissions in the review edition

The review edition must list ceremonies, branches, roles, stations, transfers,
and recovery cases that have not been selected or source-audited. A missing
minister or handoff must read as an open gap, not as an assertion that the
role or action does not exist.

## Reader order

1. Compact title with a conspicuous priestly-review/hold notice.
2. Visible omissions and review-state key.
3. Relational symbol key.
4. People, roles, and stations shown in compositions.
5. Objects and handoffs shown as source-verified giver, receiver, and
   destination relationships.
6. Branch-dependent objects and special ceremonies with exact status labels.
7. Training comparisons for confusable objects, handling boundaries, checks,
   and recovery.
8. Priestly-review questions and correction instructions.
9. Selective pronunciation, English--Latin and Latin--English indexes, visual
   index, variant and terminology notes, scope and qualifications, numbered
   source notes and references, generation metadata, and rights colophon.

Stations proceed from ordinary to branch-specific compositions. Handoffs are
grouped by verified ceremony sequence; branch-dependent records are grouped
by Mass or ceremony and then sequence. Training comparisons may group
confusable objects or handling boundaries but do not create factual
relationships. The plate manifest preserves intentional within-section order.

## Questions for priestly review

1. Are every giver, receiver, destination, station, and ordinary handler
   correct for the stated ceremony and branch?
2. Does the sequence distinguish a rubric from customary training practice?
3. Are optional, omitted, privileged, and locally directed branches marked
   without presenting one arrangement as universal?
4. Do recovery notes preserve reverence and clerical direction without
   inventing a rubrical procedure?
5. Are comparisons genuinely useful to training and free of false
   equivalences?
6. Which missing cue, transfer, or minister relationship would make a plate
   unsafe or misleading?

## Canonical dependency

This leaf is generated from the shared inventory and owns no independent
role, transfer, branch, cue, or recovery claim. All such facts, plus names,
statuses, variants, sources, artwork, and audience relevance, must come from
canonical records conforming to `shared/schema/inventory-schema.toml`.
Selection is governed by `shared/schema/edition-selections.toml`.
Audience-specific notes must remain canonical and may not contradict or
enlarge verified base claims.
