# Paten comparison source audit

Audit date: 2026-07-27
Scope: evidence prerequisite only; no artwork generated

## Result

The source tranche closes enough evidence for an explicitly labeled
**catalog-exemplar** plate of the priest's paten:

- a top/oblique and shallow side form based on one exact museum object;
- common-scale juxtaposition with that object's paired chalice, using the
  catalog's separate dimensions; and
- a small prepared-chalice inset whose stacking relationship is controlled by
  the 1962 Missal.

It does **not** establish a universal or representative Roman paten silhouette.
It also does not close a period-authentic visual comparison with the Communion
plate. The 1962 Missal proves that the `patina` beneath communicants' chins is
functionally and terminologically distinct from the celebrant's `patena`, but
it gives no handle, edge, dimensions, or decorative morphology.

## Evidence matrix

| Prepared relationship | 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Ritus servandus* I.1, printed p. LII / artifact p. 56 | purificator over chalice mouth; paten with host over it | no dimensions, overlap tolerance, or ornament |
| Distinction from Communion plate | 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Ritus servandus* X.7, printed p. LXIII / artifact p. 65 | `patina` placed beneath communicants' chins and cleared of fragments after Communion | no handled or raised-edge form |
| Paten morphology | Metropolitan Museum of Art object 463080, Open Access image | one shallow, circular, footless gilded-silver paten with a broad rim and depressed field, beside its cataloged chalice | one French early-twentieth-century object after a 1555 original; not a norm or population sample |
| Exact and relative size | Metropolitan Museum of Art object 463080, collection metadata | paten 1.2 by 16.3 cm; chalice 23.6 by 17.8 cm | the chalice's 17.8 cm is an overall catalog dimension, not proved to be cup-mouth diameter |

## Acquisition and inspection

The exact Missal facsimile already registered in the source library was
downloaded again and matched its registered SHA-256
`648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`.
Artifact pages 56 and 65 were checked both through the embedded OCR and as
rendered page images.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art API response and linked Open Access image were
downloaded and hashed. Their exact remote artifact identities, retrieval
metadata, rights bases, dimensions, and limits are recorded in the
provider-neutral source library and pinned in the altar-server edition's
`research/source-bindings.toml`.

## Art constraint

A future replacement plate may depict the cataloged paten as an exemplar and
may combine:

1. its shallow top/side form;
2. its paired chalice at common overall-object scale; and
3. an on-chalice preparation inset governed by *Ritus servandus* I.1.

The plate must label the object as a catalog exemplar, must not imply that the
chalice's published overall width is its mouth diameter, and must not show the
Met photograph or its decorative details as though they were a universal
Roman form.

The Communion plate may be distinguished by name, Latin term, location, and
function only. A handled or high-edge Communion-plate silhouette remains
blocked pending a publicly reachable, edition-identified pre-1962 catalog or
object witness. Current manufacturer catalogs can document modern continuity,
but they cannot silently supply the missing 1962-horizon morphology.
