# Sacred-minister vestments alpha audit — 2026-07-28

## Scope

This bounded audit admits three ordinary sacred-minister records at the 1962
horizon: the deacon's stole, dalmatic, and tunicle. It does not admit the
folded chasuble, broad stole, cope, biretta, server or choir dress, pontifical
vesture, or a complete vestment corpus.

## Source boundary

The Vatican 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Rubricae generales* 117 and 133--137,
controls liturgical color and the retained stole, dalmatic, and tunicle while
expressly discontinuing folded chasubles and the broad stole. Fortescue and
O'Connell's seventh revised 1943 *Ceremonies of the Roman Rite Described*,
printed page 11, controls the bounded recognition and layering claims:
the deacon's stole runs diagonally from the left shoulder beneath the
dalmatic; the dalmatic is the deacon's outer vestment; and the tunicle is the
subdeacon's corresponding outer vestment, with a documented but non-universal
longer-and-narrower sleeve distinction. Its printed pages 86 and 95 support
only conditional acolyte vesting assistance.

No source here establishes universal dimensions, textile, ornament,
construction, fastening, folds, storage, decorative scheme, or an exhaustive
visual distinction between dalmatic and tunicle.

plates. The exact prompts and original generator bytes were not durably
transferred; the manifest records that loss and preserves only the surviving

The figures contain no words, semantic marks, color, ceremonial action, or
environment. TeX owns identity, source, status, use, and non-universality
qualifications. Full-size review must confirm that no crop, impossible
wearing, misleading ornament, or falsely categorical dalmatic/tunicle
distinction enters the rendered editions.

## Admission decision

The three records may enter all six alpha selections after the structured
inventory, artwork custody, generation, build, font, text-extraction,
full-page visual, installed-byte, and release-binding gates pass. This bounded
admission does not close the deacon-and-subdeacon completeness row or imply
