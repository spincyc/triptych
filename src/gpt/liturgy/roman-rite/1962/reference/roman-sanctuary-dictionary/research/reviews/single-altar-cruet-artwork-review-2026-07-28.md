# Single altar-cruet artwork review

Date: 2026-07-28

Asset: `shared/artwork/pencil/RPD-FIG-service-objects-0104-single-altar-cruet-v1-alpha.png`

## Purpose and boundary

This is an isolated recognition view of **one altar cruet**. It is intended for
an entry that teaches the vessel as a separately recognizable object. It does
not replace the approved paired-cruets relationship view, which remains the
appropriate illustration when the text teaches the wine-and-water service as a
formation.

The asset deliberately contains no second cruet, tray, labels, liquid, hands,
altar, or ceremonial action. Text and layout must supply the name and must not
infer which liquid this unmarked exemplar contains.

## Evidence and morphology control

The drawing is bounded by the sources already checked for
`obj-altar-cruet`:

- the 1914 Gorham ecclesiastical catalog, leaves N36-N37, establishes a dated
  family of small altar cruets with varied forms, materials, capacities, and
  heights;
- Metropolitan Museum of Art object 467483 supplies one handled, footed,
  lidded material-culture witness;
- Metropolitan Museum of Art object 200141 establishes a cataloged pair of
  altar cruets and demonstrates that handles and this drawing's silhouette are
  not universal.

Only the recognition-level features are taught: a small pouring vessel, stable
foot, pouring lip, lid, and graspable handle. The rounded body, opaque silver
appearance, hinge, knob, proportions, and ornament-free finish are
representative choices, not Roman prescriptions or a claim of a universal
altar-cruet form.

## Generation and boundary treatment

The built-in image generator produced a graphite-pencil study of one generic,
opaque silver altar cruet against a flat chroma field. The production prompt
required one object only, a complete uncropped silhouette, a stable circular
foot, short neck, lid, pouring lip, loop handle, no letters or liquid markings,
no scenery, no cast shadow, and no rectangular paper boundary.

The chroma field was removed locally with the image-generation skill's
soft-matte and despill process, including a one-pixel edge contraction. The
result has transparent corners and a narrow antialiased transition around the
object, so it can blend into the publication's paper field without a floating
rectangle or green fringe.
