## Evidence and qualification boundary

signals of the small altar bell:

- *Rubricae generales* 528 places a `parva campanula` among the prepared
  Epistle-side service objects;
- *Ritus servandus* VIII.6 appoints the warning before the Consecration and
  three strokes or continuous ringing at each elevation; and
- *Ritus servandus* X.6 appoints a warning when faithful are to communicate.

The checked Marietti third edition after the 1948 typical
*Caeremoniale Episcoporum* supports the ceremonial
presence and handling relationships of candles and candlesticks and the
placement and continued burning of lamps around the place of reservation:

- book I, chapter V, no. 6;
- chapter VI, no. 2;
- chapter XI, nos. 1 and 8; and
- chapter XII, no. 17.

> **Representative four-bell cluster.** The 1962 Missal appoints a small bell

### Altar candle — *candela*; wax candle — *cereus*

> A candle used on an altar candlestick or carried light. Exact composition,
> dimensions, and appearance vary. Keep flame and hot wax clear of hair,
> sleeves, veils, and other textiles.

The relationship between *candela* and *cereus* remains subject to lexical
review; neither term should be forced into a false one-to-one distinction
without a checked locus.

### Altar candlestick — *candelabrum*

> **Representative form.** A stable support for a liturgical candle. The
> Ceremonial distinguishes candlestick and candle and describes carried
> candlesticks held at the foot and central knop; ornament and proportions

The isolated image depicts an altar-sized visual example. It does not
establish whether that exact object is suitable as a processional
candlestick.

### Sanctuary lamp — *lampas*

> **One hanging form.** A lamp burns before the place where the Blessed

> three-chain vessel is an identification example, not the exclusive Roman

the isolated figure can teach placement by itself.

### Candle lighter and extinguisher

## Visible morphology and priest-review questions

### Altar bells

The drawing reads clearly as one four-cup handbell cluster, but the three
front cups dominate and the fourth sits partly behind the handle.

1. Does the figure immediately read as an altar-bell cluster at plausible
   hand scale?
2. Is the rear fourth cup sufficiently legible, or could a reader mistake the
   center reflection for a fifth component?
3. Should the first plate show both a single handbell and a clustered form so
   no local construction is universalized?
4. Is the loop handle plausible for quiet pickup and controlled ringing?
5. Should a server-use inset show the safe low grip and the bell resting on a
   pad, or would that overstate a local handling model?

### Candle and candlestick

The candle and support are coherent and uncluttered. The broad basin-like
wax catcher and very tall candle make the form strongly particular.

6. Does this silhouette read as a plausible Roman altar candlestick rather
   than a domestic floor or table candlestick?
7. Is the broad wax-catching bowl materially misleading?
8. Are the candle-to-stand proportions plausible, or should the candle be
   shorter and the stand more substantial?
9. Should altar and carried/processional candlesticks be separate canonical
   objects or documented variants of one object?
10. Which visual distinctions, if any, are needed among altar candles,
    acolyte candles, elevation torches, and other large wax lights?
11. Does the candidate need a separate lit state, or is an unlit morphology
