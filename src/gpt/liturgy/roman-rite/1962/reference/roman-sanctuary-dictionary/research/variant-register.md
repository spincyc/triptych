# Variant register

Status: **five canonical variant IDs; wider category audit remains open**
Inventory reconciliation: 2026-11-05

The aspergillum now has two source-audited morphological variants:
`var-aspergillum-brush` and `var-aspergillum-hollow-globe`. Fortescue's 1917
and 1943 editions document both forms. Only the hollow-globe form has a
consumer plate; brush material, globe perforation pattern, handle profile,
dimensions, proportions, and ornament remain unasserted.

The canonical mitre record also carries `var-mitre-pretiosa`,
`var-mitre-auriphrygiata`, and `var-mitre-simplex`, each linked to its existing
Ceremonial source and three-form comparison asset. This reconciliation reports
those recorded states; it is not a renewed inspection of the mitre source or
a finding that all pontifical variants are exhausted.

## Admission test

A variant receives a stable `var-...` ID only when a checked source shows a
material difference in construction, identification, ritual use, status, or
handling. Ornament, fabric pattern, workshop style, or incidental proportions
alone do not create a substantive variant.

## Required record fields

Parent object ID; variant ID; preferred names; distinguishing features;
status; period; ceremonies; source loci; evidence state; artwork IDs;
confusables; and explicit reason the distinction is substantive.


## Ordinary altar and reservation distinctions, 2026-11-05

The new altar record distinguishes fixed stone construction from an altar stone
in a supporting table and preserves the Missal's conditional antimension
provision. These are source-backed distinctions but do not yet have independent
variant IDs or an exhaustive comparative plate. Do not multiply object or
variant IDs merely for decorative altar styles.

The tabernacle and ciborium are separate objects, not two variants of a
reservation vessel. The former is the fixed enclosure; the latter is the
covered vessel placed within it. The new ciborium record preserves the
Missal's Latin `pyxis` without equating every English use of “pyx” with the
same physical form. Fortescue's smaller containers and the architectural
“ciborium” remain separate terminology work. A lid, stem profile, metal tone,
ornament or generic silhouette alone cannot establish ceremony or period.
