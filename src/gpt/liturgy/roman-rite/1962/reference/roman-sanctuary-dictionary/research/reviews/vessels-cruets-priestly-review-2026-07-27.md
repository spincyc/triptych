## Evidence and qualification boundary

The checked 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Ritus servandus* VII–XII, controls the
presence and ceremonial use of the chalice and paten. The same Missal and the
altar-server guide's disclosed manual synthesis support the wine-and-water
service and the selected two-server cruet route. These sources do not, by
themselves, establish:

- the exact silhouette, proportions, ornament, or dimensions shown;
- the apparent metal, glass, surface finish, or construction method;
- that this chalice, paten, or cruet form is universal or representative;
- the cruet's proposed Latin aliases *ampulla* and *urceolus*;
- the server-handling language as a universal rubric rather than the
  dictionary's declared safety policy; or
- a common scale among these separately generated figures.

The artwork therefore remains an identification lead. No caption may say or
imply “the prescribed form,” “the correct dimensions,” “must look like this,”
or “shown to scale.”

## Visible morphology review

### Chalice

What reads clearly:

- one coherent open cup, stem, principal node, and stable foot;
- upright symmetry and a usable isolated silhouette;
- no lid, handles, cloth, paten, host, or duplicated component.

Qualifications and questions:

- The very large, almost cylindrical cup and short overall stem may read as a
  modern oversize goblet rather than a representative mid-century Roman
  chalice.
- The faceted Gothic ornament on the node is more assertive than the brief
  intended; without a material-form source it must be treated as one
  decorative example, not a universal feature.
- The drawing cannot establish the required material or treatment of the cup
  interior. Monochrome shading should not be read as evidence of silver,
  gilding, or lack of gilding.
- No relative scale to the paten is established.

Priestly reviewer:

1. Does the silhouette immediately and unambiguously read as a usable Mass
   chalice in the 1962 Roman context?
2. Would the cup-to-stem and cup-to-foot proportions misteach a server,
   sacristan, or general reader?
3. Should the node ornament be simplified, or is it acceptable if explicitly
   captioned as a representative decorative form?
4. Is a second figure needed to show a materially distinct common form rather
   than allowing this one drawing to imply uniformity?
5. What practical feature, if any, must be visible for a young server to
   distinguish this from a ciborium when both are uncovered?

### Paten

What reads clearly:

- one coherent circular, shallow, footless, handleless metal object;
- the complete rim and upper surface are visible;
- no host, chalice, stem, foot, or duplicated dish appears.

Qualifications and questions:

- The broad raised rim and pronounced central well make the object resemble a
  domestic saucer or presentation plate. It does not clearly demonstrate the
  flatter, compact paten form that rests upon and covers a chalice.
- Because it was generated independently, its apparent diameter relative to
  the chalice is meaningless and could mislead if both are placed together
  without an explicit scale treatment.
- The figure supplies no placed view on the chalice and therefore does not
  teach the defining visual relationship.
- Material, gilding, exact curvature, and dimensions remain unverified.

Priestly reviewer:

1. Is this shape recognizable enough to retain even as a generic paten
   example, or should it be rejected and redrawn?
2. Is the central well too deep or sharply bounded for the representative
   form the dictionary should teach?
3. Should the first publishable paten figure be a flatter top/side comparison
   plus a context inset resting on the chalice?
4. Should the atlas show a common-scale chalice-and-paten set before it shows
   an isolated paten?
5. Does any feature risk confusing this with the Communion plate, and what
   context view best prevents that confusion?

### Altar cruet

What reads clearly:

- one coherent transparent vessel, with a single body, base, neck, handle,
  lip, and seated stopper;
- glass construction remains legible in monochrome;
- no liquid, lettering, second vessel, tray, lavabo object, or duplicated
  component appears.

Qualifications and questions:

- The round body and tall stopper make the object resemble a table decanter
  and may make it appear substantially larger than a practical altar cruet.
- A stopper is shown seated while the vessel has a pouring lip. This is a
  storage state, not a presentation or pouring state, and the stopper's
  removal and safe disposition are not taught.
- One empty generic cruet cannot teach how wine and water are distinguished.
  Letters, decorative motifs, colored glass, contents, tray position, or
  locally rehearsed placement can perform that function, but no one device
  has yet been established as universal.
- The isolated form does not show the pair, tray, handhold, presentation, or
  relationship to the chalice.
- The Latin headword and alternate Latin name remain under lexical hold.

Priestly reviewer:

1. Does this read as an altar cruet at plausible hand scale, or primarily as a
   household decanter?
2. Is a stoppered isolated view useful, or should the primary figure show the
   stopper removed with a separate small storage-state inset?
3. What pair arrangement most faithfully teaches the wine-and-water service
   without presenting a local marking convention as universal?
4. Should a server-use inset show the handle grip, the presentation posture,
   or both?
5. Which distinctions must the caption make among Offertory presentation,
   lavabo water, and the two ablutions?
6. Are there materially different glass and metal cruet forms that must be
   shown as substantive variants rather than decoration?

## Proposed review disposition

- **Chalice:** retain as a held morphology lead. Promote only if the priestly
  reviewer accepts its proportions and a competent material-form source
  supports the representative form; otherwise redraw with simpler node and
  revised cup-to-stem proportions.
- **Paten:** presumptively reject for publication and redraw as a flatter,
  compact paten with top, side, and on-chalice views at controlled scale.
- **Cruet:** retain as a held storage-state lead. Add a paired/common-scale
  plate and in-use inset only after source and priestly review; redraw if the
  decanter-like scale or stopper arrangement is judged misleading.
