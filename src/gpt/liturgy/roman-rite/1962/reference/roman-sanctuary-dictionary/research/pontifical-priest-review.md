## Source-horizon warning

This packet does not claim that every listed object or transfer belongs to
every pontifical Mass in 1962.

The directly inspected witness is the 1948 Marietti third edition after the
typical of the *Caeremoniale Episcoporum*, book I, chapters XI--XII and XXI.
That witness names a substantial object and minister system, but the project
has not yet proved:

- whether the 1948 edition was unmodified at the selected 1962 horizon;
- which later decrees, instructions, privileges, or local rules qualify it;
- which rules apply only to the diocesan bishop in his own church;
- which rules differ at the throne and at the faldstool;
- which objects belong to a bishop celebrating, a bishop assisting, a
  cardinal, an archbishop, or another prelate;
- which articles are independently specified objects rather than practical
  local supports; or
- which English and Latin names are suitable as canonical dictionary
  headwords.

Please mark an item only from a source or practice you can identify. “I have
seen this” is useful as a lead, but is not by itself evidence of universal
1962 status.

Suggested marks:

- **C** — confirmed from a named source;
- **P** — confirmed only as a particular/local practice;
- **H** — historical or no longer applicable by 1962;
- **Q** — questionable or materially incomplete;
- **N/A** — outside the reviewer's competence;
- **?** — needs further research.

|  | Exact book used at a station | Marked and placed at a named support or credence | Book minister holds or assists the assistant priest; height and side follow the bishop's station | Returned to named support or retained for the next reading | |

Questions:

1. Is *bugia* the suitable canonical Latin headword, an Italian/common name,
   or only an alias in this source?
2. Is *scotula* a valid contemporary synonym at this horizon?
3. When does the assistant priest hold the book, and when does a dedicated
   minister hold or support it?
4. Does the type of support change at the throne, faldstool, altar, or vesting
   station?

Questions:

1. Are there four hand washings when the bishop celebrates solemnly at the
   1962 horizon: before vesting, after the Offertory is read, after incensing
   the offerings, and after Communion?
2. Is *urceus* the correct headword for the ewer and *pelvis* for the basin?
3. Does the pontifical set differ materially from an ordinary Lavabo set, or
   chiefly in scale, material, ministers, and sequence?
4. What distinguishes the pontifical hand towel from the ordinary Lavabo
   towel visually?
5. How should used towels be represented and disposed without adding an
   unsupported local custom?
