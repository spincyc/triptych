## Qualified visual variants

### Ordinary sanctuary furnishings

`RSD-ART-001-sanctuary-furnishings.png` shows one high-backed three-place
sedilia group, a simple backless stool, an open-shelf linen-covered credence,
a short baluster rail with a cloth, and a wooden prie-dieu. These are
recognition examples only.

- Sedilia may be separate movable seats, a joined bench, or architectural
  seats; the depicted high-backed joinery is not normative.
- A `scabellum` is represented by a backless wooden stool. Whether the term
  at a particular locus implies this exact construction is not claimed.
- Credences vary materially in size, shelf arrangement, covering, and number;
  the plate shows one practical form.
- The rail segment is held. The drawing must not be used to assert that every
  sanctuary has a rail, that every rail is used for Communion, or that the
  shown cloth is universally attached or arranged in that manner.
- `Genuflexorium` describes liturgical function more securely than the exact
  prie-dieu construction shown.

### Pontifical seating

`RSD-ART-002-pontifical-seating.png` contrasts a folding X-frame faldstool, a
canopied seat on a two-step platform, a fixed high-backed chair, and a compact
kneeler. The contrast is pedagogical.

- The X-frame faldstool is a representative traditional morphology, not yet a
  source-verified construction specification for every 1962 use.
- The canopied throne represents the visual class of the proper pontifical
  seat. Canopy, platform, step count, textiles, and ornament require separate
  ceremonial and material-form verification.
- The fixed chair is held as a comparison lead. It must not yet be labelled
  as a distinct 1962 liturgical object if `cathedra`, `sedes pontificalis`,
  and `solium episcopale` are contextually overlapping terms rather than
  separate inventory objects.
- The compact kneeler and the larger prie-dieu may be material variants of one
  `genuflexorium` object rather than distinct canonical objects.

## Questions for the priest reviewer

1. In the 1962 ceremonial vocabulary, should `sedes pontificalis` and
   `solium episcopale` be one canonical object with alternate Latin names?
2. Should a fixed cathedral *cathedra* be treated as that same object, as a
   material/architectural variant, or as a separate historical and canonical
   concept?
3. Does the faldstool entry need two functional views—seated and kneeling—or
   should the kneeling use be explained only in a contextual composition?
4. Is `genuflexorium` best represented by one canonical object with large
   prie-dieu and compact pontifical variants, rather than the present two
   records?
5. Is `scabellum` adequately translated as “sanctuary stool” for this atlas,
   or should the English headword remain “assistant's stool” or simply
   “stool”?
6. Should `abacus` be the principal Latin headword for the credence, with
   `credentia` marked later usage, or does the selected 1962 corpus support a
   different ordering?
7. Which seats in an ordinary Solemn Mass should the atlas call *sedilia*,
   and which should be described generically as ministers' seats to avoid
   overextending the term?
8. Is the Communion rail properly inside the universal 1962 inventory, or
   should it be an architectural/local-presence entry whose use and cloth are
   handled as variants?
9. Does the depicted rail cloth risk conflating the Communion cloth with a
   permanently attached rail covering?
10. Are a canopy and raised platform defining enough for the throne
    comparison, or would that falsely universalize one visual arrangement?
11. Are any of the illustrated furnishings likely to be mistaken for clergy-
    only objects that a server may move? If so, what concise handling boundary
    should appear in the server edition?
12. For a future refinement, which claims would benefit from comparison with
    an edition-specific ceremonial manual in addition to the
    *Caeremoniale Episcoporum*?
