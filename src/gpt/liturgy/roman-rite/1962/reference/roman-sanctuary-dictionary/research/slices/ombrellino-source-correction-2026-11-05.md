# Eucharistic ombrellino: exact-witness correction

Checked 2026-11-05. This correction supersedes the Eucharistic-source identity
and pagination in the 2026-07-28 combined conopaeum/ombrellino audit. It does
not reconstruct that earlier review or alter the generated artwork.

The former binding `edition.catholic-church.rituale-romanum.typical-1952`
did not resolve. More substantially, the linked
[Liturgia PDF](https://www.liturgia.it/content/ritrom.pdf) is a modern
Laudate Dominum resetting of the **1925** Ritual. Its title names Pius XI,
and its displayed decree on PDF page 2 is dated 10 June 1925. The complete
download has 519 pages, 6,447,801 bytes, SHA-256
`d020e285160e67b6acd1a4b0bde0f09c42a0afa0f6fc5216a339599db7b9aabf`.
It is distinct from the already registered 518-page resetting with hash
`78f405d7`; neither is a facsimile of a Vatican printing.

Displayed PDF pages **71–72** (also the reset page numbers) were inspected.
Titulus IV, caput IV, *De communione infirmorum*, n. 10 begins on page 71
and continues on 72 with the umbrella-or-baldachin availability clause;
nn. 12–13 on page 72 put the priest carrying the Sacrament beneath it.
The old locator 70–71 missed most of that passage. This supports an
attributed account of the reproduced 1925 text. It does not establish
unchanged wording in 1952, a complete 1962 operational sequence, or freedom
from resetting errors.

The registered 1952 edition remains catalog-level only. A second search
found [Maranatha's page headed as a 1952 Ritual
edition](https://www.maranatha.it/rituale/32text.htm), but its body explicitly
credits Trimelloni's 2007 manual and includes modern Italian commentary.
The identically derived modern Communion PDF is likewise not a Latin 1952
facsimile. Neither was promoted as governing evidence. Catalog notices and
the modern scholarly facsimile description establish the existence of the
1952 edition, not the wording needed here.

The independently checked **Fortescue–O'Connell seventh revised edition
(1943), printed p. 17, PDF p. 38**, describes a smaller umbrella-like
canopy on one rod for short transfers of the Blessed Sacrament within a
church, including one altar to another. The same complete scan and title
verification used for the server-clothing slice applies: SHA-256
`10acb8de9b161ed2c08be7ce767515418bca5d1ed7ddfff2de902179e1b62c7a`,
[Internet Archive artifact](https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.56750/2015.56750.Ceremonies-Of-The-Roman-Rite-Described_text.pdf).
This supports the Latin headword *umbella*, the small one-rod distinction,
and the expressly dated transfer context. It is not independent collation
of the 1952 Ritual. The 1943 color directions are not newly imported into
the drawing or promoted as a universal 1962 rule.

The former Encyclopedia binding also did not resolve and misidentified
the article. The linked [New Advent article](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03297c.htm)
is **Patrick Morrisroe, “Canopy,” volume 3 (1908)**, transcribed by Victoria
Theresa Scarlett. The complete HTML was read and hashed: 11,178 bytes,
SHA-256 `98f2788ab56a59420b6a774bad3b7a2ddd49deb50dd3259758ee4797000c1e5a`.
Its paragraph beginning “Two kinds of canopy” describes the small flat
form on a long staff and distinguishes it from the larger rectangular
baldacchino. The original printed article was not collated. The precise
checked witness is therefore the 2026-11-05 web transcription; its use is
limited to historical terminology and broad morphology. *Ombrellino* is
the Italian name reported there, not a Latin alternate headword.

The object remains a recognition entry in the 1962-horizon dictionary,

prepares or stores the object were removed. Any actual ceremonial
assignment still requires the exact applicable rite. No claim that the
