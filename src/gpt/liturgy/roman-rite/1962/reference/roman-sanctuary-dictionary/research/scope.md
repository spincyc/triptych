# Scope

Status: **held prototype**
Audit date: 2026-07-27

## Governing question

What material objects, vestments, linens, books, furnishings, insignia, and
object compositions must an adult pictorial dictionary show to give a
source-audited account of the universal Roman Rite at the 1962 horizon, while
also identifying substantive documented variants and objects discontinued
before that horizon?

## Publication family

The owner supplies one comprehensive US Letter portrait, monochrome,
image-dominant dictionary and these derived views:

- altar server: everything documented as possibly present around a server,
  whether handled or only recognized;
- sacristan: preparation, assembly, placement, folding, storage, and
  ceremonial changeover;
- master of ceremonies and trainer: roles, transfers, branches, checks, and
  recovery;
- general reader: identity, purpose, brief sourced symbolism, and history;
- pontifical ceremonies: pontifical objects plus the ordinary objects needed
  to understand their compositions.

All facts and artwork have one owner. Derived leaves may select or annotate
canonical records but may not copy or enlarge them independently.

## Included

- Ordinary Low Mass, Missa Cantata, Solemn Mass, pontifical Mass, Requiem
  Mass, Nuptial and other ritual Masses, and the 1962 Holy Week rites.
- A separately marked related-ceremonies section for Asperges, processions,
  Exposition, Benediction, Communion outside Mass, and ceremonies directly
  surrounding Mass.
- Materially distinct, well-documented regional, institutional, or
  religious-community variants.
- A historical section for discontinued Roman objects, arranged
  chronologically and then by functional type.
- English and Latin headwords, substantive aliases, compact practical
  description, handling status, and at most one brief source-bound symbolic
  association per entry.

## Excluded

- Post-1962 status, norms, or additions; these require a parallel
  edition-specific postconciliar publication.
- Other rites and uses except where a documented Roman relationship is
  necessary to prevent misidentification.
- Decorative differences that do not change construction, identification, or
  use.
- An attempted census of every parish custom or surviving artifact.
- Unsourced dimensions, folds, cross placement, colors, symbolism, ritual
  functions, or chronology.
- Pronunciation in ordinary entry blocks. A selective appendix may cover
  commonly spoken Latin terms after lexical review.

## Completeness claim

No completeness claim is authorized. The official source corpus has not been
closed; the material-culture corpus has not been defined; the canonical
inventory contains 38 records but no publication-ready record; and the
completeness matrix has open rows in every category. Seven records are
labelled `source-audited`, seven `art-reviewed`, and seven
`priestly-review-ready`, but those intermediate workflow labels neither close
a category nor establish eligibility for an authoritative edition.
Reader-facing leaves must retain prototype/hold language until the source,
inventory, variant, artwork, rights, review, and release conditions close.

## Status vocabulary boundary

Canonical `workflow_state` describes editorial progress: `lead`,
`identified`, `source-audited`, `art-reviewed`,
`priestly-review-ready`, `publication-ready`, or `held`. It must not be
confused with an object's liturgical status.

The governing profile requires object status to distinguish universal Roman,
conditional or privileged, authorized or regional, religious-community,
practical or local furnishing, historical, and unresolved use. The current
structured schema does not yet represent that vocabulary exactly: it lacks
dedicated conditional-or-privileged and practical-or-local values and uses
`religious-order` and `institutional` instead of the profile's broader
classes. Until the schema and existing records are reconciled, no structured
status count is evidence that the corresponding corpus boundary is complete.

## Evidence boundary

Official, edition-identified liturgical and ceremonial books control ritual
presence, status, function, and relationships. Contemporary ceremonials may
support practical detail where competent and checked. Dated artifacts,
catalogs, manufacturers, museums, and specialist studies may support
morphology or material history but cannot by themselves establish ritual use.
OCR, search snippets, current retailer photographs, and image-generation
outputs are leads only.

## Jurisdiction and date

Rite: Roman Rite.

Language of publication: English, with Latin headwords.
Jurisdiction: universal Roman provision unless a record explicitly declares a
documented narrower status.
