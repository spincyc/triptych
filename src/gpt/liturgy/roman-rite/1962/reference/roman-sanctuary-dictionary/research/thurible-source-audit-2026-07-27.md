# Thurible source and artwork audit

## Evidence boundary

The exact 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Ritus servandus* IV.4, VI.5, VII.10,
and VIII.8, names the *thuribulum* and *thuriferarius* and establishes the
checked Solemn-Mass and sung-Mass incense relationships. These rubrics control
object identity and role, not morphology, dimensions, chain technique,
charcoal procedure, or swinging instruction.

The Benziger Brothers *Church Goods Catalogue* (1927), Internet Archive item
`churchgoodscatal00benz`, was downloaded as an exact 228-page PDF. Its
SHA-256 is
`b09d9bf5920d3a0e137519e53e8064fb42a6e0702aa26779edf2178200df8ab0`.
Printed pages 116–117 / artifact PDF pages 154–155 were rendered and visually
inspected. They show multiple commercial censers with footed bowls, removable
pierced lids, three bowl-support chains, and a separate central lid-lifting
chain through the upper control. Model 4145 is listed at 10½ inches total
height with a 4½-inch bowl; model 4144 is listed at 15 inches with a 7-inch
bowl. The publication uses the smaller exemplar only to bound scale and uses a
neutral synthesis rather than catalog ornament, material, or exact silhouette.

Metropolitan Museum of Art object 464340 is retained only as a variation
control. The checked API record identifies one Swiss censer made before 1477
and separately measures its body without chain, lid, base, and chain. Its
historical material, dimensions, ornament, date, and silhouette are not
generalized and did not control the drawing.

## Safety boundary

The altar-server entry teaches recognition and non-operational safety only:
a trained thurifer, control at the upper ring and chains, rehearsal with a
cold empty vessel, and an appointed safe resting place. It gives no
charcoal-lighting, loading, extinguishing, or swinging procedure.
