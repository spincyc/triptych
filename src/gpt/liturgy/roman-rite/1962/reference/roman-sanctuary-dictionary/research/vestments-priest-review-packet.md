## How to record review

For each question, record:

- reviewer name and competence relevant to the question;
- review date;
- exact source, edition, and locus checked;
- answer: `correct`, `incorrect`, `ambiguous`, or `outside competence`;
- a concise correction instruction;
- whether the defect blocks object identity, liturgical use, or only visual
  polish.

“Looks traditional” and “matches local practice” are useful leads but are not
source verification. Regional or institutional examples must be labelled as
such.

## Plate VEST-001: priestly vestments

Asset:
`shared/artwork/pencil/RSD-ART-VEST-001-priestly-vestments-study.png`

Current disposition: **rejected study; do not publish**

### Questions for a priest or ceremonialist

1. Does the plate include the complete ordinary vesting sequence required for
   the celebrant in the selected 1962 Mass contexts, without confusing the
   cope or biretta with items worn throughout Mass?
2. Are the priest's stole and maniple recognizably distinct in proportion,
   termination, and worn use?
3. Which worn stole arrangements need separate figures: crossed before the
   breast, hanging straight, or another ceremony/rank-specific form?
4. Is the biretta form shown adequate as a representative secular-clergy form,
   and which peak/tuft/rank variations are substantive enough to illustrate?
5. Should the cope appear on this plate as a Mass-adjacent vestment or move to
   a related-ceremonies plate?

### Questions requiring textile/material expertise

1. The generated amice resembles a square bib with a formed neck opening. Is
   that morphology false or misleading for the representative Roman amice?
   Specify the correct rectangle, neck relationship, tapes, and fold/worn
   views.
2. Do the Roman and Gothic chasubles show genuinely diagnostic construction,
   or merely two generated silhouettes? Identify the minimum defensible front,
   back, side, and worn views.
3. Is the cope's hood/shield, orphrey, and clasp relationship structurally
   plausible?
4. Which ornamental differences must not be mistaken for object variants?

### Required correction before regeneration

- Replace the amice with a source-led flat and tied comparison.
- Separate stole and maniple into unambiguous isolated specimens and worn
  insets.
- Regenerate chasuble forms only from identified, reusable visual witnesses.
- Retain the current plate only as a layout-density study.

## Plate VEST-002: deacon and subdeacon

Asset:
`shared/artwork/pencil/RSD-ART-VEST-002-sacred-ministers-study.png`

Current disposition: **rejected study; do not publish**

### Questions for a priest or ceremonialist

1. Confirm the complete worn ensemble for deacon and subdeacon at Solemn Mass
   in the selected 1962 horizon, including maniple and the presence or absence
   and arrangement of the stole.
2. Confirm the deacon's diagonal stole path and fastening point. Which parts
   must remain visible when the dalmatic is worn?
3. Confirm that the subdeacon has no stole in this setting.
4. Identify exact ceremonies, dates, and reform dispositions governing folded
   chasubles and the broad stole. Should these be in the main 1962 body,
   a branch plate, or the chronological historical section?
5. Identify any Mass or related-ceremony context in which a dalmatic or
   tunicle appears on another minister and therefore needs a separately
   qualified record.

### Questions requiring textile/material expertise

1. Which construction features reliably distinguish dalmatic and tunicle at
   this horizon? If no stable visual distinction exists apart from convention
   or ornament, say so explicitly.
2. Are the sleeves, side openings, length, and orphrey arrangements in the
   generated isolated garments plausible?
3. Does the folded study at the bottom represent a real garment state, or an
   invented folded textile that should be discarded?
4. What front/back/worn views make the deacon's stole and maniple legible
   without showing impossible layering?

### Required correction before regeneration

- Discard the first figure as a representation of a complete deacon: it shows
  an alb and diagonal band without the requested dalmatic ensemble.
- Generate the deacon and subdeacon separately, first as plain construction
  studies and then as complete worn ensembles.
- Hold folded-chasuble and broad-stole art until direct chronology and
  ceremonial loci are bound.

### Questions for a priest, MC, or experienced sacristan

1. Which cassock-and-surplice ensemble should be the neutral representative
   server form without universalizing local color, buttons, or cut?
2. Are surplice, cotta, and rochet separate required headwords for this
   dictionary? State the liturgical/rank distinction, not merely a local
   vocabulary preference.
3. Who may wear the rochet in the relevant 1962 contexts, and what must a
   server recognize without treating it as server dress?
4. Are apparelled amice and alb universal, authorized variants, institutional
   uses, or historical examples at this horizon?
5. Which choir members or assisting clerics require additional dress records
   beyond cassock and surplice?

### Questions requiring textile/material expertise

1. Identify reliable differences in neckline, sleeve construction, body
   length, gathering, and lace placement among surplice, cotta, and rochet.
2. Does the generated rochet wrongly resemble a shortened alb or surplice?
3. Are the generated apparelled collar-like details false? If so, specify the
   correct placement and form of apparels.
4. Which front/back views can share a scale without implying universal
   dimensions?

### Required correction before regeneration

- Discard the two collar-like “apparelled” details unless an inspected witness
  confirms them; they are not approved representations.
- Use one sourced garment family per generation call rather than asking a
  model to infer three confusable white garments simultaneously.
- Place rochet on the prelatial/choir cross-reference plate after rank and
  morphology are verified.

## Release boundary

A priest's approval of ceremonial identity does not by itself approve textile
morphology, rights, print quality, or completeness. Conversely, a museum
object's accurate construction does not establish its universal Roman use in
1962. Publication approval requires both evidence tracks, the ordinary
artwork audit, a monochrome print check, and the repository's independent
review.
