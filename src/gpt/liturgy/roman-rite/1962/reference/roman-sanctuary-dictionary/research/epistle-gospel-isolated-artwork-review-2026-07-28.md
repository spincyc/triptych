# Epistle- and Gospel-book isolated artwork review

## Purpose and source boundary

The existing in-use figures teach ministerial relationships but do not give
the reader a clear isolated view of each book. These two project-generated
figures add object recognition while preserving the object records' explicit
limit: the 1962 Missal establishes distinct books and their uses, not a
universal binding design.

## Accepted figures

- `RPD-FIG-books-0008-epistle-book-isolated-alpha.png` shows one representative
  substantial closed lesson book with visible spine, page block, and ribbons.
- `RPD-FIG-books-0009-gospel-book-isolated-alpha.png` shows one representative
  substantial open lesson book with page block and ribbons. Its page texture is
  non-semantic and contains no legible text.

The closed/open contrast is a teaching composition, not a claim that the
Epistle book is always closed or the Gospel book always open outside the
depicted relationships.

## Production and visual review

The built-in OpenAI image-generation interface produced each graphite study on
a flat green removal ground. The prompts prohibited persons, hands, stands,
candles, altars, companion books, words, labels, heraldry, crosses, borders,
and watermarks. ImageMagick removed only the flat green ground at ten-percent
fuzz after the installed Pillow-based helper was unavailable.
The retained pencil subjects were then normalized to grayscale while
preserving their alpha channels, eliminating residual green edge color
without introducing a rectangular matte.

Both alpha PNGs have transparent corners, continuous pencil edges, generous
padding, and no floating rectangular ground. They remain paired with—not
substituted for—the checked in-use figures.
