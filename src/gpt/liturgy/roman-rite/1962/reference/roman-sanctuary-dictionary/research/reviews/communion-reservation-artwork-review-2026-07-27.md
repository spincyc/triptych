# Independent review: Communion, reservation, and exposition

Review date: 2026-07-27 (America/Chicago).

Review object: `research/slices/communion-reservation.md` and generated leads
`DIC-ART-CR-001` and `DIC-ART-CR-002`.

Decision: **not publication-ready**. Retain the records and images as
generated research leads only.

## Evidence used

This review used the slice's disclosed evidence ceiling, the registered 1962
*Missale Romanum* identity and checked ordinary-Mass loci already recorded in
the altar-server-guide source audit, and the registered *Rituale Romanum*
records. The latter presently provide:

- a catalog identity, but no exact artifact, for the 1952 typical edition; and
- a verified reading of title IV, chapter 2 in a restricted modern resetting
  of the 1925 typical edition, explicitly retained as historical and
  comparative evidence rather than a controlling 1962 witness.

No checked source presently controls the morphology of a ciborium, pyx,
tabernacle veil, sanctuary lamp, monstrance, or lunette in this slice. The
1962 Missal evidence can support selected ordinary-Mass relationships among
the chalice, paten, Communion plate, purification, and distribution, but it
does not by itself verify the shapes generated on these plates.

## Inventory findings

The slice correctly labels every proposed record as an identified lead and
does not claim publication readiness. Its principal distinctions are useful
and should be preserved:

- the Mass paten is not the handled Communion plate;
- a lunette is related to, but is not the monstrance itself;
- English “pyx,” English “ciborium,” and Latin *pyxis* require a
  period-specific lexical audit; and
- reservation, Communion outside Mass, exposition, and Benediction cannot be
  controlled by the ordinary-Mass loci alone.

The following remain release blockers:

1. No exact 1952 *Rituale Romanum* artifact and loci have been checked for the
   relevant rites.
2. No claim-level legal source has been checked for reservation, the
   tabernacle, or the sanctuary lamp at the 1962 horizon.
3. No competent, dated material-form source supports the depicted
   construction, relative scale, or admitted variants.
4. `obj-mass-paten` conflicts with the existing canonical-style
   `obj-paten` identity elsewhere in the owner tree. The owner must reconcile
   the stable ID before promotion; two independent canonical identities must
   not be created merely to emphasize the distinction from the Communion
   plate.
5. The proposed handling statements remain prudential leads. “Recognizes
   only,” “must not handle,” and similar formulas need a declared audience,
   ceremony, and authority or must be presented as the dictionary's explicit
   safety policy rather than as universal rubrics.

## `DIC-ART-CR-001`

### Acceptable as a research lead

- The plate is clean monochrome pencil-style art without generated labels.
- The ciborium and chalice each have a coherent bowl, stem, node, and foot.
- A shallow unhandled plate and a handled Communion plate are visually
  separable.
- Closed and open case views make the intended pyx comparison easy to locate.

### Publication blockers

1. **The two pyx views are not mechanically consistent.** The closed view has
   both a rectangular projecting clasp and a round front fitting. The open
   view retains only the round fitting; the projecting clasp has no visible
   counterpart on the lid or body. They cannot safely be labeled as closed
   and open states of one reference object.
2. **The pyx reads as a modern hinged trinket or pill case.** No checked
   source in the slice establishes that case construction as the
   representative Roman form at the declared horizon.
3. **Relative scale is unstated.** The large ciborium, pyx, two plate forms,
   and chalice share a plate without a scale key. A reader could infer that
   the shallow Mass paten is nearly as wide as the ciborium or that the
   handled plate is smaller than intended.
4. **The handled plate is not self-identifying.** Without a contextual inset
   beneath a communicant's chin or a verified construction source, it can be
   mistaken for a domestic tasting pan or handled dish.
5. The image remains 8-bit sRGB rather than the project's ordinary stripped
   grayscale publishing state. Normalization may occur only after content
   approval and must receive a new recorded hash and full-size comparison.

Verdict: **reject for publication; retain as generated lead**. A corrected
plate needs a source-verified pyx form, mechanically identical closed/open
views, a scale treatment, and a small use inset for the Communion plate.

## `DIC-ART-CR-002`

### Acceptable as a research lead

- The tabernacle, separate textile cover, hanging lamp, monstrance, and two
  intended lunette views are spatially separated.
- The tabernacle has one coherent door, hinges, lock, and enclosure.
- The hanging lamp has one vessel and a coherent three-chain suspension.
- The monstrance has one coherent stem and foot and reads immediately as a
  sunburst exposition vessel.

### Publication blockers

1. **The lunette is visually ambiguous and potentially misleading.** The
   isolated lower-center object is a bare crescent. The inset and the
   monstrance center also show a crescent at the bottom of a circular opening.
   Nothing depicts an unmistakable complete circular Host holder or explains
   the relationship among the crescent support, glazing or frame, Host, and
   monstrance aperture. A reader could conclude that “lunette” simply means a
   loose crescent ornament.
2. **The main monstrance appears empty.** Its central aperture contains the
   same crescent motif but no Host. This may be defensible as an empty
   assembly study only if expressly labeled; it is unsafe as an unlabeled
   in-use exposition reference.
3. **The textile is only a generic fitted cover.** No checked source
   establishes its cut, fringe, cross placement, or manner of fitting as a
   representative 1962 tabernacle veil.
4. **The tabernacle is shown as a freestanding cabinet without context.** The
   image does not establish its altar relationship, anchoring, interior, or
   reservation state. It may serve as a silhouette lead, not as a complete
   sanctuary-composition reference.
5. **The lamp establishes one hanging form only.** It cannot support a general
   identification entry or a “substantive variant” claim until standing and
   hanging forms and their status are sourced.
6. As with `CR-001`, the received sRGB asset must not be normalized into a
   publishing raster until its content is accepted.

Verdict: **reject for publication; retain as generated lead**. A replacement
must show the lunette assembly unambiguously in isolated, loaded, and
in-monstrance states, or split the components into separately labeled,
source-verified figures. The tabernacle veil and lamp require their own
verified form decisions.

## Re-review gate

The slice may return for publication review only after:

1. the exact governing editions and claim-level loci are bound;
2. lexical and stable-ID conflicts are resolved;
3. at least one competent, dated source supports each depicted morphology;
4. use-context insets prevent the pyx, Communion plate, and lunette from being
   mistaken for ordinary objects or loose decorative components;
5. an artwork record supplies scale treatment, exact hash, grayscale state,
   effective resolution, consumers, and independent full-size review; and
6. the canonical object, variant, artwork, plate, completeness, rights, and
   source records agree.
