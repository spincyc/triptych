# Credence table and sedilia source audit

Date: 2026-07-28

## Question and boundary

This pass asks only what the altar-server alpha can safely teach about the
credence table and sedilia: identity, ordinary ceremonial relationship,
representative movable forms, local placement variation, confusables, and the
absence of handling permission from appearance alone. It does not settle
architectural sedilia, pontifical seating, universal material or dimensions,
cloth color or drop, decorative form, or local sanctuary planning.

## Exact controls checked

- Adrian Fortescue, *The Ceremonies of the Roman Rite Described*, first
  edition (1917), exact Digital Library of India facsimile delivered through
  Internet Archive, SHA-256
  `10acb8de9b161ed2c08be7ce767515418bca5d1ed7ddfff2de902179e1b62c7a`.
  Printed pp. 8–9 / artifact PDF pp. 29–30 were read visually after OCR
  location. Page 8 places the credence and the sacred ministers' three-place
  seat on the Epistle side in Fortescue's teaching model; page 9 separately
  locates servers' seats. The passage supports function and distinction, not a
  universal construction or exact sanctuary geometry.
- The same exact facsimile, printed pp. 78–79 / artifact PDF pp. 99–100,
  was rechecked as contextual evidence: the Asperges preparation names
  vestments at the sedilia and later has acolytes carry candles to the
  credence. It corroborates ceremonial use but adds no furniture morphology.
- *Missale Romanum* (1962), *Ritus servandus* VI.4 and the already checked
  use controls remain the governing edition-specific evidence for objects
  prepared or returned at the credence. They do not prescribe the generated
  table's construction.
- Patrick Morrisroe, “Credence,” *Catholic Encyclopedia* 4 (1908), exact New
  Advent HTML SHA-256
  `1ff6feaae16bf8c835e7198b39bc160177fdc1c61af0f78ee7247070dcffe043`,
  was read in full as a dated terminology and variation aid. Its materials,
  dimensions, cloth prescriptions, and exact placement are not generalized.
- George Cyprian Alston, “Sedilia,” *Catholic Encyclopedia* 13 (1912), exact
  New Advent HTML SHA-256
  `141a6eeb682562b53a8e40e7c064f99ae8604da13bc64884c5077d6c87ed8e38`,
  was read in full as a dated historical and morphology-variation aid. It
  supports the existence of movable chairs, movable benches, and architectural
  forms; chronology, regional prevalence, levels, decoration, and seat order
  are outside this consumer.

The two New Advent artifacts are restricted remote records: the underlying
articles are public domain in the United States, but no redistribution basis
is assumed for the host's exact transcription, revisions, markup, or
presentation.

## Artwork disposition

The legacy
`shared/artwork/pencil/RSD-ART-001-sanctuary-furnishings.png` is rejected for
these two altar-server consumers because its exact generation provenance and
rights record are incomplete. The file and its history remain untouched for
other held consumers.

Two replacement rasters were generated without reference images and normalized
to stripped 8-bit grayscale:

- `art-credence-table-isolated`: a plain, empty, four-legged rectangular table
  with optional lower shelf and representative unornamented white linen;
- `art-sedilia-movable-variants`: three matching separate movable chairs and
  one plain movable three-place bench.

Both rasters contain no words, labels, semantic marks, people, ritual objects,
altar-like frontal, cross, tabernacle, throne, canopy, heraldry, niche, or
fixed side geometry. They are not evidence. TeX owns the Epistle-side teaching
model, alternatives, local qualifications, confusables, server boundary, and
not-to-common-scale notice.

## Consumer synthesis

The credence entry teaches a pickup-and-return station whose form, covering,
contents, and precise location vary. Presence on the credence never grants
permission to handle an object.

The sedilia entry teaches sacred-ministers' seating through two representative
movable forms and distinguishes it from local server seating and pontifical
furnishings. Fixed or movable furniture is handled only when the sacristan or
MC assigns the task.
