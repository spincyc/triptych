Owner boundary: the sacristy as the ministers' immediate preparation and

handwashing, storage, safe object return, and the departure signal; and the
distinctions a server needs in order not to confuse practical furniture with
liturgical objects. Incidental office, janitorial, mechanical, and domestic
contents remain outside the dictionary unless they create a real
identification or safety problem.

## Evidence ceiling

The narrow checked evidence establishes:

- the 1962 Missal's prepared place for the priest's handwashing and the
  sacristy cross-or-image reverence;
- a contemporary ceremonial manual's vesting table and customary departure
  bell;
- a specialist period synthesis's sacristy function, labelled vestment cases,
  prominent cross or image, lavatory, and customary bell; and
- a specialist historical synthesis's broad piscina or sacrarium function,
  variable location, and several broad forms.

Those sources do **not** establish one required room plan, a universal
furniture suite, cabinet construction, storage hardware, dimensions, hidden
plumbing, or the representative morphology of the generated plate.
`research/source-audit.md` records exact bindings and loci.

## Expansion queue for reviewer triage

These are candidates only. They are not canonical objects or claims. The
reviewer is asked to mark each **essential**, **useful**, **incidental**, or
**out of scope** before further source and artwork work.

### Storage and preparation

- flat vestment press or broad shallow vestment drawers;
- hanging cope or vestment wardrobe;
- linen cupboard or labelled linen drawers;
- liturgical-book cupboard or bookcase;
- secured sacred-vessel cupboard or safe;
- fitted chalice, ciborium, cruet, and accessory storage;
- server cassock, surplice, and cotta wardrobe or rail;
- vesting counter distinct from a freestanding vesting table;
- clean/used/soiled linen containers where that distinction is taught to
  servers or sacristans.

### Processional, flame, and incense return

- processional-cross and staff rack;
- tall candlestick and torch rack;
- candle cupboard and taper rack;
- thurible suspension stand;
- ventilated or fire-resistant charcoal preparation and return station;
- ash receptacle and flame-extinguishing station.

These forms are strongly local and safety-driven. No item should be shown as a
universal Roman furnishing. A review should identify which are genuinely
recognition-critical for young servers and which belong only in the
sacristan edition.

### Nearby devotional and practical furnishings

- holy-water font at or near the sacristy door, documented as customary;
- kneeler or prie-dieu used for preparation, if a ceremony-specific or
  institutional source supports it;
- server bench or seating only where it affects line-up or vesting;
- sacristan's desk, register cabinet, clock, rota board, mirror, garment-care
  equipment, utility sink, step stool, and cleaning cupboard.

The last group is presumptively incidental. It should enter only when a
reviewer identifies a concrete liturgical, recognition, or safety reason.

## Shared pencil review plate

### `art-sacristy-preparation-plate`

Asset:
`shared/artwork/pencil/RSD-ART-001-sacristy-preparation.png`.

The plate shows an empty table; low shallow-drawer case; tall hanging
wardrobe; crucifix; ordinary wall basin; niche and column piscina candidates;
and wall-mounted pull bell. It is a dense monochrome orientation sheet, not a
room composition and not to common scale.

The plate is useful for asking whether the categories are visually separable,
but it is **rejected as publication art in its current state**:

1. the two piscina candidates carry generated cross ornaments that were
   expressly excluded from the brief and are not source controlled;
2. the drawer case and wardrobe have no checked material-culture reference;
3. the crucifix may visually suppress the rubric's alternative of a suitable
   sacred image;
4. the ordinary basin is a modern representative form, not a form prescribed
   by the Missal;
5. the shared plate does not show scale or use context; and
6. none of the furnishings has passed priestly, factual, full-size print,
   consumer, or release review.

The full prompt, received and normalized hashes, technical properties, and
failed visual-review findings are recorded in
`research/artwork-manifest.toml`. The asset should be displayed to a reviewer
only with this qualification.

## Promotion boundary

No candidate may move to `publication-ready` merely because a priest
recognizes it from experience. Promotion still requires:

1. written reviewer answers tied to the stable question IDs;
2. reconciliation of universal, customary, institutional, and local status;
3. exact lexical review of English and Latin headwords;
4. competent material-form evidence for anything illustrated as a
   representative object;
5. corrected artwork with unsupported ornament removed;
6. independent factual, visual, monochrome-print, rights, and consumer
   review; and
7. agreement among object, artwork, plate, source, rights, completeness, and
   edition records.
