## Evidence ceiling

The narrow checked evidence establishes:

- the 1962 Missal's prepared place for the priest's handwashing and the
  sacristy cross-or-image reverence;
- a contemporary ceremonial manual's vesting table and customary departure
  bell;
- a specialist period synthesis's sacristy function, labelled vestment cases,
  prominent cross or image, lavatory, and customary bell; and
- a specialist historical synthesis's broad piscina or sacrarium function,
  variable location, and several broad forms.

Those sources do **not** establish one required room plan, a universal
furniture suite, cabinet construction, storage hardware, dimensions, hidden
plumbing, or the representative morphology of the generated plate.
`research/source-audit.md` records exact bindings and loci.

## Expansion queue for source-and-scope triage

These are candidates only. They are not canonical objects or claims. The
next inventory pass must classify each as **essential**, **useful**,
**incidental**, or **out of scope** from documented recognition, handling, or
safety needs before further source and artwork work.

### Storage and preparation

- flat vestment press or broad shallow vestment drawers;
- hanging cope or vestment wardrobe;
- linen cupboard or labelled linen drawers;
- liturgical-book cupboard or bookcase;
- secured sacred-vessel cupboard or safe;
- fitted chalice, ciborium, cruet, and accessory storage;
- server cassock, surplice, and cotta wardrobe or rail;
- vesting counter distinct from a freestanding vesting table;
- clean/used/soiled linen containers where that distinction is taught to
  servers or sacristans.

### Processional, flame, and incense return

- processional-cross and staff rack;
- tall candlestick and torch rack;
- candle cupboard and taper rack;
- thurible suspension stand;
- ventilated or fire-resistant charcoal preparation and return station;
- ash receptacle and flame-extinguishing station.

These forms are strongly local and safety-driven. No item should be shown as a
universal Roman furnishing. Source-and-scope analysis must identify which are
genuinely recognition-critical for young servers and which belong only in the
sacristan edition.

### Nearby devotional and practical furnishings

- holy-water font at or near the sacristy door, documented as customary;
- kneeler or prie-dieu used for preparation, if a ceremony-specific or
  institutional source supports it;
- server bench or seating only where it affects line-up or vesting;
- sacristan's desk, register cabinet, clock, rota board, mirror, garment-care
  equipment, utility sink, step stool, and cleaning cupboard.

The last group is presumptively incidental. It should enter only when the
record identifies a concrete liturgical, recognition, or safety reason.

## Promotion boundary

No candidate may move to `publication-ready` merely because someone
recognizes it from experience. Promotion still requires:

1. reconciliation of universal, customary, institutional, and local status;
2. exact lexical checks of English and Latin headwords;
3. competent material-form evidence for anything illustrated as a
   representative object;
4. corrected artwork with unsupported ornament removed;
5. passed source, rights, identity, safety, and basic visual-usability checks;
   and
6. agreement among object, artwork, plate, source, rights, completeness, and
   edition records.
