# Bugia or hand-candlestick source audit

Audit date: 2026-07-28 (America/Chicago)

Status: source-checked disposition for later canonical-record and artwork
remediation; no shared dictionary source or publication was changed in this
lane.

## Identity and 1962-horizon use

The 1948 Marietti printing of the received *Cæremoniale Episcoporum*
establishes the controlling ceremonial identity at this horizon, subject to
later competent changes:

- I.XI.1 assigns distinct ministers of the book and candle; I.XI.4 says the
  candle minister also serves at the altar.
- I.XX.1 calls the object a small silver instrument commonly called the
  *bugia*. It bears a lighted candle. Its minister approaches regularly at
  the right of the minister holding the book near the bishop.
- The same locus expressly requires it even when the ambient light is bright
  enough that no reading light is needed. It is therefore inaccurate to
  explain its 1962-horizon use as merely an emergency reading lamp.
- I.XX.2--4 supplies the immediate reading context and branches: the bishop
  reads specified Mass texts from the minister-held Missal when assisting,
  and all Mass texts from it when he himself celebrates, subject to the
  precedence exception in section 3.

The exact source-library passage is
`passage.catholic-church.caeremoniale-episcoporum.marietti-third-after-typical-1948.book-1.chapter-20`.
The broader minister passage remains
`passage.catholic-church.caeremoniale-episcoporum.marietti-third-after-typical-1948.book-1.chapters-11-12`.

## English and alternate headwords

“Hand-candlestick” is the clearest reader-facing English headword.
The governing Latin text says that the instrument is *vulgo Bugiam vocant*:
*bugia* is explicitly a common name, not presented there as a formal
rubrical Latin object name. “Bugia” is therefore suitable as the familiar
alternate headword.

Fortescue's earlier public-domain *Ceremonies of the Roman Rite Described*
calls it a “small hand-candle in a portable candlestick” and gives *scotula*
or *palmatorium*. That evidence supports historical English terminology, but
the inspected copy is not the twelfth revised 1962 edition. Do not promote
either synonym as the controlling 1962 headword until the exact 1962 pages
are inspected.

## Server relationship

The candle minister is not the book minister. A useful in-use plate should
show the candle bearer beside the book bearer, with the bugia near the book
and the candle minister normally on the book minister's right. Avoid a
generic isolated caption implying that an ordinary altar server carries it
at every Mass. The source assigns a pontifical minister and the exact
ceremony supplies the branch.

The primary locus controls the relationship, not the pose of any surviving
metalwork exemplar. It establishes no one-handed grip, elevation, distance
from the page, procession posture, or handoff choreography.

## Morphology ceiling

Safe universal depiction:

- a small portable candle-bearing instrument;
- silver in the governing witness;
- one lighted candle.

Not established by the governing locus:

- long versus short handle;
- a broad circular pan or a narrow bobeche;
- feet, thumb rest, matchbox, socket profile, dimensions, or ornament;
- gold or gilt variants;
- candle composition, diameter, or length.

One rights-clear documentary exemplar is now registered as
`artifact.maximilian775.bishop-watterson-bugia-photograph.wikimedia-commons-2025.commons-original-jpeg-e6addffc`.
The photograph is CC BY 4.0 and its visible museum card dates the identified
bugia to the episcopate of John A. Watterson, 1880--1899. This exemplar
visibly has a low broad circular pan, central socket, short feet, and a
candle. It does **not** visibly establish a handle: the apparent handled form
at the left belongs to a separate object in the crowded display. Only the
pan, socket, candle, and feet can control a drawing explicitly identified as
this historical exemplar, and even those features cannot be generalized as
the only permissible form.

The Commons photograph contains a cluttered museum display and should not be
used directly as a dictionary plate. It is admissible as a checked reference
for a project-created isolated drawing with attribution recorded in the
artwork manifest.

## Artwork disposition

Create two complementary views:

1. an isolated, explicitly exemplar-based line study derived from the
   Watterson photograph, retaining only its visible pan, socket, candle, and
   feet and making no dimensional or handle claim; and
2. an in-use composition controlled by *Cæremoniale Episcoporum* I.XX.1:
   bishop reading, book minister, and distinct candle minister at the book
   minister's right, with the lit bugia beside the book.

Label the combined scene “Book and hand-candlestick service.” Keep the
hand-candlestick as its own entry because it is materially and functionally
distinct and can be understood in isolation. Do not merge it with the book,
ordinary altar candlesticks, Gospel-procession candles, or the Elevation
candle.

The existing generated side-handled specimen is rejected: neither the
governing ceremonial locus nor the registered exemplar supports that handle.
The current evidence supports a bounded exemplar-based reconstruction of the
visible pan, socket, candle, and feet, not an elaborate or universal
morphology claim.
