- *Rubricae generales* 528, printed p. XXXVI / artifact PDF p. 34, requires
  the wine and water cruets, small basin (*pelvicula*), manuterge
  (*manutergium*), small bell, and Communion plate to be prepared on the
  Epistle-side service table. The correct printed locator is p. XXXVI, not
  p. LII.
- *Ritus servandus* VII.6 begins on artifact PDF p. 60 and continues on p. 61
  (printed pp. LVIII–LIX). It directs the celebrant to the Epistle side, where
  the minister pours water while the celebrant washes the tips of his thumbs
  and index fingers.
- VII.7 on artifact p. 61 says that the celebrant dries his hands.
- VII.10 on artifact p. 61 names the acolytes ministering the water cruet with
  the small basin and manuterge after the celebrant is incensed at Solemn
  Mass.

These loci establish presence, pairing, preparation-table relationship,
washing and drying functions, and the Solemn-Mass acolyte relationship. They
do not prescribe material, ornament, dimensions, basin silhouette, towel
fold, disposal method, or the division of articles between two servers at Low
Mass.

## Historical terminology aid

Joseph Braun's signed 1910 “Manuterge” article in *The Catholic Encyclopedia*,
volume 9, was inspected in an exact New Advent HTML response. It distinguishes
the Mass hand towel from the sacristy towel and places the Mass manuterge with
the finger-bowl and cruets. The consumer uses that terminology and distinction
only. Its reported customary dimensions, lace, embroidery, circular
sacristy-towel form, pontifical statements, and historical claims are not
generalized as a 1962 norm.

## Material-culture variation and exclusion

The Metropolitan Museum of Art Collection API identifies object 188520 as a
late-sixteenth- or early-seventeenth-century pewter “Lavabo basin,” measuring
17.8 by 30.5 by 22.9 cm. Its Open Access image shows a broad oval basin with a
projecting lip and architectural backplate. The record does not identify it as
a Roman Mass vessel. It therefore serves only as a historical variation and
exclusion witness: none of its distinctive morphology, material, or dimensions
controls the source-minimal Mass teaching plate.
