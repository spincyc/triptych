# *Caeremoniale Episcoporum* source audit

Audit date: 2026-07-27.

## Identified witness

The acquired witness is the Marietti *Caeremoniale Episcoporum*, *editio
tertia post typicam*. Its title page says that the book was issued by order of
Clement VIII, Innocent X, and Benedict XIII and revised by authority of
Benedict XIV and Leo XIII. Marietti is identified as printer of the Apostolic
See and Sacred Congregation of Rites. The following leaf records conformity
with the approved exemplar and an imprimatur at Turin on 11 February 1948.

The exact 271-page scan is registered in the reusable source library. Its
title and approval leaves were checked at page images. OCR was used only for
navigation.

## Evidentiary value for this dictionary

This is now a directly inspectable principal witness for pontifical ministers,
the solemn-pontifical-Mass sequence, episcopal insignia, sanctuary and altar
preparation, and the named objects placed on the credence or entrusted to
ministers. The first object-bearing ranges mapped for later claim-level
collation are:

| Locus | Artifact pages | Dictionary use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| book I, chapters XI-XII, printed pp. 31-42 | 43-53 | ministers and their objects; sanctuary, altar, vestment, book, credence, incense, torch, and service-object inventory |
| book I, chapters XVI-XVII, printed pp. 49-53 | 61-64 | pallium, three named kinds of mitre, and pastoral staff |
| book II, chapter VIII, printed pp. 112-123 | 123-134 | Solemn Pontifical Mass sequence and object relationships |

The source supports liturgical identity, named relationships, prescribed
placement, and ceremonial use at the limits of each checked locus. It is not a
visual-design atlas. A drawing's exact profile, decoration, construction,
dimensions, or claim that one artistic form is normative needs separate
object-history or collection evidence.

## 1962-horizon limit

The 1948 printing is not an unqualified transcript of every rule operative in
1962. It predates the restored Holy Week and the 1960 code of rubrics.
Accordingly:

- its ordinary pontifical and general sanctuary provisions are candidate
  controls only where no later competent book or decree changed them;
- its book II, chapters XXI-XXVIII, must not control the dictionary's 1962
  Holy Week plates;
- Holy Week requires the *Ritus Pontificalis Ordinis Hebdomadae Sanctae
  instaurati*, editio typica (Vatican Polyglot Press, 1957), together with the
  identified restored Holy Week and 1962 Missal witnesses; and
- any apparent calendar, rank, or Mass-category assumption must be checked
  against the 1960-1962 rubrical corpus.

A library catalogue identifies the 1957 *Ritus Pontificalis* as an editio
typica of 55 pages. Its work and edition identity are now registered, but no
exact artifact was located or inspected. It therefore remains a named
acquisition gap, not a source represented as read.

The official *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* volume 49 has now been acquired and
registered as an exact restricted artifact. Its printed pp. 91-95 contain the
Sacred Congregation of Rites' *Ordinationes et declarationes circa Ordinem
hebdomadae sanctae instauratum*, dated 1 February 1957. The act incorporates
the declaration of 15 March 1956, preserves the 16 November 1955 decree and
instruction except where the new act innovates, and supplies twenty-two
numbered provisions. This official amendment witness must accompany the
restored Order in any 1962 Holy Week source stack. It does not substitute for
the missing object-by-object pontifical ceremonial.

## Verification state and next checks

The title and approval identity and the official 1957 amendment act are
verified. The mapped *Caeremoniale* chapters are
inspected source ranges, not blanket-verified transcriptions. Before an object
record cites this book, the editor must:

1. open the narrow rendered page containing the claim;
2. record its book, chapter, paragraph, printed page, and artifact page;
3. check whether a post-1948 book or decree modifies the matter;
4. distinguish an object the book requires from one it permits, presumes,
   lists for a particular solemnity, or reserves to episcopal use; and
5. bind any separate morphological or symbolic claim to its own evidence.

The comprehensive and pontifical editions may now cite this source family for
checked 1948 ceremonial claims. They may not yet describe the pontifical or
Holy Week inventories as complete.
