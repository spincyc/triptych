# Rijks Ecce Homo chapel graphite review

Reviewed on: 2026-07-28

## Decision

The generated graphite derivative of Rijksmuseum object
`RP-F-1997-29-26` is accepted for the comprehensive dictionary only as one
dated, atypical material-culture composition witness.

Full-size comparison with the exact retained Rijks image confirmed the same
frontal chapel bay, round arch and stone surround, elevated altar and steps,
split low rail, flanking kneeling figures, upper devotional figure, paired
branching lights, and dominant suspended lamp structure. The derivative
contains no labels, watermark, border, modern furnishing, freestanding
lectern, or obvious impossible geometry.

The transformation simplifies and regularizes architectural texture and
minor metalwork, sharpens details obscured in the photograph, and cannot
prove the identity or exact count of every small vessel, candle, ornament, or
lamp. Those details are not independently admissible object evidence.

## Source and scope limit

The source is a nineteenth-century photograph of a pilgrimage chapel in
Jerusalem. Its devotional niche, numerous lights, suspended lamp structure,
stone architecture, and partly obscured altar make it atypical. Neither the
source nor this derivative establishes a standard 1962 high altar, a required
object count, reservation arrangement, architectural norm, universal
proportions, or ordinary sanctuary plan.

The derivative may appear only in `ed-comprehensive`, labeled as this dated
chapel witness with those limits. It must not replace the still-held
normative altar-composition plate.

## Production review

The received 1077 by 1461 RGB PNG was generated through the OpenAI built-in
image-generation interface from the retained Rijks image; the exact model and
prompt were not exposed to this reviewer. Admission added no compositional
edit. The accepted file was deterministically converted to stripped 8-bit
grayscale PNG.

The pale, feathered perimeter is intentional page ground rather than a
rectangular frame or transparent cutout. It blends into a white publication
page. Maximum declared placement is 3.5 by 4.75 inches, preserving at least
307 effective dpi.
