# Books and book supports: source pass

Status: **bounded primary-source pass; some 1962 claims verified, morphology
and corpus closure still held**

Audit date: 2026-07-27

## Question and boundary

This pass asked what the Roman books at the 1962 horizon establish about the
Missal, Gospel and Epistle books, markers, cushions, bookstands, lecterns,
covers, and materially different ways of supporting a book. It did not audit
chant books, the complete Pontifical or Ritual, bindings as material objects,
or the historical development of these articles.

The controlling Mass witness was the already registered 1962 Vatican typical
Missal facsimile,
`artifact.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-1962.cmaa-facsimile-pdf`
(SHA-256
`648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`).
Its rendered page images were inspected for the loci below. The separately
registered 1962 Benziger OCR artifact, whose exact hash matched its record,
was used only to locate likely words and passages; it controls no wording.

A 1948 Marietti printing of the *Caeremoniale Episcoporum*, *editio tertia
post typicam*, was also inspected from a complete scan (271 artifact pages;
SHA-256
`af65e16a8a64413db3d5694164d4e612fa4b8e02074939307455c26736729505`;
source delivery:
<https://librinostri.catholica.cz/download/CaeremoniEpisc1948-r0-t.pdf>).
Its title and approval leaves identify Turin and Rome, Marietti, an
imprimatur/conformity approval dated 11 February 1948, and the work as the
edition issued by order of Clement VIII, Innocent X, and Benedict XIII and
revised by authority of Benedict XIV and Leo XIII. It is a strong
edition-identified primary ceremonial witness. Its precise legal continuity
through 1962, including intervening decrees, remains to be established before
it is labelled the unqualified controlling edition.

## Pontifical and morphology controls from the 1948 ceremonial

These findings may support records once the *Caeremoniale Episcoporum*
edition is registered and its 1962 applicability audit is closed:

- Book I, chapter XII, n. 15, printed p. 39, artifact PDF p. 51, names the
  Missal, Gospel book, and Epistle book; directs that they be covered in silk
  of the same color as the other vestments; and places them on the credence
  with a cushion of the same silk and color, **or** with a small silver or
  finely made wooden stand. This is direct evidence for materially different
  pontifical supports (soft cushion; silver stand; wooden stand) and for
  color-matched book covers in that context.
- Book I, chapter VII, n. 5, printed p. 19, artifact PDF p. 31, has the
  assistant priest carry the book with its cushion to the altar, remove the
  Missal and cushion during incensation, and replace them afterward. This
  establishes that the cushion is a movable support used with the book, not
  merely a generic altar textile.
- Book I, chapter X, n. 2, printed p. 30, artifact PDF p. 41, treats a
  minister holding a book as acting *instar legilis* (“in the manner of a
  bookstand”) and remaining motionless; this supports a separate “held-book”
  presentation mode without identifying the minister as a physical object.
- Book II, chapter VIII, nn. 44–45, printed pp. 121–122, artifact PDF
  pp. 132–133, gives three Gospel-book support arrangements: the subdeacon
  holds the open book; a lectern or pulpit may be used; or an architectural
  stone ambo reached by steps may be used. When a lectern/pulpit is used, it
  is covered and ornamented with gold cloth or silk of the vestment color.
  These are substantive spatial and support variants, not decorative variants
  of one portable stand.

**Evidence ceiling:** inspected in the rendered scan and checked against the
edition’s printed chapter, number, and page. Treat as an
edition-identified primary ceremonial witness, but **hold the 1962 normative
label** pending confirmation of the exact operative edition and material
amendments. The ceremonial’s pontifical silk-cover and precious-material
directions must not be generalized to every Low or Solemn Mass.

## Covers, bindings, and morphology: present limits

- The 1962 Missal rubrics inspected in this pass do not prescribe the external
  binding, boards, spine, clasps, corner pieces, fore-edge treatment, cover
  ornament, number or colors of ribbons, or exact dimensions of the altar
  Missal.
- The 1948 ceremonial supplies a real contextual rule for silk-covered
  Missal, Gospel, and Epistle books at pontifical Mass; it does **not** prove
  that every Roman liturgical book must have a color-matched textile cover.
- The terms *legile*, pulpit, and ambo overlap by function in some ceremonial
  passages but do not identify one morphology. Canonical records should
  distinguish at least: small altar Missal stand; portable sanctuary lectern;
  Gospel lectern/pulpit; architectural ambo; and a minister holding the book.
- A sloped metal or wooden stand and a cushion are substantive alternatives
  because the governing sources expressly contrast them and they support the
  book differently. Ornament alone does not create another variant.
- Representative drawings of bindings, ribbons, cushions, silver stands,
  wooden stands, and architectural ambos require dated, provenanced
  material-culture witnesses. The liturgical sources establish ritual
  identity and relationships, not enough morphology for publication artwork.

## Historical versus 1962 disposition

All findings in the first table belong to the 1962 main body, including the
special Holy Week compositions then printed in the Missal. The 1948
ceremonial is a near-horizon witness for pontifical practice, not evidence that
an article was already obsolete before 1962.

This pass did not establish the introduction, cessation, or chronological
development of any book or support. In particular, the ceremonial’s reference
to stone ambos surviving according to ancient custom is not enough to date an
object, prove uninterrupted Roman use, or place it in the historical section.
No candidate should be classified as “previous version” from this pass.

## Remaining source work

1. Register and bind the exact *Caeremoniale Episcoporum* artifact, then audit
   the decrees and revisions that establish its operative status in 1962.
2. Inspect the complete 1962-horizon *Pontificale Romanum* and *Rituale
   Romanum* for named books and special supports rather than inferring their
   inventory from the ceremonial.
3. Resolve official chant-book identities and uses separately: Gradual,
   Kyrial, Antiphonal, Processional, and any ceremony-specific books.
4. Audit whether *Epistolarium*, *Evangeliarium*, *liber Epistolarum*, and
   *liber Evangeliorum* are stable one-to-one headwords or contextual aliases.
5. Acquire dated, provenanced material examples for morphology, dimensions,
   bindings, covers, markers, cushions, altar stands, portable lecterns,
   pulpits, and architectural ambos.
6. Perform a separate chronological source pass before placing any book or
   support in the historical section.
