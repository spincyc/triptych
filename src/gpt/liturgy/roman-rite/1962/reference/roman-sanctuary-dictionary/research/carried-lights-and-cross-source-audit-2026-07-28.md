# Carried lights and processional cross source audit

Audit date: 2026-07-28

## Exact controls

- The 1962 *Missale Romanum*, *Ritus servandus* II.5 and VI.5, names two
  candle-bearing acolytes in the solemn entrance and Gospel formation. It
  controls their role, not candlestick morphology.
- Adrian Fortescue, *The Ceremonies of the Roman Rite Described* (1917),
  printed p. 17, distinguishes the acolytes' tall, footed candlesticks that can
  stand on the credence from footless elevation torches kept in a rack. The
  same page identifies the processional cross as a crucifix on a long staff
  that cannot stand alone.
- Fortescue, printed pp. 78–79, says a carried cross is not the Roman custom
  for a priest's High Mass and is not usually named by the Roman ceremonial
  books, while also recording its permitted customary use in many churches.
  When used, it is carried between the acolytes and placed in an appointed
  sanctuary stand.
- Fortescue, printed pp. 89–90, has torchbearers enter without torches, take
  lighted torches in the sacristy, and return at the Sanctus for the Elevation
  service. Acolytes acting as torchbearers use torches, not their
  candlesticks.

## Canonical consequences

1. `obj-acolyte-candlestick` is separate from `obj-altar-candlestick`.
   Reusing the empty footed-candlestick art is allowed only with a
   representative-morphology caveat.
2. `obj-processional-cross` is source-audited as an institutional or local
   customary object for a priest's High or Solemn Mass, never as universal or
   Missal-required. A stable stand is part of its safe placement relation.
3. `obj-elevation-torch` replaces the misleading processional label. Its
   entrance implication is removed.
4. The current torch image remains held: its generated foot makes the depicted
   object self-standing and contradicts the checked defining distinction.

## Deliberate limits

The audit does not prescribe material, overall dimensions, ornament,
candle composition, one universal carrying grip, or modern fire-handling
procedure. The fixed sacristy cross or image used for the ministers'
reverence remains a different object from the portable processional staff
crucifix.
