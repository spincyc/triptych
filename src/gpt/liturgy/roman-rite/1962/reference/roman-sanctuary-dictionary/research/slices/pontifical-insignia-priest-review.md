## Purpose and evidence boundary

Please review the two graphite studies for recognizable ceremonial identity,
plausible morphology, and suitability at the Roman 1962 horizon. The images
are AI-generated editorial candidates. They were not generated from
photographs, museum objects, or a verified vestment pattern.

The accompanying research pass directly checked the 1948 printing of the
Leo XIII recension of the *Cæremoniale Episcoporum*:

- I.XI.1, 5, 6, and 9 for the ministers of the staff, mitre, candle, and
  gremial and their handling;
- I.XVII.1--6 for the three forms of mitre and the bishop's use of the
  pastoral staff;
- I.XX.1 for the *bugia* and its lit candle beside the book.

Those loci support ceremonial identity and use, not every shape, dimension,
fabric, ornament, fastening, or fold in the drawings. A separate source and
review must still establish that no material change between the checked
recension and the declared 1962 horizon affects these objects.

### Questions for the reviewer

- **Crosier:** Is the overall staff immediately recognizable? Is the ornate
  figural crook too particular or misleading for the principal dictionary
  specimen? Should the atlas instead lead with a plain volute and reserve
  figural crooks for a documented variant plate?
- **Mitres:** Do the three silhouettes and paired lappets read correctly?
  Does ornament density adequately distinguish *pretiosa*,
  *auriphrygiata*, and *simplex* in monochrome? Does any shown ornament imply
  an incorrect class? Is the simple mitre's treatment of the lappet fringe
  adequate?
- **Gremial:** Does the rectangular lap-cloth form accord with the form you
  know from the older pontifical ceremonies? The checked ceremonial locus
  supports use on the bishop's lap and folded handling, but it does **not**
  establish the pictured side ties. Should the ties be deleted pending a
  pattern or artifact source?
- **Bugia:** Is the short candle, socket, broad pan, and side handle a
  recognizable older Roman hand-candle? Does the vessel resemble a domestic
  chamberstick too strongly? What construction should distinguish the
  pontifical object?
- **Vimpae:** Do the paired isolated specimens look like ministers' shoulder
  veils, or are they liable to be confused with stoles? Should this isolated
  view be omitted unless a documented laid-flat form can be sourced?
- Is any object or variant shown here anachronistic, merely local, or
  insufficiently representative for a universal-Roman main plate at the 1962
  horizon?

## Study B: mitre minister context

Asset:
`shared/artwork/pontifical-insignia/DIC-ART-PI-002-vimpa-in-use.png`

The intended state is one clerical minister in cassock and cotta/surplice,
wearing the oblong silk cloth described in *Cæremoniale Episcoporum* I.XI.6
and supporting the mitre without bare-hand contact.

### Questions for the reviewer

- Does the cloth pass and drape correctly from the neck over the minister's
  hands, or has the generated image made it too broad or humeral-veil-like?
- Does the minister hold the mitre correctly, including the direction and
  disposition of the lappets?
- Is the dress correct for this depicted branch? The source permits a
  cope-wearing branch in which the separate cloth is not used; this study
  intentionally shows the non-cope branch.
- Is *vimpa* / plural *vimpae* the appropriate English dictionary headword
  for this article at the 1962 horizon? The checked governing passage itself
  says *velum, seu mappa sericea oblonga*, not *vimpa*.
- Would a rear or three-quarter inset better demonstrate the continuous
  neck-to-hands construction?

## Requested disposition

For each study, please record one of:

- **accept identity only** — useful as a research image, but morphology needs
  redrawing from evidence;
- **revise** — list the exact object, feature, and correction;
- **reject** — state the ceremonial or morphological failure;
- **approve for print trial** — only if every visible construction is
  acceptable for a provisional plate.

Please also distinguish:

- a universal Roman requirement;
- a legitimate but optional or local form;
- an artistic example that must be labelled as a variant;
- a form not supportable at the 1962 horizon.
