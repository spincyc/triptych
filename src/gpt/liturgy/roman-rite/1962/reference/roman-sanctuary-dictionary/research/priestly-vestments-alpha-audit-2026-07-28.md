# Ordinary priestly vestments alpha audit

Audit date: 2026-07-28

## Bounded result

This tranche admits six ordinary priestly vestment records: amice, alb,
cincture, maniple, priest's stole, and chasuble. It does not admit or revise
the cope or biretta; diaconal or subdiaconal vesture; folded chasubles; the
broad stole; or a pontifical vesting sequence.

The 1962 *Missale Romanum* is the governing control. *Ritus servandus* I.2--3,
printed p. LII / registered CMAA artifact PDF p. 56, requires vestments that
are intact, clean, beautiful, and blessed where blessing applies, and gives
the ordinary priest's sequence:

1. amice;
2. alb;
3. cincture;
4. maniple on the left arm;
5. stole around the neck, crossed over the breast, with both ends secured by
   the cincture; and
6. planeta or chasuble.

Only I.3 expressly describes a minister presenting the cincture from behind
while raising and arranging the alb. It does not establish a general server
handling assignment for all six vestments. I.4 separately controls the
bishop's or blessed abbot's hanging stole and materially different maniple
timing; ordinary-priest arrangement is therefore not silently transferred to
the pontifical edition.

The *Rubricae generales* supply two further limits. Number 117, printed
p. XIX / PDF p. 17, makes color follow the Mass or Office while preserving
legitimate indults and customs. Numbers 133--137, printed p. XX / PDF p. 18,
require the priest's planeta or casula at Mass, distinguish a cope branch in
which no maniple is worn, and state that folded chasubles and the broad stole
are no longer used.

## Practical and visual controls

Fortescue--O'Connell, seventh edition (1943), printed p. 10 / artifact PDF
p. 31, supplies checked contemporary recognition descriptions: rectangular
linen amice with long ties, long linen alb reaching the feet, waist cincture,
short maniple on the left arm, longer stole crossed and cincture-secured, and
the chasuble as the final garment with a head opening. Printed pp. 38--39 /
PDF pp. 59--60 supply only a practical/custom preparation and assistance
model: a sacristan or, by custom, a server may prepare the reverse vesting
stack; the server checks readiness; and assistance may include handing the
vestments or arranging the alb and stole. These statements do not create a
universal sacristan/server allocation, folding system, storage method,
fastening method, or handoff.

Fortescue's 1917 first edition, printed pp. 9--10 / Wikisource scan leaves
43--44, is a public-domain period line-art and gross-form witness. Its later
Low Mass sequence is not used wherever it differs from the governing 1962
Missal. Casanowicz, United States National Museum Bulletin 148 (1929), p. 30,
is a public-domain federal material-culture witness. It does not universalize
dimensions, tassels, crosses, fringe, material, or symbolism. Metropolitan
Museum object 43.22.4a--d (French, nineteenth century, public domain) is a
matched chasuble/maniple/stole/burse exemplar. Object 2009.300.2952a--c
(Spanish, public domain) is an altered set. Both are morphology witnesses
only, never ritual norms.

## Routing

- Comprehensive: all six are required in ordinary vesting order.
- Altar server: all six are required for recognition; assistance remains
  conditional to the exact Missal branch, the checked manual model, custom,
  and direction.
- Sacristan: all six are required with reverse-stack, condition, readiness,
  and local-allocation controls; no fold or storage procedure is invented.
- MC/trainer: all six are required for sequence, layering, giver/receiver
  branches, and distinctions among priest, bishop, deacon, and subdeacon.
- General reader: all six are required as short identity and layer records,
  without handling instruction.
- Pontifical: all six are required as a relational ordinary-priest baseline,
  but their priestly arrangement is never presented as episcopal.

All six figures passed identity, factual-ceiling, text-free, crop,
monochrome, full-object, and rendered-size review. The chasuble comparison is
explicitly not to common scale. No plate supplies evidence for a morphology,
and no artwork makes one decorative form normative.
