## Evidence boundary

The Marietti 1948 *Cæremoniale Episcoporum* controls the ceremonial
relationship: *caligae* and *sandalia* are distinct objects, they are used in
combination, and the right leg and foot are vested before the left. It does
not control their morphology.

The isolated buskin is bounded to the visually inspected 1948 R. and K. Wood
illustration. That witness supports a stocking-like garment with foot and
lower-leg portions. Its ties, seams, pattern, height, textile, and exact cut
are not generalized.

The isolated sandal is bounded to Sailko's visually inspected photograph of a
circa-1900–1950 pair from Pisa Cathedral. That witness supports a low
closed-shoe form. Its red taffeta, metallic-looking trim, bow, proportions,
and hidden construction are not generalized. The source photograph is CC BY
3.0; its attribution and license remain in the central artifact record.

The combined figure teaches only the source-controlled two-object layering
and paired right/left formation. A textual callout, not the image itself,
must teach the right-then-left vesting sequence.

## Risks and required qualifications

The corrected generated buskin has a very plain silhouette because the
available witness does not control construction details. It must therefore
appear with its isolated headword and in proximity to the separate sandal or
the combined layered formation; it must not be used as generic footwear clip
art or embellished from the rejected witness details.

The sandal retains a bow and edge trim because the admitted Pisa exemplar
contains them. Those details cannot be described as required or usual on the
strength of this witness. A future broader morphology corpus may justify a
more neutral replacement, but the current bounded drawing is preferable to an
invented universal form.

An earlier combined candidate was rejected because it copied a long side tie,
bands, and panel seams from the 1948 witness onto only one side. The corrected
candidate uses the same neutral, tie-free and seam-free buskin on both sides
and matching low closed shoes. Its upper legs are intentionally cropped; no
claim that they are complete or uncropped is permitted.

The corrected combined view does not itself encode which pictured side is vested first.
TeX owns the explicit `right first, then left` callout. This prevents an
unlabeled left/right inference from an image whose viewing orientation can
change in layout.
