# Pontifical-ceremony object candidate audit

Status: **lead inventory only; no entry below is source-audited or renderable**
Audit date: 2026-07-27

The companion `pontifical-priest-review.md` turns this discovery queue into
object-by-object transfer questions for competent external review.

## Purpose and boundary

This file tests whether the pontifical candidate queue extends beyond personal
vesture and insignia to the furnishings, books, service articles, textiles,
supports, and object relationships needed for a complete visual account. It
does not establish a Latin headword, prescribed presence, handler, rank
distinction, or 1962 use. Those claims remain held until the exact
*Caeremoniale Episcoporum*, *Pontificale Romanum*, Missal, amending acts, and
checked contemporary ceremonial loci are bound and read.

Candidates are grouped by the visual question a reader must answer. An item
that proves to be ordinary Mass equipment used in a pontifical composition
will keep its general canonical object ID and receive pontifical presence and
relationship claims; it will not be duplicated as a “pontifical” object.

## Witness inspected in this pass

An acquired scan of *Caeremoniale Episcoporum Clementis VIII, Innocentii X et
Benedicti XIII iussu editum, Benedicti XIV et Leonis XIII auctoritate
recognitum*, third edition after the typical (Turin/Rome: Marietti, 1948),
was hashed
`af65e16a8a64413db3d5694164d4e612fa4b8e02074939307455c26736729505`
and inspected at book I, chapters XI--XII and XXI. The scan was retrieved on
2026-07-27 from
`https://librinostri.catholica.cz/download/CaeremoniEpisc1948-r0-t.pdf`;
its bytes remain run-temporary and are not proposed for tracking.

The inspected page images, with OCR used only to locate them, directly support
these discovery conclusions:

- I.XI identifies ministers for the book, candle, pastoral staff, mitre,
  thurible and boat, candlesticks, gremial, cruets, and credence; it gives the
  mitre minister an oblong silk veil and traces the gremial and cruets back to
  the credence.
- I.XII.12 directs seven altar candlesticks when a bishop celebrates; the
  same chapter describes the altar relationship that results.
- I.XII.15 and 19 identify the Missal, Gospel and Epistle books with cushion
  or small stand and enumerate a prepared credence including mitres and their
  small under-cap/support, the mitre minister's veil, basin, ewer, four hand
  towels, incense equipment, gremial, stockings, sandals, and a Psalm book.
- I.XXI.1 describes the small silver hand-candle instrument commonly called
  a *bugia*, borne beside the book even in sufficient daylight.

This inspection establishes that these are real candidates in the named 1948
witness; it does **not** yet establish an unamended 1962 norm. The operative
1962 corpus, intervening decrees, terminology, English headwords, material
forms, and every branch remain open.

The pontifical plates must identify object relationships, not merely display
equipment. The following are role leads, not verified universal offices:

| Book-bearer | book and cushion/support | exact book, support, side, height, and transfer route |
| Hand-candle bearer | bugia/scotula | relationship to book-bearer and omission conditions |
| Gremial bearer | gremial and support | preparation, placement, removal, fold, and disposition |
| Ewer/basin/towel ministers | hand-washing set | separate bearers, sequence, and return place |
| Train-bearer | vestment train where applicable | rank, vestment, place, and whether present at the 1962 horizon |

Every admitted composition must account for the object's preparation, first
holder, every giver and receiver, waiting station, and final disposition. A
minister title alone is not evidence that the role or object occurs in every
pontifical Mass.

## Candidate substantive variants

These questions should enter the variant register only after direct evidence:

- throne versus faldstool composition, including jurisdictional and
  visiting-bishop conditions;
- fixed, movable, and architecturally integrated throne forms;
- mitre support as tray, cushion, stand, or combined article;
- bugia/scotula terminology and materially different holder forms;
- vimpa construction and whether insignia-specific distinctions exist;
- washable and ornamented gremials where use or handling differs;
- ordinary and pontifical ewer/basin sets only if morphology or handling,
  rather than decoration, differs materially;
- seventh-candle presence and omission branches;
- ordinary, archiepiscopal, and other privileged processional-cross forms;
- processional canopy forms only where construction changes identification or
  handling;
- book support as hand-held cushion, rigid support, altar stand, or floor
  lectern.

## Plate and artwork consequences

No asset is commissioned by this audit. Source closure must precede artwork
because an attractive composition could otherwise freeze an invented rank,
object, or transfer into the visual system. Once the corresponding records
reach `source-audited`, the minimum useful pontifical set is:

1. throne and faldstool composition comparison;
2. books, supports, hand candle, and bearing relationship;
3. vimpa, gremial, humeral veil, and confusable textile comparison;
4. ewer, basin, towel, cruets, and preparation table;
5. insignia supports and disposition;
6. pontifical altar and entrance composition, including conditional objects;
7. one full relationship plate tracing preparations, bearers, transfers, and
   return stations.

## Closure checklist

- Bind the exact operative editions and later amendments before assigning
  status.
- Search the official corpus once by object term and again by minister,
  station, and action so unnamed or relational articles are not missed.
- Reconcile every Latin lead against the actual edition; do not publish the
  leads above as headwords.
- Separate a materially distinct object from an ordinary object used in a
  special relationship.
- Record rank, jurisdiction, place, Mass type, and adjacent-ceremony limits.
- Audit every candidate against the historical section so obsolete papal or
  court articles are not presented as ordinary 1962 episcopal equipment.
- Commission artwork only after the object and composition records identify
  all depicted parts and branches.
