    \small
    \textbf{Ecce Homo Chapel, Jerusalem}\par
    \vspace{0.08in}
    This graphite study follows a dated photograph by Félix Bonfils. Look
    across the whole scene: an arched bay, an elevated altar reached by
    steps, a divided rail, devotional figures, branching lights, and
    suspended lamps all shape the space together.

    \vspace{0.14in}
    \textbf{A witness, not a model.}\par
    This is an atypical nineteenth-century pilgrimage chapel, not a standard
    plan for a 1962 high altar. It establishes no required number of lights,
    reservation arrangement, architecture, proportions, or ordinary
    sanctuary layout. The lights and suspended lamps also obscure details
    that the photograph does not securely identify.

    \vspace{0.10in}
    \textbf{Read the rubric beside the witness.}\par
    \vspace{0.04in}
    {\scriptsize
      \textbf{Altar crucifix}. A sufficiently large cross bearing the
      crucified Christ stands in the middle.\par
      \vspace{0.05in}
      \textbf{Lights on either side.} Required candles and candlesticks
      vary with the class of Mass.\par
      \vspace{0.05in}
      \textbf{Three altar cards.} The three
      \textit{tabellae secretarum} are placed for Mass.\par}
  \end{minipage}
