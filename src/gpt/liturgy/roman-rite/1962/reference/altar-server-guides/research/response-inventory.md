# Response inventory: 1962 altar-server guides

## Authority, ownership, and notation

This is the owner-level audit of the verbal bank rendered from
`shared/response-reference.tex`. It does not supply a second editable render
source. The Latin and sequence were checked there against the 1962 *Missale
Romanum*; the loci below were also reconciled with the repository Ordinary's
sequence inventory and source matrix. Research is current to 2026-07-22.

The form abbreviations are **L** (Low Mass), **C** (*Missa cantata*), and **S**
(Solemn Mass). “Choir/public” means the voices appointed to sing at that
celebration, not an instruction that every server sing. The ordinary voice
plans are:

- at L, the server normally gives the audible spoken replies;
- at C, server(s) give the quiet altar dialogue and the appointed choir or
  congregation gives the outward sung replies; and
- at S, the sacred ministers give the quiet ministerial replies and the
  appointed choir or congregation gives the outward sung replies.

The entries distinguish six layers. **T** is the facsimile-checked received
answer. **N** is displayed canonical Latin: printed accent marks are not
reproduced and typographic ligatures are expanded to `ae` or `oe`; no word is
changed. **L** adds syllable breaks and stress, **R** is an English-like sound
line, **I** is editorial broad IPA, and **M** is a project-written meaning.
Only T/N is liturgical text. L/R/I/M are teaching aids and are never said at
Mass. Full L/R/I fields and their rule audit are in
`pronunciation-audit.md`.

## Render and learning reconciliation

All three full guides import the same owner source. It renders the form-specific
sequence maps, compact pronunciation key, A1–A22 six-lesson
course, covered and shuffled drills, 24 response cards, and the 24-card
Latin-to-pronunciation-and-meaning deck P01–P07, P08A–P08C, and P09–P22.
Each cards-only companion imports the exact matching C and P faces from that
source without duplicating the teaching data. The full guide's card module
states the selected form's actor and mode rule once before the decks; the
companion relies on that paired key, and individual faces do not repeat a Voice
field. Conditions that change
whether an answer is made remain on the affected face. The shared voice data
still controls the teaching bank and Mass-order map, including MC/A1/A2 at C
and the subdeacon at S for the Last Gospel.

Every fixed C-card cue is printed as a complete formula or verse rather than
as an ending fragment. C02 supplies complete fixed Amen cues; C03 supplies the
complete dismissals and John 1:14; C05--C07 supply the complete Psalm verses;
C10 supplies the celebrant's complete Confiteor; C11--C13 use the complete
preceding exchange or study chunk to divide A8 without inventing exchanges;
C15 prints the full ninefold practice pattern; and C16A spells out complete
Gospel-title announcements without an unspoken N placeholder. Because an
appointed Epistle or Gospel is variable, C03 and C16B direct the trainer to
read its complete final sentence or verse from that day's Missal. Former
composite cards C14, C16, and C18 are retired; C14A--C14C, C16A--C16B, and
C18A--C18B each train one response point. C20 uses A22 directly.

Each face puts its stable ID alone at upper left and Cue or Answer at upper
right. Card backs remain prompts rather than independent pronunciation
records: abbreviated sound lines point back to the A-bank. P08A–P08C divide
A8 into three print-sized study parts; they are card IDs, not new response
IDs. The two sung guides and their companions append separate action-card sources; those do not
alter this inventory.

The ladder order is: A9/A13/A15; A16/A17/A19–A21; A1/A6/A10–A12; A2–A5;
A7/A8/A18; then A14 and ordinary/Easter/procession dismissal variants. Mastery requires correct answers
to shuffled unseen cues, not page completion. On a missed cue, the learner
asks for it again; after a second miss the trainer supplies the bank ID and
returns that cue after three unrelated cards. Pronunciation mastery separately
requires a competent human listener.

## Exclusions and negative controls

- The celebrant's opening *In nomine Patris … Amen* contains its own *Amen*;
  the server does not add A9.
- The 1962 Communion rite does not appoint a second *Confiteor* before the
  faithful's Communion. A7–A8 are not repeated there.
- The threefold *Domine, non sum dignus* at Communion belongs to the
  celebrant in the printed Order, and the communicant adds no answer to the
  priest's Communion formula; neither is a server-bank entry.
- The complete Gloria, Credo, Sanctus–Benedictus, Agnus Dei, and proper chants
  are not short cue-to-answer entries. A server sings them only through a
  separately assigned choir or congregational role; no choir text is added as
  a Low-Mass server response.
- No response is added after a chanted Epistle or Gospel. A17 is confined to a
  Gospel read in the Low-Mass manner.
- The priest's exceptional self-answer *de manibus meis* is not a learner
  variant of A18.
- If *Benedicamus Domino* announces a following liturgical procession, A15
  answers it, but there is no blessing and no Last Gospel.
- If the Last Gospel is omitted under General Rubrics 509–510—including at
  the third Mass of Christmas—its A13, A16, and A15 responses are omitted
  with it.
- The Leonine prayers occur after Low Mass and are outside the Order and all
  three form families. The other adjacent rites and special functions
  excluded in `scope.md` do not silently add responses to this bank.
- Local division of sung parts may differ. No local custom is encoded as a
  received alternative answer; the learner follows the actual celebrant and
  master of ceremonies rather than answering merely because a row is printed.
