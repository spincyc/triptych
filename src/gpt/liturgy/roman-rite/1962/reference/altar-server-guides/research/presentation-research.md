# Presentation research: Low Mass server package

Research cutoff: 2026-07-25 (America/Chicago).

## 2026-07-26 action-first reconciliation

The child publication now presents each stage as a field guide: identify the
actor, perform the action, then check the manner of execution. Short movement
rehearsals and a local-sanctuary mapping sheet make model assignments and
local choices explicit. The trainer remains page-aligned with the child
publication but adds meanings, correction points, and adult preparation
notes instead of duplicating the child's prose word for word.

This is an evidence-informed editorial application of the hierarchy,
connected-media, and rehearsal principles recorded below. It has passed
internal source and screen review only; it has not received new intended-reader
testing, independent pedagogical review, or physical-use testing.

## 2026-07-25 density correction

The forced one-lesson-per-sheet rule produced several pages whose content
occupied only about one third of the printable height. The child and trainer
now use one physical page for two adjacent short sections when both complete
units fit at the established type sizes and the trainer rail remains aligned:
Confiteor parts 2 and 3, normal places and Kyrie, and Gospel response and
post-Gospel branches. Trial pairings that created an orphaned continuation
were rejected after raster review. The final pair is 30 rather than 33 pages,
with no type reduction and with identical child/trainer physical pagination.

## Status and scope

This record governs the presentation redesign of the child booklet, its
page-matched trainer manual, and the Low Mass flash cards. It addresses how
the material is found, read, rehearsed, and checked. It does not verify the
Latin, pronunciation, translation, ceremonial route, sanctuary model, or
current authorization to use the 1962 books; those claims retain their
existing owners and review requirements.

The approved contract is implemented in the TeX sources and supporting source
and card records. The exact child booklet, trainer manual, and flash-card PDFs
recorded in `production-manifest.md` remain installed as an evaluation trio.
The installed child booklet and trainer manual include the illustration,
choreography, and density corrections. The shared model-sanctuary drawing
system and selected two-acolyte sequence now correctly identify the
selected two-acolyte sequence for the Gospel-side Missal move, Communion
plate, ablutions, return of the Missal, and the priest's prepared chalice. It
does not change the Low-Mass flash cards. Its production and every-page screen
review status is recorded in `production-manifest.md`; it remains uninstalled
and has not received renewed actual-size child-and-parent or photocopy
testing. The maintainer expressly authorized the preceding installation after
the pending actual-size, physical-duplex, photocopy, paired-use, independent,
rights, and ecclesiastical-review gates were disclosed. The override does not
assert that those gates are complete and does not grant exact-snapshot release
clearance.

## Evidence classes

The following classes must remain distinct:

1. **Local testing evidence** is the feedback reported from this package's
   readers. It is directly relevant to this design, but it is not a
   generalizable study.
2. **External evidence** is the published accessibility and learning research
   cited below. It supports general principles, not this package's exact page
   architecture or liturgical accuracy.
3. **Evidence-informed synthesis** applies those two bodies of evidence to the
   package. It remains editorial reasoning.
4. **Approved editorial decisions** are the maintainer's selected product rules.
   They are authoritative for implementation even when no cited source
   prescribes the exact choice.

## Local testing evidence

The reported test readers included children ages eight to ten and mothers
reading as parents or trainer proxies. Feedback was negative across both the
core documents and the cards:

- pages and cards felt cluttered;
- typography and visual hierarchy were inconsistent; and
- tables obscured the order in which the Mass unfolds.

The feedback justified replacing the former presentation rather than merely
polishing individual boxes. It does not, by itself, establish the best
replacement. No sample size, recruitment method, task script, observation
notes, completion times, or verbatim transcripts were supplied for this
record. Future testing must preserve those details so later claims can be
checked.

The finding about tables is local evidence about these documents, not evidence
that tables are inherently inaccessible. Tables remain suitable for genuinely
two-dimensional comparisons or audit data; they must not carry the child's
primary chronological route.

## External evidence

### Connected media, not decorative pictures

CAST Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0, Consideration 2.5,
[Illustrate through multiple
media](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/multiple-media/),
explains that text alone can create barriers when teaching concepts and
processes. It recommends representations such as illustrations, diagrams,
models, movement, and storyboards when they are explicitly connected to the
words they explain. CAST's
[Support multiple ways to perceive
information](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/perception/ways-perceive-information/)
also cautions that visual or auditory information is not universally
accessible and calls for descriptions or equivalents.

For this package, that supports pairing a written step with a nearby,
front-facing sanctuary diagram and a trainer's live demonstration. It does
not establish the ceremonial correctness of a diagram, and it does not support
decorative imagery. A diagram must name the same stage, actors, level, objects,
and action as the adjacent text; essential information must remain available
in words.

CAST Consideration 5.1,
[Use multiple media for
communication](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/multiple-media/),
concerns how learners express what they know. It supports rehearsal through
speech, pointing, movement, or demonstration when the medium itself is not the
learning goal. Applying that principle to oral cue-and-response practice and
walk-through rehearsal is an editorial application, not a CAST prescription
for altar-server training.

### Clear, stable structure

W3C's supplemental cognitive-accessibility pattern
[Use a Clear and Understandable Page
Structure](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o2p03-page-structure/)
recommends logical sections, visible grouping, whitespace, meaningful
headings, and consistently positioned elements. It explains that predictable
placement can support spatial memory and warns against dense text with little
whitespace. W3C's
[Writing for Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/) and
[Headings tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/)
likewise recommend short descriptive headings and a meaningful hierarchy.

Those sources concern web content, and the cognitive pattern is supplemental
rather than a WCAG conformance requirement. This record applies their
structure principles by analogy to print and PDF. They support a stable visual
grammar and meaningful headings; they do not prescribe the labels, lanes,
Mass stages, or left-side trainer rail selected below.

### Typeface and clear-print floor

The Braille Institute created the Atkinson Hyperlegible family for low-vision
readers and now recommends
[Atkinson Hyperlegible Next](https://www.brailleinstitute.org/freefont/) as
its current everyday face. Its design emphasizes differentiated letterforms,
open counters, and distinctions among commonly confused characters. This is
the typeface designer's authoritative account, not independent proof that the
font improves comprehension, recall, reading speed, or performance for
children ages eight to ten.

An earlier implementation selected Atkinson Hyperlegible Next 2.001 after
visual comparison of the capital `I`, `J`, and `L`. Its local pdfTeX route
embedded Type 1 regular, bold, regular italic, and bold italic; Atkinson
Hyperlegible Mono 2.001 regular was confined to compact file-name or URL text
in terminal apparatus. The Google Fonts
[license record](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/atkinsonhyperlegiblenext/OFL.txt)
identifies SIL Open Font License 1.1. The installed 2.001 font's own name-table
notice separately permits free commercial and noncommercial use without
derivatives or alteration, and its OS/2 metadata permits editable embedding.
The discrepancy is recorded without treating either wording as if it were the
other. The PDFs embed subsetted font programs; that technical observation
does not reconcile the license evidence or characterize subsetting as a
permitted alteration. The TeX support files retain the LaTeX Project Public
License. This evaluation is retained as superseded design history.

The current implementation uses Libertinus, whose traditional book forms give
the guides a more reverent character while retaining clear differentiation,
open counters, full Latin coverage, and regular, bold, and italic hierarchy.
The local pdfTeX route embeds the Type 1 Libertinus faces actually used.
Installed Libertinus font metadata identifies SIL Open Font License 1.1; the
`libertinus` TeX support files identify the LaTeX Project Public License.

The selected family must still be tested in the actual document, at actual
size, on the intended printer and paper. Production must confirm every Latin
character and diacritic used and prove from the final PDF that no fallback
font has entered reading text. A future version, original-family substitution,
or added Mono use requires explicit visual and rights review.

The UK Association for Accessible Formats' specialist guidance,
[G003: Creating clear print and large print
documents](https://www.ukaaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/G003-UKAAF-Creating-clear-print-and-large-print-documents-v4.pdf),
sets 12 point as its minimum for clear print and recommends 14 point. It notes
that nominal point size is not directly comparable across typefaces because
x-height and other characteristics differ. It also advises keeping captions,
notes, page numbers, and similar text at least as large as the body text.

Official UK guidance on
[Accessible communication
formats](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-communication/accessible-communication-formats)
also recommends concise standard documents with a 12-point minimum and says
to reduce the amount of text before reducing point size when space is tight.

Accordingly, continuous child-facing and trainer main-lane instructional prose
uses 12 point as its floor, with 14 point the preferred prototype target.
Compact interface and actor labels, trainer-only rail text, diagram labels
whose full-size adjacent words carry the same instruction, title or terminal
apparatus, and a separately audited overview may use fixed smaller sizes.
None may auto-fit or shrink, and no essential child instruction may exist only
below 12 point. Every exception still requires actual-size print testing.
Passing the numeric floor is not proof of readability.

The approved 11.5-point flash-card body is a deliberate exception below the
12-point clear-print benchmark. No source reviewed here validates that
exception. It must never shrink further, and it requires successful
actual-size testing. The project must not claim that 11.5-point Libertinus is
equivalent to 12 point in another face without measuring the rendered type and
testing it with readers.

### Guided retrieval for primary-school learners

Karpicke, Blunt, Smith, and Karpicke's 2014 classroom study,
[Retrieval-based learning: The need for guided retrieval in elementary school
children](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Karpicke_etal_JARMAC.pdf)
([DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2014.07.008)), reports three
experiments with elementary-school children using curriculum texts. In one
experiment, 94 children ages nine to eleven found unsupported free recall and
concept mapping difficult. Specific questions and partially completed maps
made retrieval more successful, and guided question mapping outperformed
rereading on a later free-recall measure.

Karpicke, Blunt, and Smith's 2016 study,
[Retrieval-Based Learning: Positive Effects of Retrieval Practice in
Elementary School
Children](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4786565/)
([DOI record](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00350)), found benefits from
cued retrieval over restudy with 88 fourth graders, whose mean age was ten.
That experiment used simple word lists and category-plus-letter cues, so it is
corroboration for guided retrieval, not a validation of these Latin cards.

Together, the studies support a short, specific cue followed by an attempted
response and prompt checking or trainer feedback. They do not establish an
eight-up layout, a twenty-two-card deck, a particular cue selection, or
liturgical competence. The cards are retrieval prompts, not a self-teaching
course or mastery record; a trainer must model pronunciation, correct errors,
and assess performance live.

## Evidence-informed design synthesis

The redesign uses one stable route rather than several competing
representations of sequence. Each page answers the child's immediate
questions in the same order: where are we, what do I hear, what do I say, and
what do I do? The visual hierarchy repeats rather than changing by lesson.

Pictures have an instructional job. A sanctuary diagram appears only when
place, orientation, level, posture, or object movement is clearer spatially
than in prose. Its words and nearby step must agree, and no instruction may
depend on color or on the picture alone.

Retrieval practice is separated from first instruction. The booklet first
places the exchange in Mass order and supplies pronunciation and meaning at
the first occurrence. The flash card then gives one uncluttered cue and asks
for the response. A trainer supplies modeling, correction, and the judgment
of readiness.

The redesign also separates audiences without creating two divergent lessons.
After the edition-specific covers, the child sees the same main-lane words and
page order in both books. The trainer gains only a terse, consistently placed
rail, so the adult can coach without making the child's route denser.

## Approved implementation contract

The rules in this section are approved editorial decisions unless explicitly
identified as an evidence-informed rule. Exact stage names and interface
labels are literal strings.

### Shared print and typography

- Use US Letter portrait pages and black ink on white paper. Meaning, role,
  posture, sequence, and warnings must remain clear in monochrome photocopies;
  color may not carry information.
- Use the approved Libertinus family consistently, subject to the glyph,
  embedding, and fallback checks above. Use regular and bold deliberately; do
  not introduce competing display faces.
- In the child booklet and trainer manual, continuous child-facing and trainer
  main-lane instructional prose is at least 12 point, with 14 point preferred
  where the actual-size prototype permits. Compact functional labels,
  trainer-only rail text, diagram labels repeated by adjacent full-size words,
  title or terminal apparatus, and a separately audited overview may use
  fixed smaller sizes. This size rule and its exceptions are
  evidence-informed editorial applications. Do not auto-fit or shrink a
  crowded block, and do not place an essential child instruction only in the
  smaller text. Cut words, move adult detail to the trainer rail, or add space.
- Use the same heading, label, type weight, spacing, and block order for the
  same function throughout. A new typographic treatment must signal a real
  change in function.
- Do not use a table or dense grid as the primary Mass-order route. Use a
  single vertical reading sequence with explicit stage transitions.
- Keep essential labels and explanations in text. Diagrams reinforce the
  route; they do not replace it.

### Child booklet

- Present one continuous Mass-order sequence. Its six stages, in order, are:
  `Foot/Altar`, `Readings`, `Offertory`, `Canon`, `Communion`, and `Ending`.
  Each stage has a prominent, consistent heading, and no alternate lesson
  order interrupts the route.
- Present spoken exchanges under two stable lanes labeled
  `TRAINER / PRIEST` and `SERVER`. Do not alternate the lane positions.
- Within an exchange, label the cue `PRIEST`, the shared response `BOTH`, the
  pronunciation aid `SAY IT`, and the gloss `MEANING`. `SAY IT` and `MEANING`
  appear only at the first occurrence of a response; later occurrences retain
  the complete cue and response without repeating the aids.
- Put the action beside the exchange or transition that triggers it. Do not
  send the child to a separate action inventory to discover the next step.
- Use actual front-facing model-sanctuary diagrams where a spatial
  relationship materially helps. Every diagram must label the applicable
  level and make the written action recoverable without the picture.
- Use one restrained monochrome illustration grammar across all such diagrams:
  open architectural outlines for the sanctuary and altar; fixed
  high-contrast role labels; stable numbered level markers; visibly different
  standing and kneeling silhouettes; recognizable object drawings; and
  subordinate, uncrossed movement paths. Do not let a route, label, or object
  obscure an actor, level, or destination.
- Use exactly three level names: `First Step`, `Second Step`, and `Predella`.
  Use one consistent front-facing orientation throughout.
- Distinguish standing and kneeling unmistakably in both label and drawing.
  Do not rely on a subtle change in body angle.
- Do not depict a genuflection. When the verified route requires one, state
  the instruction in words and let the trainer demonstrate it live.
- At the opening position, show the first server on the priest's right, which
  is picture-right in this model. Do not reverse this relationship in later
  diagrams without an explicit, sequence-grounded move.
- Show the Missal beginning on the Epistle side and moving only when the
  sequence calls for the move. Its position in every later diagram must agree
  with the preceding step.

### Trainer manual

- After the edition-specific page-1 cover, mirror child pages 2--33
  page-for-page and word-for-word in the main lane. Each such page has the
  same page number, stage, dialogue, action, diagram, and order.
- Keep the child content unchanged in the trainer edition. Adult wording must
  not be inserted into, substituted for, or allowed to reflow the child's
  blocks.
- Reserve a narrow trainer-only rail on the left side of every instructional
  page. Its position and width remain stable. It must not move to the right,
  alternate sides, or interrupt the child's vertical sequence.
- Keep the rail terse. It may contain a correction cue, demonstration note,
  local-route checkpoint, safety reminder, or rehearsal prompt. It may not
  introduce a second chronology, repeat the lesson, or carry material the
  child must read to complete the route.
- If a trainer note does not fit at the approved size, shorten it or move it
  to a separately identified trainer appendix. Do not shrink the rail or the
  mirrored child content.

### Low Mass flash cards

- Use `Low Mass Flash Cards`, not `Low Mass Cue Cards`, as the publication
  filename, PDF title, and catalog label. Individual card faces carry no
  product-type label; their center header remains `LOW MASS`.
- Use US Letter portrait, black ink on white paper, printed at actual size,
  two-sided, and flipped on the long edge.
- Impose eight cards per face in two columns by four rows. A complete deck of
  twenty-two physical cards therefore occupies three duplex sheets, or six
  PDF pages.
- Mirror the two columns on each back so fronts and backs align after
  long-edge duplex printing. Preserve row order: the back of each left-hand
  front occupies the corresponding right-hand back cell, and vice versa.
- Put exactly one cue-to-response pair on each physical card. Use the full
  phrase where possible. For a formula that recurs, use one selected exemplar
  rather than duplicate cards.
- The deck contains exactly twenty-two response-only cards. It contains no
  action cards and no full Confiteor card. The Confiteor is taught and checked
  live rather than compressed into this deck.
- Set card body text at exactly 11.5 point and never shrink it. Resolve
  overflow by editing the selection or contextual wording, not by scaling,
  condensed type, or tighter automatic fitting.
- On every front, use this three-part header:
  left `NUMBER · PRIEST`, center `LOW MASS`, right `STAGE`.
- On every back, use this three-part header:
  left `NUMBER · BOTH`, center `LOW MASS`, right `STAGE`.
  The back-header schema may later substitute `FIRST` or `SECOND` when a
  response is genuinely assigned that way, but all twenty-two cards in this
  deck are `BOTH`.
- Remove actor labels from the body. The header is the sole actor field.
- Set the cue in regular weight and the response in bold. Place the body near
  the top of the usable card area so the reader encounters the exchange
  immediately.
- Print no pronunciation, meaning, action, mastery field, score, or
  self-certification on a card. Those functions belong to the booklet or live
  trainer.
- On the final sheet, populate six cells and leave two corresponding
  front-and-back cells wholly unused. The unused cells have no border, label,
  filler message, crop box, or decorative mark.
- The cards supplement the booklet. They do not carry the Mass sequence,
  replace pronunciation modeling, or certify readiness to serve.

## Required prototype and verification record

Ordinarily before installation, and always before treating these checks as
complete, test the exact full-size outputs rather than a screen thumbnail.
Preserve the participant count, ages or reader roles, task script, printer and
paper, observations, errors, and changes made. For this evaluation set, the
maintainer expressly authorized installation after the pending tests were
disclosed. That one installation-state override neither counts as a test nor
changes the evidence standard below.

The child-booklet test must check whether a new reader can:

- identify the current stage and next step without consulting a table;
- distinguish the two dialogue lanes and locate the response;
- find first-occurrence pronunciation and meaning without expecting them on
  every repetition;
- distinguish standing from kneeling;
- name `First Step`, `Second Step`, and `Predella`;
- keep picture-right orientation, first-server position, and Missal movement
  consistent through the route; and
- explain every diagrammed action from the adjacent words.

The trainer-manual test must compare every page against the child booklet for
wording, order, page identity, and diagram identity. It must also confirm that
the left rail can be scanned without diverting the child's route or forcing
smaller type.

The flash-card test must include:

- an automated count of twenty-two fronts and twenty-two matched backs;
- actual-size inspection of the 11.5-point Libertinus body;
- a check that all headers, stages, regular cues, and bold responses are
  consistent;
- a content check for one cue-to-response pair per card and no Confiteor,
  pronunciation, meaning, action, or mastery material;
- duplex overlay or cut-and-turn inspection of every front/back cell;
- confirmation that the two unused cells on both final faces are unbordered;
  and
- black-and-white photocopy inspection for hierarchy, clipping, and cut
  safety.

Failure to read a card comfortably at actual size is a content-selection
failure, not permission to go below 11.5 point. Failure to keep the child and
trainer pages identical is a source-architecture failure, not a cosmetic
variance.

The current installed evaluation set has completed its automated checks and an
every-page screen review, as recorded in `production-manifest.md`. Actual-size
child reading, physical duplex alignment, black-and-white photocopying, and
renewed child-and-trainer testing remain pending. The installed Low-Mass child
PDF has changed, and the trainer and flash-card identities are newly installed;
none of those three snapshots is release-authorized.

## Liturgical exchange and display hierarchy — 2026-07-27

The seven-publication series retains Libertinus Serif as its readable body
face, with Libertinus Sans, Mono, TIPA, and Latin Modern math symbols confined
to their existing functional jobs. A restrained missal-inflected hierarchy now
uses Libertinus small capitals for major display headings; it does not impose
an ornamental face on instructions, pronunciation, tables, card controls, or
metadata.

Semantic source macros compose the traditional slashed versicle and response
signs because those Unicode signs are outside the T1 text encoding. They appear
only beside genuine spoken or sung cue-and-response material: Low-Mass
dialogue lanes and response cards, and the shared response lessons and
integrated response cards used by Missa Cantata and Solemn Mass. Existing
`Priest`, `Both`, `Cue`, `Answer`, actor, mode, form, condition, and lesson
labels remain in place. Action-card prompts, movement cues, object handoffs,
and other nonverbal triggers do not receive a versicle sign.

The resulting marks and hierarchy were checked in all seven complete review
sets and at full size on representative Low-Mass and sung-form response-card
faces. They remain subordinate to actor and mode information and do not imply
that a lay server says a choir or sacred-minister response.
