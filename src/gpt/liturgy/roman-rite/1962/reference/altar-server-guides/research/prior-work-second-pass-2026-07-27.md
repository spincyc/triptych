## Proportionate archaeology pass

The deleted linen-cloths paragraph is not implausible. A specialist lead was
located in Jodi Magness, “Ancient Jewish Tombs and Burial Customs (to 70
C.E.),” in *The Archaeology of the Holy Land* (Cambridge University Press,
2012), pp. 230--255, DOI `10.1017/CBO9781139013833.012`. Its accessible
publisher abstract supports a narrower proposition: rock-cut tomb evidence in
the relevant late Second Temple setting is associated especially with the
Jerusalem elite. Additional scholarly leads include Amos Kloner and Boaz
Zissu, *The Necropolis of Jerusalem in the Second Temple Period*, and Byron
McCane, *Roll Back the Stone*.

That search does not yet restore the full paragraph. Exact pages must be
inspected and registered for:

1. geographical and socioeconomic variation;
2. primary deposition followed by secondary collection in ossuaries;
3. the functions of aromatic substances in Jewish burial;
4. the limits of comparison with Egyptian mummification.

The first two propositions appear readily supportable from specialist
archaeology. The aromatic-function and mummification sentences require their
own exact controls rather than inference from John 19:39--40. Until those are
available, the publication's narrower textual wording remains the sound
result.

## Doctrinal-authority second pass

The authority commit introduced new works rather than revising an earlier
tracked edition, so there is no recoverable Git deletion set beyond the
publication audit's own disclosures. The relevant source families were
nonetheless checked:

- official authority-schema claims remain controlled by *Donum veritatis*,
  *Mysterium Ecclesiae*, the 1998 doctrinal commentary on the concluding
  formula of the *Professio fidei*, and *Ad tuendam fidem*;
- the retained patristic claim is bounded to the exact inspected Irenaeus
  witness;
- the Mariological companion correctly distinguishes dogmatic definition,
  related doctrine, devotion, and private revelation.

No material theological conclusion in these two studies was found to have been
removed solely because research stopped early. A broader patristic panorama
could be a future enrichment, but it is not needed to repair a present
doctrinal gap and should not be restored without exact editions.

## Disposition

- **Potential future restoration:** the linen study's archaeological context,
  after exact specialist passage registration.
- **Do not restore:** the universal folded-napkin negative, Cyril anti-theft
  attribution, complete-code verification, second Communion `Confiteor`, or
  universalized ceremonial allocations.
- **Evaluate as design, not evidence:** whether the shortened Solemn Mass
  common track gives learners enough rehearsal context. Its underlying
  instructions remain in the source inventory.

No commit was made.
