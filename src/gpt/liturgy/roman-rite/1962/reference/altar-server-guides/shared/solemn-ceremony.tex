\section{Start Here: Trainer and Server}

You do not need to teach every job at once. First find the server's assigned
role, then practise one scene at a time. The complete team chronology later in
the guide is the MC and trainer's reference when a cue or handoff needs more
detail.

\begin{coachbox}{FOR THE TRAINER: Use this guide in four short steps}
In this guide, the trainer is the responsible adult who helps the server
practise. The priest or MC decides the liturgical route, and the adult
sacristan controls flame and hot charcoal.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Ask the priest or MC to mark today's route.} The trainer follows
the completed route and does not choose a rubric, sanctuary route, or flame
assignment.
\item \textbf{Choose one role.}  Write the server's role, partner, base place,
and objects in the box below; then turn to \emph{My Role from Sacristy to
Sacristy}.
\item \textbf{Practise safely.}  Walk the seven scenes with empty hands, then
with cold and empty practice objects.  Use flame, charcoal, vessels, and the
Communion plate only in the guide's supervised rehearsal.
\item \textbf{Join the team.}  When the personal route is steady, use the
numbered chronology for a scene-by-scene team run and finish with the mastery
check.
\end{enumerate}
\end{coachbox}

\begin{learnerbox}{The safe reset}
If you lose your place, stop, face the altar, hold your object safely, and look
at the MC.  Do not hurry across the sanctuary to repair a small mistake.  The
MC will give one quiet signal or let the action continue.

\subsection{Know this model before choosing a role}

This guide teaches one ordinary, non-pontifical Solemn Mass with a celebrant,
deacon, subdeacon, choir, and the following lay-server team:

\begin{center}
\textbf{MC + thurifer + boat bearer + two acolytes + four torchbearers}
\end{center}

A processional cross-bearer is shown as an alternate route. The incense model
requires a separate thurifer and boat bearer; it offers no combined-role
shortcut. If the cross is absent, the table says where its formation work
goes. The priest, deacon, and subdeacon are the sacred ministers. Their actions
appear only as things for a server to watch; this guide never trains a server to
take their place.

\begin{coachbox}{The three labels beside your actions}
\MissalRule{} means that the action or condition is stated in the
1962 Missal.  \ModelRule{} means this guide's original practical route
within the Fortescue--O'Connell ceremonial family, always subordinate to the
1962 Missal where the Missal does not give every traffic detail.
\LocalChoice{} means
that the church, priest, or MC must choose the route or assignment.  A model
direction is a real direction for this rehearsal, but it is not a universal
rubric.
\end{coachbox}

\begin{watchbox}{What is outside this walk-through}
Do not use this route for a Requiem, Holy Week, a ritual or nuptial Mass, a
pontifical function, Mass before the Blessed Sacrament exposed, or the
\emph{Asperges}.  Those ceremonies change actions and need their own rehearsal.
This guide also stops at the sacristy after the recessional; it does not add
prayers or devotions after Mass.
\end{watchbox}

\subsection{Meet the team}

\rolecard{Master of ceremonies (MC) --- core model}{The MC checks the route and
objects, gives small signals, receives the sacred ministers' birettas at the
entrance, serves books, clears the Missal for incensation, coordinates the
Gospel group, places the humeral veil, and starts each procession. He watches
the whole team; he does not make large corrections during Mass.}

\rolecard{Thurifer (Th) --- core model}{The thurifer prepares and carries the
thurible, presents it at the three incense preparations, helps form the Gospel
procession, incenses the deacon, servers, and people after the Offertory
incensations, and incenses the Sacred Host and chalice at the elevations.  A
young thurifer handles live charcoal only after hands-on training and under
the sacristy's safety rule.}

\rolecard{First and second acolytes (A1 and A2) --- core model}{The acolytes
carry the two candles, serve at the credence, flank the Gospel book with their
candles, bring the cruets and lavabo articles, and support the ablutions.  In
this model A1 is also the bell and Communion-plate server; A2 looks after the
chalice veil. At the sedilia A1 serves the celebrant's and deacon's birettas;
A2 serves the subdeacon's. These assignments do not change during Mass.}

\rolecard{Four torchbearers (Tb1--Tb4) --- core model}{The torchbearers attend
Mass from their assigned place until the end of the Preface, then carry four
lighted torches; the Missal requires at least two.  They kneel in a straight
line for the Consecration and leave together at the correct branch.}

\rolecard{Boat bearer (Bb) --- core model}{Bb carries
the incense boat to each preparation. At the first, Gospel, and Offertory
stations Bb gives it to the deacon; at the elevation preparation Bb gives it
to MC. Bb receives it back and returns it to its marked station. Bb does not join the Gospel
procession after that handoff. This guide requires Bb whenever its Solemn-Mass
route is used. The separate role is a declared teaching model, not a claim
that the Missal universally requires a separate boat bearer.}

\rolecard{Cross-bearer (Cb) --- optional local addition}{Cb carries the
processional cross only in the entrance and recessional, unless the parish has
given another explicit duty.  When carrying the cross, Cb remains upright
instead of genuflecting, turns with the whole formation, and never tries to
bow the cross.  If no cross is carried, A1 and A2 walk as a pair.}

\subsection{The movement words your MC will use}

\begin{description}
\item[Hands joined.] Palms together at the breast, fingers pointing upward,
with shoulders relaxed.  When one hand carries an object, place the free hand
flat against the breast unless the rehearsed safe grip needs both hands.

\item[Simple bow.] Incline the head.  Use it only at the words or handoffs the
MC has marked. Free-handed servers bow the head whenever the Holy Name of
Jesus is pronounced; an object-bearer uses the safe small inclination taught
for that object.

\item[Deep bow.] Bend from the waist without dropping the head or stepping.
Make it slowly enough that your partner can match you.

\item[Genuflect.] Stop, face the object of reverence, touch the right knee to
the floor beside the left heel, and rise without bouncing.  Partners arrive,
genuflect, and rise together.  Before Mass, learn whether the Blessed Sacrament
is reserved at the altar and which reverence the MC has assigned there.

\item[Kneel on both knees.] Put both knees down, remain upright, and keep your
hands joined unless you hold a torch, thurible, or bell.  At the Consecration
the Missal directs the ministers to kneel on both knees.

\item[Turn together.] Partners turn toward one another and then toward the new
direction.  Do not spin away from your partner or walk backward on steps.

\item[The middle.] The line directly before the center of the altar.  Come to
the middle before crossing the sanctuary; make the prescribed reverence there
and continue.  Never cut diagonally through another minister's route.

\item[Long way and short way.] The long way passes before the center and
includes its reverence.  The short way stays on the same side.  Which route is
possible depends on the sanctuary, so it is always a \LocalChoice.

\item[Present; do not push.] Hold a book, cruet, boat, or veil steady at the
height practised.  Let the receiver take its weight before you let go.  Look
at the object during a handoff, then return your eyes to the altar.
\end{description}

\begin{watchbox}{When sung words require a genuflection}
If the proper text marks sung words for a genuflection, the MC gives the cue
and every free-handed server faces the altar and genuflects with the celebrant.
At the Solemn Gospel, A1 and A2 remain upright while holding their
candlesticks. Never guess from a familiar word: follow the proper rubric and
the MC. \MissalRule: General Rubrics 519 and 521(d).
\end{watchbox}

\section{Full-Run Mastery Check}

Do one rehearsal with empty cruets, an unlit thurible, and unlit torches.  Then
do one supervised rehearsal with the real weights and safe flames.  The team is
ready only when it can complete all of these checks:

\begin{itemize}
\item \masterybox{Every server can name his base place, the middle, and his
safe reset.}
\item \masterybox{Every object has a prepared place, a named carrier, a
receiver, and a final place.}
\item \masterybox{The Gospel group forms, stations, and returns without a
collision or an extra genuflection by the candle bearers.}
\item \masterybox{A1 and A2 can perform cruets, lavabo, bell, veil, Communion
plate, and ablution duties without being called to two places at once.}
\item \masterybox{Th can perform all three incense preparations and distinguish
blessed incense from the unblessed elevation incense.}
\item \masterybox{Torchbearers can state both departure branches before they
pick up a torch.}
\item \masterybox{The team can explain why no second Communion
\emph{Confiteor} appears.}
\item \masterybox{The team can recognize the normal \emph{Ite, missa est}
ending and the \emph{Benedicamus Domino} replacement route.}
\item \masterybox{On one deliberately missed cue, the server stops, secures the
object, faces the altar, and recovers from the MC's signal.}
\end{itemize}

\begin{learnerbox}{Last word before serving}
Know your own next action and your partner's next cue.  Move together, leave
space for the sacred ministers, and let a small mistake stay small.  Quiet,
steady service is easier for the whole church to follow than hurried service.
\end{learnerbox}
