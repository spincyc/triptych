## Historical comparison sources

- Pius V, *Quod a nobis* (1568) and *Quo primum* (1570): post-Tridentine standardization boundary.
- Pius X, *Divino afflatu*, AAS 3 (1911), 633–638: structural psalter and precedence reform.
- Sacred Congregation of Rites, *Cum hac nostra aetate*, AAS 47 (1955), 218–224: simplified ranks, vigils, octaves, and commemorations.
- Sacred Congregation of Rites, *Maxima redemptionis nostrae mysteria*, AAS 47 (1955), 838–847: revised Holy Week boundary.
- Paul VI, *Mysterii Paschalis* and *Calendarium Romanum* (1969): successor-calendar boundary only.
- Thomas J. Talley, *The Origins of the Liturgical Year*, 2nd ed. (Liturgical Press, 1991): secondary synthesis for the long formation of the annual cycle.

## Edition decisions

- “1962 calendar” means MR-1962 plus RI-1960, not a modern traditionalist Ordo.
- The fixed calendar follows MR-1962 even where modern spelling or a later feast date differs.
- U.S. rows require an explicit nationwide heading. Mere presence in a formulary collection is insufficient.
- The reported 1963 transfer of Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini to November 13 is not incorporated until the relevant decree or dated national Ordo is directly collated.
- `Cum sanctissima` is displayed as a later optional layer and never merged into the fixed 1962 tables.
