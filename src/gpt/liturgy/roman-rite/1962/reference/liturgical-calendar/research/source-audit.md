# Source audit

## Source hierarchy

1. Typical liturgical book and official AAS enactments.
2. Official Holy See web publication of later decrees and explanatory notes.
3. Edition-specific approved proper with dated imprimatur and explicit jurisdiction headings.
4. Scholarly historical synthesis for background only.
5. Composite scans, searchable OCR, catalog records, modern Ordos, and secondary calendars as leads only.

OCR was never treated as the final witness. It was used to locate material, after which the relevant facsimile pages were visually checked.

## Completed checks

### Universal fixed calendar

- Collated MR-1962 physical PDF pp. 47–55, printed pp. XLV–LIII.
- Visually inspected all nine source pages after text extraction.
- Recorded every date carrying a feast, vigil, octave day, or commemoration.
- Preserved subordinate commemorations on the same date row.
- Checked the February leap-year rule directly at printed p. XLVI / PDF p. 48.
- Kept the movable Holy Name, Holy Family, Seven Sorrows in Passiontide, and Christ the King entries out of arbitrary fixed-date rows.
- Result: 275 fixed date rows, plus the movable calendar rules in the Temporale section.

### Temporale and classes

- Checked RI-1960 General Rubrics nn. 9–34 for Sundays, ferias, and vigils; nn. 63–90 for octaves, seasons, Saturdays of Our Lady, and Litanies; and n. 91 for precedence.
- Checked the MR-1962 *Proprium de tempore* for the named cycle and formularies’ order.
- Did not assign a separate “feast class” to the Minor Litanies; they are observances connected to the occurring days.
- Recorded the rule that the final Sunday is always the twenty-fourth after Pentecost and omitted post-Epiphany Sundays are resumed according to General Rubrics n. 18.

### United States layer

- Verified the 28 Sept. 1961 imprimatur and the distinct U.S. proper title in US-DES-1961.
- Visually inspected the six nationwide headings and classes:
  - Cabrini: PDF p. 47, printed p. 9, December 22, class II.
  - Philip of Jesus: PDF p. 56, printed p. 18, February 5, class III.
  - Isidore the Farm-Laborer: PDF p. 63, printed p. 25, March 22, class III.
  - Turibius of Mogrovejo: PDF p. 69, printed p. 31, April 27, class III.
  - Francis Solano: PDF p. 93, printed p. 55, July 24, class III.
  - Isaac Jogues, John de Brébeuf, and Companions: PDF p. 102, printed p. 64, September 26, class II.
- Each source heading says “in all dioceses of the United States.”
- Negative checks: Our Lady of Guadalupe is expressly restricted to named western dioceses on December 12 and to the Province of New Orleans on November 16; Peter Claver says “in many dioceses.” Neither was nationalized.
- The appended 1937 material in US-COMP was treated as diocesan evidence, not a 1962 national proper.

### `Cum sanctissima`

- Read the official English decree and presentation note in full.
- Mapped publication paragraphs 1–8 one-for-one to the decree’s numbered provisions.
- Counted the annex by month: 9 + 3 + 6 + 3 + 6 + 6 + 6 + 8 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 5 = 70.
- Preserved the decree’s cut-off date, 26 July 1960, and effective date, 19 March 2020.
- Distinguished the festal-Mass option from the optional-commemoration option.

## Rights and quotation audit

- No Mass or Office prayer text, chant notation, or substantial source prose is reproduced.
- Latin celebration titles, dates, classes, jurisdiction labels, and bibliographic facts are used as necessary calendar data.
- US-DES-1961 and the Missal scans are linked for verification but are not installed or redistributed by this leaf.
- Official Vatican material is summarized; the protected-feast titles follow the Holy See's published English annex.
- Talley is paraphrased at a high level and not quoted.
