\section{How to read the 1962 calendar}

\subsection{Classes, ferias, and commemorations}

The 1960 Code assigns liturgical days to class I, II, III, or IV. Class is a category inside a larger precedence system, not a score that can be compared without regard to the kind of day. The Code's table of precedence, occurrence rules, and special seasonal rules decide which office and Mass prevail.

The tables use ``Comm.'' when the printed calendar gives only a commemoration. A commemoration is not a fifth class and not an independent feast. When a III-class feast has an additional commemoration, both are shown on the same row. The commemoration's actual admission can still depend on the occurring day and the general rules.

\subsection{Occurrence and transfer}

When two observances claim the same day, the higher item in the table of precedence normally prevails. The defeated observance is transferred only where the Code so provides; otherwise it is commemorated or omitted according to the rules. A bare annual list that moves every collision to the next free date is therefore unreliable.

Sundays of class I, the Sacred Triduum, privileged ferias and vigils, octaves, and particular feasts all have special places in the precedence table. The complete decision procedure belongs to the companion study \emph{Assembling the Mass}; this volume supplies the calendar inputs.

\subsection{Civil date and liturgical day}

The liturgical day generally runs from midnight to midnight. The Office's rules of concurrence and Vespers are a related but distinct question. This study's date tables identify the day and do not attempt to print a daily Office directory.

\begin{studybox}{Three layers to consult for an actual day}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item Generate the Temporale from Easter and the weekday/date rules.
\item Add the universal fixed entry and any legitimately approved particular entry.
\item Apply precedence, occurrence, transfer, commemoration, and any applicable later permission; then check the competent Ordo.
\end{enumerate}
\end{studybox}
