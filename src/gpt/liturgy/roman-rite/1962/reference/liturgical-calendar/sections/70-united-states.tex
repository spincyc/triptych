\section{United States national layer}

\subsection{What the surviving edition witness establishes}

A Desclée appendix titled \emph{Proper Feasts Kept in the Dioceses of the United States of America}, with a Tournai imprimatur dated 28 September 1961, distinguishes nationwide celebrations from feasts confined to named dioceses, provinces, or religious bodies. The six rows below are the entries for which that witness expressly says ``in all dioceses of the United States.'' They are the national layer attested by this edition-specific source; they are not reconstructed from a modern calendar.

These celebrations are \emph{proper}, not ``optional memorials'' in the terminology of the postconciliar calendar. Their actual treatment when they meet a universal celebration or a privileged feria is controlled by the 1960 precedence and occurrence rules. For example, the Desclée Cabrini page itself directs a commemoration of the Advent feria.

\subsection{The Cabrini date boundary}

The source just collated unambiguously prints Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini on December 22 as a nationwide class-II feast. A later calendar-change notice is reported to have moved the U.S. observance to November 13 in 1963. That notice has not been collated in an authoritative decree or dated national Ordo for this publication. Accordingly, December 22 is retained as the fact proven by the 1961 appendix; November 13 is recorded in the research audit as an unresolved later operational change, not inserted into the 1962 baseline by editorial guesswork.

\subsection{What is not nationalized}

The same appendix proves why an index or formulary collection cannot be treated as a national calendar. Its Our Lady of Guadalupe heading assigns December 12 only to the Archdioceses of San Francisco and Santa Fe and the Dioceses of Los Angeles, Sacramento, and Tucson; it assigns November 16 to the Province of New Orleans. Saint Peter Claver is headed ``in many dioceses,'' not all dioceses. Numerous other entries name one diocese, a group of dioceses, a province, or a religious institute. None of those limited claims belongs in the nationwide table above.

A separate composite American Missal scan contains a 1937 diocesan supplement and loose later inserts. It is useful for locating texts but not for proving a unitary 1962 national calendar. In particular, its \emph{pro aliquibus locis} index proves that a formulary was present, not that every U.S. diocese was bound to celebrate it.

\begin{studybox}{Using the U.S. layer}
For a historical reconstruction, use the six attested nationwide rows with the universal tables and the dated Ordo for the diocese in question. For present celebration, consult the competent authority and current Ordo as well: this study neither determines authorization to use the 1962 books nor converts later pastoral practice into historical calendar evidence.
\end{studybox}
