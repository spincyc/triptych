\section{Use boundaries and verification checklist}

\subsection{Historical description is not present authorization}

This study reports the contents and operation of liturgical books and later documents. It does not decide who may celebrate according to those books today, in which church, under which superior, or with which pastoral conditions. Those questions require the currently governing universal and particular law and the competent authority.

\subsection{Before producing a dated Ordo}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item Identify the civil year and compute Easter; generate the complete Temporale.
\item Verify the edition, country, diocese, church title and dedication, patron, and any religious institute calendar.
\item Use a dated, authorized particular calendar or Ordo. Do not infer nationwide force from a formulary, index, hand-missal appendix, or present-day website.
\item Apply the 1960 table of precedence and the rules of occurrence, transfer, and commemoration. Check leap-year and resumed-Sunday rules.
\item If invoking \emph{Cum sanctissima}, record which of its two options is used, the saint's qualifying date, the source of the formulary, and the protected-day check.
\item Preserve unresolved date changes as unresolved until an authoritative decree or dated Ordo is collated.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Known limits of this edition}

The universal tables have been transcribed from and checked against the 1962 typical Missal. The U.S. national table is complete for the explicit ``all dioceses'' claims in the 1961 Desclée appendix examined here. It is not a claim that no other nationwide decree ever operated during the broader 1960s. In particular, the reported 1963 Cabrini transfer still requires direct collation. Diocesan variations are intentionally excluded rather than guessed.
