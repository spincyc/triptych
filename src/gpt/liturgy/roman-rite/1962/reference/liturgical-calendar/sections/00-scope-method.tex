\section{Scope, Edition, and Qualifications}

This study describes the general calendar printed in the 1962 typical edition of the \emph{Missale Romanum}. It answers three different questions without confusing them: how that calendar acquired its form; which reforms materially changed it; and what celebrations the printed calendar actually contains. The tables therefore reproduce calendar data, not Mass propers or prayer texts.

\subsection{The edition represented}

The normative baseline is the typical edition promulgated under Saint John XXIII in 1962, read with the 1960 \emph{Codex rubricarum}. The fixed calendar was collated against the calendar headed \emph{Calendarium} in a digitized copy of that edition. The movable cycle was checked against its \emph{Proprium de tempore} and the Code's rules for the liturgical year. Latin titles are documentary labels taken or normalized from those headings; this publication does not present them as newly made official English translations.

This is a calendar model, not a perpetual civil-date Ordo. The date of Easter, occurrence, transfer, local patronal feasts, a church's title and dedication, religious-family calendars, and later permissions can change what is celebrated in a particular place and year. The general tables must therefore be combined with the rubrics and a competent local Ordo.

\subsection{What ``complete'' means here}

The Temporale section gives every recurring movable observance and the rules that generate the Sundays and ferias. The Sanctorale tables give every dated celebration and every commemoration printed in the general calendar. A date absent from the fixed tables is not missing evidence: it has no universal fixed observance in the printed calendar, so its seasonal feria or an occurring local celebration governs.

The United States section is deliberately separate. A national calendar, diocesan calendars, and the Missal's collection \emph{pro aliquibus locis} are not interchangeable. Only a nationwide item established by a national or edition-specific witness belongs in the national overlay; named diocesan entries are not silently promoted into it.

\begin{studybox}{Evidence labels used in this study}
\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=0.28\linewidth,labelwidth=0.25\linewidth,itemsep=0.3em]
\item[Printed baseline] Data directly attested in the 1962 typical Missal or the 1960 Code.
\item[Particular layer] Data attested for the United States or another competent particular calendar, kept distinct from the general calendar.
\item[Later option] A permission enacted after 1962, such as \emph{Cum sanctissima}; it does not retroactively alter the printed baseline.
\item[Unresolved lead] Material found only in a composite scan, index, modern Ordo, or secondary compilation and therefore excluded from a normative table until its authority and date are established.
\end{description}
\end{studybox}
