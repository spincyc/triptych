# Assembling the Mass — Research Scope

## Identity

- **Provider:** OpenAI GPT, represented by the structured `generation-metadata.tex` record in the document leaf.
- **Collection / genre:** *Fides, Cultus, Ius*; edition-specific Roman liturgical reference work.
- **Rite and edition:** Roman Rite as printed in the 1962 typical edition of the *Missale Romanum*, applying the 1960 *Codex rubricarum* incorporated into that edition.
- **Document language:** English exposition with Latin technical terms and titles retained where precision requires them.
- **Calendar level:** universal calendar as the baseline. National, diocesan, religious, church-title, dedication, patronal, and indult cases are conditional and require the approved proper source named by the case.
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-13 for the research and publication record. This date does not turn the study into an opinion on current authorization to celebrate with the 1962 Missal.

## Research question

The document answers how, after choosing the governing calendar, a worker determines the occurring day, its precedence, the disposition of every impeded item, the kind of Mass admitted, and the placement of Ordinary, proper, Common, commemorative, seasonal, votive, Requiem, and ritual material from entrance through the Last Gospel.

It deliberately separates five decisions:

1. occurrence of temporal, sanctoral, and proper-calendar entries;
2. precedence under the twenty-eight-position table and named exceptions;
3. omission, commemoration, accidental transfer, or perpetual reposition of each loser;
4. admission and class of the actual Mass category;
5. complete textual and rubrical assembly of that Mass.

## Included subjects

- Sundays, ferias, vigils, the three surviving octaves, Our Lady on Saturday, universal and hypothetical sourced proper feasts;
- the complete table of liturgical-day precedence and its Sunday, Christmas, vigil, occurrence, transfer, and reposition qualifications;
- privileged and ordinary commemorations, number and order of prayers, identity exclusions, and the Low / conventual / nonconventual-sung distinction;
- Masses of the Office, repeated ferial formularies, resumed Sundays after Epiphany, festive Masses, votive Masses, and external solemnities;
- First Thursday / First Saturday Christ-the-Priest, First Friday Sacred Heart, and First Saturday Immaculate Heart permissions, including place, exercises, number, day class, and same-Person boundaries;
- All Souls, funeral, death-day, burial, anniversary, cemetery, eight-day, and daily Requiems;
- the Nuptial Mass, Rogations, exposition and related ritual contexts to the extent needed to demonstrate assembly and admission;
- all variable locations from the ministers' entrance through the Last Gospel, including Gloria, Creed, Preface, proper Canon forms, prayer over the people, dismissal, blessing, and form-of-Mass consequences;
- thirty-five worked cases and a reproducible worksheet with independent-answer checks.

## Excluded questions

- present universal or particular authorization to celebrate the 1962 Missal;
- current faculties, sacramental canonical form, civil law, pastoral scheduling, and local permissions;
- creation of a real national, diocesan, religious, church, patronal, titular, dedication, or indult calendar;
- a complete ceremonial for Solemn, pontifical, ordination, funeral, marriage, procession, exposition, or other ritual actions;
- replacement of the Missal, Roman Ritual, Roman Pontifical, competent Ordo, or lawful superior's direction;
- translation of the complete copyrighted or liturgical texts. The work explains sources and slots rather than reproducing a hand missal.

## Source hierarchy

1. John XXIII's *Rubricarum instructum* and the 1960 code as officially printed in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* 52 (1960), 593–740.
2. The 1962 typical *Missale Romanum*: *Rubricae generales Missalis Romani*, *Ritus servandus*, *Ordo Missae*, Canon, formularies, Commons, votives, Requiems, and ritual pages.
3. Approved particular calendars, supplements, or indults for an actual local case; none is silently supplied in this universal reference.
4. Repository-owned, source-audited companion records for the 1962 Ordinary and Nuptial Mass, used only for their checked text boundaries and insertion points.
5. OCR and search extraction as finding aids. They never control over a page image.

## Translation and inference policy

Rubric statements in the publication are original close English paraphrases of the Latin. They are not presented as an official translation. The `rubric-index.md` record labels direct rules, necessary inferences, and editorial workflow. A direct rule may be compressed but must retain its conditions. An inference may combine two or more direct rules but may not create a new faculty. Workflow advice is identified as method, not liturgical law.

## Material uncertainties and boundaries

- Rubric 406's prohibition list does not include an ordinary II-class Sunday merely as Sunday; the publication records that textual result while expressly requiring a competent Ordo, current law, and authority for actual practice.
- Hypothetical proper-feast cases cannot become local answers until the approved proper calendar, title, patronage, dedication, or indult is produced and checked.
- A precise civil-year calendar was intentionally not asserted. Worked cases use abstract date conditions so no unchecked year is smuggled into the reference.
- The manual gives assembly rules for ritual contexts but does not supersede the governing companion ritual book or a full ceremonial.
- The official AAS OCR and the public 1962 facsimile OCR contain recognition errors; all numbered propositions were checked by navigating the corresponding facsimile text, but quotations were avoided and the Latin page remains controlling.
