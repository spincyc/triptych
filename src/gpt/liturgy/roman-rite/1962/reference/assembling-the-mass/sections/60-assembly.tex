\subsection{Completing a partial sanctoral formulary}

Create a source column before copying text:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.45em]
\item Copy every item printed proper to the feast.
\item Follow each direction to a named Common for missing chants, readings, or prayers.
\item Where the Common gives alternatives, use only choices authorized by rubric 305.
\item Apply seasonal modifications printed in the Proper or Common.
\item Determine the Preface separately under 482--499 unless the formulary names one.
\item Add only commemorations independently admitted by 106--114.
\end{enumerate}

Do not splice preferred readings from one Common into a complete proper Mass without a rule. Do not assume that a saint's historical category supplies a Preface of that category; the 1962 Preface list is enumerated, and most saints use the seasonal or Common Preface.

\subsection{The ten principal variable texts}

For an ordinary Sunday or feast, the practical core is:

\begin{center}
\small
Introit $\rightarrow$ Collect $\rightarrow$ Epistle $\rightarrow$ Gradual/Alleluia or Tract $\rightarrow$ Gospel\\
$\rightarrow$ Offertory $\rightarrow$ Secret $\rightarrow$ Communion $\rightarrow$ Postcommunion,
\end{center}

with Gradual and Alleluia often counted separately to make ten. This map is useful but incomplete. A Sequence, multiple lessons, proper Preface, proper Canon formulas, prayer over the people, procession, ritual blessing, or Requiem variant can add essential material. The particular page controls.

\subsection{Gloria}

Rubrics 431--432 give the decision:

\begin{itemize}
\item In a Mass corresponding to the Office, Gloria follows whether the Office used \latin{Te Deum}.
\item Festive Masses under 302 use it.
\item Votive I, II, and III use it unless violet excludes it under the stated rule.
\item IV-class votives omit it except Masses of angels on any day and Marian votives on Saturday.
\item Requiems omit it.
\end{itemize}

This prevents two common errors: retaining the Sunday Gloria when the Sunday formulary is repeated on an ordinary feria, and omitting the Gloria from a qualified First Friday votive simply because it falls in Lent.

\subsection{Creed}

Rubrics 475--476 require the Creed:

\begin{itemize}
\item on every Sunday, even when its Office yields or a permitted II-class votive is celebrated;
\item on I-class feasts and I-class votives;
\item on II-class feasts of the Lord and Mary;
\item through the three surviving octaves, even in occurring feasts and votives;
\item on the named apostolic and evangelist feasts.
\end{itemize}

Other II-class feasts, II-class votives, festive or votive III/IV Masses, commemorations as such, and Requiems do not receive it. A II-class external solemnity on Sunday has the Creed because Sunday independently requires it.

A prayer joined under one conclusion is written on the same conclusion line but remains governed by rubrics 444--446. The Nuptial Collect, ritual prayer, papal or episcopal anniversary prayer, missionary prayer, imposed prayer, and free votive prayer each have their own admission rule; they are not interchangeable “extra collects.”

\subsection{Preface hierarchy}

Apply rubric 482 in this order:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.45em]
\item a Preface proper to the selected Mass;
\item otherwise, the Preface of the liturgical season under 484--494;
\item otherwise, the Common Preface.
\end{enumerate}

The seasonal list includes Christmas, Epiphany, Lent, Holy Cross/Passiontide, Easter, Ascension, Holy Spirit, and Trinity for the Sundays it governs. Marian, Saint Joseph, apostolic, Sacred Heart, and Christ the King Prefaces apply to their festive and votive Masses as specified. Rubric 483 expressly prevents a commemoration from changing the Preface.

\subsection{Proper Canon forms}

Rubric 501 and the Canon's annotated texts control variations in \latin{Communicantes}, \latin{Hanc igitur}, and \latin{Qui pridie}. During the Christmas octave, its proper \latin{Communicantes} is said even in Masses not of the octave and even when those Masses have a proper Preface. During the Easter and Pentecost octaves, both the proper \latin{Communicantes} and the proper \latin{Hanc igitur} apply on the same terms. This is a genuine overlay: the selected feast or votive remains the Mass celebrated, while the octave supplies only the forms actually appointed to it.

\subsection{The resumed Sundays after Epiphany}

When Septuagesima prevents the Third through Sixth Sundays after Epiphany, rubric 18 assigns the needed formularies before the final Sunday after Pentecost. Rubric 298 constructs each resumed Mass from two sources:

\begin{itemize}
\item Introit, Gradual, Alleluia, Offertory, and Communion from the Twenty-third Sunday after Pentecost;
\item Collect, Epistle, Gospel, Secret, and Postcommunion from the resumed Epiphany Sunday.
\end{itemize}

The Mass keeps its proper resumed-Sunday identity. Rubric 18 first fixes each nominal resumed
formulary in its numbered slot; occurrence is a separate operation. A feast that wins one slot
impedes that slot's Sunday under Rubrics 14 and 16, but does not pull later resumed formularies
backward. In a twenty-eight-Sunday year, therefore, XXIV--XXVII remain Third--Sixth after
Epiphany even when Christ the King displaces the Third at XXIV. The Twenty-fourth and Last
Sunday after Pentecost always remains last. A hand missal that simply points to “the preceding
Sunday” cannot replace this explicit composite. A dated Ordo must still verify an actual civil
year and its proper-calendar overlays; the twenty-three-Sunday shortfall remains fail-closed.

\subsection{Low, sung, and Solemn execution}

Rubrics 271--273 define Low and sung Mass, with Solemn and pontifical forms. Rubric 473 removes from the celebrant the lessons sung by ministers or a reader. Rubrics 511--516 distinguish audible parts and solemn or ferial tones. The most consequential assembly difference is commemorations: ordinary commemorations occur in all Low Masses and the conventual Mass, but not in a nonconventual sung Mass (108). The text sheet should therefore identify the actual form of each Mass before printing its prayer triads.
