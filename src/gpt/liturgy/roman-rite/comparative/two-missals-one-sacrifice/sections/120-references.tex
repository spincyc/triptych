\subsection*{Scripture, doctrine, and sacramental theology}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.12em,topsep=0.15em]
\item Holy Scripture: Exod. 12 and 24; Mal. 1:11; Matt. 26:26--29; Mark 14:22--25; Luke 22:14--20; John 6 and 13--17; Acts 2:42; 1~Cor. 10:16--21 and 11:23--34; Heb. 7--10; Rev. 4--5 and 19. These loci govern sacrifice, memorial, Communion, covenant, priesthood, and eschatological worship.
\item Council of Trent, Session VII, Decree on the Sacraments (3 March 1547), canons 1, 8, 11, and 13; Session XIII, Decree on the Eucharist (11 October 1551); Session XXII, Doctrine and Canons on the Sacrifice of the Mass (17 September 1562); Session XXIII on Orders; Session XXIV on Matrimony; Session XXV continuation entrusted to the pope. Norman P. Tanner, ed., \emph{Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils} (Georgetown University Press, 1990), checked against the Latin conciliar corpus.
\item Second Vatican Council, \emph{Sacrosanctum Concilium} (4 December 1963), especially 7, 10, 14, 21--23, 34, 36, 47--58, 112, and 116, \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html}{official English}; \emph{Lumen gentium} 10 on the ordered but essential distinction between common and ministerial priesthood.
\item Thomas Aquinas, \emph{Summa theologiae} III, qq.~60--65 and 73--83, especially q.~64 on ministers and q.~82 on the minister of the Eucharist; Supplement, qq.~42--68 for the received scholastic Matrimony material. Used as Thomistic causal and sacramental synthesis, not as current positive law.
\item Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1066--1209, 1322--1419, 1536--1666; especially 1127--1128 on \latin{ex opere operato}, 1362--1372 on memorial and sacrifice, and 1373--1381 on Presence.
\item Pius XII, \emph{Mediator Dei} (20 November 1947), especially 20--23, 59--64, 80--109, and 152, \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_20111947_mediator-dei.html}{official English}; \emph{Sacramentum ordinis} (30 November 1947), on matter and form of Orders.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Roman Missals and historical legislation}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.12em,topsep=0.15em]
\item \emph{Missale Romanum Mediolani, 1474}, Robert Lippe, ed., Henry Bradshaw Society 17 and 33 (1899, 1907), photographic and diplomatic witness; Library of Congress, \sourceurl{https://www.loc.gov/collections/moldenhauer-archives/articles-and-essays/guide-to-archives/missale-romanum/}{guide to the early printed Roman Missal}. Used to establish pre-Tridentine textual ancestry, not uniform medieval practice.
\item Pius V, \emph{Quo primum} and \emph{Missale Romanum} (14 July 1570), \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/library/docs_pi05qp.htm}{public English translation}; Eötvös Loránd University Usuarium, \sourceurl{https://usuarium.elte.hu/book/480/view}{digitized 1570 witness}. The Latin controls the two-hundred-year exception and legislative grammar.
\item Clement VIII, \emph{Cum sanctissimum} and 1604 \emph{Missale Romanum}; Urban VIII, \emph{Si quid est} and 1634 typical edition. Used to test claims that the 1570 disciplinary text prevented papal revision.
\item \emph{Missale Romanum ex decreto Sacrosancti Concilii Tridentini restitutum}, Leonine recension (Pustet, 1889), \sourceurl{https://archive.org/details/missaleromanumex1889cath/page/n13/mode/1up}{digitized front-matter decree leaf}. Its title and prefatory corpus witness Leo XIII's recension, the 15/29 September 1714 order to print accumulated decrees after the General Rubrics, and Pius IX's 10 July 1862 direction to print three older ceremonial rulings in a forthcoming edition.
\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, \emph{Decreta authentica}, vol.~2, no.~3103, pp.~427--430, decisions concerning two forthcoming Roman Missals (25 September 1860), approved by Pius IX (27 September 1860), \sourceurl{https://archive.org/details/decretaauthentic02cath/page/426/mode/2up}{digitized official collection}. Used for the comparison with Clementine and Urban archetypes, the 1714 Propaganda edition, four early post-Urban printings, and authentic decrees, and for the fifteen resulting textual and rubrical responses.
\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, \emph{Decreta authentica}, vol.~2, no.~3119, pp.~440--441, Pius IX, \emph{Quod iampridem} (25 September 1863), \sourceurl{https://archive.org/details/decretaauthentic02cath/page/440/mode/2up}{digitized official collection}; vol.~3, nos.~3249 and 3252, pp.~9--12, printer's response (22 April 1871) and Pius IX, \emph{Inclytum Patriarcham} (7 July 1871), \sourceurl{https://archive.org/details/decretaauthentic03cath/page/8/mode/2up}{digitized official collection}. Used for changes to universal propers, the Creed rule, \latin{A cunctis}, and the exact relation between a decree and a Missal printing.
\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, \emph{Additiones et variationes in rubricis generalibus et specialibus Breviarii et Missalis Romani} (approved 7 December and confirmed 11 December 1897), authorized Pustet edition (1898), \sourceurl{https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb11867577?page=56}{digitized copy and decree}. Used for the Leonine reconciliation of rubrics with later decisions and the command that the variations enter new editions.
\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, decree \emph{De editione typica Missalis Romani} (25 July 1920), AAS 12 (1920), 448--449, \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-12-1920-ocr.pdf}{official AAS volume}. The decree identifies the 1900 \latin{altera typica} as its base, limits its own innovations to consequences of recent prescriptions and Breviary changes, adds approved local Masses in an appendix, and declares the new edition typical.
\item United States Conference of Catholic Bishops, \sourceurl{https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/roman-missal}{Roman Missal historical chronology}. Used to corroborate the sequence of papally promulgated editions, including Leo XIII's 1884 and Benedict XV's 1920 books, while distinguishing minor revisions and reprints.
\item Pius X, \emph{Divino afflatu} (1 November 1911) and related rubrical changes; Sacred Congregation of Rites, \emph{Additiones et variationes in rubricis Missalis} (1911). Used for the Psalter, calendar, and Missal sequence before 1962.
\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, \emph{Dominicae resurrectionis vigiliam} (1951), \emph{Maxima redemptionis nostrae mysteria} (16 November 1955), and General Decree \emph{Cum nostra hac aetate} (23 March 1955), official AAS texts. Used for Holy Week and rubrical transition.
\item John XXIII, \emph{Rubricarum instructum} (25 July 1960), and Sacred Congregation of Rites decree declaring the 1962 Vatican \emph{Missale Romanum} typical (23 June 1962); \sourceurl{https://media.churchmusicassociation.org/pdf/missale62.pdf}{facsimile witness}. The Order, rubrics, calendar, and Saint Joseph decree are edition-specific objects.
\item Paul VI, \emph{Sacram liturgiam} (25 January 1964), \emph{Inter Oecumenici} (26 September 1964), \emph{Musicam sacram} (5 March 1967), \emph{Tres abhinc annos} (4 May 1967), and \emph{Mysterii paschalis} (14 February 1969), official AAS / Holy See texts.
\item Paul VI, apostolic constitution \emph{Missale Romanum} (3 April 1969), \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_p-vi_apc_19690403_missale-romanum.html}{official English}; general audiences of \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/it/audiences/1969/documents/hf_p-vi_aud_19691119.html}{19 November} and \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/it/audiences/1969/documents/hf_p-vi_aud_19691126.html}{26 November 1969}, official Italian.
\item \emph{Missale Romanum}, editiones typicae 1970, 1975, and 2002; 2008 emended reprint; \emph{Institutio Generalis Missalis Romani}, third typical edition, \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/wss/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20030317_ordinamento-messale_en.html}{official English witness}. Current United States edition and adaptations are separately identified.
\item Benedict XVI, letter to the president of the German Episcopal Conference on translating \latin{pro multis} (14 April 2012), official German text; Holy See approval history of the former English institutional narrative. Used to distinguish a translation requiring correction from sacramental invalidity and to preserve universal sufficiency / differentiated fruit.
\item United States Conference of Catholic Bishops, \sourceurl{https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-4}{GIRM chapter IV}, especially 118, 154, 160--162, 178, 191, and 284--287; other chapters checked in the same approved territorial edition.
\item Sacred Congregation for Divine Worship, 28 October 1974 notification concerning obligatory use of the revised Missal, \emph{Notitiae} 10 (1974), \sourceurl{https://www.cultodivino.va/content/dam/cultodivino/rivista-notitiae/1970/notitiae-10-\%281974\%29/Notitiae-099-1974.pdf}{official periodical scan}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Historical and liturgical studies}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.12em,topsep=0.15em]
\item Adrian Fortescue, \emph{The Mass: A Study of the Roman Liturgy}, sec.~IV.8, pp.~124--126, \sourceurl{https://gorettipub.org/fortescue_mass_study_liturgy.pdf}{digitized 1912 witness}. Used as a contemporary specialist account of the 1884 recension, its calendar and rubrical objects, the non-revision under Benedict XIV, and the continuity of the Ordinary and Canon; its historical judgments remain secondary to the books and decrees.
\item Josef A. Jungmann, \emph{The Mass of the Roman Rite: Its Origins and Development}, trans. Francis A. Brunner (Benziger, 1951/1955). A foundational developmental synthesis whose date and hypotheses are weighed against later scholarship.
\item Uwe Michael Lang, \emph{The Roman Mass: From Early Christian Origins to Tridentine Reform} (Cambridge University Press, 2022), especially \sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/roman-mass/tridentine-reform/9DAE3EB1F1C1D547EA07EA342A49C0B5}{“The Tridentine Reform”}; used for the Curial/Franciscan line and scope of standardization.
\item Annibale Bugnini, \emph{The Reform of the Liturgy 1948--1975}, trans. Matthew J. O'Connell (Liturgical Press, 1990). Participant testimony checked against official acts; not treated as the sole narrator.
\item Alcuin Reid, \emph{The Organic Development of the Liturgy} (Ignatius Press, 2005). Used for the problem of method and scale, with the author's normative judgments identified as such.
\item Lauren Pristas, \emph{Collects of the Roman Missals} (T\&T Clark, 2013). Used for comparative euchological method and the limits of source labels.
\item Paul F. Bradshaw, Maxwell E. Johnson, and L. Edward Phillips, \emph{The Apostolic Tradition: A Commentary} (Fortress, 2002). Used to bound confident claims that Eucharistic Prayer II simply restores an uncontested Hippolytan text.
\item Adrian Fortescue, revised by J. B. O'Connell and Alcuin Reid, \emph{The Ceremonies of the Roman Rite Described}; Peter Elliott, \emph{Ceremonies of the Modern Roman Rite}; each used to check enacted ceremonial against the proper edition, never to override its rubrics.
\end{itemize}
