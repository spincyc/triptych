# Two Missals, One Sacrifice — Edition Manifest

Checked 2026-07-13. This record prevents a colloquial “TLM versus Novus Ordo” comparison from silently combining different books, translations, territories, and customs.

## Controlling comparison witnesses

| Side | Exact witness | Language / territory | Role in the paper | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Older Roman Mass | *Missale Romanum ex decreto sacrosancti Concilii Tridentini restitutum, Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum*, Vatican typical edition, decree dated 23 June 1962 | Latin; universal Roman edition under its historical law | Controls sequence, texts, rubrics, calendar terminology, and the proper meaning of “1962 Missal” | Does not include pre-1955 Holy Week, the pre-1960 ranking system, vernacular hand-missal paraphrases, or local preconciliar customs unless separately identified |

## Companion sources

- **1962 ceremonial:** the 1962 *Ritus servandus*, *Ordo Missae*, General Rubrics / Code of Rubrics, and edition-appropriate chant and ceremonial sources.
- **Postconciliar ceremonial:** the 2002/2008 Latin GIRM and Order; current approved U.S. GIRM for territorial applications.
- **Readings:** the 1962 Missal’s Mass lections and gradual complex; the postconciliar *Ordo Lectionum Missae* and approved U.S. Lectionary architecture. The paper describes scope but does not reproduce copyrighted vernacular readings.
- **Other sacraments:** the last typical books in force in 1962 and the revised rites promulgated by Paul VI are treated only at the level needed to answer validity and current authorization. They are not silently read into either Mass Order.
- **Current law:** 1983 Latin Code as amended, *Traditionis custodes*, 2021 Responsa, 2023 rescript, public special provisions, and 2026 DDF SSPX acts through the cutoff.

## Translation and quotation policy

The paper ordinarily names Latin incipits or provides original analytical paraphrase. It does not reproduce the Ordinary, approved English Missal, or Lectionary at length. Short official English phrases identify legal or doctrinal propositions. The Latin typical text remains controlling where a vernacular source could introduce a difference.

## Direct-comparison gate

Every comparison was tested against these questions:

1. Are the units genuinely corresponding in ritual function?
2. Is an element omitted, relocated, optional, or supplied elsewhere rather than simply “removed”?
3. Does a claim belong to the book, later universal law, U.S. adaptation, architectural custom, music, or local abuse?
4. Does “validity” refer to a capable approved rite or to a concrete enacted attempt?
5. Is a historical source being used for ancestry rather than made immediately normative?

## Review state

The edition boundaries and principal legal texts were checked. A line-by-line independent review against physical copies of the 2008 Latin reprint and current U.S. altar book remains outstanding; the publication therefore remains a source-audited working paper.
