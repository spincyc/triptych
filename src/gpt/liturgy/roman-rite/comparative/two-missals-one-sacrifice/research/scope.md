This record supports `src/gpt/liturgy/roman-rite/comparative/two-missals-one-sacrifice/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** GPT; Roman Rite comparative liturgy.
- **Primary genre:** a discursive historical, theological, and canonical paper. `guidance/articles.md` governs its argument, authority calibration, and publication state.
- **Comparative module:** `guidance/liturgy/ordinary-expositions.md` governs every direct comparison of liturgical editions, quotations, rubrics, and translations. Each witness retains its own source trail.
- **Sacramental module:** the universal doctrinal and validity synthesis is checked against the repository's sacramental reference work and its primary sources, but that reference work's template is not imposed on this article.
- **Question:** How did the Roman Missal standardized after Trent develop into the 1962 typical edition, how did the postconciliar Missal arise and differ, what judgments may Catholics make without denying either the Church's sacramental doctrine or her liturgical authority, what presently governs reception and lay ministry, and which corporate communities materially centered on older Latin books can be located and canonically qualified?
- **As-of date:** 2026-07-13. Current law, permissions, and community status are mutable. The cutoff is especially material to the SSPX and Sons of the Most Holy Redeemer in 2026.

## Edition, territory, and terminology

1. **Older comparison witness:** the 1962 Latin typical edition of the *Missale Romanum* promulgated under John XXIII. “Traditional Latin Mass” and “TLM” are treated as current colloquialisms, not exact historical names. Earlier Roman editions and surviving Latin uses are identified in their own right.
2. **Postconciliar comparison witness:** the 2002 third Latin typical edition, with its 2008 emended reprint, and the current United States English Missal and GIRM where territorial discipline is discussed. The 1969 *Ordo Missae*, 1970 first typical edition, 1975 second typical edition, and transitional books remain separate historical objects.
3. **Territory and law:** universal Latin law plus United States adaptations for concrete reception examples. A territorial permission is never represented as universal.
4. **Other rites:** Ambrosian, Mozarabic, Bragan, Lyonese, Dominican, Carmelite, Carthusian, Premonstratensian, and other older Latin uses are not aliases for the 1962 Roman Missal. Eastern Catholic liturgies lie outside the comparison.
5. **“Novus Ordo”:** reported as a widespread postconciliar label. In analytical prose the paper uses “postconciliar Roman Missal,” “Missal of Paul VI,” or an exact edition because *novus ordo* can ambiguously name the 1969 Order, the 1970 book, a later typical edition, a vernacular translation, or local practice.

## Community-census inclusion rule

No public, current, universal Holy See registry of “TLM congregations” was located. The published appendix is therefore an authority-qualified census, not a claim of metaphysical completeness.

A row is included when a corporate ecclesial body, autonomous monastery, jurisdiction, institute, society, public association, or organized separated body was located by the search protocol and its stable common life or proper identity materially centers on the 1962 Roman books or another pre-reform Latin use. The census excludes:

- individual diocesan Mass sites, parishes, chaplaincies, and oratories unless the corporate body itself is the object;
- individual priests, informal groups of faithful, directories, publishers, and lay promotion organizations;
- an otherwise ordinary religious institute merely because one house or member sometimes celebrates an older use;
- Eastern Catholic communities, the Anglican Ordinariates, and postconciliar Latin celebrations;
- a house already counted under a single centralized institute, unless it is canonically autonomous.

The search protocol combined the former Pontifical Commission *Ecclesia Dei* archive, Holy See and diocesan acts, official institute and monastery pages, Catholic hierarchical directories as secondary leads, broad dynamic directories as discovery tools only, and targeted searches through the cutoff. Each row states the strongest status that a competent or first-party source actually established. “No current competent decree located” means precisely that; it is not a declaration of illegality. `community-census.md` records the row-level audit.

## Governing doctrinal distinctions

- **Rite-level orthodoxy and validity:** both officially promulgated Roman Missals confess and enact the Catholic Eucharistic sacrifice and are capable of valid celebration.
- **Act-level validity:** matter, sacramental form, valid minister, intention to do what the Church does, and a capable subject where relevant must converge. A valid rite does not guarantee that every attempted celebration is valid.
- **Liceity:** valid celebrations can be unlawful; unlawfulness does not automatically erase sacramental reality.
- **Canonical communion:** a validly ordained minister may act outside full communion or without authorization. Valid Orders or Eucharist do not establish jurisdiction, faculties, or ecclesial mission.
- **Fruitfulness:** objective sacramental action and subjective reception are distinct without being separable. Valid Communion can be received sacrilegiously.
- **Authorization, permission, and preference:** a practice may be valid yet unauthorized; authorized yet pastorally unwise in a concrete setting; or lawful without being obligatory. The paper never substitutes aesthetic preference for any of these categories.

## Included scope

1. Pre-Tridentine Roman diversity and the immediate printed antecedents of 1570.
2. Trent's actual liturgical acts, Pius V's commission and *Quo primum*, the two-hundred-year exception, and a documented distinction between directly displaced younger Roman uses, protected older uses, later voluntary abandonment, and extinction through other causes.
3. Development from 1570 through the 1962 typical edition.
4. The preconciliar movement, Vatican II's exact mandates and limits, Consilium, promulgation, transition, translations, and later typical editions.
5. Structural and theological comparison of the two normative witnesses, including Scripture, offertory, Eucharistic prayer, participation, language, orientation, music, calendar, ministries, options, and ars celebrandi.
6. A seven-sacrament validity matrix covering the 1962 and revised Roman rites, common invalidating defects, faculties and canonical form, and the status of sacraments celebrated by irregular ministers.
7. Current Eucharistic discipline, including preparation, posture, reception on the tongue or in the hand, Communion under both kinds, self-communication, intinction, extraordinary ministers of Holy Communion, and abuse-versus-invalidity distinctions.
8. The legal sequence from 1971 through *Traditionis custodes*, its Responsa and rescript, particular FSSP provision, and the 2026 SSPX endpoint.
9. A bounded census of located communities with canonical-status qualification and explicit uncertain or separated categories.
10. A pastoral decision framework and integrated timeline.

## Excluded or bounded scope

- This is not a word-for-word parallel Missal, a ceremonial manual, or an authorization to celebrate either edition.
- It does not reproduce copyrighted vernacular liturgical books. Reproduced liturgical wording comes from identified received witnesses; surrounding English is analytical summary, not recitation text.
- It does not decide whether an individual minister has valid orders, faculties, supplied jurisdiction, or a valid marriage delegation; those are fact-specific canonical questions.
- It does not infer a community's current permission from a former *Ecclesia Dei* relationship, a website's continued schedule, or its canonical erection alone.
- It does not count every TLM location in the world. A Mass-location directory answers a different question from the corporate census requested here.
- It does not declare every member or attendee of a separated body excommunicated. The competent acts and formal-adherence criteria are reported without judging subjective culpability.
- It does not present Communion in the hand as intrinsically irreverent or ordinary lay distribution as the ideal. It gives the exact present law and then assesses sign, necessity, and prudence within that law.
