# Advent in the postconciliar Roman books

The study assumes a Catholic household using the diocesan U.S. calendar. Its
liturgical horizon is the Roman Missal, third typical edition, the U.S.
Lectionary for Mass, second typical edition, and the U.S. Liturgy of the Hours,
vol. I. The reader can start during any Advent. The weekday series and the
twelve Sunday entries supply the Bible references. The contemplative exercises
belong to the household. A local proper calendar can change the celebration.

## Why give each edition a handbook?

The reasonable alternative is one history and book of Advent devotions with two
calendar inserts. It serves readers following only the O Antiphons or the
history of the Incarnation. Test it by asking what a person preparing for the
actual Mass and Office needs to know.

| Test at the books | 1962 Missal and Breviary | Postconciliar Missal, U.S. Lectionary and Hours | Consequence for the reader |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The beginning of Advent | Rom 13:11–14a and Lk 21:25–33 each First Sunday. The opening prayer asks Christ to stir up his power and come. | Year A pairs Romans with Matthew; B has Mark, C has Luke. The collect asks the Father to give his people the will to meet Christ with righteous deeds. | A meditation on one book's pairing or collect will miss words actually proclaimed in the other. |
| An Advent weekday | The Sunday Mass ordinarily recurs on a feria without its own Mass. Ember Wednesday after Gaudete has the sign to Ahaz and the Annunciation. | Annual weekday cycle, some proper days and options. Isaiah and the Annunciation come together December 20 when the weekday is celebrated. Ember and Rogation observances are entrusted to episcopal conferences under Universal Norms 45–47. | A general list of pious readings cannot simultaneously identify both appointments. |
| Gaudete through the Nativity | Annual Gospel on the Baptist, three Ember Masses, and the Vigil of the Nativity on December 24, even when it displaces Advent IV. | Three distinct Third Sunday Gospels, eight date-keyed weekdays subject to Sunday precedence, Advent IV when December 24 is Sunday; Christmas Vigil in the evening. | An integrated calendar would branch several times within a single week; it could send someone to the wrong Mass on Christmas Eve. |
| Prayer after Mass | Breviary Matins and its lessons and responsories; Magnificat at Vespers. | Office of Readings with a scriptural and a patristic or ecclesiastical lesson in its ordinary course, Morning and Evening Prayer as the principal Hours; O Antiphons with the Magnificat. | Mary's song can be prayed together at home, but finding a Father's text and following the ecclesial day require different books. |

The publication decision is two independent handbooks with the same historical,
theological and devotional standard. Common ancient witnesses can have one
source record and inform both editions. Their force within the particular
liturgy is explained in each book. No demographic or spiritual judgment about
one congregation or the other follows from this comparison. See the
[controlling witnesses](source-audit.md), the [U.S. readings](lectionary-audit.md)
and the [historical witnesses](tradition-audit.md).

## Teaching sequence

The centurion gives the beginner a way to pray before opening a calendar.
The history distinguishes the first evidence of the feast from the development
of a season. The spiritual writers teach the two comings, grace and charity.
The four collects and the morning and evening canticles teach how a word
returns in another part of the liturgy. Sunday and weekday chapters lead from
each appointment into a received prayer. The O titles are read beside their
biblical antecedents. The final chapter follows the Vigil genealogy to the
night, dawn and day Masses. The comparison, discipline and references follow
the devotional course.
