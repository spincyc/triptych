# Reading Advent in the parish and at home

A fresh reader who had taken no part in the manuscript read the complete
generated web edition, its nine chapters and twenty-one footnotes, in order.
They then tried the third Sunday falling on December 17, Sunday morning and
evening on December 24, and Wednesday of the second week. The first two tests
reached the Sunday Mass, Wisdom at Vespers on the 17th, and the Christmas
Vigil on the evening of the 24th. The midweek test reached Lectionary 183,
Matthew 11:28–30, Teresa's *Life* 9.5 and the group's printed prayer.

| Reading difficulty | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| The chapter offered a Latin name to pray but gave several titles only in English. | All seven evenings now supply the Latin invocation beside its English sense. |
| A parishioner can hear Luke at the Christmas Vigil, after expecting Matthew from the threshold chapter. | The chapter now describes the choice, for pastoral reasons, among the Christmas readings. It continues to distinguish the proper Vigil, night, dawn and day sets. |
| The Douay numbering in a home Bible could make the December 23 Malachi references difficult to find. | The reading lists identify the U.S. Lectionary numbering and the December 23 entry gives the Douay parallel. |
| The account of Christmas Eve assumed the priest proclaimed the Gospel and divided the hearers of the genealogy by sex. | The narrative now fits a parish with a deacon and invites the whole assembly to hear the names of both Abraham and Ruth. |
| The closing meditation could return to a Father and make the wonderful exchange concrete. | Leo's Nativity Sermon 21.2–3 now returns at the crib. The reader considers Christ's gift of his own life in Baptism. |

The reader found the historical distinction between the St Gall witness and
the seven Roman dates intelligible. Cyril's garments, Bernard's bread and
Teresa's empty bucket conveyed their respective arguments.
