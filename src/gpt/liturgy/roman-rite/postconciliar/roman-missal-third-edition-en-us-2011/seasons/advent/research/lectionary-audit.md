# U.S. Advent readings and home course

This audit covers the four Sunday sections and the weekday course. The U.S. *Lectionary for Mass*, second typical edition, supplies the reading appointments. The 2011 English U.S. *Roman Missal*, third edition, governs the celebration. The Douay–Rheims supplies four prayers for home. None of the modern lectionary translations, refrains or Missal prayers is reproduced.

In no. 175 Isaiah 4:2–6 may be used in Year A when Isaiah 2:1–5 was actually read on the preceding Sunday. Isaiah 2 remains the principal reading. The U.S. daily pages for both selections print the complete five-strophe Psalm 122. The Latin OLM marks a single parenthesized *block* within its Psalm 121 at no. 175. These U.S. daily pages provide no corresponding short-form rubric. The handbook gives the complete U.S. strophes. No. 197 allows Zephaniah 3:14–18a in place of Song 2:8–14 in any cycle. Nos. 7 and 178 offer an Alleluia response to the same psalm. Nos. 192 and 201 contain weekday Gospel-acclamation materials for the respective parts of Advent. They do not supply missing Saturday or December 25 readings. The rubric before no. 193 permits the readings of a date displaced by Sunday to be anticipated or taken later in the week, particularly to avoid duplication. The U.S. Archdiocese of Louisville's 2016 seasonal directives apply that rubric to Sunday, December 18. [Archdiocese of Louisville, “Advent 2016,” December 18 entry](https://archlou.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Advent-2016-for-worship.pdf)

The *Introduction to the Lectionary* 65–69, 93–94 explains the cycles, the themes of each Advent Sunday, the early Isaiah series, the Gospels about John from the second Thursday, and Matthew 1 and Luke 1 in the last days. The local calendar and the working English U.S. Lectionary control American verse divisions. In particular, U.S. no. 2 cites Isaiah 64:2–7, no. 188 cites Psalm 34:6–7 within its second strophe, and no. 200 includes 2 Samuel 7:12. The source records for the Latin OLM and the U.S. annual-calendar PDFs serve distinct purposes here. [Bishops’ Conference of England and Wales, Introduction 93–94](https://www.cbcew.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/05/Lectionary-Mass-Introduction.pdf)

## Occurrence and feasts

The *Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year and the Calendar* 5, 11, 39–42, 59–60 and the Table of Liturgical Days give the seasonal boundaries, rank and occurrence rules. The *General Instruction of the Roman Missal* 355a, 357–358 and 362 gives the place of the memorial collect and the normal use of weekday readings, truly proper readings of saints and other pastoral options. The local calendar can raise a patron or dedication to a solemnity. On a Sunday the U.S. calendar observes Advent I, as in 2025. November 30 can also precede Advent. For each of the following seven openings, every day from Advent I to the morning of December 24 was matched to the printed Lectionary number in the designated USCCB calendar. The checks covered 175 civil dates and exercised all 40 sets in the chapters: twelve Sundays, seventeen recurring weekdays, eight dates and three fixed celebrations. The week-three column describes possible temporal positions before the independent precedence of feasts. A separate pass through the Gregorian cycle, 2000–2399, found the same seven shapes. ## Home readings, editions and rights

The selections and acts of charity in the home course are editorial. References to writers are beside the corresponding days or in the short rule for their week. The four prayer quotations were checked against the held text of the Challoner Douay–Rheims, Project Gutenberg 1581: Psalm 24:4b, Psalm 84:8, Luke 1:38 and Luke 1:46–47. The U.S. psalm equivalents in the Sunday chapter are 25:4 and 85:8. The fragments begin or end where the worshipper's words begin or end. The same held Challoner text gives the plea in U.S. Isaiah 63:19b at 64:1 and the clay of U.S. 64:7 at 64:8, the Bethlehem oracle of U.S. Micah 5:1–4a at Micheas 5:2–5a, and U.S. Malachi 3:23–24 at Malachias 4:5–6. For Guadalupe, U.S. Zechariah 2:14–17 corresponds to Zacharias 2:10–13, and the blessing in Judith 13:18bcde, 19 is found within Douay 13:23–25. Judith is given as a place to find the blessing in the Vulgate tradition, without assuming word-for-word agreement with the Greek. Douay 1 and 2 Kings are 1 and 2 Samuel in the U.S. text. The Luke prayers keep their verse numbers. The modern U.S. biblical text remains with its rights holder. [Douay–Rheims, Gutenberg 1581](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1581)
