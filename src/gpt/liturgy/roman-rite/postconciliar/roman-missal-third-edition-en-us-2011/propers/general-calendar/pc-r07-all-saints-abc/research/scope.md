## Consequential questions and disagreements

1. Clean robes in blood (Revelation7:14) are not a claim that suffering cleanses
   automatically. Victorinus9 locates baptism in the Lamb's blood and keeping
   grace; Bede14 explicitly extends the cleansing beyond martyrs. The cause
   and agent of purification remain Christ's saving blood. The exact scope
   of “great tribulation” retains Bede's final-persecution emphasis, without
   dating a future persecution.
2. The number144,000 and the unnumbered multitude are not two arithmetic
   capacities for salvation. Bede4 gives symbolic ecclesial fullness; Bede9
   also permits an Israel/Gentiles sequence. Preserve both options, and
   preserve the tribal list as context omitted by the appointment.
3. “Now” sons, yet “not yet” manifested (1John3:2), and “sanctifieth himself”
   (3:3) create a real problem of agency and completion. AugustineIV.5–7
   answers by real present adoption, future vision and willing dependence on
   God's help. Aquinas87340 adds that moral acts dispose toward a happiness
   consummated in vision; they are not themselves the full vision.
4. AugustineI.2.4 reads the Beatitude's earth as stable eternal inheritance;
   ChrysostomXV.5 argues for a sensible earthly reward as well as future gifts.
   Aquinas87340 reports Chrysostom, then prefers a future interpretation and
   lists alternatives. These are real exegetical differences. Do not credit
   Chrysostom with rejecting all future reward or Augustine with teaching that
   holiness has no present effect.
5. AugustineI.2.9 begins peacemaking with ordered desires; ChrysostomXV.7
   requires reconciling other people. Aquinas87340 explicitly includes both.
   These are complementary emphases, not rival definitions of Christian charity.
6. ChrysostomXV.7–9 conditions persecution on justice, Christ and false
   accusation; merely being criticized or injured is insufficient. His
   discussion of earlier prophets goes beyond the appointed12a endpoint and
   must be cited as commentary/context, not added to the Gospel text.
