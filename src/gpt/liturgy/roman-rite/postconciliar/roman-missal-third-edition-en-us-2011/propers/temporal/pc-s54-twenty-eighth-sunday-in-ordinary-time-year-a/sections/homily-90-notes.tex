\section{Source and delivery note}
This homily addresses an adult parish assembly for the Twenty-eighth Sunday in Ordinary Time, Year A, 11 October 2026, in the U.S. Roman Missal, Third Edition; Lectionary no. 142. Its argument draws on the reviewed studies' reading, ``The feast sustains those who still hunger.'' The decisive turn is Paul's commendation of help in Philippians 4:14 immediately after his affirmation of strength in Christ. Chrysostom explains that turn; Thomas Aquinas's distinction between the Church's banquet and heaven supports the Eucharistic and future horizon. Practical examples are applications, not reports of events.

The speech uses Matthew 22:1--10, common to both authorized Gospel forms. It neither assumes proclamation of the garment scene nor selects a Communion antiphon. The Collect and Prayer after Communion are explained by subject, without supplying substitute prayer texts. Biblical wording is original exposition from the public-domain Douay--Rheims/Challoner study witness, not a reproduction of the approved U.S. Lectionary. The speech contains no recited prayer or attributed direct quotation.

Spoken body: \textbf{1,208 words}; estimated \textbf{10.1--11.0 minutes} at 110--120 words per minute. The prose was silently reread for oral sense, sentence length, transitions and the return to the opening table image. No audible rehearsal or timed human delivery is claimed. This is authored Catholic preaching prose, not an official liturgical text or evidence of ecclesiastical approval.
