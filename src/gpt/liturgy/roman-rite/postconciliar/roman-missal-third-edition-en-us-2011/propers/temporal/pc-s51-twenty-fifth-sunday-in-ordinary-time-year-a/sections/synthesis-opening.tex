The first workers receive their promised wage. Their disappointment begins when they see the last workers receive as much. The vineyard exposes a desire that can survive faithful service: the desire for another person's good to be smaller than our own. Isaiah's call to return, Paul's willingness to remain for others, and the Mass's petitions for obedient love all address that cramped measure of goodness.

Augustine asks what God's gift makes possible in the receiver. Chrysostom asks how a person called early or late must respond now. Gregory the Great asks whose vineyard the worker has entered. Together these readings lead from generosity received to charity practiced. Their accounts of the vineyard, hours, and payment differ, however, and those differences disclose distinct dimensions of the hope offered.
