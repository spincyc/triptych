## Historical orientation

- **Psalms:** Psalm 28’s inherited title names David; Psalm 69’s title assigns the lament to Davidic tradition; Psalm 145 is an acrostic praise psalm bearing a Davidic title. These headings shape reception but do not securely fix autograph place or date.
- **Jeremiah:** the book locates Jeremiah’s ministry in Judah and Jerusalem from the late seventh into the early sixth century BC. Chapter 20 follows his beating and confinement by Pashhur. The book’s final literary formation reflects later transmission and editing; exact stages remain debated.
- **Matthew:** ancient tradition names the Apostle Matthew and a Palestinian Jewish-Christian audience; the modern majority dates the canonical Greek Gospel about AD 80–90, often proposing Antioch without certainty. Jesus’ discourse belongs to the Galilean ministry about AD 28–29, with no exact speaking site preserved.
- **John:** tradition associates the Gospel with the Apostle John and Ephesus late in the first century; modern composition judgments vary. John 15 locates the acclamation’s source in Jesus’ final-supper discourse in Jerusalem about AD 29–30; John 10 belongs to the Jerusalem ministry.
- **Romans:** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to Roman house churches. Romans 5 develops Adam–Christ typology after the proclamation of justification and hope.

## Reception used

### Direct reception

- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 34 on Matthew*, directly treats Matthew 10:26–33. He reads the sparrows and numbered hairs as assurance that nothing is hidden from divine knowledge or outside providential care, while expressly refusing to infer that God positively causes every fall: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/200134.htm>.
- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 10 on Romans*, directly treats Romans 5:12–15. Adam and Christ are not equal measures: the abundance of grace and gift exceeds the trespass and its consequences: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210210.htm>.
- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 69*, directly receives the afflicted psalm in relation to Christ and his body: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801069.htm>. This is reception of the psalm, not proof that Augustine knew Lectionary 94.

### Doctrinal and liturgical illumination

- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 302–314 illuminates creation’s journey under providence, divine action through secondary causes, and the mystery of evil. It prevents a simplistic inference that every injury is positively willed by God.
- *Catechism* 402–412 and the Council of Trent, Session V, illuminate Romans 5’s Adam–Christ framework while keeping the apostolic text primary.
- GIRM 46–90 and 352–367 and the *General Introduction to the Lectionary* 65–69, 78–91, and 93–110 govern liturgical function and relationship labels. *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 supplies a Sunday Preface unless the selected authorized Eucharistic Prayer carries its own; GIRM 365(b) preserves that flexibility for Eucharistic Prayer II, while 365(d) makes Eucharistic Prayer IV and its invariable Preface one coupled selection.

No direct patristic commentary on the complete Week XII formulary is claimed. No direct early witness to the exact Jeremiah–Matthew pairing was retained; that relationship rests on official Lectionary structure.
