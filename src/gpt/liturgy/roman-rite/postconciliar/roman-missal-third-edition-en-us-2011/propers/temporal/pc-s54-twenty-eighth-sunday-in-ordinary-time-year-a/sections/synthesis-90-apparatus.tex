\section{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}
This concise study treats the Twenty-eighth Sunday in Ordinary Time, Year A, on 11 October 2026 in the U.S. national calendar: Roman Missal, Third Edition (2011), and Lectionary no. 142. The common Missal formulary belongs to Week 28; no local overlay or selection was supplied. Both Gospel lengths and both Missal Communion alternatives are treated. Permitted chant routes, sprinkling, Preface/Eucharistic Prayer choices and concluding blessings remain unselected; no unique Preparation chant, Preface, insertion, Sequence or concluding text is added. Ordinary Sunday Gloria, Creed, homily and Universal Prayer retain their places (GIRM 48, 51, 53, 61--71, 74, 87--90, 360, 364--365).

The Old Testament--Gospel correlation differs from the semi-continuous apostolic course; Week 28's Missal prayers serve all three Sunday cycles (General Introduction to the Lectionary 66--68, 106--107). The two whole-formulary interpretations are editorial syntheses of the accepted expansive study; each witness is cited for his own passage. Four-sense language extends to composed prayers by theological analogy. The bounded source inquiry ends at 6 October 2026, and is not an exhaustive reception history.

Scriptural English used for study is the public-domain Douay--Rheims/Challoner witness, not the approved proclaimed English: the U.S. Lectionary controls the celebration. Restricted Missal and Lectionary bodies are neither reproduced nor reconstructed. Oration subjects are explanations, not substitute prayers. Common-formulary evidence remains bounded: the inspected Latin is a secondary 2002 reproduction; the official Antiphonary and FDLC excerpts cover their respective elements; the 2008 variation list is selective. Exact publisher-specific 2008/2011 altar-book collation and an exact U.S. English Prayer over the Offerings exemplar remain unavailable. FDLC's Collect conclusion predates current U.S. wording.

Gregory and Thomas are cited from Latin digital witnesses; Augustine and Chrysostom from historical NPNF English. The Psalm exposition is abridged, and no new critical-language collation is claimed. The chronology preserves distinct attributed settings, events and composition hypotheses, not contemporary unanimity. This is a Catholic study aid, not an official liturgical book or an ecclesiastically approved commentary.
