## Historical orientation

- **Psalms:** Psalm 17 bears a Davidic prayer heading; Psalm 65 is a communal hymn of praise for divine visitation and harvest; Psalm 84 belongs to the Korahite pilgrimage collection. The headings and cultic associations orient reception but do not prove autograph sites or dates. Final-form dates remain uncertain.
- **Isaiah 55:** traditional attribution receives the whole book under Isaiah. Modern Catholic study commonly places chapters 40–55 in the Babylonian exile near its end, about 540 BC, speaking consolation to displaced Judah through an anonymous prophetic voice. Exact speaker, place, and compositional stages remain debated.
- **Matthew:** ancient tradition identifies Matthew the Apostle writing for Jewish believers in Palestine; the modern majority dates the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, commonly c. AD 80–90, with Antioch plausible but unproved. The narrated scene is at the Sea of Galilee: Jesus sits in a boat while crowds stand on shore; exact shoreline and civil date are not supplied.
- **John:** ancient tradition identifies the Apostle and the Ephesian church horizon; modern judgments about authorship and compositional stages vary, with a late first-century final form commonly proposed. John 6 locates the bread discourse in the Capernaum synagogue after the feeding and crossing of the sea.
- **Romans:** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to mixed Jewish and Gentile churches at Rome. The appointed verses personify creation’s expectation and labor while believers await bodily redemption.

## Reception used

### Direct reception

- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 14 on Romans*, directly treats Romans 8:18–23: present suffering and future glory, creation’s personification, subjection in hope, labor toward incorruption, firstfruits, and bodily liberation: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210214.htm>.
- St. Augustine, *Tractates on John* 26.11–18, directly receives John 6’s eating, abiding, ecclesial unity, and life language. It illuminates Communion Antiphon B only if that branch supplies the printed antiphon: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701026.htm>.

Both were previously source-audited in repository records. Chrysostom expounds the appointed second reading; Augustine expounds the source of an optional Communion antiphon. Neither is presented as commentary on the complete Week XV/Year A formulary.

### Doctrinal and liturgical illumination

- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 541–550, already used in the repository’s Luminous Mysteries study, illuminates Christ’s Kingdom disclosed through parables, conversion, signs, and communion.
- The repository’s Eucharist treatise and its audited sources govern the limited treatment of John 6 and sacramental abiding; the Communion alternative is not expanded into a substitute Eucharistic treatise.
- GIRM 46–90 and 352–367 and the *General Introduction to the Lectionary* 65–69, 78–91, and 93–110 govern liturgical function, chant choices, official correlation, and the semi-continuous apostolic course.

No repository-audited direct patristic commentary on Isaiah 55:10–11 or Matthew 13:1–23 was imported. The guide treats them through close canonical exegesis and the official Lectionary relationship rather than an invented reception claim.

## Branch and pastoral limits

- The long and short Gospels are mutually exclusive proclamation paths. The guide treats both but never narrates both as one enacted Mass.
- The shorter form ends after the summons to hear. It omits the disciples’ question, the Isaiah citation, the beatitude, and Jesus’ interpretation; branch-specific claims identify this absence.
- The official page shows one Psalm response and no alternative refrain.
- The permitted Sunday sprinkling rite may replace the Penitential Act; this unresolved ritual substitution is not a proper-text row.
- Actual music, Preface, Eucharistic Prayer, and Communion-antiphon selection remain unknown. `compatible-preface-and-eucharistic-prayer` follows Ordinary Time rubric 5; `eucharistic-prayer-iv-with-preface` separately records GIRM 365(d)'s inseparable EP IV Preface and exclusion from a proper-Preface pairing. There is no Missal Offertory antiphon.
- Week XV ferias use independent weekday Cycle II in 2026. Ferial selection of Week XV orations is possible but not automatic Sunday spillover.
- The soil imagery must not be used to assign fixed spiritual castes, blame trauma or disability for weak response, or declare living persons irredeemably barren. Matthew describes real obstacles and fruit while the Gospel call itself seeks conversion.
