\section{Appendix: Liturgical Resolution}

The First Sunday of Advent in the 2027 liturgical year falls on 29 November 2026 in the United States. The color is violet, the Gloria is omitted and the Creed is said. The First Sunday prayers serve the Sunday cycles in common. The U.S. \textit{Lectionary for Mass}, vol.~I, no.~2, appoints the Year B readings. There is one Mass without a separate vigil set. The Sunday directs the priest to Preface I of Advent. Its use depends also on the Eucharistic Prayer's provisions. Eucharistic Prayer IV has an invariable Preface.

The Missal antiphons and Psalm 80 are the basis for the comparison. The General Instruction of the Roman Missal (GIRM 48, 51, 61--63, 74 and 87) permits other approved chants and the Sunday sprinkling. When the psalm is sung, the assembly may sing the seasonal response (Lectionary no.~173) with Psalm 80. It may also sing either of the two common Advent psalms, Psalm 25 (24) or Psalm 85 (84), each with its own response (no.~174). The first of these psalms asks for instruction in God's paths. The second includes the verses about truth and fruit discussed above. The acclamation and the printed Communion antiphon retain their own places whichever psalm is sung. The U.S. Missal prints no Offertory antiphon for this Sunday. The wreath blessing, if celebrated within Mass, takes place at the close of the intercessions (U.S. \textit{Book of Blessings}, nos.~1509, 1517--1520). The Advent solemn blessing is an option at the dismissal. Local choices are unknown.

\section{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}

The U.S. Lectionary supplies the authorized English for proclamation. Biblical study wording follows the public-domain Douay--Rheims Bible, Challoner revision, Gutenberg 1581. In psalm citations the number in parentheses is Vulgate, the other Hebrew. The U.S. opening of Mark 13:33 lacks Challoner's additional instruction to pray. In verse 34 the U.S. Gospel specifies an assignment for each servant. In the subsequent garden scene Jesus expressly calls his disciples to prayer. The modern Missal prayers and approved English liturgical texts are reserved. The U.S. altar book was available in a third-party digital copy and the Latin third edition in its 2002 printing. The 2008 list of corrections records no change here. The revised Latin altar book was unavailable for complete collation.

The ancient and later commentators explain the individual Scriptures indicated below. The comparisons between them and the prayers at Mass are the interpretations proposed here. The bishops' commentary concerns the modern prayers directly. O'Sullivan's historical English Bellarmine abridges some philological discussion. Chrysostom and Cyril are cited through historical English translations. Jerome's Isaiah and Bede's Mark follow the Latin wording identified at the passages.
