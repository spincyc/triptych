## Relationship classification

- 1 Kings 3 and Matthew 13 are **officially correlated** by the Lectionary's Old Testament/Gospel arrangement. Solomon's request concerns discerning governance; the Gospel presents decisive recognition of the Kingdom's value and, in the long form, discernment at the consummation. The relation does not make Solomon a reader of Matthew's parables or reduce either passage to a generic lesson in cleverness.
- Psalm 119 is **responsorial** to 1 Kings. Its love of divine instruction, preference for it over wealth, and request for understanding answer Solomon's approved choice without becoming another narrative scene.
- The acclamation is **acclamatory** and comes from Matthew 11, not Matthew 13. Receptive humility prepares hearing but is not an extra verse in either Gospel branch.
- Romans 8:28–30 is **semi-continuous**. Its chain of divine purpose and conformity to the Son must be interpreted in its Pauline context, not represented as selected to explain the treasure, pearl, or net.
- Communion Antiphons A and B are **mutually exclusive appointed alternatives** within the Eucharistic action. Their scriptural contexts and the unresolved music layer remain distinct.
- The Week XVII orations and antiphons are **shared A/B/C Missal material**. They can be synthesized with the Year A readings for this guide, but no claim is made that they were composed for Lectionary no. 109.
