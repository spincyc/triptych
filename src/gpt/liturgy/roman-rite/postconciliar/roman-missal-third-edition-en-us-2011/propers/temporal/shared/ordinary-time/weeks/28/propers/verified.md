## Rights and remaining collation

The governing edition is the 2008 emended third typical Latin edition and the
2011 U.S. English third edition. The inspected 2002 reproduction establishes
neither exact 2008 wording nor publisher-specific U.S. 2011 page locations.
The research stage inspected the official 2008 variation notice: *Notitiae*
44 (2008), nos. 503–504, printed pp. 367–387, especially p. 371,
*Tempus per annum* (physical PDF p. 53). The exact reacquired artifact is
`artifact.holy-see.notitiae.volume-44-numbers-503-504-2008.official-pdf-74c8d174`,
SHA-256 `74c8d1740284984d0f4d64b2223edf805dadfa55aec8a92f8cfba9ab901eca0f`.
Its introduction and complete Ordinary Time correction block were read in
page images. No Week XXVIII alteration is listed there. The introduction
selects changes relevant to translators; absence is not proof that every
2008 letter, accent, punctuation mark and layout matches 2002. The governing
2008 edition remains cataloged, without an exact inspected altar-book artifact.

A second search on 2026-10-06 checked the official USCCB/publisher web
surface for Week XXVIII offerings and the 2008 formulary identifier. It
yielded no accessible exact U.S. 2011 altar-book page or complete 2008
exemplar. David Schütz’s 2011 discussion at
<https://scecclesia.com/archives/5852> supplies a secondary English
Prayer over the Offerings and a Latin transcription with an apparent
`Us`/`Ut` error. It corroborates the subject but is not an official
exemplar and supplies neither text authority nor a publication license.
No text from that page is adopted. The English offerings and exact
2008/2011 printed-page collation gaps therefore remain explicit.

The FDLC Collect has the pre-2021 English conclusion. The current U.S.
[GIRM 54](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-2),
opened 2026-10-06, gives the current conclusion; the old exemplar must not be
represented as the present complete recitation text. No new translation is
supplied. The FDLC reflection's ancient-source statements are leads awaiting
independent verification, not evidence of exact derivation.

Under the postconciliar study profile, retained text routes are public-domain
Douay–Rheims biblical study passages, clearly distinguished from antiphon
adaptations; minimal Latin identifiers; and original explanation. Protected
Missal and Lectionary bodies are not copied into the study or its source tree.
The source library's artifact-specific rights remain controlling; the ICEL
web permission does not authorize these downloadable studies. Historical or
project-created translations in `postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml`
are not adopted as the target's approved English. Research must evaluate any
ancient antecedent independently without importing another family's formulary.
