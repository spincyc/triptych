## Historical orientation

- **1 Kings:** the appointed scene is Solomon's dream at Gibeon near the beginning of his reign. The final author, date, and place are not named by the book; modern accounts commonly discuss a longer Deuteronomistic history and later editorial formation, but no single reconstruction is made a premise of the guide. The request for a listening or discerning heart belongs first to Israel's account of royal responsibility and judgment.
- **Psalms:** Psalm 119 is an alphabetic Torah meditation whose individual author and exact date remain unknown. Psalms 67 and 102 carry inherited Davidic headings in the received tradition; those headings orient reception without settling modern questions of compositional history. Their Israelite praise, instruction, and remembrance retain their own literal horizons.
- **Romans:** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to churches at Rome containing both Jewish and Gentile believers. Romans 8:28–30 belongs to a sustained account of life in the Spirit, suffering, hope, intercession, divine purpose, and inseparability from divine love. Its sequence does not call evil good or erase human agency.
- **Matthew:** ancient tradition identifies Matthew the Apostle and a Jewish-Christian audience; the modern majority commonly places the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, often c. AD 80–90, with Antioch plausible but unproved. Matthew 11 and Matthew 13 are distinct narrated moments in the Galilean ministry. Matthew 13:44–52 closes a parable discourse with treasure and pearl, and only in the long branch continues through net, separation, understanding, and the householder's new and old.

### Reception controls

- CCC 121–123 and 128–130 present the Old Testament as an indispensable and permanently valuable part of Christian Scripture.
- Matthew 13:52 presents new and old as treasures brought forth together. Jesus, the first disciples, Israel's Scriptures, and the Gospel's originating world are Jewish.
- Romans 8:28 does not rename human wrongdoing as a good, excuse an offender, silence lament, or prevent safeguarding, reporting, restitution, treatment, resistance, or justice. The checked reception concerns God's capacity to work toward the good amid real adversity.

### Doctrinal and liturgical illumination

- CCC 121–123 and 128–130 govern the enduring value of the Old Testament, the unity of Scripture, and attention to Israel's covenantal and historical horizon.
- CCC 541–546 illuminates the Kingdom's presence, demand for conversion, and disclosure through parables; CCC 546 is especially relevant to the hearer's response to the treasure and pearl.
- *Gaudium et spes* 22 places conformity to the Son in the incarnate and paschal mystery; CCC 311–312 states both that God is not the cause of moral evil and that Providence can bring good from evil's consequences without making evil good.
- GIRM 46–90 and 352–367 and the *General Introduction to the Lectionary* 65–69, 78–91, and 93–110 govern liturgical function, chant choices, first-reading/Gospel harmony, the Psalm's responsorial role, and Romans' semi-continuity.
- Ferraro's signed *Notitiae* study traces the Week XVII Collect's protector language to Vulgate Psalm 17, its dependence to John 15, its ruler-and-guide titles to wider biblical usage and Exodus, and its passing/enduring contrast to 1 Corinthians 7. His study explicitly preserves work within the world and the value of creatures in Christ. It is scholarly illumination published in a Dicastery journal, not a magisterial act or proof of the prayer's original sources.
- The Collect, the offering prayer's return of gifts received from divine bounty, and the postcommunion's Passion memorial may be synthesized with the appointed readings. They are reusable A/B/C Missal texts and are not proved to have been composed for Lectionary no. 109.

## Branch, occurrence, and pastoral limits

- Long and short Gospel forms are mutually exclusive. The short form ends after the treasure and pearl; only the long form adds the net, final separation, understanding, and the scribe or householder who brings out new and old.
- The official occurrence page shows one Psalm response and no alternate refrain. Its wording is protected and is not reproduced here.
- The permitted Sunday sprinkling rite may replace the Penitential Act; this unresolved ritual substitution is not a proper-text row.
- Actual Entrance, Offertory, and Communion music, Preface, Eucharistic Prayer, and printed Communion-antiphon selection remain unknown. `compatible-preface-and-eucharistic-prayer` follows Ordinary Time rubric 5; `eucharistic-prayer-iv-with-preface` separately records GIRM 365(d)'s inseparable EP IV Preface. There is no Missal Offertory antiphon or proper Eucharistic Prayer insert.
- The Memorial of Saints Joachim and Anne is omitted on this 2026 Sunday without commemoration or transfer. It creates no authorized leaf branch and contributes no proper, reading, or title.
- Week XVII ferias use independent weekday Cycle II readings in 2026. The Monday beginning 2026-07-27 does not inherit Sunday's Year A readings, Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.
- The treasure and pearl do not teach that grace can be bought, that property is intrinsically evil, or that a person may abandon dependents or just obligations. The net's final separation belongs to divine judgment; it does not authorize labeling named living persons “bad fish,” assuming their damnation, or mistreating them.
- “All things work for good” must never be used to pressure a harmed person into silence, reconciliation without safety, or denial of evil. Pastoral use must preserve lament, prudence, competent reporting, medical care, civil and ecclesial justice, and the freedom and responsibility of human actors.
