## Historical orientation

- **Psalms:** the inherited titles associate Psalms 34 and 145 with David, while Psalm 48 belongs to the Korahite collection. Those headings orient reception but do not establish autograph places or dates. Modern historical dates and final-form settings remain uncertain; the guide therefore distinguishes traditional attribution from recoverable composition history.
- **Zechariah:** the book’s heading names Zechariah son of Berechiah in the Persian period. Modern Catholic introductions commonly distinguish chapters 9–14 from the dated oracles of chapters 1–8 and place their final collection later in the postexilic period; exact authorship, location, and date remain disputed. The reading addresses Daughter Zion and Jerusalem with a royal oracle whose peace reaches the nations.
- **Matthew:** ancient ecclesial tradition identifies the Apostle Matthew writing for Jewish believers in Palestine early in the apostolic mission. The modern majority dates the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, often around AD 80–90, with Antioch plausible but not certain. Matthew 11 locates Jesus’ thanksgiving and invitation within the Galilean ministry after sayings about the unbelief of named Galilean towns; no exact site or civil date is given.
- **Romans:** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to mixed Jewish and Gentile house churches at Rome. Romans 8 develops life in the Spirit after the argument about sin, law, and grace; the appointed verses omit 8:10 but must not be made to deny the body’s goodness or resurrection.

## Reception used

### Direct reception

- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 14 on Romans*, directly treats Romans 8:12–17, including the obligation not to live according to disordered flesh, the real warning attached to such life, and mortification of evil deeds rather than bodily substance: <https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210214.htm>.
- St. Augustine, *Sermon 156* §§6–15, directly treats Romans 8:12–17, grace-enabled action, filial life, and the boundary between servile and filial fear: <https://www.augustinus.it/latino/discorsi/discorso_201_testo.htm>.
- St. Thomas Aquinas, *Commentary on Romans* VIII, lect. 3, nos. 634–651, directly treats the relevant Pauline movement of mortification, Spirit-led life, adoption, and inheritance: <https://aquinas.cc/en/la/~Rom.C8.L3>.

These witnesses were already source-audited in the repository’s Eighth Sunday after Pentecost record. Their Romans loci support the appointed Pauline strand; none is presented as commentary on the postconciliar Week XIV formulary as a whole.

### Doctrinal and liturgical illumination

- *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 541–550, already used in the repository’s Luminous Mysteries study, governs the Kingdom as God’s saving action present in Christ and manifested in teaching, signs, parables, conversion, and communion. It illuminates the royal and Gospel language without replacing direct exegesis.
- GIRM 46–90 and 352–367 and the *General Introduction to the Lectionary* 65–69, 78–91, and 93–110 govern the liturgical functions, chant layers, Ordinary Time correlation, and semi-continuous apostolic series.

No repository-audited direct patristic exposition of Zechariah 9:9–10 or Matthew 11:25–30 was imported. Their detailed treatment therefore rests on close canonical exegesis, official Lectionary structure, and clearly labeled doctrinal illumination rather than an invented reception claim.

- The date identifies no enacted chant. No printed antiphon is described as sung, and no local hymn is invented.

- The guide does not confuse meekness with passivity in the face of abuse, injustice, illness, or duties of care. Christ’s yoke orders agency under charity; it does not forbid protection or proportionate action.
