### Augustine: God cultivates us and grants one eternal life

**Sermon 87 (Benedictine numbering), also NPNF New Testament Sermon 37**, §§1–2, 4–10, 14–15: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/160337.htm . R. G. MacMullen, NPNF I.6 (1888). DO NOT cite simply sermon 37 without the number equivalence.

- §§1–2: our service does not improve God; God's cultivation makes us better. His word ploughs the heart and plants the precepts; fruit benefits us, not God's wealth. This directly provides an unusually strong Gospel–acclamation–Collect–Communion Psalm bridge.
- §4: the equal payment is not defrauding the first; generosity to another need not diminish them. First/last here is equality of treatment, not an inversion of the order.
- §5: Abel/Noah, patriarchs, Moses/Aaron, prophets, Christians are called at different hours; all receive resurrection blessedness together. Later arrivals wait less time, hence may be called first to receive.
- §6: denarius = eternal life; equal endless duration coexists with different brightness/attainments. Do not turn equality into a denial of distinctions of glory.
- §§7–8: hours also describe infancy to old age; come when called, because a promised wage does not promise survival to the next hour.
- §9: Christ's going out means becoming manifest, finally in apostolic mission and Pentecost; God recruits through preaching.
- §10: both despair and presumption destroy; forgiveness is complete at conversion, but not an excuse to postpone it.
- §14: converts who persecuted Christ receive his Blood sacramentally; §15 applies conversion to Paul and urges active concern for friends. These form explicit sacramental and missionary links in the Father's own homily, beyond merely matching vineyard nouns.

**Enarratio in Psalmum 144** (Hebrew145), §§2–3, 7, 15–18: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801145.htm . URL ends 145, though the exposition's lemma is Vulgate144. J. E. Tweed, NPNF I.8 (1888). §7: pardon, justifying Spirit, charity enabling works, and eternal life are mercy; pardon is promised to conversion but tomorrow is not promised to delay. §15: God's justice is acknowledged in testing and healing. §16: to invoke God in truth is to desire God himself above his benefits. §18: mercy must not be converted into perseverance in sin. Direct treatment of appointed psalm verses, not merely doctrinal analogy.

**Enarratio in Psalmum 118, sermo4, §§1–3**, direct Latin checked at https://www.augustinus.it/latino/esposizioni_salmi/esposizione_salmo_148_testo.htm . **Important numbering correction:** NPNF8 abbreviates/renumbers as Psalm119, Aleph, §§4–6; do not cite that English numbering as Latin sermon2. Repository retained complete NPNF8 plain text at `src/sources/works/augustine/enarrationes-in-psalmos/editions/2026-09-05-english-npnf8-ccel-web-2026-09-05/artifacts/ccel-npnf108-text-d6841950/source.txt`, lines56775–56813. Direct on Communion Psalm119:4–5: nimis signifies very much, not culpable excess; the wish that ways be directed recognizes dependence on God's help. God must be asked to give what he commands. Latin §1 explicitly assigns the voice to a member of Christ or the whole body; §3 hearing must become doing. This supports corporate and sacramental interpretation as well as individual moral use.

**Enarratio in Psalmum36 [Hebrew37], third exposition, §16**, retained NPNF8 heading “On the Third Part of the Psalm,” lines10888–10900: direct Psalm37:39–40 reception for the Entrance's identified scriptural basis. Salvation and help in trouble lead Augustine to patience with sinners while awaiting final separation. Read §§14–16 in context: straighten the eye rather than judge God crooked because the wicked prosper. The saved endure trials; the Entrance does not promise immediate material relief. This is reception of the underlying verses, not evidence that Augustine explained the later composed antiphon.

**On the Predestination of the Saints I.41**: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/15121.htm . Direct Acts16:14 reuse: prayer for opened hearts shows the beginning of faith itself is divine gift. This complements Sermon 87's cultivation account. Distinguish this late anti-Pelagian emphasis from Chrysostom's cooperation language rather than claiming they word the question identically.

**Tractate on John 47.1–4**, especially §2: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701047.htm . John10:14–21. Christ enters hearts through the preaching of Christ; he bought the sheep with his Blood. At the Eucharistic table recipients are summoned to give themselves for their brethren as Christ did. Pasture is eternal life. §3: knowledge of the Father is through the Son. Direct Communion-antiphon context and explicit Eucharist-to-conduct argument.

### Chrysostom: wholehearted conversion, generosity, and willing service

**Homilies on Matthew 64.3–5**, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/200164.htm , with §§1–2 on Matthew19:27–30 read for context. NPNF I.10, George Prevost translation revised M. B. Riddle (check source footer when binding).

- §3: vineyard = commandments; working day = present life; different hours = ages at which persons come to God. Principal aim encourages those converted in extreme old age that they have no lesser portion.
- He expressly rejects envy among the saved. Their narrative complaint magnifies the extraordinary gift in the story; not every feature of a parable is separately predictive.
- §3: delayed calls are tied to readiness to obey; worker excuses cannot establish that God failed to call. §4 makes individual life-stages the principal interpretation and distinguishes the parable's equal reward from the closing maxim's actual reversal. He also permits a hint concerning Jews and changes in believers' faith/practice in that maxim. No explicit rejection of a Jewish–Gentile interpretation occurs in these checked sections; the earlier stronger claim is withdrawn.
- §5: life and practice matter; imitation should look to Christ and the virtuous, not other people's negligence. He concludes with almsgiving and self-examination, not passive assurance.

**Homilies on Philippians 3**, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/230203.htm , direct 1:20–21; full page retrieved. **Homily4**, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/230204.htm , direct 1:22–30. Hom4 opening and v27 exposition read. Paul is willing to postpone his own desired rest with Christ for others' progress. A life worthy of the Gospel, concord, and mercy are the practical result. Paul's choice is charitable service; do not preach it as hatred of embodied life or a warrant to hasten death. The discussion of 1:25–26 and 27b–30 is context, not extra proclaimed verses.

**Homilies on Acts 35**, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210135.htm , opening and recapitulation on16:13–15 read. God opens Lydia's heart; she attends. Chrysostom explicitly emphasizes both divine action and willing response, followed by hospitality as immediate fruit. That sequence helps connect acclamation, offerings, and conduct after Communion. Do not turn his incidental social description of Lydia's trade into established modern history.

### Isaiah: mercy exceeds the retaliatory mind, with transcendence retained

**Aquinas, Expositio super Isaiam ad litteram, cap.55**, Parma: Fiaccadori 1863, Opera omnia 14, printed 556–557: retained facsimile pages 2–3 of the title-and-Isaiah 55 excerpt, independently viewed with the 1863 title page. Source URL: https://archive.org/download/sanctithomaeaqui14thom/sanctithomaeaqui14thom.pdf . The complete commentary spans the two pages despite the second page's running head CAPUTLVI. The facsimile controls wording; the complete-volume OCR contains substantial recognition errors. Earlier modern lead https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/cis50.html , text 85015, was checked separately, but the Parma text is not called the 1969 Leonine text and inherits no Leonine paragraph number. The retained pages directly support urgency before adversity/death, offenses against God/neighbor, and mercy contrasted with human vengeance.

Aquinas places return from Babylon in the chapter's literal horizon and mystical gift of divine wisdom in its further reception. At55:6 he urges seeking before adversity/death. At55:7 he distinguishes offense against God and neighbor; return bears mercy. At55:8 he directly reads the contrast as humans planning revenge while God wills mercy. Excellent specific anchor against making this passage only a vague statement that God is unknowable. His reading is direct medieval exegesis of appointed verses.

**Irenaeus, Against Heresies II.13.3–4**, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103213.htm ; Roberts–Rambaut, ANF1 (1885/1887). Direct citation of Isaiah 55:8 in rejection of projecting human passions and separable mental processes into God. This is doctrinal reception of the verse, not continuous Isaiah commentary. Retain as short interleaved alternative: Aquinas stresses mercy's distance from vengeance; Irenaeus stresses the Creator's transcendence over creaturely psychology. They answer different questions; do not manufacture contradiction.

## Second-pass direct witnesses

**Jerome, Commentariorum in Isaiam prophetam libri duodeviginti, XV, on55:6–9**, [DCO Pars4 Latin digital PDF](https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/03d/0347-0420,_Hieronymus,_In_Isaiam_Pars_4,_LT.pdf), printed/PDF27–30. The complete offered228-page part (booksXV–XVIII) was acquired; only this locus and immediate55:10–11 continuation (pp30–32) were read. At6–7 Jerome urges seeking while still embodied, by faith rather than geographical movement, and abandoning former ways/thoughts. At8–9 he argues that the promised salvation of Jewish or Gentile sinners should not seem incredible: God's counsel remains stable where human plans change. The distinction in divine nature is retained, not reduced to psychology. At10–11 the effective promise continues the mercy argument. This provides direct ancient Isaiah commentary alongside, not in place of, Irenaeus's different doctrinal use. Modern typeset Latin, historical source edition unidentified, no image collation; use paraphrase only. Exact wrapper/body editorial rights remain unresolved, as stated in its canonical remote artifact record. No offline Latin source substitute is retained; citation/paraphrase is not a source artifact. This is not a complete eighteen-book acquisition.
