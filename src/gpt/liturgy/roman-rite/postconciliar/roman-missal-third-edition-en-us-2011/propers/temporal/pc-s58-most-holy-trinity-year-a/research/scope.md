## Question, editions, and exclusions

The project asks what an advanced lay reader should know before participating in this one U.S. Roman-rite Sunday formula: its appointed layers, biblical settings, inherited reception, doctrinal controls, liturgical movement, lawful branches, and unresolved local choices. The guide is keyed to `PC-S58-A`, the 2008 emended Latin third typical edition, the U.S. English Roman Missal implemented in 2011, U.S. Lectionary Volume I no. 164, the General Roman Calendar as adapted nationally, and the civil occurrence 2026-05-31.

The guide does not reconstruct a parish enactment; provide a worship aid; reproduce complete Missal or Lectionary texts; resolve diocesan or church-proper precedence; choose music, Penitential Act or sprinkling rite, intercessions, or Eucharistic Prayer; or provide a general treatise on the Trinity or Order of Mass. It treats ordinary structural units only where their status changes the formula.

## Direct scriptural inventory and historical coordinates

The historical dossier includes every direct biblical appointment or book-marked adaptation in Catholic canonical order. The composed Entrance Antiphon, orations, and Preface are analyzed liturgically but excluded from that Scripture inventory. The parenthetical Psalm 8 options printed in one musical antiphon witness are accompaniment suggestions, not direct sources of the Missal Entrance Antiphon and are therefore not promoted into this target.

### Exodus 34:4b–6, 8–9 — First Reading

- **Literary setting:** covenant renewal at Sinai after the golden calf. Moses ascends with replacement tablets; the Lord proclaims the divine name and qualities; Moses worships and asks God to accompany and pardon a stiff-necked people.
- **Narrated horizon:** Sinai in the wilderness, after Israel's deliverance from Egypt and before entry into Canaan. No exact historical year is asserted.
- **Composition:** the official [Exodus introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/exodus/0) treats Exodus 32–34 as the apostasy and covenant-renewal complex but does not supply a precise compositional date for this passage. The guide therefore preserves the traditional Mosaic narrative horizon while leaving final authorship, place, and date unresolved.
- **Control:** the proclamation reveals one God's mercy, fidelity, justice, and covenantal presence. It is not made into a hidden threefold-person proof merely because a liturgical reader can count repetitions.

### Daniel 3:52–56 — Responsorial Canticle

- **Textual status:** these verses belong to the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three, a Greek deuterocanonical addition placed between the Hebrew-Aramaic Daniel 3:23 and 3:24.
- **Narrated horizon:** three faithful Judeans bless God in the Babylonian furnace under Nebuchadnezzar, within a sixth-century-BC story world.
- **Composition:** the official [Daniel introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/daniel/0) dates the book's final form to the persecution under Antiochus IV, 167–164 BC, and calls Daniel 3:24–90 a Greek addition. It does not establish the addition's separate author, place, or date; the guide does not invent them or confuse the narrated furnace with the composition site.
- **Liturgical function:** the fivefold response makes revealed mercy doxological. It is responsorial to Exodus without being claimed as an authorial commentary on Exodus 34.

### John 3:16–18 — Gospel

- **Composition:** the official [John introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0) places final editing about AD 90–100, reports Ephesus as traditional, and leaves Syria or Antioch possible. Exact place and compositional stages remain disputed.
- **Narrated event:** early in Jesus' Jerusalem ministry around Passover, following the nocturnal exchange with Nicodemus; no exact year is asserted.
- **Literary context:** the [John 3 notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/3) treat verses 16–21 as the evangelist's reflection after the Nicodemus discourse. The Father's giving refers to Incarnation and Cross; the purpose is salvation, while the coming of light discloses a present judgment in the human response.
- **Control:** verses 16–18 directly speak of God and the Son, not all three Persons in one sentence. Their Trinitarian hearing comes from the whole Gospel, the celebration, and the adjoining Missal and Lectionary appointments, not a false claim that this pericope is itself a complete Trinitarian formula.

### 2 Corinthians 13:11–13 — Second Reading

- **Composition:** St. Paul writes from Macedonia, generally dated to autumn AD 57, to the Church at Corinth after severe conflict and movement toward reconciliation; see the official [2 Corinthians introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2corinthians/0).
- **Literary context:** final exhortations to restoration, encouragement, agreement, and peace lead into the triadic blessing. The [chapter note](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2corinthians/13) identifies it as one of the clearest New Testament Trinitarian passages and stresses ecclesial unity.
- **Numbering:** the U.S. Lectionary ends at 13:13. Older translations and St. John Chrysostom label the final blessing verse 14. Both refer to the same sentence; the guide never treats them as different passages.
- **Control:** the apostolic triad is authentic New Testament witness; it is not described as already using the fourth-century technical vocabulary that later protects its confession.

### Galatians 4:6 — Communion Antiphon

- **Composition:** the official [Galatians introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/galatians/0) preserves two principal historical placements: a North Galatian theory around AD 54–55, perhaps from Ephesus, and a South Galatian theory around AD 48–50. The guide leaves the alternative open.
- **Audience and context:** Gentile converts in Asia Minor face pressure toward Mosaic observance. Galatians 4:1–7 places the sending of the Son and the Spirit's filial cry within emancipation from slavery and divine adoption.
- **Liturgical function:** sacramental reception is voiced as adopted relation to the Father through the Spirit of the Son; the antiphon is not treated as a self-contained theory of automatic sacramental fruit.

### `Cf.` Revelation 1:8 — Gospel Acclamation

- **Composition:** the official [Revelation introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/0) places the book's present form near the end of Domitian's reign, AD 81–96, for seven churches of Roman Asia confronting the claims and pressures of empire. Patmos belongs to John's visionary setting; the exact final writing site is not proved.
- **Textual status:** the Lectionary verse uses Revelation's divine title but adds explicit Father, Son, and Holy Spirit language. The `cf.` marker is therefore retained: this is an acclamatory liturgical adaptation, not a quotation placed on the historical seer's lips.

## Missal formula and liturgical history

The shared owner verifies the proper Entrance Antiphon, Collect, Prayer over the Offerings, Preface, Communion Antiphon, and Prayer after Communion. The Collect moves from the sending of the Word of truth and Spirit of sanctification to profession of Trinity and Unity. The Prayer over the Offerings joins invocation of the divine Name, sanctification of the gifts, and the worshippers' becoming an enduring offering. The proper Preface supplies the solemnity's doctrinal grammar: one God and Lord, three distinct Persons, one substance, equal majesty. The Prayer after Communion asks sacramental reception and confession of eternal Trinity and undivided Unity to advance bodily and spiritual salvation.

Leo XIII, [*Divinum illud munus* 3](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_09051897_divinum-illud-munus.html), records that the feast entered the universal calendar through John XXII and insists that divine operations are inseparable even when works are appropriately spoken of in relation to one Person. No exact universalization year is supplied in the guide because this primary witness does not give one.

### Broader doctrinal reception retained

- **St. Augustine, *De Trinitate* I.4.7:** [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/130101.htm). Augustine confesses Father, Son, and Spirit as one substance in indivisible equality, distinguishes the Persons, identifies only the Son as incarnate, and preserves inseparable divine operation. This is direct doctrinal exposition, not commentary on the later formulary.
- **St. Gregory Nazianzen, *Oration 40* §§41–43, especially §41:** [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310240.htm). In a baptismal setting Gregory holds one Godhead and power with three distinct Persons and returns contemplation from unity to three and from three to unity.
- **St. Augustine, *City of God* X.5–6, 20:** sacrificial doctrine used to control the Prayer over the Offerings, not presented as commentary on the proper prayer.

The reader-facing commentary labels direct verse exposition, adjacent-context use, later doctrinal reuse, and liturgical adaptation separately. No ancient source is presented as commenting on the complete Trinity Sunday formula.

## Magisterial and doctrinal controls

The principal control is the [Catechism of the Catholic Church 232–267](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_2_the_father.html):

- 234 calls the Trinity the central mystery of Christian faith and life.
- 236 distinguishes the divine life in itself from the works by which God reveals and gives himself, while refusing to separate them.
- 237 prevents reading Old Testament traces as though reason or Israel's authors already possessed the full revealed mystery.
- 239 states that God transcends the human distinction of the sexes; calling God Father is not the assertion that God is male.
- 249 expressly receives the triadic 2 Corinthians blessing in the Church's Eucharistic liturgy.
- 253–255 exclude three gods, modes, and merely verbal distinctions: one divine nature, Persons really distinct by relations.
- 258 preserves one common divine operation while recognizing each Person's personal property.
- 260 identifies the economy's end as creaturely entry into the blessed Trinity's communion and indwelling.

These loci govern the guide against tritheism, modalism, subordinationism, gender literalism, and division of external operations. The guide distinguishes the missions of Son and Spirit in salvation history from the eternal processions without claiming that the economic texts exhaust the immanent mystery.

## Relationship classification

- **Seasonal or ritual:** every Missal element is proper to the solemnity; the Lectionary set is its Year A path.
- **Responsorial:** Daniel 3 is appointed as the response after Exodus 34. The guide can compare divine mercy and blessing without attributing literary dependence.
- **Acclamatory:** the adapted Revelation verse welcomes Christ about to speak in the Gospel; it is not used as an independent historical proof text.
- **Textual observation:** the Collect's sending of Word and Spirit, 2 Corinthians' grace-love-communion, and Galatians' Son-Spirit-Father movement each use triadic grammar, but with different immediate purposes.
- **Documented reception:** Chrysostom reads 2 Corinthians' blessing in an explicitly Trinitarian manner; Augustine reads John 3 through salvation and judgment; the Catechism and the proper Preface supply dogmatic controls.
- **Source-grounded synthesis:** the guide traces revelation of mercy, gift of Son, communion in Spirit, filial adoption, offering, and doxology. It does not claim that the human authors or formulary compiler designed every element as one literary argument.
- **Editorial or AI proposal:** the five proposals audited below---presence moving from “among” to “within,” inheritance received through the One given, God teaching liturgical speech, non-condemnation enabling repair, and mercy widening by scale---are reserved for the named speculative section with explicit limits.

## Source-grounded themes and four senses

1. **Divine self-disclosure and gift:** covenant mercy in Exodus; the Father's saving gift in John; apostolic communion in 2 Corinthians; adopted address in Galatians.
2. **Economy and confession:** the missions of Word and Spirit lead liturgically to worship of three Persons in one undivided divine life.
3. **Mercy before mastery:** doctrine protects the identity of the One who pardons, sends, saves, and indwells; it does not replace the biblical mercy with abstraction.
4. **Ecclesial practice:** Paul's triadic blessing follows commands to mend relations and live in peace. Trinitarian faith bears a communal and moral form.
5. **Doxology:** the Entrance Antiphon, Daniel canticle, Preface, and Communion layer frame the Mass as praise received and returned.

- **Literal:** covenant renewal; persecuted Israel's Greek canticle; Paul's reconciliatory conclusion; the evangelist's reflection on gift, faith, salvation, and judgment; filial adoption.
- **Allegorical:** the one God's Sinai mercy is heard in the economy of Father, Son, and Spirit; the Church's triadic confession is anchored in the full canon and rule of faith rather than projected into each author's historical intent.
- **Moral:** worship, repair relationships, live in peace, believe, come to the light, and live as adopted children whose worship becomes self-offering.
- **Anagogical:** eternal life, communion and indwelling, confession of the eternal Trinity, and everlasting doxology.
