# Appointments of the First Sunday of Advent, Year B

Collated 23 November 2026. Appointed-text completeness: `rights-limited`. PC-S01-B, United States, 29 November 2026, one Sunday form, one Lectionary set, no readings of alternative length. The [instance](../instance/manifest.md) lists the twelve elements in sequence. ## U.S. Lectionary, vol. I, no. 2

The United States national calendar gives the First Sunday in violet and the Isaiah, Pauline and Mark references on printed p. 11. The dated USCCB proclamation page supplies the response and acclamation as well as the reading bodies. The 1981 Latin *Ordo lectionum Missae*, printed p. 5 (PDF p. 59), no. 2 B, confirms the disposition and points back to no. 1 for the acclamation only. *Praenotanda* 93 identifies Advent I's Gospel as concerning the Lord's coming at the end of time. [U.S. reading witness](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/112926.cfm) · [U.S. calendar](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2027cal.pdf)

| Key | Proclaimed locus and boundary of accompanying public-domain study English |
| --- | --- |
| `first-reading` | Is 63:16b–17, 19b; 64:2–7 U.S. The appeal begins with the second address to the Father in Challoner 63:16, continues through v. 17, jumps to 64:1 for the rent heavens (not Challoner 63:19), then to 64:3–8, until the potter and clay. Native 64:2 on burning waters is unappointed. Latin no. 2 prints 64:2b–7. The two translations differ in the wonderful deeds line and its continuation: the U.S. text does not repeat the descent/melting close of Challoner 64:3. At U.S. 64:4 the speakers wish for God to meet **them** doing right and remembering him. Challoner 64:5 says God has already met **him** who rejoices and does justice, then shifts to what **they** will remember. Its last clause extends the confession of sin and affirms future salvation. The U.S. passage goes directly from anger and sin to the community’s uncleanness at 64:5. Identify those native additions and changes wherever the lawful study excerpt is printed. U.S. 64:5–7 / Challoner 64:6–8 share uncleanness, the withering leaf, the absence of anyone calling upon God, the hidden face, and the final appeal from clay to its Father. Native 64:7 describes God crushing them in the hand of their iniquity, where U.S. 64:6 has God giving them over to guilt. The U.S. close names the potter expressly, while Challoner 64:8 names the maker. Stop before the further plea in native v. 9. |
| `psalm` | Ps 80 (79):2ac, 3b, 15–16, 18–19; response v. 4. U.S. heading gives 2–3 without the half-verse letters, but omits the Joseph line in v. 2 and three tribes in v. 3. Distinguish the vineyard, planted shoot and man at God's right hand, and preserve the shift between prayer about him and the worshippers' “we” in v. 19. An original U.S. opening vocative in the response differs from the full native verse. |
| `second-reading` | 1 Cor 1:3–9, greeting through call to fellowship. Verse 2 names the recipients in Corinth. Read with 16:8 and Prat, “St. Paul,” third mission, it locates Paul’s greeting from Ephesus to that church. The address and the appeal over divisions immediately following the reading belong to its literary context. The Lectionary adds its salutation to the assembly. |
| `acclamation` | Alleluia, Ps 85 (84):8, request for mercy and salvation. No. 2 directs to no. 1. One verse on this Lectionary route; a selected alternative chant is unknown. |
| `gospel` | Mk 13:33–37, alertness through the universal command. The Lectionary's introductory speaker cue is liturgical. Challoner includes “and pray” at 13:33; the dated U.S. witness has no counterpart there. At v. 34 the U.S. text assigns a task to each servant; distinguish the gatekeeper's particular command and the closing address to all. |

## Sung-psalm options

Under U.S. GIRM 61, the seasonal choices may replace the corresponding text when the psalm is sung, to help the assembly join the response. U.S. *Lectionary for Mass*, vol. I, no. 173 has the Advent common response “Come, O Lord, and set us free.” No. 174 supplies two Advent psalms:

| U.S. heading (Hebrew psalm number) | Strophes in the 1981 Latin *Ordo*, no. 174, p. 97 | U.S. refrain identifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ps 25:4–5, 8–9, 10 and 14 | Ps 24:4bc–5ab, 8–9, 10 and 14 | “To you, O Lord, I lift my soul” (v. 1, Latin v. 1b) |
| Ps 85:9–10, 11–12, 13–14 | Ps 84:9ab–10, 11–12, 13–14 | “Lord, show us your mercy and love” (v. 8a) |

The U.S. diocesan guide prints whole-verse headings. The half-verse divisions in the middle column come from the Latin page image. The two U.S. musical transcriptions confirm the sung selections: the first stanza opens with the request to learn God’s ways and ends with God as savior, and the second psalm’s opening moves from the proclamation of peace to the nearness of salvation. [U.S. sung Psalm 25](https://www.modernpsalter.com/us/Lectionary.aspx?n=174&s=0) · [U.S. sung Psalm 85](https://www.modernpsalter.com/us/Lectionary.aspx?n=174&s=1)

The seasonal response under no. 173 has no verse number there. The Sunday assignment under no. 2 remains Psalm 80 with its own response from v. 4. The seasonal strophes enter the branch audit. Neither they nor the wreath prayers enlarge the directly appointed Sunday chronology. Local use is unknown.
