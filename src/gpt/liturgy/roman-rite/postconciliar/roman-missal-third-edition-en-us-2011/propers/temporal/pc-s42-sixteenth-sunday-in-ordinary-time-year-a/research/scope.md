## Historical orientation

- **Psalms:** Psalms 54 and 86 have inherited Davidic headings; Psalm 111 is an alphabetic praise psalm without an individual author named. These titles and cultic uses orient reception but do not prove autograph sites or dates. Final-form dates remain uncertain.
- **Wisdom:** the book speaks in the persona of Solomon, while modern Catholic scholarship generally places its Greek composition among Alexandrian Jews in the late first century BC. It addresses a community negotiating fidelity amid Hellenistic culture; exact author and date remain unknown.
- **Matthew:** ancient tradition identifies Matthew the Apostle writing for Jewish believers in Palestine; the modern majority dates the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, commonly c. AD 80–90, with Antioch plausible but unproved. Matthew 11 and Matthew 13 belong to the Galilean ministry but are distinct narrated moments; Matthew 13 moves from shore-side teaching to private explanation in a house.
- **Romans:** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to mixed Jewish and Gentile churches at Rome. Romans 8:26–27 continues the movement from creation’s and believers’ groaning to the Spirit’s aid in prayer.
- **Revelation:** John writes from Patmos to seven churches in Roman Asia. A late first-century setting, often around AD 95, is traditional and widely used, while exact compositional history remains debated. Revelation 3:20 occurs inside the risen Christ’s admonition to Laodicea.

## Branch and pastoral limits

- Long and short Gospel forms are mutually exclusive. The guide treats both but never narrates them as one enacted proclamation.
- The short form ends with the harvest instructions in the parable. It omits mustard seed, leaven, Matthew’s fulfillment notice, Jesus’ private explanation, final judgment, and the righteous shining in the Father’s kingdom.
- The official page shows one Psalm response and no alternative refrain.
- The permitted Sunday sprinkling rite may replace the Penitential Act; this unresolved ritual substitution is not a proper-text row.
- Actual music, Preface, Eucharistic Prayer, and Communion-antiphon selection remain unknown. `compatible-preface-and-eucharistic-prayer` follows Ordinary Time rubric 5; `eucharistic-prayer-iv-with-preface` separately records GIRM 365(d)'s inseparable EP IV Preface and exclusion from a proper-Preface pairing. There is no Missal Offertory antiphon.
- Week XVI ferias use independent weekday Cycle II in 2026. Ferial selection of Week XVI orations is possible but not automatic Sunday spillover.
- The weeds parable does not forbid safeguarding, reporting abuse, removing dangerous persons from access, proportionate ecclesial discipline, medical treatment, or resisting injustice. It forbids servants from assuming the Master’s final, infallible judgment and destroying wheat through premature zeal; the competent application of discipline requires other governing texts and law.
- “Children of the evil one” is a role within Jesus’ explanation, not authorization to declare named living persons ontologically evil, beyond repentance, or fit for mistreatment.
