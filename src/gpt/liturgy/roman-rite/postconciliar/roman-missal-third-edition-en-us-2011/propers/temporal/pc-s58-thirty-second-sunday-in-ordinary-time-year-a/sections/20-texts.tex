\section{Appointed passages and prayer locators}
The biblical English below is the public-domain Douay--Rheims, Challoner revision, in the registered Gutenberg 1581 witness. It is a study translation, not the English proclaimed at Mass: the approved U.S. \textit{Lectionary for Mass} controls proclamation. For Missal adaptations, the biblical source passage is printed as such, not as the appointed antiphon or refrain. Verse numbers follow this historical witness unless otherwise identified.

\subsection{Entrance antiphon}
\label{proper-entrance}
Source passage: Psalm 87:3 Vulgate / 88:3 Hebrew; the Missal uses an adaptation.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{3} Let my prayer come in before thee: incline thy ear to my petition.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Collect}
\label{proper-collect}
\textit{Omnipotens et misericors Deus}. Roman Missal, Week 32, Collect.

Full approved English and modern Latin prayer bodies are omitted. The petition concerns freedom for service to God; see the common Missal source locators in the References.

\subsection{First reading}
\label{proper-first-reading}
Wisdom 6:12--16 in the appointment; 6:13--17 in this Vulgate-based study witness. The final verse belongs to the appointment.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{13} Wisdom is glorious, and never fadeth away, and is easily seen by them that love her, and is found by them that seek her.

\textsuperscript{14} She preventeth them that covet her, so that she first sheweth herself unto them.

\textsuperscript{15} He that awaketh early to seek her, shall not labour: for he shall find her sitting at his door.

\textsuperscript{16} To think, therefore, upon her, is perfect understanding: and he that watcheth for her, shall quickly be secure.

\textsuperscript{17} For she goeth about seeking such as are worthy of her, and she sheweth herself to them cheerfully in the ways, and meeteth them with all providence.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Responsorial Psalm}
\label{proper-psalm}
Psalm 63:2, 3--4, 5--6, 7--8 Hebrew / 62:2--8 Vulgate. The approved response is cited as 2b; its wording is omitted. The study witness divides the desert phrase differently, so no isolated study half-verse is presented as the approved response.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{2} O God, my God, to thee do I watch at break of day. For thee my soul hath thirsted; for thee my flesh, O how many ways!

\textsuperscript{3} In a desert land, and where there is no way, and no water: so in the sanctuary have I come before thee, to see thy power and thy glory.

\textsuperscript{4} For thy mercy is better than lives: thee my lips will praise.

\textsuperscript{5} Thus will I bless thee all my life long: and in thy name I will lift up my hands.

\textsuperscript{6} Let my soul be filled as with marrow and fatness: and my mouth shall praise thee with joyful lips.

\textsuperscript{7} If I have remembered thee upon my bed, I will meditate on thee in the morning:

\textsuperscript{8} Because thou hast been my helper. And I will rejoice under the covert of thy wings:
\end{quote}


\subsection{Second reading: long form}
\label{proper-second-reading-long}
1 Thessalonians 4:13--18 in the appointment; printed 4:12--17 in this study witness, whose earlier 4:11 joins two verses.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{12} And we will not have you ignorant brethren, concerning them that are asleep, that you be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope.

\textsuperscript{13} For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again: even so them who have slept through Jesus, will God bring with him.

\textsuperscript{14} For this we say unto you in the word of the Lord, that we who are alive, who remain unto the coming of the Lord, shall not prevent them who have slept.

\textsuperscript{15} For the Lord himself shall come down from heaven with commandment and with the voice of an archangel and with the trumpet of God: and the dead who are in Christ shall rise first.

\textsuperscript{16} Then we who are alive, who are left, shall be taken up together with them in the clouds to meet Christ, into the air: and so shall we be always with the Lord.

\textsuperscript{17} Wherefore, comfort ye one another with these words.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Second reading: short form}
\label{proper-second-reading-short}
1 Thessalonians 4:13--14 in the appointment; printed 4:12--13 in this study witness. This is an alternative to the preceding form.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{12} And we will not have you ignorant brethren, concerning them that are asleep, that you be not sorrowful, even as others who have no hope.

\textsuperscript{13} For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again: even so them who have slept through Jesus, will God bring with him.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Gospel acclamation}
\label{proper-acclamation}
Cf. Matthew 24:42a, 44. The Lectionary verse is a selection/adaptation. The complete biblical source verses below give its context; their concatenation is not the liturgical text.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{42} Watch ye therefore, because you know not what hour your Lord will come.

\textsuperscript{44} Wherefore be you also ready, because at what hour you know not the Son of man will come.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Gospel}
\label{proper-gospel}
Matthew 25:1--13. The historical study witness includes the bride at verse 1; the appointed U.S. English names the bridegroom. There is no appointed short Gospel.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{1} Then shall the kingdom of heaven be like to ten virgins, who taking their lamps went out to meet the bridegroom and the bride.

\textsuperscript{2} And five of them were foolish and five wise.

\textsuperscript{3} But the five foolish, having taken their lamps, did not take oil with them.

\textsuperscript{4} But the wise took oil in their vessels with the lamps.

\textsuperscript{5} And the bridegroom tarrying, they all slumbered and slept.

\textsuperscript{6} And at midnight there was a cry made: Behold the bridegroom cometh. Go ye forth to meet him.

\textsuperscript{7} Then all those virgins arose and trimmed their lamps.

\textsuperscript{8} And the foolish said to the wise: Give us of your oil, for our lamps are gone out.

\textsuperscript{9} The wise answered, saying: Lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you, go ye rather to them that sell and buy for yourselves.

\textsuperscript{10} Now whilst they went to buy the bridegroom came: and they that were ready went in with him to the marriage. And the door was shut.

\textsuperscript{11} But at last came also the other virgins, saying: Lord, Lord, open to us.

\textsuperscript{12} But he answering said: Amen I say to you, I know you not.

\textsuperscript{13} Watch ye therefore, because you know not the day nor the hour.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Prayer over the Offerings}
\label{proper-offerings}
\textit{Sacrificiis praesentibus}. Roman Missal, Week 32, Prayer over the Offerings.

Full approved English and modern Latin prayer bodies are omitted. The prayer concerns celebration of the Son's Passion and its inward reception. The qualified English exemplar is identified in the scope appendix.

\subsection{Communion antiphon: Psalm alternative}
\label{proper-communion-psalm}
Cf. Psalm 22:1--2 Vulgate / 23:1--2 Hebrew. Biblical source passage for the first Missal alternative.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{1} A psalm for David. The Lord ruleth me: and I shall want nothing.

\textsuperscript{2} He hath set me in a place of pasture. He hath brought me up, on the water of refreshment:
\end{quote}


\subsection{Communion antiphon: Luke alternative}
\label{proper-communion-luke}
Cf. Luke 24:35. Biblical source passage for the second Missal alternative. This is an alternative to the preceding antiphon.

\begin{quote}
\textsuperscript{35} And they told what things were done in the way: and how they knew him in the breaking of bread.
\end{quote}


\subsection{Prayer after Communion}
\label{proper-after-communion}
\textit{Gratias tibi, Domine, referimus}. Roman Missal, Week 32, Prayer after Communion.

Full approved English and modern Latin prayer bodies are omitted. Thanksgiving follows reception, with petition for the Spirit's preservation of personal integrity.
