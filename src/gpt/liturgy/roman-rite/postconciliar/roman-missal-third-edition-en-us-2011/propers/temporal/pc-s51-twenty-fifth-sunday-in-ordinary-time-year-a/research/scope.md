## Real disagreements and limits

Chrysostom identifies the vineyard with commandments and the day with present life, principally applying the hours to personal ages (Matt64.3–4). This differs from Gregory's first identification, but it is not an express rejection of an ethnic reading:64.4 allows a hint concerning Jews in the closing maxim. Gregory identifies the universal Church and successive eras, while also admitting ages of one person's life. Augustine admits both historical and individual readings but assigns historical hours differently. Keep those identifications apart.

Chrysostom64.3 and Gregory19.4 expressly exclude actual envy or murmuring in heaven. Augustine87.5–6 answers the payment-order/equality question and admonishes against murmuring; do not attribute the identical explicit denial argument to him. Their explanations differ. Chrysostom treats the complaint as narrative magnification of the gift rather than a prediction of heavenly jealousy. Gregory interprets ancient fathers' delayed entry until Christ as the murmuring; his thief-before-Peter illustrates swift reward. Augustine emphasizes common resurrection payment and shorter waiting by later arrivals. The three may support common hope without being made one timetable.

Augustine on grace's initiative and Chrysostom on opened-heart attendance cannot be flattened into identical language; neither source here establishes a simple contradiction. Aquinas's Isaiah mercy-reading and Irenaeus's Creator-transcendence argument answer different questions. The Gospel is not a modern labor-policy tract, and theological wage imagery does not by itself establish an economic rule.
