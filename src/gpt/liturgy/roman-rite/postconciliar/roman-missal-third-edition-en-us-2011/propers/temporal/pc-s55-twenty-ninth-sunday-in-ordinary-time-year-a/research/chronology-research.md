The expansive study needs a same-owner historical appendix that explains
the following distinctions. It may print the corpus's raw labels, or the
generated display labels through their proper interface, but must not invent
compressed date ranges, average rival dates or quietly select a favourite.

- **Entrance Psalm16/17:** the generated historical-setting answer is
  unresolved, and the only dated claim returned concerns the Psalter's
  composition boundary. The full Douay chapter's title is a prayer of David;
  this is received attribution, not a dated flight, battle or composition
  occasion. No specific event can be recovered merely from enemies, wings
  or the petition for protection. The corpus does not return a dated David
  attribution for this element; do not borrow the Communion row's era to
  fill it. Augustine's Christ-and-body speaker is an exegetical reading,
  not a newly dated historical setting.
- **All three Psalms:** the NABRE introduction says that individual Psalms
  cannot be securely dated and bounds the collection before the Maccabean
  period. The generated composition label is a common latest boundary, not
  an assertion that Psalms16,32 and95 were composed together or at that
  boundary. Read the uncertainty with the date, including in concise prose.
- **Communion Psalm32/33:** the Douay and Clementine titles attribute the
  psalm to David without naming an occasion. The corpus connects this to
  Corbett's explicitly “usual chronology” of David's reign; the same
  paragraph distinguishes later datings influenced by Assyrian evidence.
  The generated disputed attribution is an author's reference era, not a
  composition date or an identified event in this psalm. Its historical
  setting remains unresolved.
- **Responsorial Psalm95/96:** the Septuagint/Vulgate title identifies a
  house built after captivity, while the Hebrew title does not supply that
  superscription. The corpus connects it with Zorobabel's raising of the
  second Temple, following the opening of the historical encyclopedia's
  “Temple of Jerusalem” article. The binding expressly admits that the
  title does not distinguish this raising from the later completion; report
  it as the corpus's superscription-setting association, not a securely
  established date of the Psalm's composition or of an unambiguous dedication.
  Augustine's new-building exposition supplies Christian reception, not an
  alternative historical excavation report.
- **The Psalm's prophetic referent:** the generated Nativity alternatives
  come from John Gerard's “General Chronology” and James Howlett's
  “Biblical Chronology.” The
  first reports a correction attributed to many interpreters of Dionysius;
  the second argues from Herod and the infancy evidence and concludes with
  a probable date. Both are marked disputed. These are dates assigned to
  the Christian referent, not to the song's composition or to its literal
  worship scene. The Psalm does not itself name Bethlehem or supply the
  Nativity's year.
- **Isaiah45:** Souvay's traditional Isaias ministry era and the NABRE
  introduction's late-exile prophetic activity belong to different source
  frameworks. The former is traditional attribution of the book, not an
  independently dated encounter between Isaiah and Cyrus. The latter
  concerns the activity underlying chapters40–55, not a claim that every
  word or the final book arrangement was completed simultaneously. Neither
  generated claim supplies a date for Cyrus's capture of Babylon or decree;
  do not insert one from memory. The prophecy names the Persian ruler and
  Israel's restoration; Isaiah44:26–28 provides Jerusalem and its Temple
  as the restoration's destination.
- **1 Thessalonians:** Prat's “St Paul” chronology and Drum's “Epistles to
  the Thessalonians” give different traditional composition schemes.
  Drum's paragraph also mentions a critical alternative not selected by this
  corpus profile; it must not be silently added as a third returned date.
  His argument places writing at Corinth by combining the three senders,
  Acts18:5 and the Timothy report; the letter addresses Thessalonica.
  Composition is not the date of every event remembered in the thanksgiving.
- **Philippians:** Prat's schedule and the separate encyclopedia article
  give different Roman-captivity composition dates. Preserve the disputed
  alternatives. The latter argues for Rome rather than Caesarea and discusses
  uncertainty about the stage of imprisonment. This is the reported source
  framework, not a modern consensus claim excluding other scholarship. The
  place phrase must distinguish the writer in Rome in that account from
  recipients at Philippi.
- **Matthew22:** the narrated-event date is unresolved in the corpus. The
  Jerusalem temple setting comes from Matthew21:23 and the continuing
  controversies, not from a calendar reconstruction of Holy Week. Jacquier's
  Matthew article reports competing early-author testimony and subsequent
  datings, explicitly finding interpretive uncertainty in the Irenaeus
  argument. Alfred Durand's “The New Testament” article's earlier original-Matthew date is
  coupled with uncertainty over the Greek rendering. All six generated
  composition alternatives remain attributed and disputed; none dates this
  exchange with the Pharisees and Herodians. The reader should understand
  why overlapping ranges remain separate source answers.
- **Mark10:45:** MacRory's Mark article reports early subscriptions and
  chronicles, explains their possible derivation, then weighs conflicting
  testimony and internal evidence toward its preferred broader interval.
  Durand's “The New Testament” article gives another interval. The 1912 PBC responsa
  V–VI supply relative order and a limit before Jerusalem's destruction/
  begun siege, not a numerical year. These are all composition claims.
  The generated orientation currently gives no separately dated event row
  for this Communion excerpt; do not turn its composition dates into dates
  of Christ's journey or Passion. Mark10:32–45 situates the teaching on
  the ascent toward Jerusalem; verse46 reaches Jericho after it. The
  precise roadside location of the saying is not named.

These sources are a bounded corpus of historical Catholic and modern
official witnesses, not a claim to survey every current scholarly date.
The traditional, critical, reported, relative and disputed designations
remain visible. Neither the 2026 occurrence nor the Missal's publication
year is a biblical date.
