\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Isaiah calls for return; the
psalm praises the Lord's mercy and nearness; Paul weighs being with Christ
against fruitful service; the landowner keeps his promise while welcoming late
workers. The prayers ask for love to become obedience and sacramental
reception to become conduct.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ cultivates
hearts and gathers his people. Augustine's fruitful gift, Chrysostom's
vineyard of commandments, and Gregory's Church through the ages illuminate
different dimensions of the one caller. Communion accents either enabled
obedience or belonging to the self-giving Shepherd.\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Receive mercy without despair or
deliberate delay. Seek God himself, attend to his word, and make another's
good a reason to work. Early service creates responsibility for others, never
ownership of their admission or a claim that their portion must be smaller.\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The common hope is life
with Christ, shared without rivalry. Augustine's endless denarius preserves
different glory; Chrysostom excludes envy from heaven; Gregory sees earlier
and later generations gathered into the kingdom opened by Christ.\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
