\section*{The Propers: Notable and Quotable}

{\small
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.35em,topsep=0.2em,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Matthew~13:45--46, both Gospel branches --- Nathaniel Hawthorne, \emph{The Scarlet Letter} (1850).} Hester's language of a pearl ``of great price'' redirects the Gospel image toward the child understood as her costly and only treasure amid Puritan shame and exclusion. The chapter marks the purchase logic; the novel's moral world is Hawthorne's own, not an interpretation of Matthew.

\item \textbf{Matthew~13:45--46, both Gospel branches --- \emph{The Pearl of Great Price}.} Franklin D.~Richards's 1851 compilation and its later institutional reception turn the Gospel phrase into the title of a scriptural anthology. An official study manual explains the title dependence. The dependence is one of title, and it settles nothing about the collection's religious claims.

\item \textbf{Romans~8:28, independent Second Reading --- Margaret Murray Robertson, \emph{Christie Redfern's Troubles} (1866).} A chapter titled ``The Secret of Peace'' places the verse inside a dispute over trust, passivity, suffering, and who may rightly claim the promise. Narrative argument, not an isolated citation, supplies the transformation; the novel receives the promise and holds no doctrinal authority.

\end{itemize}

\section*{The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities}

\noindent\emph{These exploratory proposals arose in an AI-assisted editorial process. They are attributed to none of the cited authorities and claim no historical compositional intent. Each states its anchors and limits.}

\begingroup
\footnotesize
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{A listening treasury.\enspace\elementref{Coll.; First Reading; Responsorial Psalm; long Gospel}}
Solomon's listening heart, the Psalm's word and understanding, and the long-form scribe's new and old treasures can be heard with the Collect's petition for guidance. Ambrose and Benedict XVI illuminate the listening heart, while Origen illuminates the scribe; no checked witness supplies the complete chain. The conjunction encourages teachability and responsible handing-on rather than information hoarding. The scribe is long-form only, and no compositional intent is claimed.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Value conversion is not purchase of grace.\enspace\elementref{Coll.; Responsorial Psalm; Gosp.; Prayer over Offerings}}
The Psalm values instruction above gold, the parables picture decisive disposal, the Collect distinguishes passing from lasting goods, and the offering prayer returns gifts first received. Ambrose, Chrysostom, Francis, and Ferraro illuminate individual movements; no checked source joins all four texts. Together they invite examination of money, time, attention, and allegiance as gifts ordered toward the Kingdom. Grace is not merchandise, possession is not intrinsically evil, and duties to others remain.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{The authorized branches teach different endings.\enspace\elementref{Gospel branches; First Reading}}
The short form closes with value, joy, and disposal; the long form adds net, judgment, understanding, and the scribe, which can be heard beside Solomon's discernment. Patristic commentaries follow the Gospel sequence, while this contrast arises from the modern Lectionary's alternative endings. Attending to the enacted ending helps preacher and reader respect what was actually proclaimed. The short form is authorized and complete, not deficient; long-only material may never be smuggled into its literal account.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Providence transforms persons without making harm morally good.\enspace\elementref{Second Reading; Prayer after Communion}}
Romans' purpose and conformity to the Son can be heard with the perpetual memorial of the Passion and the prayer that divine love advance salvation. Chrysostom and Aquinas address Providence amid adversity; the liturgical prayer supplies the Passion memorial. Their conjunction supports resilient hope, truthful lament, and action shaped toward Christ rather than denial of injury. The proposal never sanctifies abuse or displaces safeguarding, treatment, reporting, restitution, or justice.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{House, siblings, and net form an ecclesial test.\enspace\elementref{Entrance; Second Reading; long Gospel; Communion B}}
A united dwelling, the firstborn among many siblings, a net of every kind, and mercy with purity form a proposed sequence of gathering, formation, and judgment. Existing reception illuminates these images separately, and no checked witness joins all four. Its fruit is hospitality joined to integrity and humble accountability. The net and Communion B are unresolved branch-dependent anchors; final separation belongs to God.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Memory loosens possession.\enspace\elementref{Communion A; Prayer over Offerings; Gosp.; Prayer after Communion}}
The summons not to forget benefits, the return of received bounty, the Gospel's selling, and the Passion memorial suggest a movement from gratitude through stewardship toward self-gift. The checked witnesses illuminate the separate motions without establishing this single liturgical chain. Its fruit is grateful use rather than anxious possession or forgetful consumption. Communion A remains unresolved, and the proposal condemns neither property, contracts, saving, nor provision for dependents.
