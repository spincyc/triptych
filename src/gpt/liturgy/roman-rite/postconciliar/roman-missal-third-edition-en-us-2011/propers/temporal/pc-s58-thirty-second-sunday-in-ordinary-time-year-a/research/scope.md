The currentness follow-up requested by context is resolved to the bounded
national-calendar claim in `context.md`: fresh official calendar, readings and

The four returned liturgical loci were actually read. Honorius, *Gemma
animae* IV.67–68, shares only the shortened Collect incipit; the full petition

quotation requires image collation not performed here. No returned locus
establishes a whole-formulary historical interpretation of the present Mass.

The proportionate second search pursued direct medieval Wisdom exegesis,
Aquinas on the whole Gospel and Thessalonian pericope, and Bellarmine on all

formulary search yielded no checked direct Father or saint exposition of the

## Biblical contexts and consequential boundaries

The complete public-domain study contexts read were Wisdom 5–7, Psalms 22,
62 and 87, 1 Thessalonians 4–5, Matthew 24–25, and Luke 24. Exact artifacts and
native loci are pinned in `source-bindings.toml`. Wisdom addresses rulers
under judgment before describing the eager search for wisdom; chapter 7's
speaker has ordinary mortal origins and receives wisdom by prayer. The
appointment is no elite claim that wisdom is achieved without a gift. The
verse in which Wisdom herself goes out to meet the seeker is appointed and
must survive the modern/Vulgate offset.

Psalm 87 is a sustained lament which does not end in a narrative rescue.
Its one appointed entrance verse asks to be heard; it cannot be made an
assurance that suffering has already ceased. Psalm 62's desire is embodied,
its night remembrance depends on God's help, and its praise does not erase
the surrounding danger. Psalm 22's whole poem supplies the shepherd's care,
death-shadow, table and abiding home; only the adapted opening verses are
the Communion appointment. Interior peace is not a promise of material ease.

The Thessalonian context begins with holiness, brotherly charity, work and
quiet service; chapter 5 returns to watchfulness and hope without supplying
a timetable. The long appointment promises that the dead do not lose their
place with Christ; the short form preserves death/resurrection and hope but
does not proclaim the later trumpet, order of rising or meeting in clouds.
The word “sleep” is contextual: death in 4:13, negligent conduct in 5:6.
Matthew's virgins all sleep. Do not turn the wise into people who stayed
physically awake or turn illness, exhaustion or ordinary sleep into the
parable's fault.

Matthew 24's unknown hour leads into entrusted service, and Matthew 25's
parables lead to judgment involving the hungry, stranger and prisoner.
Those surrounding passages illuminate the oil question without becoming
appointed readings. The approved Gospel lacks the additional “and the bride”
found in the held Challoner and some patristic witnesses. Luke 24 joins the
scriptural explanation, hospitality, breaking bread, recognition, disappearance
and return to Jerusalem. Verse 35 reports the recognition; it does not itself
narrate a second breaking of bread in Jerusalem.

## Differences that must survive authoring

The two principal readings answer the same question differently: what
makes apparently ready persons unready? Chrysostom indicts withheld mercy
and treats the poor as the sellers; Augustine and Gregory indict outward
service detached from inward charity and treat sellers as flatterers.
“Both mean be good” loses the exegetical argument. Mercy and inward charity
are compatible Christian duties, but the identification of one narrative
figure is not identical in these explanations. Augustine expressly allows
outward alms among the foolish, so do not rewrite his lamps as no good works.

The inference from all sleeping, the inability to transfer preparation, the
unexpected cry, and the locked door require explicit explanation. Neither
reading licenses present refusal of a neighbor's need, payment for grace,
or neglect of the dying. Readiness is enabled by prior grace in Wisdom and
sustained by the Spirit in the final prayer; personal responsibility does not
mean self-salvation. Avoid assigning oil uniquely to a single sacrament as
if the named witnesses agreed on that meaning. The final welcome is not
promised irrespective of life; the warning is not permission to predict a
particular person's damnation.
