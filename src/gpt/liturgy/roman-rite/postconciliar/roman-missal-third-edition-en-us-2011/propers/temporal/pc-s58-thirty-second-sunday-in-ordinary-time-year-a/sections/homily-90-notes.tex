\section{Note on sources and delivery}
This homily addresses an adult parish assembly for the U.S. Thirty-second Sunday in Ordinary Time, Year A, 8 November 2026, Lectionary no. 154. It develops the accepted studies' interpretation \textit{A lamp sustained by charity within}: Augustine's distinction between visible good works and inward charity, the personal responsibility disclosed by the vessels, and the common sleep understood as death. Wisdom's approach, the Collect's service, inward reception of the Passion, and the Spirit's sustaining help are connected in the homily's own voice. Those connections derive from the studies; they are not attributed to Augustine as an exposition of this modern Mass. Aquinas supplies the explanation of grief compatible with resurrection hope.

The spoken body has 1,284 whitespace-delimited words, excluding this note, References, title and provenance. At an assumed 110--125 words per minute it takes approximately 10.3--11.7 minutes before longer pauses. A silent textual rehearsal checked the sequence, sentence clarity, attributions and closing application. This is an estimate, not a timed or audible human delivery. The homily is authored preaching prose, not an official liturgical text or a claim of ecclesiastical approval.

Only the shared content of the long and short second reading is described as heard at Mass. Neither Communion antiphon is assumed to have been selected. The prayer descriptions are analytical commentary, not translations or recitation texts. Full approved English and modern Latin prayer bodies remain omitted under the studies' rights-limited mode. The complete 2008 altar-book authentication remains incomplete; the offerings analysis derives from the inspected Latin reproduction and the qualified U.S. 2011 digital-book candidate, whose publisher transmission has not been independently authenticated. No new source verification is claimed. The study's exact loci and qualifications remain controlling.
