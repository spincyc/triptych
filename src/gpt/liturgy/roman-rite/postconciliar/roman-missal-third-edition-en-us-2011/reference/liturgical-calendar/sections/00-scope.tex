\section{Object, Authority, and Limits}

\subsection{The object documented}

This reference describes the recurring postconciliar Roman calendar in force for the Roman Missal, third typical edition, as emended, together with universal changes promulgated through 17~July 2026 and the Proper Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America. It has three inventories that must not be collapsed: the movable Proper of Time, the fixed General Roman Calendar, and the United States territorial overlay.

The calendar is an ordered legal and liturgical structure, not simply a list of saints. The annual Paschal cycle and the weekly Sunday cycle form its center; seasons and temporal celebrations articulate the mysteries of Christ; the fixed cycle adds celebrations of the Lord, the Blessed Virgin Mary, angels, and saints; and an approved particular calendar joins celebrations proper to a territory. A diocese, parish, church, or religious family can add another proper layer.

\subsection{Edition, territory, and English labels}

The Latin base is the \emph{Missale Romanum}, editio typica tertia (2002), checked in its \emph{Calendarium Romanum generale} and \emph{Normae universales de anno liturgico et de calendario}. Later entries and rank changes are taken from decrees of the competent dicastery. The territorial control is the Proper Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America, including the 2010 confirmations and later national amendments, as implemented with the 2011 English Roman Missal and in current official USCCB calendars.

Celebration labels in the tables are concise editorial English identifiers, collated where possible to current USCCB usage. They are not presented as a reproduction of the copyrighted English Missal, and this work reproduces no prayers, readings, chants, or formularies.

\subsection{What ``complete'' means}

Complete here means every recurring temporal identity, every fixed universal celebration and rank current at the cutoff, and every national entry or national transfer rule in the identified United States proper. It does not compute any one civil year. A feast may be impeded, transferred, omitted, or combined with a local proper when the governing norms are applied to an actual date. For that final resolution, consult the competent annual and diocesan Ordo.
