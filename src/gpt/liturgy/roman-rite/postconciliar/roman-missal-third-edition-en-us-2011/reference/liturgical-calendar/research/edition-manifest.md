# Edition Manifest

## Normative source hierarchy

| Level | Witness | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promulgation | Paul VI, apostolic letter *Mysterii Paschalis*, 14 February 1969 | Promulgates the revised liturgical year and General Roman Calendar, effective 1 January 1970. |
| General norms | *Normae universales de anno liturgico et de calendario*, including the table of liturgical days | Governs temporal identities, rank, precedence, occurrence, transfer, and omission. |
| Printed base | *Missale Romanum*, editio typica tertia, declared typical 20 April 2000 and published by Typis Vaticanis in 2002, *Calendarium Romanum generale* | Supplies the base fixed universal inventory and ranks. |
| Emended base | *Missale Romanum*, editio typica tertia, *reimpressio emendata* (2008), Prot. N. 652/08/L | Integrates post-print additions including St Pius of Pietrelcina, St Juan Diego, and Our Lady of Guadalupe, and removes “Martyr” from the 29 August calendar title; does not constitute a fourth typical edition. |
| Later universal law | Dicastery calendar decrees, 2014–2026 | Adds celebrations, raises Mary Magdalene to feast, and broadens the 29 July memorial. |
| Territory | Proper Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America, including the 24 July 2010 confirmations and later national decrees | Adds, moves, or raises national celebrations; places Epiphany and Corpus Christi on Sunday; and supports province-dependent Ascension transfer. |
| Current implementation controls | USCCB, *Liturgical Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America—2026* | Checks current English labels, ranks, Ascension implementation, collisions, and the 2025/2026 universal additions. It does not replace the promulgating sources. |

## Historical reconstruction controls

- Paul F. Bradshaw and Maxwell E. Johnson, *The Origins of Feasts, Fasts, and Seasons in Early Christianity* (2011), introduction and chapter 19: early weekly, Paschal, Nativity–Epiphany, martyr, and later saints cycles.
- Pius V, *Quod a nobis* (1568) and *Quo primum* (1570): post-Tridentine standardization boundary.
- Pius X, *Divino afflatu*, AAS 3 (1911), 633–638: structural Psalter and precedence reform.
- Sacred Congregation of Rites, *Cum hac nostra aetate*, AAS 47 (1955), 218–224, and *Maxima redemptionis nostrae mysteria*, 838–847: simplified ranks, vigils, and octaves, and revised Holy Week.
- John XXIII, *Rubricarum instructum*, and the *Codex rubricarum*, AAS 52 (1960), 593–729: four-class calendar governing the 1962 Missal.

## Territory and language guardrails

- The Latin typical edition and universal decrees control universal identity and rank.
- Official USCCB English controls national names and ranks.
- English names for universal entries not independently checked in the licensed 2011 altar book are marked by the publication's global statement as editorial identifiers.
- A national row never establishes a diocesan or religious-family proper.
- The document cutoff is 2026-07-17; later decrees require a new audit and revision.

## Non-controlling aids

Extracted text from temporary research PDFs was used for search and transcription, then checked against page images or official web records where amendments mattered. Search snippets, commercial calendars, crowd-sourced calendars, and devotional lists were not used to establish a normative row.
