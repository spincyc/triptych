# Research Scope

## Identity

- **Provider:** GPT.
- **Collection and genre:** Roman-rite liturgy; edition-specific calendar reference with a historical module.
- **Rite:** postconciliar Roman Rite.
- **Typical-book base:** *Missale Romanum*, editio typica tertia (2002), read with the 2008 *reimpressio emendata* and later universal decrees.
- **Territorial implementation:** Proper Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America associated with the 2011 U.S. English third edition.
- **Normative cutoff:** 2026-07-17.
- **Language:** normative Latin base; current official USCCB English is the control for U.S. labels. Other English labels are concise editorial identifiers, not translations offered as liturgical text.
- **Governing profiles:** `guidance/liturgy/roman-calendar-references.md` governs the whole leaf; `guidance/history/historical-accounts.md` governs the construction-history and timeline; `guidance/liturgy/postconciliar-propers.md` governs edition and territorial discipline where applicable.

## Question and intended reader

The work explains how the postconciliar Roman calendar was historically constructed and deliberately reformed, identifies substantive modifications, and supplies a complete recurring normative inventory for a reader assembling or checking the General Roman Calendar as used in the United States. It is not written as a civil-year Ordo.

## Included

1. The complete movable Proper of Time at the level of recurring normative identities and date rules.
2. Every fixed General Roman Calendar date and every celebration and rank on that date through the cutoff.
3. Universal additions and rank or title changes after the printed 2002 base, including the 2008 supplement and decrees through St John Henry Newman.
4. The complete United States national overlay: additions, optional memorials, upgraded ranks, displaced universal optional memorials, the required national day of prayer, optional special days, patronal status, and national or provincial placement rules.
5. A periodized construction history and a timeline whose selection criterion is a change to architecture, precedence, temporal cycle, universal inventory, or national overlay.

## Excluded

- prayers, readings, antiphons, chants, prefaces, and full formularies;
- a computed 2026 Ordo or any other civil-year resolution;
- diocesan, parish, cathedral, titular, dedication, patronal, and religious-family calendars;
- current canonical authorization to use another edition of the Roman Missal;
- holy-day obligation advice beyond flagging that it is a separate territorial canonical question;
- exhaustive histories of individual feasts or saints.

## Terminology and method

The work uses the postconciliar ranks *solemnity*, *feast*, *memorial*, and *optional memorial*. “Optional feast days” in the request is normalized to *optional memorials*. A “fixed row” is one civil date; a row can contain multiple distinct optional memorials. The published table uses the 2002 Latin calendar's rule that an entry with no printed rank is an optional memorial, then overlays every checked amendment.

The Proper of Time, universal fixed calendar, and U.S. proper are inventoried separately. Actual occurrence must add local calendars and apply the Universal Norms and table of liturgical days.

## Material uncertainties

1. Direct page-by-page collation against a licensed 2008 Latin altar book and a licensed 2011 U.S. English altar book remains outstanding. The 2008 integrations are controlled through official *Notitiae* records and current official calendars.
2. The USCCB recurring proper webpage says it was last updated in 2015. The 2026 official annual calendar confirms current implementation and the post-2015 universal additions, but an annual calendar is not treated as the promulgating source for the recurring proper.
3. The 24 July 2010 confirmation protocols and later U.S. amendments are recovered from official USCCB records, but direct collation of their implementation against a licensed 2011 altar book remains outstanding.
4. The broad ancient and medieval history is intentionally synthetic and now controlled by identified modern scholarship. No claim is made for a single recoverable origin of the temporal cycles; independent historical review remains outstanding.
5. The current canonical effect of a transferred U.S. holy day of obligation is outside scope and is deliberately referred to the current annual calendar and complementary norms.
