Checked 13--15 July 2026. “Used” means that the source controls or materially supports a claim in the publication; it does not mean that every page of a large work was relied upon. “Official public witness” distinguishes an authority-maintained text from an unofficial transcription. “Identified, collation outstanding” means the exact edition is bibliographically controlling but a licensed physical copy has not yet been checked formula by formula in this research session.

This audit is deliberately stricter than the bibliography. It records negative controls, unresolved claims, source status, and the rights boundary. It must be updated after any exact-book collation, independent review, or artifact rebuild.

### Edition conclusion

The public official sources are sufficient to establish the structural inventory, movement functions, roles, present U.S. adaptations, principal choice rules, and the special-prayer status. They are not sufficient to claim a diplomatic collation of every Latin or English formula. Short Latin incipits used as headings are identifiers; the publication supplies no prayer text for recitation. Hands-on book collation, independent specialist review, and rights review remain outstanding; they limit maturity claims but do not by themselves decide the separately recorded exact-snapshot distribution status.

## Claim-level corrections and rejected shortcuts

1. **“The Novus Ordo is the ancient Mass restored.”** Rejected. It contains ancient inheritance, medieval survival, retrieved functions, rewritten material, and genuinely new compositions and arrangements.
2. **“Vatican II wrote the current Missal.”** Rejected. The Council mandated principles; study groups prepared; Paul VI reviewed/promulgated; later typical editions and territorial approvals are distinct.
3. **“Eucharistic Prayer II is Hippolytus’s third-century Roman Canon.”** Rejected. The ancient church-order text and its attribution are disputed; the 1967 prayer freely adapts and supplements it.
4. **“Eucharistic Prayer IV is the Anaphora of St Basil restored.”** Rejected. It is a new composition influenced by Eastern patterns, with its own Roman architecture.
5. **“Explicit Spirit epiclesis was restored to Rome.”** Rejected as genealogy. Explicit Eastern epicleses are ancient; the Roman Canon has functional consecratory petitions without explicit invocation of the Spirit over the gifts.
6. **“The memorial acclamation is an ancient Roman placement.”** Rejected. Anamnesis and popular acclamation have ancient analogues; the present *Mysterium fidei* arrangement is a 1969 Roman structural innovation.
7. **“The peace has always been where it is.”** Rejected. Justin and Jerusalem place it before the anaphora; Augustine witnesses it after the Pater; Rome retained the latter horizon.
8. **“Both kinds give more Christ.”** Rejected by Trent and current GIRM. Either species contains the whole Christ; both may express the banquet sign more fully.
9. **“*Missa* etymologically means mission.”** Rejected. It historically means dismissal; missionary meaning is authoritative later reception and is explicit in the 2008 alternatives.
10. **“Every option should be displayed in one ideal Mass.”** Rejected. Alternatives are mutually exclusive or conditional and must be chosen integrally under their laws.
11. **“A contemporary U.S. custom is universal law.”** Rejected. U.S. posture, chant, reception, and both-kinds adaptations are named territorially.
12. **“The present English prayers may be paraphrased into a free hand-Missal.”** Rejected by project prayer/rights rules. The work uses analysis, not recitation wording.
