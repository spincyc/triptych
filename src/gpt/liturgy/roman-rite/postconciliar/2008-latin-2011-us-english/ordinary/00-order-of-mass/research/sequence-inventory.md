# Sequence inventory: The Order of Mass

## How to read this inventory

The numbering below is editorial navigation, not a taxonomy promulgated by the Roman Missal. It maps the publication's received primary headings back to the 2008 Latin third typical edition, the GIRM, the *Ordo Lectionum Missae*, and the current approved United States implementation. Units differ radically in scale. A short response, a variable proper slot, a procession, and a complete Eucharistic Prayer do not receive equal theological weight merely because each has a row. Three rows deliberately group adjacent acts for readable scale while preserving their distinct conditions and analysis: Sequence/Gospel acclamation, chalice mixing/wine presentation, and *In spiritu humilitatis*/optional incensation.

No celebration traverses every row. Alternative penitential forms, the sprinkling rite, Eucharistic Prayers, Creed forms, blessings, and dismissals are mutually exclusive or conditionally selected. The inventory maps the complete received possibility space; the published prose explains how a concrete Mass remains one action.

Status terms:

- **fixed:** belongs to the ordinary course unless a governing combined rite or exceptional form replaces it;
- **proper:** the slot is stable but its appointed text changes with the celebration;
- **conditional:** present only on specified days or circumstances;
- **approved option:** one received form is selected among alternatives;
- **locally determined:** competent territorial, diocesan, or celebrant judgment operates within stated law;
- **ministry-dependent:** role or ceremonial changes with the ministers actually present.

## Four arcs and two principal liturgies

| Arc | Received purpose | Principal movements | Controlling synthesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introductory Rites | Those gathered establish communion and are disposed for Word and Eucharist | Entrance, altar veneration, sign and greeting, penitential branch, Kyrie, Gloria, Collect | GIRM 46--54 |
| Liturgy of the Word | God speaks; the Church listens, meditates, professes, and intercedes | readings, Psalm, acclamation, Gospel, homily, Creed, Universal Prayer | GIRM 55--71; OLM |
| Liturgy of the Eucharist | Gifts are prepared; the Church gives thanks and offers in Christ; the faithful are fed | preparation, Eucharistic Prayer, Communion Rite | GIRM 72--89 |
| Concluding Rites | Blessing and dismissal release the assembly for Christian witness | necessary notices, blessing, dismissal, altar veneration and departure | GIRM 90 |

GIRM 28 calls Word and Eucharist two parts so closely joined that they form one act of worship. The opening and conclusion are real rites, not two further “tables” parallel to the proclamation and sacrifice.

## Branch controls that must not be flattened

| Decision point | Received alternatives or qualification | Negative control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introductory threshold | Penitential Forms A, B, or C; Sunday sprinkling may replace the act; combined rites may omit or reshape the Introductory Rites | No celebration accumulates all forms; the presidential conclusion is not sacramental absolution |
| Word | Readings and Psalm are proper; second reading and Sequence are conditional; acclamation follows season and singing; homily and Creed have stated conditions | Non-biblical texts may not replace readings or Psalm; a weekday outline may not be projected onto Sunday |
| Universal Prayer | Stable prayer categories, locally worded intentions, differentiated roles | Project-created sample prayers are outside this study and outside the repository's prayer policy |
| Preparation | Gift procession and incense vary; bread and wine are the Eucharistic matter; alms remain spatially distinct | A collection is not offered on the altar as Eucharistic matter; unconsecrated gifts are not already Christ's Body and Blood |
| Eucharistic Prayer | Only one approved prayer is chosen; Preface and insert rules differ; Priest alone proclaims it in full | No private Eucharistic Prayer, hybrid, or communal recitation is lawful |
| Peace | Prayer and dialogue remain; invitation/sign are conditional and territorially shaped | The sign is not a general greeting or a sacramental absolution |
| Communion | One or both species; ordinary and extraordinary ministers; territorial posture and mode; eligibility governed by doctrine and law | A lawful disciplinary option is not an assertion that every option is pastorally identical or universally compulsory |
| Blessing and dismissal | Simple or enriched blessing; four dismissal formulas | Announcements do not follow the dismissal; “mission” is a received theological development, not the original lexical meaning of *missa* |

## Completeness notes

- The Kyrie is absent as a separate movement when Penitential Form C incorporates it and ordinarily after the Sunday sprinkling rite; it is not thereby abolished from the postconciliar Order.
- The appointed proper text, not this stable exposition, controls the literary exegesis of a Collect, reading, Psalm, Sequence, Offertory or Communion chant, Prayer over the Offerings, Preface, or Prayer after Communion.
- The Eucharistic Prayer begins with the Preface dialogue and ends with the Great Amen. The memorial acclamation is inside that prayer but is addressed to Christ's Paschal mystery, not to the elevated species.
- Intercessions do not occupy one universal location: Eucharistic Prayer I surrounds the consecratory center with commemorations; later prayers characteristically place extended intercession after anamnesis and offering.
- The faithful's Communion is integral to the rite's purpose but conditional upon actual communicants who are properly disposed. No sequence inventory can judge a particular person's conscience or canonical situation.
- Announcements are not a fifth part of Mass. The dismissal precedes altar veneration and departure; the rite does not prescribe a post-dismissal congregational hymn as another constituent movement.
