# Unit source matrix: The Order of Mass

This is the passage-level evidence record required by the ordinary-expositions profile. It follows the editorial numbers in `sequence-inventory.md`; the numbers are navigation, not a taxonomy promulgated by the Missal. Each row identifies the received boundary, ritual agent and mode, scriptural grammar in context, a historically calibrated witness or terminus, and the doctrinal/whole-action relation. “Analogy” means illumination without direct textual descent. “Direct” means the liturgical unit explicitly takes up the cited wording or act. “Structural” means an ancient witness attests a related liturgical function but not the present formula.

The matrix records claim classes more finely than the reader-facing exposition. It never authorizes a prayer translation. The 2008 Latin book and current 2011 U.S. book remain subject to the exact-copy collation limitation in `source-audit.md`.

| **4.1 — *Introitus***. Entrance begins as the Priest enters and lasts through arrival/veneration as chant permits. | Cross, candles, Book of Gospels where used, ministers, Deacon and Priest process; all stand and sing/listen. Fixed processional slot; text is proper or lawfully selected. U.S. chant options are territorial (GIRM 46--48, 120--122). | Israel’s processions toward sanctuary (e.g. Pss 24, 68, 122) give typological grammar, not a mandated route. | **4.4 — *Actus paenitentialis***. Invitation, silence, one of Forms A/B/C, presidential conclusion. | Priest invites/concludes; all recollect and take their assigned form; Form C ministerial tropes as provided. Current three alternatives and initial position were organized in the postconciliar reform. | Common acknowledgment disposes but the conclusion lacks sacramental-absolution efficacy (GIRM 51). Form A confesses across vertical/horizontal communion; B is compact scriptural dialogue; C invokes the merciful Christ. Controls: GIRM 51; CIC 959--997 for distinct Penance; *Didache* 14. |
| **4.5 — *Benedictio et aspersio aquae***. Sunday option replacing Penitential Act. | Priest blesses/sprinkles as provided; ministers assist; people sing. Approved Sunday option, especially Easter; Kyrie ordinarily not separately accumulated (GIRM 51; Missal Appendix II). | Ezek 36:25--27, John 3:5, Rom 6:3--11 and 1 Pet 3:20--21 directly/typologically govern baptismal washing and Pasch. | Justin *Apology* 61 and Jerusalem *Mystagogical Catechesis* II.4--6 interpret Baptism but do not describe this option. Sunday Asperges is securely a Western pre-Mass rite by 9th/10th c.; Hincmar is an early Frankish witness. Postconciliar Missal relocates it inside the opening. | The branch makes baptismal identity bodily and Paschal; it replaces rather than supplements the ordinary penitential branch. Controls: Appendix II; GIRM 51; Justin; Jerusalem; CCC 1213--1284. |
| **4.6 — *Kyrie, eleison***. Litany after penitential act unless integrated/otherwise displaced. | Ordinarily all sing/recite; cantor/choir/people alternate. Normally two invocations each, musically extensible. Fixed conditional acclamation (GIRM 52, 125). | Gospel cries to the Davidic Lord (Matt 15:22; 20:30--31; Mark 10:47--48) directly supply merciful-acclamation grammar: petition and confession coincide. | Tertullian *Adv. Marc.* IV.9 and Origen, *Disputatio* 4.24, witness related Gospel/prayer usage. Gregory I, *Registrum* IX.26 (598), securely describes Roman alternation and *Christe*; he does not claim invention. 8th-c. ordines witness ninefold grouping; the present ordinary form is postconciliar. | Addresses risen Lord, not generic guilt. Relation: need becomes Christological praise before Gloria/Collect. Controls: GIRM 52; Tertullian; Origen; Gregory IX.26; Gospel contexts. |
| **4.7 — *Gloria in excelsis Deo***. Ancient hymn when calendar prescribes. | Intoned by Priest/cantor/choir; sung by all together or alternately; never replaced. Conditional fixed hymn (GIRM 53, 126). | Luke 2:14 is direct opening within angelic Messianic announcement. John 1:29, Rev 5, Phil 2:6--11 and trinitarian doxology illuminate its progression from Father’s glory to Lamb’s mercy and Spirit’s communion. | Greek morning-hymn relative appears in *Apost. Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 38.17, supplies a patristic analogue, not textual ancestry. *OR I* shows restricted use; papal authorship notices are retrospective. | A hymn, not presidential prayer: praise expands into Christological supplication and closes trinitarianly. Seasonal omission gives Advent/Lent their received restraint. Controls: GIRM 53; early hymn witnesses; Gregory Nazianzen; *OR I*. |
| **4.8 — *Oremus* and Collect**. Invitation, silence, one proper presidential oration, long conclusion, Amen. | Priest invites and gathers at chair; all silently pray and ratify. Proper slot; fixed singular presidential prayer (GIRM 30, 32, 45, 54, 127). | Matt 18:19--20 and 1 Tim 2 illuminate common prayer; trinitarian mediation rests on Eph 2:18 and Col 3:17. No one biblical text supplies the variable Collect. | Tertullian *Adv. Roman orations survive in sacramentaries; manuscript dates do not equal composition dates. | Silence permits individual petitions; the oration “collects” ecclesial prayer. Controls: GIRM 54 and its 2021 U.S. emendment; Tertullian; Origen; Hippo; relevant proper and sacramentary. |

## 5. Liturgy of the Word

| Unit and received boundary | Agent, action, mode, and status | Scripture in context | Checked history and calibration | Doctrine, relation, and principal controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **5.1 — *Lectio prima***. Appointed first biblical pericope at ambo. | Lector or another lay reader when none instituted; all sit/listen; reader acclaims and people answer. Proper slot (GIRM 29, 55--60, 128). | Neh 8:1--12 is a structural analogy of public Torah hearing, interpretation, assent and response. Luke 4:16--21 shows Christ proclaiming fulfillment. 2 Tim 3:14--17 governs inspiration/use. | Justin 67 securely records apostolic memoirs and prophets read as time allowed. Würzburg *Comes* witnesses Roman lectionary organization; mature Roman Mass commonly had one pre-Gospel lesson. Current Sunday OT/Acts selection belongs to postconciliar OLM. | Christ is present in proclaimed Word (SC 7); Scripture is venerated with Christ's Body without becoming the same mode (DV 21) and is not replaced by nonbiblical reading (GIRM 57). Controls: OLM; DV 21; GIRM 29, 57--59. |
| **5.2 — *Psalmus responsorius***. Appointed Psalm after first reading. | Psalmist/cantor sings verses from ambo; people answer or listen in approved mode. Proper, integral Word; limited U.S. alternatives; nonbiblical substitution forbidden (GIRM 61, 129). | Psalms are inspired prayer in their own genres; Luke 24:44 locates Psalms in Christological fulfillment without erasing original voice. Col 3:16 supports ecclesial psalmody. Each appointed Psalm requires literary audit. | Athanasius, *Letter to Marcellinus* 10--12, and Augustine, *Enarr.* 85.1, 148.1, interpret the Psalter's personal/ecclesial voice. *OR I* 10 has cantor’s *Responsum*; later practice develops into ornate Gradual. Present form is not copied from one ancient order. | Heard Word becomes inspired response rather than interlude. Controls: GIRM 61; OLM; Athanasius; Augustine; *OR I*. |
| **5.3 — *Lectio secunda***. Appointed apostolic/New Testament pericope where prescribed. | Lector proclaims; all sit/listen and answer. Proper and conditional, especially Sundays/solemnities (GIRM 57--59, 130). | Acts and Epistles retain distinct historical addressees and arguments; e.g. 1 Cor 10--11 links Eucharist and ecclesial judgment. They are not merely commentary on the first reading. | Multiple readings exist in ancient traditions; Justin’s prophets/apostolic memoirs do not prove today’s three-reading cycle. Present Sunday triad and semi-continuous apostolic cycles were standardized in postconciliar OLM. | Apostolic witness mediates between Israel/Gospel without needing a forced one-word theme. Controls: OLM; GIRM 57--59; direct epistle context. |
| **5.4 — *Sequentia* and Gospel acclamation**. Two adjacent acts treated distinctly in one analytical unit: Sequence where appointed; Alleluia/seasonal verse before Gospel. | Cantor/choir begins; all stand and acclaim when sung. Sequence conditional; acclamation governed by season and omission rules if not sung (GIRM 62--64, 131). | “Alleluia” is biblical command/praise, climactic in Rev 19:1--9 amid judgment, reign and wedding feast. The verse is proper Scripture. Each Sequence needs its own literary/source audit. | Gregory’s 598 letter reports Roman Alleluia and received Jerusalem association; *OR I* 10 has Alleluia/Tract after Responsum. Sequences are medieval poetic expansions; Notker is important but not author of all. Retained sequence histories differ. | Acclamation welcomes the Lord about to speak, not commentary external to Word. Standing and song prepare Gospel. Controls: GIRM 62--64; OLM; Gregory; *OR I*. |
| **5.5 — Gospel preparation and procession**. Blessing/quiet preparation; Gospel book, incense, lights and movement to ambo. | Deacon seeks blessing and carries the book; if no Deacon is present, the Priest prepares quietly and, when the book rests on the altar, takes it to the ambo; ministers accompany as provided; all stand. Ministry-dependent (GIRM 60, 131--133, 175). | Isa 6:5--8 analogically illuminates purification and commission; Rom 10:14--17 governs sent proclamation. Candles/incense draw on biblical honor/prayer imagery without literal prescription. | Ignatius, *Trallians* 2.3, bounds diaconal-mystery interpretation but does not describe the procession. Justin attests memoirs but no developed procession. *OR I* 11 gives blessing, book, incense, lights, ambo and veneration. | Commission distinguishes Gospel ministry; material honors confess Christ’s presence without depreciating prior readings. Controls: GIRM 60, 133, 175; Ignatius; *OR I*. |
| **5.6 — *Evangelium***. Greeting, announcement/signing, appointed Gospel, acclamation and book veneration. | Deacon or Priest alone proclaims; people stand, sign, hear and answer; book may be incensed/kissed; Bishop-dependent branch follows CE 74, 141. Fixed frame with proper text (GIRM 29, 58--60, 134). | Gospel pericope controls its own exegesis. Ezek 3 and Rev 10 analogically illuminate receiving a book; Luke 4 supplies public proclamation. Signing forehead/lips/heart is received ritual, not an exegetical claim about one verse. | Irenaeus III.11.8 and Augustine *Tract. in Io.* 1.8--9 distinguish fourfold Gospel, herald and Word without dating ritual signs. Developed ceremonial is secure in *OR I*; signing/kissing have layered histories. | Christ speaks when Gospel is read (SC 7; GIRM 29). Acclamation confesses this mode, distinct from Eucharistic Presence. Controls: Irenaeus; Augustine; CE; *OR I*. |
| **5.7 — *Homilia* and silence**. Ordained preaching from sacred text/mystery, followed by suitable silence. | Bishop/Priest/Deacon according to law; congregation sits/listens. Required Sundays/holy days absent grave reason; strongly recommended otherwise; never lay preaching at Mass (GIRM 29, 45, 55--56, 65--66, 136; CIC 767). | Luke 4:21 and 24:27, 32; Acts 2; 1 Tim 4:13; 2 Tim 4:1--5 govern exposition/exhortation. Context resists making preaching personal commentary detached from readings/mystery. | Justin 67 witnesses exhortation; Augustine/Chrysostom show variety; Gregory, *Regula pastoralis* III prologue, requires differentiated address. Trent XXII.8 charged explanation; Vatican II restored liturgical status/obligation. | Homily mediates Word toward faith/life but does not replace Scripture or become political speech. Controls: SC 24, 35, 52; GIRM 65--66; CIC 767; Gregory. |
| **5.8 — *Professio fidei***. Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed; Apostles’ Creed allowed especially Lent/Easter; baptismal promises in appointed rites. | Priest and people recite/sing together, all standing; bow/genuflection as prescribed. Conditional stable text/approved alternative (GIRM 67--68, 137). | Matt 28:19 and the baptismal rule of faith underlie trinitarian confession; Phil 2 and 1 Cor 15 illuminate Christological/Paschal clauses. Creed is a canonical synthesis, not one biblical quotation. | Cyril, *Cat. Lect.* 5.12, and Tertullian, *De praescr.* 13, witness synthesis/rule of faith without supplying the current text. Nicaea/Constantinople/Chalcedon control doctrine; Toledo III orders Eucharistic use in 589; Rome adopts in 1014. Western *Filioque* and Apostles’ Creed have separate histories. | Corporate answer and ecclesial personal assent. Controls: GIRM 67--68; Cyril; Tertullian; conciliar acts; CCC 185--1065. |
| **5.9 — *Oratio universalis***. Introduction, categories of intentions, petitions/response, concluding prayer. | Priest introduces/concludes at chair; Deacon/cantor/reader/lay faithful announces at ambo or suitable place; all stand and pray. Normally present with congregation; wording local under stable categories (GIRM 69--71, 138). | 1 Tim 2:1--6 directly commands prayers for all and authorities within the one-mediator horizon; Acts 12:5 and Eph 6:18 support ecclesial intercession. | Justin 65, 67; *1 Clement* 59.2--61.3; *Apost. Const.* VIII.6--10 witness broad intercession. Comparable ordinary Roman sequence later receded; SC 53 restores function. No exact modern wording descends from an early witness. | Baptized people intercede for Church, public needs, suffering, salvation and locality. No project-authored petitions. Controls: GIRM 69--71; SC 53; Justin; *1 Clement*. |

## 6. Preparation of the Gifts

| Unit and received boundary | Agent, action, mode, and status | Scripture in context | Checked history and calibration | Doctrine, relation, and principal controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **6.1 — Altar, procession, gifts, collection, chant**. Altar prepared; bread/wine brought; other gifts received; chant accompanies. | Acolyte/servers prepare; faithful may present; Priest/Deacon receive; collection serves poor/Church and stays off Eucharistic table. Stable preparation with optional procession/incense and variable chant (GIRM 72--74, 139--140). | Gen 14; 1 Chr 29:10--14; Mal 1:11; 2 Cor 8--9 give typological/direct grammar of bread/wine, gift from God, pure offering and collection. None makes money Eucharistic matter. | Justin 65, 67: mixed bread/wine, collection for needy. Current simplified order is not verbatim reconstruction. | Faith confessed becomes material thanksgiving and charity. Only wheat bread/grape wine are Eucharistic matter; gifts for poor are distinct. Controls: GIRM 72--74; CIC 924; Justin; Irenaeus; *OR I*. |
| **6.2 — *Benedictus es, Domine* over bread**. Quiet formula, aloud with response when no chant. | Priest holds/places bread and prays; people may acclaim only under rubric. Fixed preparation formula (GIRM 75, 141). | 1 Chr 29:10--14 and Jewish/scriptural blessing idiom directly shape blessing of Creator; Gen 1--2 and Ps 104 frame creation/human labor. John 6 is theological horizon but bread is not yet consecrated. | Formula composed for postconciliar rite in biblical/Jewish blessing idiom. Ancient creation/thanksgiving in Didache, Justin and Irenaeus does not prove direct unchanged table-blessing genealogy. Consilium dossier needed for exact textual derivation. | Receives creation and labor, points toward sacramental “bread of life,” does not anticipate consecration. Controls: GIRM 75, 141; Paul VI *Missale Romanum* on revised offering. |
| **6.3 — Mixed chalice and *Benedictus es* over wine**. Two successive acts treated distinctly in one analytical unit: water is added quietly; the chalice formula follows. | Deacon or Priest mixes; Priest presents/prays; people may acclaim as above. Fixed successive action (GIRM 75, 142). | John 19:34 provides later typological resonance, not institution of mixture. Scriptural wine imagery (Ps 104; Isa 25; Synoptic cup) and 2 Pet 1:4 illuminate received prayer theology. | Justin 65, 67 securely attest mixed cup; Cyprian Ep. 63 argues from institution and reads water/people united to wine/Christ. Current private mixing prayer abbreviates late-antique Christmas collect repurposed in medieval books; form is modernly shortened. | Ancient matter-practice and later Incarnation/ecclesial symbolism meet without confusing water with a second offering. Controls: GIRM 75; Cyprian Ep. 63; CIC 924. |
| **6.4 — *In spiritu humilitatis* and incensation**. Two successive acts treated distinctly in one analytical unit: quiet self-commendation; then optional incensation of gifts/cross/altar/Priest/people. | Priest bows/prays; Priest/Deacon incenses as prescribed. The U.S. assembly remains seated through the preparation until *Orate, fratres*. Quiet formula fixed; incense optional (GIRM 43, 75, 143--144). | Dan 3:38--40 is direct: exiled Israel without sanctuary asks contrite self-offering be accepted. Ps 141:2 and Rev 8:3--4 analogically illuminate incense/prayer. | Prayer belongs to 9th--11th-c. Western offertory family and survives reform. Incense ancient at Roman entrance/Gospel; Amalarius c.832 is first clear offertory witness and says then unknown at Rome. Current route is simplified. | Humility judges offerer; incense honors gifts, altar/cross, ordained minister and baptized people in differentiated communion. Neither smoke nor subjective contrition consecrates. Controls: GIRM 43, 75; Daniel 3; Amalarius. |
| **6.5 — *Lavabo***. Priest washes hands at side with quiet prayer. | Server pours; Priest washes and privately seeks cleansing. Fixed ministerial action (GIRM 76, 145). | Ps 51:4 is direct current formula context of sin, cleansing and restored praise. Exod 30:17--21 offers priestly-washing typology; Matt 27:24 is an inappropriate contrary context. | *OR I* 14 records practical washing after receiving gifts. Jerusalem Myst. Cat. V.2 gives 4th-c. moral interpretation of washed hands, not Roman genealogy. Medieval Roman rite expands Ps 25; current formula uses Ps 51 and reduced action. | Practical origin and inward desire coexist. Washing does not imply sacramental absolution or make the Priest uniquely sinful; it marks ministerial threshold. Controls: GIRM 76; *OR I*; Jerusalem V.2. |
| **6.6 — *Orate, fratres* and response**. Public invitation after preparation, people answer. | Priest faces people/invites; all stand in U.S. from invitation and answer. Fixed dialogue (GIRM 77, 146; U.S. GIRM 43). | Rom 12:1; 1 Pet 2:5; Heb 13:15--16 directly/analogically govern baptismal self-offering and praise. Hebrews 7--10 keeps Christ’s singular priesthood/sacrifice central. | Invitation-response forms grow through Carolingian/high-medieval books; received pair printed in 1474 Roman Missal. Retained through reform; not patristic restoration. | “My and your” distinguishes ordained and baptismal modes while “our” joins one sacrifice. Controls: LG 10; GIRM 77; Trent XXII; 1474 Missal. |
| **6.7 — *Oratio super oblata***. One proper presidential prayer, short conclusion, Amen. | Priest proclaims audibly; all stand and assent. Proper slot (GIRM 30, 32, 77, 146). | Biblical offering/mediation language varies by proper; Eph 5:2 and Heb 9--10 give Christological control. No universal exegesis substitutes for actual prayer. | Ancient Roman *orationes super oblata* survive in Veronese/Gelasian traditions. Title *secreta* and quiet recitation develop separately; mid-8th-c. Frankish direction is first secure quiet evidence. Reform restores audible proclamation/people’s Amen. Each proper differs in age. | Seals preparation and crosses into thanksgiving; prayer does not consecrate in isolation. Controls: GIRM 77; proper Missal text; sacramentary audit. |

## 7. Eucharistic Prayer: common movements

| Unit and received boundary | Agent, action, mode, and status | Scripture in context | Checked history and calibration | Doctrine, relation, and principal controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **7.1 — *Dialogus praefationis* and Preface**. Greeting/lift/thanksgiving dialogue through proper or bound Preface. | Priest and all exchange dialogue; Priest alone continues presidential thanksgiving. Fixed dialogue; Preface proper/selected; some inseparable (GIRM 78--79a, 147--148, 364--365). | Lam 3:41 and Col 3:1--4 analogically illuminate lifted hearts; 1 Thess 5:18 and Eph 5:20 directly govern thanksgiving; Rev 4--5 supplies heavenly horizon. | Cyprian *Dom. or.* 31 interprets *Sursum corda*; Jerusalem V.4--5 and disputed *Apost. Trad.* 4 witness dialogue. Ancient across regions, no single Roman author. Roman sacramentaries preserve many Prefaces; each has separate provenance. | Assembly assents to the Priest’s address to Father through Son in Spirit. “Hearts above” is moral/eschatological attention, not spatial escape. Controls: GIRM 78--79a; Cyprian; Jerusalem. |
| **7.2 — *Sanctus***. Common acclamation closing Preface. | Priest and all people sing/recite with heavenly powers; fixed (GIRM 79b, 148). | Isa 6:1--8 directly supplies thrice-holy temple vision of judgment/purification/mission; Ps 118:25--26 and Gospel entry narratives supply blessed-coming acclamation; Rev 4:8 joins heavenly worship. | Jerusalem V.6 and *Apost. Const.* VIII.12 securely place Sanctus in 4th-c. Eastern anaphora; 7th-c. Roman-Canon manuscripts securely transmit combined form. Disputed *Apost. Trad.* specimen lacks it. No secure 4th-c. Western locus claimed. | Joins earthly Church to angels and turns salvation thanksgiving toward consecratory petition. Not an interruption outside EP. Controls: GIRM 79b; Isaiah/Revelation contexts. |
| **7.3 — Presidential voice and assembly**. One EP from dialogue through Amen. | Priest alone proclaims full prayer; concelebrants assigned parts; Deacon does not recite; people participate in faith, silence and acclamations. Fixed ministerial distribution (GIRM 30--34, 78, 147, 216--236). | Heb 7--10 establishes Christ’s singular priesthood; 1 Cor 12 and 1 Pet 2 distinguish one body/baptismal priesthood. Rev 5:8--14 shows ordered voices/acclamation analogically. | Justin 65 emphasizes presidential thanksgiving and popular Amen; later orders distribute anaphoral ministry variously. Present concelebration/voice governed by current book, not inferred from ancient examples. | Active participation is differentiated communion; collective EP recitation falsifies presidential form. Controls: LG 10; GIRM 30--34, 78; RS 51--54. |
| **7.4 — Epiclesis**. Consecratory and communion-sanctification petitions distributed by each prayer. | Priest extends hands over offerings where prescribed; prayer remains presidential. Constitutive function; explicitness/location vary (GIRM 79c). | Luke 1:35 and Acts 2 analogically illuminate Spirit’s sanctifying power; Rom 8 and 1 Cor 12:13 ground communion/unity in Spirit. No verse is a ritual formula. | Jerusalem V.7 and *Apost. Const.* VIII.12 explicitly invoke Spirit on gifts; Basil, *De Spiritu Sancto* 27.66, supplies broader pneumatic/liturgical control; disputed *Apost. Trad.* has a reconstructed petition. Roman Canon’s *Quam oblationem*/*Supplices* function differently. | Church asks Father’s sanctification of gifts and communicants; Word/Spirit and institution cannot be opposed. Not “restored ancient Roman epiclesis.” Controls: GIRM 79c; Basil; CCC 1104--1107; each EP. |
| **7.5 — Institution narrative and consecration**. Lord’s words/actions within each approved EP; showing/genuflection. | Priest narrates and speaks received words in persona Christi within prayer; people adore in silence; bells/incense as allowed. Fixed in each EP (GIRM 79d, 150--151). | Matt 26:26--29; Mark 14:22--25; Luke 22:14--20; 1 Cor 11:23--26 are direct institution controls in Paschal, covenantal, ecclesial contexts. Heb 9--10 guards once-for-all sacrifice. | Justin 66 witnesses tradition; *De sacramentis* IV.4.14--17, IV.5.21--24 supplies a Western parallel; Aquinas, *ST* III q.78 and q.82 a.1, controls effective speech/minister. Host elevation spreads around 1200; chalice later. Paul VI standardized words. | By Christ’s words and Spirit, bread/wine become Body/Blood; one Calvary sacrifice made sacramentally present, not repeated. Controls: Trent XIII/XXII; Ambrose; Aquinas; CCC 1373--1381; GIRM 79d. |
| **7.6 — *Mysterium fidei* and memorial acclamation**. Invitation after chalice showing; one approved people’s acclamation. | Priest invites; people acclaim; Priest does not join their assigned text as a substitute role. Fixed invitation/one option (GIRM 34, 151; *Ordo Missae*). | 1 Cor 11:26 directly joins eating/drinking with proclamation of death until coming; 1 Cor 15 gives Paschal confession; Rev 22:20 gives eschatological prayer. | Older Roman Canon placed *mysterium fidei* within chalice formula. Paul VI explicitly moved it to introduce popular acclamation in 1969. Ancient anamneses/acclamations are analogues, not proof of current Roman placement/formulas. | Acclamation addresses/proclaims Christ’s Pasch and coming, not the elevated species as grammatical addressee. It belongs inside one EP. Controls: Paul VI *Missale Romanum*; GIRM 34, 151. |
| **7.7 — Anamnesis and oblation**. Church remembers Pasch and offers victim/gifts in each prayer’s order. | Priest voices; concelebrants may join assigned portion; people unite through silence/acclamation. Constitutive (GIRM 79e--f). | Luke 22:19; 1 Cor 11:24--26 directly command memorial; Exod 12 gives covenantal typology; Heb 9--10 defines once-for-all self-offering; Rom 12:1 governs ecclesial self-oblation analogically. | Disputed *Apost. Trad.* 4 remembers death/resurrection and offers bread/cup; *De sacramentis* IV.6 parallels Roman *Unde et memores*, patriarchal offerings and heavenly altar. Widespread function, not identical wording/sequence. | Memorial is liturgical proclamation/action, not recollection; Church offers spotless Victim and herself in him. Controls: GIRM 79e--f; Trent XXII; CCC 1362--1372. |

## Cross-row negative controls

- A secure ancient **function** does not establish the exact current text, position, minister, or legal condition.
- An Eastern or North African mystagogy can illuminate Roman worship without becoming its genealogy.
- A current approved option is not inferior merely because it is modern, and not universal merely because it is common locally.
- The Eucharistic Prayer is one presidential prayer. Analytical rows 7.1--7.9 must never justify fragmenting it or constructing a hybrid.
- The proper of the day supplies literary content to variable prayers, chants, readings, Psalm, acclamation and Preface. This stable matrix explains those positions, not every proper text.
- No English prayer wording in this record is intended or sufficient for recitation. Exact approved books control celebration.
