# Internal interpretive and completion review

Reviewed 15 July 2026 against `guidance/editorial.md` and `guidance/liturgy/ordinary-expositions.md`. This records an independent internal editorial and source audit of the integrated draft. It is not independent specialist, theological, canonical, legal, or ecclesiastical approval.

## Coverage result

- The publication, sequence inventory, and passage-level source matrix contain the same 62 editorial units: 8 Introductory Rites, 9 Liturgy of the Word, 7 Preparation of the Gifts, 9 common Eucharistic-Prayer movements, 4 principal Eucharistic Prayers, 9 prayers for particular circumstances, 12 Communion movements, and 4 Concluding Rites.
- Every unit has a received boundary and status, ministerial/action field, principal Scripture read in context, calibrated historical witness or limit, doctrinal relation, and whole-order purpose in the matrix.
- The Sequence/Gospel acclamation, mixed chalice/wine presentation, and *In spiritu humilitatis*/incensation rows are disclosed as editorial groupings of adjacent but distinct acts. Their conditions and expositions remain separate.
- Headings use received names, standard ritual designations, or incipits as the primary identity and subordinate the interpretive title. Units receive proportionate rather than mechanically equal treatment.

## Corrections made after the audit

- Harmonized the GIRM 54 chronology: confirmation on 3 February and U.S. effect on 17 February 2021, distinct from the emendations promulgated on 22 October 2021. Added the official conference record and the official June 2026 status record for the future U.S. Lectionary.
- Added the tabernacle-in-sanctuary genuflection branch at entrance and departure, including the head-bow rule for cross and candle bearers; removed an orientation-dependent statement after *Orate, fratres*; completed the Priest-without-Deacon Gospel procession; clarified the Deacon/Priest division at the Great Doxology; and removed an unsupported posture exception during incensation.
- Reconciled the bibliography and source audit with the sources actually invoked, including the chant and ceremonial books, patristic and early Christian witnesses, sacramentary editions, medieval formula witnesses, twentieth-century reform acts, Eucharistic-Prayer dossiers, and current U.S. records. Corresponding movement-matrix rows were expanded where the cited witness materially controls the unit.
- Recast overview paragraphs for the current Eucharistic Prayers as remote architectural and theological analysis rather than sequential substitutes for their approved wording. No current English prayer, ritual dialogue, or project-created translation is supplied.
- Removed the internal `O00` ordering key from the reader-facing title page and relabeled the repeated movement field as “Status and conditions.”

## Doctrinal and historical findings

- No material internal contradiction was found concerning Eucharistic sacrifice, Real Presence and transubstantiation, the whole Christ under either species, the relation of common and ministerial priesthood, the unity of the Eucharistic Prayer, or the distinction between received rite and editorial synthesis.
- Historical language is calibrated especially for the *Didache*, the work conventionally called the *Apostolic Tradition*, Jerusalem mystagogy, *Ordo Romanus I*, Eucharistic Prayers II and IV, the position of the peace, epiclesis terminology, and the history and later missionary reception of *missa*.
- The Latin typical edition, U.S. territorial implementation, options, proper slots, customs, combined-rite exceptions, and separately authorized children's prayers remain distinct.

## Limits that remain visible

- Exact formula-level collation against licensed 2008 Latin and 2011 U.S. altar books remains outstanding. The work therefore uses only short identifying Latin incipits and third-person analysis.
- Independent review remains outstanding in Roman liturgical history, patristics, Latin, sacramental theology, canon law, music, and current U.S. practice.
- Work-specific rights review and any needed liturgical-text permission remain unresolved. The public-release manifest must therefore retain this publication on `hold`; no existing authorization hash may be extended to it by inference.
- Production verification and the final artifact hash are recorded in the source audit only after the reviewed PDF is built, inspected page by page, and installed.
