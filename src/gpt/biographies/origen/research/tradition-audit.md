# Origen — tradition audit

This record separates earliest located witness, later reception, historical
status, and responsible significance. “Not established” means not established
within the stated source search; it is not proof that no local story ever
circulated. “Early” does not mean eyewitness, neutral, or physically verified.

| Tradition or memorable claim | Earliest located witness / absence | Later reception | Historical status | Responsible significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origen was a pagan Greek who converted as an adult. | Porphyry, hostile notice quoted in Eusebius, HE 6.19, describes movement from Greek life to Christian teaching; Eusebius's HE 6.1–3 instead gives Christian childhood and a martyr father. | Conversion-biography shorthand | Witnesses conflict; Porphyry is valuable hostile N/E but may use a conversion frame or confusion. Eusebius is later and apologetic, yet supplies the sustained family account. | Preserve conflict. “Greek” in Porphyry proves education/cultural placement more readily than modern ethnicity. |
| Adamantius means Origen was literally made of steel or was officially surnamed “Man of Steel.” | Eusebius knows the additional name; no birth record or original explanation was located. | Praise of tireless labor and endurance | Name early E; status as given name, cognomen, sobriquet, and original force unresolved. | Symbolic interpretation may explain later memory only when not presented as bodily fact or registered name. |
| Origen founded a university formally named the Catechetical School of Alexandria. | Eusebius, HE 6.3–6 and 6.15, describes teachers, Demetrius's oversight, pupils, and changing division of instruction; no charter or register survives. | Institutional succession histories | Real teaching and episcopal relation E; modern corporate continuity, campus, departments, degrees, and fixed curriculum unproved. | “School” can name a network and practice if institutional development remains visible. |
| Clement was certainly Origen's long-term personal master. | Eusebius, HE 6.6, calls Origen Clement's pupil. | Alexandrian lineage from Pantaenus through Clement to Origen | Early received relation; duration, location, curriculum, and degree of direct study lack independent control. | Honor intellectual and probable personal influence without fabricating a continuous faculty appointment. |
| Origen studied with the same Ammonius Saccas who taught Plotinus. | Porphyry's fragment in HE 6.19 names Ammonius but does not securely identify him with Plotinus's teacher. | Christian-Platonist pedigree | Possible K, not established; shared names and Porphyry's purposes matter. | Serious philosophical formation is secure without constructing a documentary master-to-master lineage. |
| Origen's mother hid his clothes to prevent his joining Leonides in martyrdom. | Eusebius, HE 6.2.4–6, about a century later. | Hagiography of youthful zeal and maternal prudence | Specific E biographical scene without independent corroboration. | Preserves remembered maternal agency and immature zeal when not embellished as eyewitness dialogue. |
| The words Origen sent Leonides survive as a complete autograph letter. | Eusebius, HE 6.2.6, preserves a short exhortation from a now-lost letter. | Martyrdom anthologies and quotation culture | A fragment through E, not an extant autograph or complete text. | The line witnesses remembered filial exhortation; translation and context must not be invented. |
| Origen certainly castrated himself because of Matthew 19:12. | Eusebius, HE 6.8.1--3, gives act and motives; the extant Greek *Commentary on Matthew* 15.1--5 rejects genital mutilation as the verse's meaning. Crouzel/Trigg/Hanson accept the story; Chadwick/Cox/Szram doubt or suspend judgment; Caner supplies practice context. | Ascetic warning, scandal, polemic, sensational biography | Early specific but disputed E against a later A counter-locus. No admission, independent contemporary witness, medical record, or body survives. | Keep as serious unresolved report about ascetic literalism and controversy—not comedy, certainty, or an episode to hide. |
| Demetrius expelled Origen solely from jealousy. | Eusebius, HE 6.8 and 6.23, supplies jealousy as defender's moral explanation. | Innocent-genius versus envious-bishop narrative | Rupture secure; exclusive private motive unproved. | Episcopal authorization, irregular ordination, ascetic status, teaching, doctrine, and personality may be relevant, but their weights cannot be invented. |
| Two Alexandrian synods conducted a fully documented heresy trial and legally exiled Origen. | Photius, *Bibliotheca* codex 118, ninth-century summary of the lost apology, distinguishes two assemblies. | Precise trial/deposition chronologies | May preserve early dossier material; no acts, charge sheet, votes, complete defense, sentence, or exile order survives. | Report the late two-stage tradition explicitly; do not call permanent departure a juridical exile without an act. |
| Ambrose was definitively a Valentinian—or definitively a Marcionite—before Origen converted him. | Eusebius, HE 6.18.1, says Valentinian; Jerome, DVI 56, says Marcionite. | Sectarian conversion biographies | Conversion, wealth, patronage, and relation converge; prior affiliation conflicts. | Preserve both witnesses rather than harmonizing an unsupported identity or inventing stages. |
| Julia Mamaea was baptized or secretly became Christian through Origen. | Eusebius, HE 6.21.3–4, says she summoned him and heard Christian teaching. | “First Christian court” narratives | Audience/instruction E; baptism and formal affiliation unproved. | Shows Christianity becoming legible near imperial power without supplying a sacramental record. |
| Philip the Arab was baptized by Origen and became the first Christian emperor. | Eusebius, HE 6.34, has a separate Easter story about an unnamed emperor; HE 6.36 notes Origen's letters to Philip and Severa. | Harmonized imperial-Christian chronology | Correspondence E likely; baptism by Origen and identification of the penitent emperor unproved. | Letters do not become initiation records, and separate notices must not be silently merged. |
| Juliana gave Origen Symmachus's manuscripts at Caesarea, proving the entire Hexapla was made there. | Eusebius, HE 6.17, reports Juliana and inherited Symmachian writings without securely locating transfer or project stage. | Caesarean library narratives | Custody E; location and inference about total project chronology unsupported. | Credit a woman transmitter without assigning an unwitnessed city, workshop role, or single-stage project. |
| The Hexapla was exactly six columns throughout and functioned as the first modern critical Bible. | Eusebius, HE 6.16–17, and dispersed fragments attest comparative columns and extra versions for some books. | Histories of textual criticism | Monumental comparative philology secure; physical format, stages, Tetrapla relation, markings, and purposes debated. | Modern analogy may orient only if ancient aims and material limits remain explicit. |
| Origen wrote exactly six thousand books. | Ancient and later lists count works, books within works, homilies, scholia, and letters differently; Jerome's Letter 33 gives a large but different inventory. | Prodigy topoi and encyclopedic summaries | Extraordinary output secure; exact popular total methodologically unstable. | Name surviving works and transmission instead of using a number as proof of sanctity or genius. |
| Origen taught reincarnation or transmigration of human souls into animals. | *Against Celsus* 8.30 and *Commentary on Matthew* 13.1 reject animal transmigration. | Polemical summaries of pre-existence | Contradicted by controlled Origenian loci; pre-existence and multiple-age speculation remain separate real issues. | Correct the false attribution without using that correction to erase other contested anthropology. |
| Origen settled that every rational creature, necessarily including the devil, must be saved. | Restoration trajectories in *On First Principles* 1.6 and 3.6 and *Against Celsus* 8.72; other passages stress freedom and judgment; transmission is mixed. | Universalist and anti-universalist polemic; later Origenist systems | Restoration is genuinely Origenian; modal certainty, universality, devil, recurrence, and freedom remain disputed. | Neither erase hope nor make a maximal later schema his settled sentence. Catholic teaching on hell remains a distinct boundary. |
| Origen simply denied bodily resurrection. | *On First Principles* 2.10, *Against Celsus* 5.18–23, and *Dialogue with Heraclides* 24–27 confess transformed bodily resurrection; ancient opponents contest continuity. | Heresiological shorthand and modern rehabilitation | Simple denial false; some formulations remain genuinely problematic and not identical with later Catholic precision. | Keep confession, metaphysics, objections, and doctrinal reception distinct. |
| Allegorical reading meant Origen believed biblical events never happened. | *On First Principles* 4.2.4 and 4.2.9–4.3.5 joins scriptural senses while identifying some impossibilities or stumbling blocks; commentaries also practice grammar and history. | “Father of allegory” praise and dismissal | False as a universal rule; balance varies by work and passage. | Test each reading, including whether spiritual interpretation honors or displaces body, history, and Israel. |
| Origen's use of Jewish learning proves either uncomplicated respect or uniform hatred of Jews. | *Letter to Africanus* 4–9 and *Against Celsus* 2.4–8 join dependence and scriptural honor with removal allegations, supersession, abandonment, and collective punishment. | Christian–Jewish apologetic and ethical reassessment | Representative exact-locus A; no complete whole-corpus lexicon was performed. | Name dependence and harm at controlled passages without claiming every interlocutor or Jewish figure functions identically. |
| Origen was condemned as a heretic during his lifetime by the whole Church. | Alexandria repudiated his ministry; Palestinian and other bishops continued to receive, ordain, invite, and consult him (HE 6.19, 6.23, 6.27, 6.30–37). | Compressed orthodox/heretic timelines | False universalization of a real local rupture and divided reception. | Always name authority, jurisdiction, act, evidence, and surviving effect. |
| “Origenism” is an exact synonym for everything Origen wrote. | Methodius, Pamphilus, fourth-century reception, Evagrius (*Kephalaia Gnostika* 2.69--75; 3.41, 49--52; 5.19, 22, 25), the 390s controversy, and sixth-century systems show selection and transformation. | Heresiological and rehabilitative grand narratives | Historically false collapse; genealogy is real but every proposition needs its witness and transmission layer, including Greek-fragment/Syriac control for Evagrius. | Trace what was received, altered, translated, or condemned instead of solving reception by labels. |
| Pamphilus alone—or Pamphilus and Eusebius in an exactly known division—authored all six books of the apology. | Ancient reception associates the defense with Pamphilus and Eusebius; only book 1 survives substantially through Rufinus. | Caesarean defense history | Collaboration and book-level authorship remain disputed; complete Greek work lost. | Cite the surviving layer and admit uncertainty rather than assigning invisible labor precisely. |
| Constantinople II either condemned every sentence Origen wrote or never named Origen. | Received anathema 11 names Origen and impious writings; Tanner excludes the famous fifteen anti-Origenist anathemas from the formal conciliar decrees. | Confessional controversy over 553 | Both absolutes false. Relation of the fifteen to pre-conciliar deliberation remains debated. | State the received act, distinguish 543 from 553, and do not turn later propositions into direct quotations without proof. |
| Origen was a canonized saint and executed martyr whose feast was later suppressed. | No competent canonization, stable received universal cult, execution record, or suppressed-feast dossier was located; HE 6.39 says he survived torture. | Private honor, modern universalist and restoration narratives | Confessor secure; canonized saint, martyr by death, and suppressed feast unproved in this audit. | Admiration and private devotional language must not masquerade as competent liturgical or juridical action. |
| Origen's grave and relics at Tyre have continuous authentication. | Jerome, DVI 54, gives Tyre and burial more than a century later; no contemporary burial act or custody chain was located. | Local tomb memory and later travel claims not fully collated | Tyre is early received E; exact grave, remains, later translation, and present identity unestablished. | A dated place tradition may be studied without authenticating remains or deriving history from current signage. |
| Benedict XVI canonized, rehabilitated, or formally declared Origen a Doctor of the Church in 2007. | Holy See general audiences of 25 April and 2 May 2007 praise Origen's life, exegesis, prayer, and ecclesial teaching. | Modern Catholic reception and “rehabilitation” claims | Important M catechesis; no canonization, feast, formal Doctor declaration, or reversal of Constantinople II. | Receive the praise within its actual genre and object; “master” or lowercase “doctor” in translation is not a juridical title act. |
| The 2012 Munich discovery recovered Origen's autograph or twenty-nine wholly unknown sermons. | BSB Cod. graec. 314 is a first-half twelfth-century manuscript identified by Marina Molin Pradel; four homilies have Rufinus parallels and twenty-five were then unavailable as complete homilies. | “Lost Origen found” headlines | Major K discovery and strong attribution; not autograph, and “unknown” requires qualification. | Celebrate critical recovery while showing manuscript date, parallels, catalogue evidence, and attribution process. |
| Any attractive spiritual saying labeled “Origen” is authentic. | Origen's corpus survives in Greek, Latin translation, fragments, catenae, anthologies, and polemical excerpts; online sayings often omit all controls. | Devotional anthologies, memes, universalist quotation sites | Unsupported until work, section, language, edition, and translator are identified. | Exact attribution protects Origen and readers especially where translators announced correction. |

## Traditions not advanced because no controlled witness was located

- a stable ancient feast later removed from a named calendar;
- a competent canonization act or universal liturgical cult;
- an execution, exact deathbed speech, or contemporary statement that Decian
  torture medically caused death;
- a continuous Tyre grave or relic custody chain;
- a securely contemporary portrait or physical likeness;
- an authenticated building for either Alexandrian or Caesarean teaching;
- a competent current patronage or shrine claim; and
- any detached saying without work, exact locus, language, edition, and
  translator.

These are bounded negative results from the sources actually inspected, not a
claim that every local archive, calendar, manuscript, image, or oral tradition
has been exhausted.

## Items requiring further tradition-history work

1. earliest uses and semantic development of “Adamantius” and “man of steel”;
2. full Photius/Pamphilus transmission behind the two-synod account;
3. earliest and subsequent harmonizations of the Julia Mamaea and Philip the
   Arab conversion traditions;
4. transmission of exact large work-count claims;
5. proposition-by-proposition genealogy from Origen through Evagrius and the
   sixth-century Palestinian factions;
6. complete textual history of the 543 dossier, fifteen anathemas, formal 553
   acts, and later conciliar reception;
7. earliest local honor, “saint,” feast, martyr, tomb, relic, and suppression
   claims, with competent custodians and calendars identified;
8. item-level iconographic history before explaining visual attributes;
9. modern “rehabilitation” language before assigning a legal or doctrinal
   effect to twentieth- and twenty-first-century praise; and
10. exact authentication and translation history for every detached saying
    proposed for future inclusion.
