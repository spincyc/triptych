# Origen — chronology audit

## Method

Dates use proleptic Common Era. They are claims, not decorative coordinates.
Ranges and relative order are retained where Eusebius groups events by reign,
episcopate, age, and literary production rather than supplying modern dates.
Codes follow `guidance/biographies.md`: A is an Origenian witness qualified by
transmission; N is near-contemporary external evidence; E is early received
memory; M is an official ecclesial act; K is named modern reconstruction.

The two life endpoints are dependent calculations. HE 6.2.12 says Origen was
not quite seventeen at Leonides's death; HE 7.1 says he died aged sixty-nine.
Neither notice is a civil record, and Tyre comes from Jerome rather than HE 7.1.

## Competing chronology and dependencies

1. **Birth and death:** c. 184/185 and c. 253/254 are linked to Eusebius's two
   age notices. They are not two independent dated records.
2. **Severan persecution:** converting “tenth year of Severus” and the tenure of
   Laetus to 202 or 203 varies; that variation propagates into the birth range.
3. **Early travel:** Rome is relatively anchored by Zephyrinus. The first
   Arabian visit, the Caracallan identification of Alexandrian violence, and
   Julia Mamaea's audience have weaker absolute dates.
4. **Hexapla:** origin, Tetrapla/Hexapla sequence, additions of extra versions,
   physical format, and distribution between Alexandria and Caesarea remain
   disputed. Juliana's gift is not geographically fixed by HE 6.17.
5. **Ordination and rupture:** 230–232 is safer than a single year. Photius's
   two-synod report is much later than the event; no surviving act establishes
   precise stages or juridical exile.
6. **Caesarean works:** Gregory's stay, individual commentaries, consultations,
   *Dialogue with Heraclides*, and public homily recording overlap and cannot be
   arranged into a complete annual diary.
7. **Death:** HE 7.1 supplies age and imperial sequence only. Tyre and burial
   are Jerome's later additions; torture-caused death is a modern or received
   inference from sequence.
8. **Sixth century:** Justinian's c. 543 action, local synodal reception, the
   fifteen-anathema collection, and Constantinople II's formal 553 acts must be
   dated and cited separately.

## Chronological questions deliberately left open

- exact birth year and site;
- exact date of Leonides's execution within 202/203;
- dates and institutional stages of Origen's early teaching;
- timing, truth, and use of the disputed bodily report;
- exact dates and order of Arabia, Rome, Palestinian flight, and Julia Mamaea;
- chronology of *On First Principles*, the early commentaries, and every
  Hexaplar stage;
- exact ordination year and sequence of Alexandrian actions;
- years of Gregory's study and individual consultations;
- dating and venue of the *Dialogue with Heraclides*;
- place, duration, and aftermath of Decian confinement;
- death year, death place, cause, and burial; and
- precise textual and procedural relationship among the 543 dossier, the
  fifteen anathemas, and the formal 553 sessions.
