\subsection{Origenian works actually inspected}

\begin{itemize}
\item Origen. \emph{On First Principles}, preface 2--10; 1.1--3; 1.6;
2.3, 2.6, 2.9--10; 3.1, 3.6; and 4.2--3. Public-domain English research
translation by Frederick Crombie, in \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~4,
through \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0412.htm}{New Advent's
four-book index}; accessed 2026-07-19. The study distinguishes Rufinus's Latin
recension from the Greek excerpts embedded in that edition and does not treat
the English rendering as a critical text.
\item Origen. \emph{Against Celsus}, especially 2.4--8, 3.41, 5.18--23, and
8.12, 8.30, 8.33--34, 8.68--75. Public-domain Crombie translation in
\emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~4,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0416.htm}{eight-book index};
accessed 2026-07-19. The work's extensive representation of the lost Celsus is
used as Origen's controlled rebuttal, not an independent edition of Celsus.
\item Origen and Julius Africanus. \emph{Letter to Africanus} and Africanus's
letter concerning Susanna, especially Origen 4--9 and 12--13,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0414.htm}{public-domain ANF
research translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. The disagreement is preserved as
correspondence; personal friendship is not inferred.
\item Origen. \emph{Letter to Gregory},
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0415.htm}{public-domain ANF
research translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for philosophy as preparatory
discipline and the priority of Scripture and prayer.
\item Origen. \emph{On Prayer}, especially 2 and 22--30, trans. William A.
Curtis,
\sourceurl{https://www.ccel.org/ccel/origen/prayer.html}{public-domain online
text}; accessed 2026-07-19. Its early-twentieth-century English is a research
aid; the publication paraphrases it.
\item Origen. \emph{Homilies on Leviticus} 2.4, \emph{Homilies on Genesis}
10.4, and \emph{Homilies on Exodus} 13.3, all through Rufinus's Latin, in
Wilhelm A. Baehrens, ed., \emph{Origenes Werke}, vol.~6, GCS 29 (Leipzig:
J. C. Hinrichs, 1920), pp.~296--297, 98--99, and 274--275 respectively,
\sourceurl{https://archive.org/details/origeneswerke06orig}{public-domain scan};
accessed 2026-07-19. Locus and Latin were cross-checked against Ronald E.
Heine, trans., \emph{Homilies on Genesis and Exodus}, Fathers of the Church 71
(Washington, DC: Catholic University of America Press, 1982), pp.~164--165 and
380--381, and Gary W. Barkley, trans., \emph{Homilies on Leviticus 1--16},
Fathers of the Church 83 (1990), p.~47. Protected modern translations are
paraphrased, not reproduced.
\item Origen. \emph{Commentary on Matthew}, especially 13.1 and 15.1--5, and
surviving books of the \emph{Commentary on John}, including the Heracleon material,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1016.htm}{Matthew} and
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1015.htm}{John}, public-domain
ANF research translations for the books they cover; accessed 2026-07-19.
Matthew 15.1--5, absent from ANF, was controlled in Erich Klostermann and Ernst
Benz, eds., \emph{Origenes Werke}, vol.~10, GCS 40 (Leipzig: J. C. Hinrichs,
1935), pp.~348--361, and Ronald E. Heine, trans., \emph{The Commentary of Origen on the
Gospel of St Matthew}, 2 vols., Oxford Early Christian Texts (Oxford: Oxford
University Press, 2018). Fragment, book, language, and translation boundaries
remain explicit; protected modern wording is paraphrased.
\item Origen. \emph{Exhortation to Martyrdom}. Greek work inspected in an
online research translation through
\sourceurl{https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?docId=/Fathers-Synchronized-EN/Origenes__Exhortatio_ad_martyrium.en.html}{Catholic
Library}; accessed 2026-07-19. No distinctive modern wording is reproduced.
\item Origen. \emph{Treatise on the Passover and Dialogue with Heraclides and
His Fellow Bishops on the Father, the Son, and the Soul}, trans. and annotated
by Robert J. Daly. Ancient Christian Writers 54. New York: Paulist Press, 1992.
The \emph{Dialogue}, especially 1--4 and 24--27, and \emph{On Pascha} were
used through this copyrighted modern translation and critical guidance; all
conclusions are paraphrased and no reuse right is inferred.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ancient and medieval external witnesses}

\begin{itemize}
\item Eusebius of Caesarea. \emph{Ecclesiastical History} 6.1--39 and 7.1,
trans. Arthur Cushman McGiffert, in \emph{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers},
second series, vol.~1 (1890),
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250106.htm}{book 6} and
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm}{book 7}; accessed
2026-07-19. This is the principal early biography and library witness, read as
a Caesarean defense rather than a transcript of every event.
\item Gregory Thaumaturgus, attributed. \emph{Address of Thanksgiving to
Origen}, especially 6--15,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0604.htm}{public-domain ANF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used as a near-contemporary panegyric by an
elite pupil, with identity and chronology qualified.
\item Porphyry. Anti-Christian biographical fragment quoted by Eusebius,
\emph{Ecclesiastical History} 6.19. Used as a hostile near-contemporary witness
to philosophical reading; his account of Origen's Christian beginnings is not
silently harmonized with Eusebius.
\item Jerome. \emph{On Illustrious Men} 54, 56, 75, and 83,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for Jerome's received age and Tyre
notices, Ambrose's conflicting prior affiliation, Pamphilus's library, and
Methodius's writings. Jerome 54 points to Letter 33 for the work list; it is not
itself represented as containing that list.
\item Pamphilus of Caesarea. \emph{Apology for Origen}, book 1, in Rufinus's
Latin translation, with Rufinus's epilogue, in the public-domain NPNF text,
\sourceurl{https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf203/npnf203.vi.vi.html}{CCEL};
accessed 2026-07-19. Only the surviving book is treated as inspected; the
six-book authorship and collaboration problem remains open.
\item Methodius of Olympus. Fragments of \emph{On the Resurrection}, preserved
principally through Epiphanius, \emph{Panarion} 64, in the public-domain ANF
collection,
\sourceurl{https://ccel.org/ccel/methodius/banquet/anf06.xi.html}{Methodius
index}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for the existence and principal object of
early criticism, not for a reconstructed complete treatise.
\item Athanasius of Alexandria. \emph{On the Decrees of Nicaea} 27,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2809.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for selective quotation of Origen on
the Son's eternal generation.
\item Augustine of Hippo. \emph{Confessions} 5.14.24,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110105.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-19. It establishes Ambrose's spiritual reading
as received by Augustine, not an explicit claim by Augustine of direct study
under Origen.
\item Jerome and Rufinus. Jerome, \emph{Apology against Rufinus}; Rufinus,
\emph{Apology against Jerome}, and the prefaces surrounding \emph{On First
Principles}, in NPNF, second series, vol.~3,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2705.htm}{public-domain reciprocal
texts}; accessed 2026-07-19. Each is a partisan witness to translation and
ecclesial conflict.
\item Socrates Scholasticus. \emph{Ecclesiastical History} 6.7--17, trans.
A. C. Zenos, in NPNF, second series, vol.~2,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26016.htm}{book 6}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used as a later pro-Origen counter-history, not a neutral
eyewitness account.
\item Epiphanius of Salamis, \emph{Letter to John of Jerusalem}, especially
3--6, preserved in Jerome's Latin as Letter 51; and Theophilus of Alexandria,
synodical and paschal letters preserved as Jerome, Letters 92, 96, and 98, in
the public-domain NPNF collection,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001.htm}{Jerome letters index};
accessed 2026-07-19. These partisan episcopal texts provide direct controls for
the first controversy and Theophilus's campaign; they are not treated as
neutral descriptions of opponents.
\item Cyril of Scythopolis. \emph{Life of Sabas}, conciliar conclusion
(section 85 in the source division reproduced by F. K. Haarer and 90 in R. M.
Price's English translation), in Price, trans., \emph{Cyril of Scythopolis:
The Lives of the Monks of Palestine}, Cistercian Studies 114 (Kalamazoo, MI:
Cistercian Publications, 1991), p.~208. The locus was cross-checked against
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.1515/9780748636792-068}{Haarer's document record}
and an online excerpt on 2026-07-19. Used as near-contemporary Sabaite
hagiographic reception of the sixth-century conflict, not as neutral conciliar
minutes; copyrighted English is paraphrased.
\item Photius. \emph{Bibliotheca} codex 118, for the lost apology dossier and
two-assembly report. The locus was controlled through R. P. C. Hanson,
``The Condemnation of Origen,'' \emph{Church History} 28 (1959): 391--399,
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.2307/3161990}{article DOI}, pp.~394--395, and
Peter Van Nuffelen, ``Two Fragments from the \emph{Apology for Origen} in the
\emph{Church History} of Socrates Scholasticus,'' \emph{Journal of Theological
Studies} 56 (2005): 103--114,
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.1093/jts/fli005}{article DOI}. The late report is
not represented as extant Alexandrian minutes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Imperial, conciliar, and Catholic reception}

\begin{itemize}
\item Justinian. \emph{Letter to Mennas against Origen} and the 543
Constantinopolitan action, treated through Richard Price, \emph{The Acts of the
Council of Constantinople of 553}, 2 vols. (Liverpool: Liverpool University
Press, 2009). The imperial letter, home synod, and later ecumenical sessions are
kept distinct.
\item Second Council of Constantinople (553), anathema 11, in Norman P. Tanner,
ed., \emph{Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils}, vol.~1 (Washington, DC:
Georgetown University Press, 1990), pp.~105--106 and 119; compare the public online
translation at
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3812.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Tanner's edition controls the exclusion of the famous fifteen
anti-Origenist anathemas from the formal conciliar decrees.
\item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church} 362--366,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_6_man.html}{body and soul};
988--1004 and 1016--1017,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_11.html}{resurrection of the body}; and 1033--1037,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_12/iv_hell.html}{hell}, official Vatican text; accessed 2026-07-19. Used only as the Catholic
doctrinal boundary concerning the human person, resurrection, and hell, not as
biography of Origen.
\item Benedict XVI. General Audiences,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20070425.html}{25 April 2007} and
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20070502.html}{2 May 2007}; accessed
2026-07-19. These official catecheses establish modern Catholic praise in their
actual genre; they are not canonization, cult authorization, or a new conciliar
act.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Translation and reuse boundary}

Ancient Greek and Latin are public domain. The nineteenth-century and early
twentieth-century ANF, NPNF, and Curtis English research translations cited
above are public domain; the hosting sites' presentation is not project-owned.
Modern translations, critical editions, scholarship, Vatican English,
manuscript images, and institutional metadata retain their own rights. This
publication paraphrases them, uses no extended translated passage, and
reproduces no third-party image. Online access is never treated as a reuse
license.
