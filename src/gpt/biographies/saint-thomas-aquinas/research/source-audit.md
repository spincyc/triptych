# Saint Thomas Aquinas — source audit

Online witnesses, institutional records, and publisher records were inspected
through **2026-07-20**. Codes follow `guidance/biographies.md`. “Used” means
that the publication may rely on the source only for the identified claim and
within the stated genre, transmission, access, and rights limit. Searchable OCR,
old translations, catalogs, publisher pages, and official retrospective
summaries are research controls, not substitutes for an exact primary witness.

## Method and source hierarchy

1. Thomas's authenticated works (**A**) control his teaching, but are not a
   private autobiography. Work, locus, genre, date, authenticity, textual
   witness, and article structure are identified. Objections and *sed contra*
   material are not treated as his final answer.
2. Contemporary documents and near-contemporary witnesses (**N**) control
   offices, institutions, assignments, conflicts, and dates where extant. They
   do not ordinarily disclose Thomas's private motive.
3. The 1319 and 1321 inquiries and the early Lives (**E/M**) preserve otherwise
   lost memories. They are read speaker by speaker and redaction by redaction.
   Canonization process, Dominican promotion, Cistercian custody, and
   hagiographic form remain visible.
4. Later Catholic acts (**M**) control canonization, Doctor status, feast,
   patronage, recommendation, and later teaching only within the exact act's
   force. They are not medieval eyewitnesses or blanket approvals of every
   opinion.
5. Named modern scholarship (**K**) coordinates chronology, witnesses,
   manuscripts, intellectual setting, and alternatives. Only inspected claims
   and arguments are used. Publisher descriptions and abstracts do not make an
   unavailable book or article fully available.

Principal access points are the [Corpus Thomisticum *Fontes vitae*
map](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/ibfonvit.html), its [work
index](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html), the [combined *Fontes
vitae* scan](https://archive.org/details/fontesvitaesthom00pr), and the exact
official and modern records below. The scan's OCR is a finding aid; page images
or controlled editions govern consequential wording.

*Fontes vitae* loci below use the printed fascicle pagination, not the PDF
reader's leaf number. The added chapter, deposition, and document boundaries
were located through OCR and checked against the scan images; no quotation is
controlled by OCR alone. Tocco, Gui, and Calo frequently belong to a dependent
biographical dossier, so their repetition is not counted as independent
corroboration. A linked publisher page is bibliographic metadata only unless a
separately inspected page or argument is expressly named.

## Representative teaching and difficult inheritance

| Consequential claim | Code | Witness, date, and genre | Exact locus / controlled record | Use and necessary limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sacred doctrine reasons from revealed principles and considers creatures in relation to God. | A | *Summa theologiae*, systematic teaching | ST I, q. 1, especially aa. 2, 5, 7–8; [Latin corpus](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html) | Representative mature synthesis, not a complete theory of every science or an autobiographical creed. |

| Created causality remains real because God's universal causation is not competition within one finite field. | A/S | *Summa*, *Summa contra gentiles*, creation/providence loci | Representative loci in ST I, qq. 22, 44–49, 103–105; SCG II–III | Project synthesis across exact loci. No claim that one slogan settles every later causality debate. |
| One substantial human being is composed of body and rational soul; the intellectual soul is the body's form. | A | *Summa theologiae* and Paris polemic | ST I, qq. 75–76; *De unitate intellectus* | Controls mature position and controversy. Does not by itself settle every embryological, resurrection, or individuation question. |
| Complete human beatitude is not a created good but the vision of God. | A | *Summa theologiae* | ST I–II, qq. 1–5 | Representative moral architecture; later grace and Christological completion must remain visible. |
| Virtue perfects powers and passions; theological virtues order the person to a supernatural end. | A | *Summa theologiae* | ST I–II, qq. 55–67; II–II by virtue | A structured synthesis, not a license to detach one definition from prudence, law, grace, and end. |
| Eternal, natural, human, and divine law are distinguished within an order of reason and common good. | A | *Summa theologiae* | ST I–II, qq. 90–108 | The first practical precept does not mechanically generate every contingent legal conclusion without further premises. |
| *Studiositas* governs desire for knowledge; “curiosity” names disordered pursuit rather than all inquiry. | A | *Summa theologiae* | ST II–II, q. 166 | Exact virtue/vices structure. Modern information habits are application (S), not Thomas's explicit social analysis. |

## Consequential negative results

Within the controlled search, no source established:

- an exact civil birth date, complete childhood record, school transcript, or
  verbatim childhood vocation dialogue;
- one uncontested detention duration or a contemporary witness for the
  prostitute/firebrand, angelic girdle, “dumb ox,” Albert prophecy, infant
  Marian paper or crucifix dialogue;
- a single commission or audience for the *Summa contra gentiles*, or blanket
  authorship of every Corpus Christi hymn, office, Mass, sequence, and prayer;
- Reginald of Piperno's own surviving deposition for the December 1273 “straw”
  words;
- a clinical diagnosis, contemporary poisoning accusation, autopsy, or exact
  verbatim final speech;
- a Paris decree naming Thomas or condemning all his works in 1277, or a 1325
  act certifying every disputed proposition as true;
- a continuous, untouched, entire-body chain; forensic authentication of every
  Toulouse or competing skull/relic claim; or a securely contemporary portrait;
- one official act supporting every popular patronage claim, detached saying,
  or honorific origin; or
- an ecclesial act making every Thomistic school, interpretation, or conclusion
  mandatory.

These are bounded negative results, not proof that every archive, manuscript,
calendar, image, reliquary, local church, or oral tradition has been exhausted.
