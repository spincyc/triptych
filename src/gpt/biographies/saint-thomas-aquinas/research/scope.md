# Saint Thomas Aquinas — research scope

## Document identity

- **Publication ID:** `biographies/saint-thomas-aquinas`
- **Title:** *Saint Thomas Aquinas: Friar, Teacher, and the Work of Wisdom*
- **Genre:** source-first historical and hagiographic biography under
  `guidance/biographies.md`; not a critical edition, theological summa,
  canonization dossier, devotional Life, or comprehensive history of Thomism
- **Subject:** Thomas of Aquino, Dominican friar, biblical and systematic
  theologian, university master, preacher, philosophical commentator, saint,
  and Doctor of the Church
- **Life range:** born about 1225, probably at Roccasecca; died at Fossanova on
  7 March 1274. The 1224–1226 birth range is a modern reconstruction from later
  age notices and educational chronology, not a contemporary vital record;
  1224 or 1225 is usual, while a current reconstruction favors 1226.
- **Principal places:** Roccasecca and the Aquino family lands; Monte Cassino;
  Naples; Paris; Cologne; Orvieto; Rome; Viterbo and papal-court settings;
  Fossanova; Avignon, Toulouse, and later sites only in reception history
- **Languages and ecclesial frame:** Thomas wrote principally in Latin and
  worked within the Dominican Order, the Latin universities, the papal court,
  and the thirteenth-century Catholic Church. Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, and Jewish
  authors generally reached him through particular Latin translations,
  florilegia, interlocutors, and inherited dossiers whose accuracy varies.
- **Mutable-claim cutoff:** current papal, liturgical, institutional,
  custodial, edition, and online-source claims were checked through
  **2026-07-20**. A historical patronage or relic notice is not silently
  converted into a present universal claim.

## Reader and governing question

The intended reader is a serious general reader, cleric, student, or researcher
who needs a sustained life without either devotional credulity or an account
reduced to disembodied ideas. The governing question is how a younger son from
a politically exposed southern Italian household became a Friar Preacher,
university master, and exceptionally productive teacher of sacred doctrine,
and how his work was produced, disputed, canonized, edited, institutionalized,
and criticized.

## Governing thesis and boundary

Thomas's work of wisdom was collaborative and institutional. Household,
abbey, mendicant order, university, papal court, translations, assistants,
controversies, worship, and vowed religious life made his writing possible.
His account of creation, grace, reason, virtue, Christ, sacrament, and
beatitude has proved durable; his inherited dossiers, biology, legal premises,
and coercive conclusions also disclose the limits of a thirteenth-century
teacher. The same evidence discipline is required for both achievement and
failure.

This is a historical synthesis within a Catholic source-first collection. It
does not make Thomas's sanctity or formal Doctor status contingent on a modern
biographical judgment. Conversely, canonization and Doctor status do not
authenticate every story, solve every textual question, make every empirical
premise true, or render every philosophical, penal, civil, or social conclusion
irreformable.

## Included

- Aquino household, Monte Cassino schooling, Naples education, Dominican
  vocation, family detention, and the political and institutional interests
  surrounding them;
- Paris and Cologne formation under Albert the Great; the bachelor and master
  stages; the mendicant controversy; the Italian teaching years; the second
  Paris regency; and the final Naples school;
- the principal chronological arc of the *Scriptum super Sententiis*, *De ente
  et essentia*, *Summa contra gentiles*, disputed questions, Aristotelian and
  biblical commentaries, polemical works, *Catena aurea*, and unfinished *Summa
  theologiae*;
- autograph, dictation, secretaries, reportationes, translation dossiers,
  catalogs, authenticity judgments, the posthumous *Supplement*, and the
  continuing Leonine edition as conditions of responsible attribution;
- representative exact-locus accounts of sacred doctrine, faith and reason,
  creation and secondary causality, human nature, virtue, law, grace, Christ,
  sacraments, Eucharist, contemplation, and teaching;
- difficult conclusions concerning coercion of baptized dissenters and
  execution of persistent heretics, legal and theological treatment of Jews,
  women and Aristotelian generation, and servitude, together with later
  official Catholic reception that must not be projected backward;
- December 1273 cessation, journey toward Lyon, Fossanova death and burial,
  with visionary, medical, poisoning, final-speech, and cause-of-death claims
  kept in their proper evidence classes;
- the 1270 and 1277 Paris acts, Oxford prohibitions, the limited 1325 Paris
  revocation, Dominican defense, canonization inquiries and bull, Doctor
  declaration, Thomist plurality, modern critical editing, and papal reception;
- the individual attribution history of the Corpus Christi office and related
  texts, rather than one blanket liturgical authorship claim; and
- memorable childhood, school, chastity, crucifix, straw, death, miracle,
  relic, portrait, title, quotation, feast, and patronage claims audited as
  traditions rather than silently admitted to the historical life.

## Excluded or deliberately bounded

- no complete catalogue, chronology, authenticity argument, manuscript
  census, textual collation, translation history, autograph analysis, or
  edition history for Thomas's corpus;
- no complete exposition of either *Summa*, all biblical or Aristotelian
  commentaries, every disputed question, every sermon, or every philosophical
  and theological development;
- no complete reconstruction of Albert, Bonaventure, Siger, Pecham, Tempier,
  Kilwardby, the secular-mendicant dispute, Parisian Aristotelianism, the Greek
  reunion question, or every later school called Thomist;
- no whole-corpus lexical or ethical census concerning Jews, women, Muslims,
  non-Christians, war, punishment, servitude, political rule, sexuality, or
  disability; publication-level judgments stay with identified loci;
- no attempt to reconstruct another community from Thomas's polemical or
  inherited description alone, and no claim that *Summa contra gentiles* had
  one conclusively demonstrated missionary audience;
- no medical diagnosis of the December 1273 cessation, route illness, tree
  incident, or death; no adjudication of poisoning allegations;
- no forensic authentication of a body, skull, bone, reliquary, tomb, grave,
  portrait, or miracle, and no complete relic, local-cult, calendar,
  iconographic, patronage, or shrine census;
- no independent canon-law opinion about the medieval condemnations, modern
  cause judgment, theological note for every proposition, imprimatur,
  ecclesiastical approval, or declaration of sanctity; and
- no reproduction of protected critical editions, translations, scholarship,
  manuscript images, archival scans, or institutional photography.

## Evidence classes

- **A — subject-authored or Aquinas-attributed text:** controlled by work,
  exact locus, genre, date, audience, authenticity level, surviving witness,
  and degree of authorial supervision. An autograph, an author-supervised
  treatise, a reportatio, and the posthumous *Supplement* are not one kind of
  evidence.
- **N — contemporary or near-contemporary external witness:** charters,
  university and ecclesiastical acts, letters, named colleagues, and other
  evidence close to Thomas's lifetime. Proximity does not remove institutional
  purpose, retrospective memory, or textual transmission.
- **E — early received report:** principally the canonization inquiries,
  William of Tocco, Bernard Gui, Peter Calo, Bartholomew of Lucca, and related
  early Dominican or Cistercian memory. Some witnesses knew Thomas; others
  identify chains. The inquiry and Lives remain sanctity-promoting genres.
- **L — later tradition:** subsequent hagiography, detached sayings,
  miracle elaboration, poisoning accusation, liturgical harmonization,
  iconography, relic claims, popular titles, patronage, and school legend;
  evidence first for the period that transmitted it.
- **M — official ecclesial reception:** a papal inquiry, canonization bull,
  Doctor declaration, feast assignment, patronage act, council or catechetical
  text, or papal teaching, limited to the object and force of that exact act.
- **K — named modern critical reconstruction:** used only to the inspected
  claim, premises, alternatives, and available portion. A catalog abstract or
  publisher description does not provide the argument of uninspected pages.
- **S — source-grounded project synthesis:** editorial inference from
  identified evidence; never put into Thomas's voice or treated as an official
  ecclesial determination.

## Principal corpus and transmission controls

The subject-authored control is the work-by-work corpus and authenticity index
maintained through the Corpus Thomisticum, checked where necessary against
Leonine volumes and their apparatus. The *Summa theologiae* and other exact
loci are read as structured arguments: objections and *sed contra* material
are not automatically Thomas's determination. Reported lectures, sermons, and
the posthumous *Supplement* are identified as such. Item-level attribution is
required for the Corpus Christi office, Mass, sequence, and *Adoro te*; one
probable item does not authenticate every associated text.

The principal early-life dossier is the *Fontes vitae S. Thomae Aquinatis*,
whose witnesses include William of Tocco's repeatedly revised *Ystoria* and the
1319 Naples and 1321 Fossanova canonization depositions. The combined public
scan and searchable OCR are finding aids. Claims and quoted Latin must be
checked in the page image or a controlled critical edition. Tocco was a
Dominican promoter of the cause and wrote in 1318–1323, more than four decades
after Thomas's death; he is the oldest substantial biographer, not a neutral
contemporary observer. The inquiry preserves named deponents and hearsay chains
and is strongest when those chains remain visible.

Modern reconstruction is controlled principally by Jean-Pierre Torrell,
Pasquale Porro, Dominic Legge, the current Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
entry, and work-specific scholarship actually inspected. Claire le
Brun-Gouanvic's critical edition controls Tocco where consulted. Official
reception is controlled by the exact act or record: John XXII's 1323
canonization; the 1325 Paris act; Pius V's 1567 Doctor declaration; Leo XIII's
1879 and 1880 acts; Paul VI's 1974 *Lumen Ecclesiae*; later papal teaching; and
Leo XIV's 2025 statement naming Thomas and John Henry Newman together as
co-patrons of the Church's educational mission.

## Material uncertainties

- birth year within 1224–1226, exact birthplace and day, family members'
  intentions, childhood placement, school curriculum, and the historicity of
  childhood sayings;
- date within c. 1244 of Dominican entry; route, legal form, exact duration,
  and locations of family detention; and every miraculous or chastity-story
  detail attached to it;
- fine chronology and curriculum at Paris and Cologne, Thomas's exact relation
  to Albert, and the earliest form of the “dumb ox” story;
- work dates, revision stages, secretarial practice, reportatio status, and
  itinerary within several Parisian and Italian periods;
- precise commission, authorship, revision, and purpose of the *Summa contra
  gentiles*, *Contra errores Graecorum*, Corpus Christi texts, and several
  biblical or liturgical works;
- relationship of particular Thomistic theses to the 1270 Paris, 1277 Paris,
  and 1277 Oxford dossiers, and the precise scope of the 1325 partial
  revocation;
- date and chain of the crucifix dialogue, exact December 1273 event and
  “straw” wording, illness, tree incident, deathbed speeches, final Song of
  Songs exposition, and cause of death;
- item-level route, division, examination, identity, and present custody of
  claimed remains, including competing skull traditions;
- contemporary likeness, appearance, and the development of standard visual
  attributes;
- earliest and competent force of each honorific, feast, patronage, quotation,
  miracle, and devotional claim; and
- the conceptual and institutional boundaries among Thomisms across centuries.

## Located leads not used beyond their inspected scope

- The Corpus Thomisticum supplies electronic Latin, corpus indexing,
  authenticity classifications, and a life-source portal. Its digital
  presentation is not an authorial manuscript or a license to reproduce the
  protected database wholesale.
- The Internet Archive scan of the *Fontes vitae* fascicles and its OCR locate
  witnesses. The OCR is not sufficient for a consequential quotation, date,
  or named chain, and the combined volume contains editorial layers with
  uncertain or mixed rights.
- Torrell's third edition, Porro's intellectual biography, Legge's 2022
  overview, and the Stanford Encyclopedia entry orient chronology and
  historiographic disagreement. Only the inspected arguments and records carry
  claims; no unseen page is hidden support.
- Mews and Räsänen's study locates modern scholarship on medieval custody and
  relic division. It does not constitute forensic authentication of any
  present object.
- The Toulouse Jacobins' current site is competent for its present civic and
  custodial description, not for scientific proof of every medieval transfer
  or competing relic.
- Complete university registers, Dominican chapter acts, papal registers,
  manuscript stemmata, Leonine apparatuses, every canonization witness,
  reciprocal controversy dossiers, local calendars, reliquary inventories,
  images, and modern scientific examinations remain research leads rather than
  unreported evidence.

## Rights boundary

Project-created prose and organization are intended for CC BY 4.0. Thomas's
medieval Latin and other public-domain historical wording may be quoted at
controlled loci, but modern critical establishment, apparatus, translations,
scholarship, databases, archive presentation, manuscript photography, scans,
and institutional images retain their own rights. Corpus Thomisticum marks its
digital edition © 2019; it is cited and used for short-locus verification, not
vendored. Vatican and other official online texts are publicly accessible but
not assumed to carry an open reuse license. They are paraphrased except for
brief necessary wording with attribution.

The *Fontes vitae* scan has mixed textual and editorial layers. Its OCR is used
only as a lead and no scan image is reproduced. Torrell, Porro, Legge, the
Stanford Encyclopedia, the PIMS Tocco edition, modern journal articles, modern
translations, and publisher or institutional prose remain protected according
to their individual terms. Open access does not itself mean unrestricted
reuse; the identified Cavedon article states CC BY 4.0, while every other open
item requires its displayed license to be checked before reproduction. No
third-party image is included in the draft.

This is a preliminary research rights boundary, not an independent legal or
work-specific distribution review. No claim is made here that a built PDF or
all eventual URLs, quotations, fonts, and embedded material have passed final
rights review.
