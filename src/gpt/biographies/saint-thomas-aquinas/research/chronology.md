# Saint Thomas Aquinas — chronology audit

## Method and source convention

Dates use proleptic Common Era. Thomas left no autobiography or complete annual
record. The chronology coordinates dated ecclesiastical and university acts,
Dominican assignments, work-internal references and revision stages, early
catalogs, canonization testimony, William of Tocco and related early lives, and
named modern reconstruction. A conventional date is not converted into a
contemporary record by repetition.

Codes follow `guidance/biographies.md`: **A** is a subject-authored or
Aquinas-attributed text; **N** a contemporary or near-contemporary external
witness; **E** early received memory; **L** later tradition; **M** an official
ecclesial act; **K** named modern critical reconstruction; and **S**
source-grounded project synthesis. Principal controls are the [Corpus
Thomisticum life-source portal](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/ibfonvit.html),
its [work and authenticity index](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/iopera.html),
the *Fontes vitae S. Thomae Aquinatis*, inspected modern reconstructions, and
the exact official acts identified in the source audit.

## Competing chronology and dependencies

1. **Birth is a reconstruction, not a birthday.** The usual 1224/25
   reconstruction and Legge's probable 1226 depend on later age and educational
   sequences. The publication therefore says about 1225 and preserves a
   1224–1226 range; no option has an independent birth act.
2. **Childhood sequence is stronger than childhood dialogue.** Monte Cassino
   and Naples fit family, institutional, and educational evidence. The abbacy
   plan, “What is God?” question, and predictive childhood signs belong to
   sanctity-shaped memory.
3. **Detention length is witness-dependent.** Medieval accounts differ over
   one, roughly two, or more years and over locations. Modern “months/about a
   year” coordinates the itinerary but is not a new witness.
4. **Work dates are usually ranges.** Autograph stages, cross-references,
   academic office, order assignment, and intellectual development combine to
   date works. They do not yield a daily writing schedule, and redaction can
   outlast first delivery.
5. **The Italian itinerary overlaps.** Orvieto, Rome, Viterbo, Naples, and
   papal-court movement cannot all be arranged from one continuous travel
   diary. Place labels should not claim uninterrupted residence.
6. **Corpus Christi attribution is item-specific.** Urban IV's feast extension
   and Tocco's commission story are different evidence. The office, Mass,
   sequence, hymns, and *Adoro te* require distinct textual histories.
7. **Paris and Oxford censures are not one event.** The 1270 Paris act, 7 March
   1277 Paris act, later-March Oxford act, and 1325 limited revocation have
   different authorities, articles, and effects.
8. **December 1273 has one explicit hearsay chain.** Bartholomew of Capua
   reports John of Anagni's report of Reginald's words. Tocco's related scene
   is not an independent eyewitness deposition merely because it agrees.
9. **Death chronology does not diagnose death.** Cessation, journey, weakness,
   Fossanova, and 7 March are strong in outline. Stroke, subdural injury,
   exhaustion, poisoning, or a single causal narrative lack clinical evidence.
10. **Canonization dates reception, not every memory.** The 1319 and 1321
    inquiries and 1323 bull establish official process and cult. They do not
    make every earlier or later miracle account, relic claim, or detached
    speech historically certain.
11. **Relic chronology is divided by design and disruption.** Fossanova burial,
    medieval openings/divisions, the 1368–69 Toulouse translation, later
    dispersal, and revolutionary movement prevent a single unbroken “whole
    body” sentence.
12. **Patronage is mutable.** The 1880 patronage act is a real historical act;
    the 2025 papal statement uses current co-patron language with Newman.

## Chronological questions deliberately left open

- exact birth year, day, room, and earliest household itinerary;
- precise dates, legal form, locations, and duration of Dominican profession,
  seizure, detention, and release;
- exact arrival dates, courses, pupils, secretaries, travels, and annual
  residence within Paris, Cologne, Orvieto, Rome, Viterbo, and Naples;
- first delivery, redaction, revision, and author-supervision dates for every
  work, reportatio, sermon, and liturgical text;
- exact purpose and audience of the *Summa contra gentiles* and exact
  commission/content of each Corpus Christi item;
- proposition-level relation between Thomas and each 1270/1277 article, and
  the complete institutional effect of the 1325 act;
- exact date, experience, wording, and cause of the December 1273 cessation;
- route, accident, disease, final teaching, final speeches, and cause of death;
- every opening, division, transfer, loss, substitution, and present identity
  in the relic history;
- earliest competent dates for “Angelic Doctor,” “Common Doctor,” and each
  popular patronage or quotation; and
- item-level formation and divergence of every later Thomist school.
