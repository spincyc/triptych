# Saint Thomas Aquinas — tradition audit

This record separates earliest located witness, source dependency, later
reception, historical status, and responsible significance. Its mutable-status
cutoff is 2026-07-20. “Not established” means not established within the
recorded source search; it is not proof that no local, lost, or uninspected
tradition ever existed. An early canonization witness can establish that a
story was told and received without becoming contemporary evidence for the
event it narrates.

The principal early memory is not a set of wholly independent witnesses.
Tolomeo of Lucca, William of Tocco's successively revised *Ystoria sancti
Thome*, the Naples inquiry of 1319, the Fossanova inquiry of 1321, and the lives
of Bernard Gui and Peter Calo preserve direct observation, named oral chains,
common report, institutional advocacy, and literary borrowing in different
proportions. William knew Thomas briefly at Naples and then promoted his cause;
Bartholomew of Capua often reports what companions or pupils had told him;
later lives reuse earlier dossiers. Repetition across these sources therefore
does not by itself supply independent corroboration.

Thematic affinity is not an official appointment. |
| “Angelic Doctor” and “Common Doctor” are civil names or proof of angelic knowledge and universal agreement. | These are received ecclesial and scholastic titles, but no exact first-use document or page was established in the inspected corpus. The claim is retained as a bounded reception statement and negative control; the titles are not names in Thomas's contemporary personal documents. | Papal texts, schools, liturgy, art, devotional biography | Later honorific reception secure; exact origins unresolved and literalized biographical inferences false. | Explain purity, angelology, intellectual service, and broad reception without turning an epithet into evidence. |
| A familiar round-faced Dominican portrait reproduces Thomas's actual appearance. | No securely contemporary portrait, death mask, or physically authenticated likeness was located, and no image is claimed as a likeness in this audit. Later images use the habit, book, pen, sun or jewel, church, host, monstrance, and ox as semantic attributes. | Medieval and modern painting, sculpture, print, school seals, online imagery | Iconographic traditions L secure for their own periods; bodily resemblance unestablished. | Label creator, date, location, and attribute. Images witness theology, office, and reception rather than portrait photography. |
| Any detached maxim labeled “Aquinas” is authentic, including English wording circulated without a work and locus. | Thomas's writings survive in authorial, reported, revised, translated, excerpted, and spurious layers. No source control exists for an unattributed English sentence merely because it appears in an anthology or image. | Sermons, quotation books, apologetics, social media | Unsupported until work, exact locus, language, edition, and translator are identified. | Exact attribution protects both Thomas and readers; a spiritually compatible sentence is not thereby his. |

## Traditions not advanced because no controlled witness was located

- an exact birth date, baptism date, or authenticated birth room;
- a contemporary transcript of the Monte Cassino question, the “dumb ox”
  exchange, the crucifix dialogue, or Thomas's final words;
- a surviving Urban IV commission naming Thomas as author of every Corpus
  Christi text, or an autograph authenticating every hymn and prayer;
- an independent witness to the Aquinas–Bonaventure office competition;
- a clinical record establishing stroke, traumatic brain injury, poisoning, or
  another precise cause of Thomas's final decline and death;
- an untouched whole body or one continuous chain covering every relic claimed
  in Thomas's name;
- a securely contemporary portrait, death mask, or authenticated physical
  likeness;
- a competent act for every popular local or professional patronage attributed
  to Thomas; or
- an authenticated detached saying without work, exact locus, language,
  edition, and translator.

These are bounded negative results from the subject corpus, canonization
records and documentary translations, early Dominican and Cistercian
reception, official Catholic acts, current institutional material, and modern
critical scholarship actually inspected. They do not claim that every local
archive, calendar, manuscript, reliquary, image, or oral tradition has been
exhausted.

## Items requiring further tradition-history work

1. complete the mapping of remaining biographical scenes to the critical chapter or deposition in
   Tolomeo, Tocco's successive recensions, the 1319 and 1321 inquiries, Bernard
   Gui, and Peter Calo, recording dependence rather than counting repetitions;
2. complete an item-level attribution and manuscript history for the Corpus
   Christi offices, *Pange lingua*, *Lauda Sion*, *Verbum supernum*, and *Adoro
   te devote* before making any corpus-wide authorship claim;
3. inventory every miracle and appearance proposed for publication by date,
   witness, beneficiary, evidentiary chain, inquiry, and later canonical use;
4. identify the exact earliest Dante and commentary witnesses for the poisoning
   allegation and trace how Charles of Anjou entered later biography;
5. reconcile the medieval burial, opening, division, Fossanova custody,
   1368–1369 translation, revolutionary movements, 1974 return, later
   recognitions, and present Toulouse custody object by object;
6. investigate competing head, hand, bone, garment, and girdle claims through
   archival inventories and current competent custodians before naming any as
   physically authenticated;
7. build an item-level iconographic chronology before assigning a face,
   physical characteristic, attribute, or earliest visual type;
8. trace the first controlled uses and changing meanings of “Angelic Doctor,”
   “Common Doctor,” and related honorifics;
9. audit local, religious-order, professional, educational, and universal
   patronage separately through the competent act and its current force; and
10. authenticate the Latin source, textual form, and translation history of
    every detached saying proposed for inclusion.
