\subsection{Jerome and late-antique witnesses actually inspected}

\begin{itemize}
\item Jerome. Letters 3, 5, 7, 14--16, 19--22, 35--39, 45, 49, 51--52, 57,
84, 108, 125, 127, 130, 138--139; \emph{De viris illustribus} 135;
biblical prefaces;
\emph{Against Rufinus} 3.22; \emph{Against John of Jerusalem}; \emph{Against
Jovinian}; \emph{Against Helvidius}; \emph{Against Vigilantius}; and the
\emph{Dialogue against the Pelagians}. English research
text: W. H. Fremantle, G. Lewis, and W. G. Martley, trans., in Philip Schaff and
Henry Wace, eds., \emph{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers}, second series, vol.~6
(Buffalo, 1893), through
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001.htm}{New Advent's Jerome
index}; accessed 2026-07-16. The translation is public domain; the publication
mostly paraphrases it. The short wording from Letter 22.30 is identified as its
rendering.
\item Jerome. \emph{Prefaces}, especially the Gospel preface to Damasus and the
Old Testament prologues,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3002.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-16. Prefaces are claims by the translator and
are tested against textual history.
\item Augustine and Jerome. Letters 28/56, 40/67, 71/75, 73/76, and 82/116,
with ancient dual numbering preserved. Public-domain NPNF translation,
including
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102082.htm}{Augustine Letter
82 / Jerome 116}; accessed 2026-07-16.
\item Augustine. \emph{On the Proceedings of Pelagius} 66,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1505.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; Innocent I, Letters
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001136.htm}{136}--
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001137.htm}{137}; and Jerome,
Letters \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001138.htm}{138}--
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001139.htm}{139}; accessed
2026-07-17. Used for the reported 416 attack; their convergence does not supply
a casualty register, complete legal dossier, or neutral attribution of
perpetrators and command.
\item Rufinus of Aquileia. \emph{Apology against Jerome} 2.5--6, 32--44,
trans. William Henry Fremantle, in NPNF, second series, vol.~3,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/27052.htm}{public-domain research
text}; accessed 2026-07-17. Used as a partisan reciprocal witness to the
Ciceronian dream, ascetic rhetoric, Hebrew project, and Origenist dispute.
\item Jerome. \emph{Life of Paul} 1; \emph{Life of Hilarion} 1; and
\emph{Life of Malchus} 1--2, trans. W. H. Fremantle in NPNF, second series,
vol.~6,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3008.htm}{Paul},
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3003.htm}{Hilarion}, and
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3006.htm}{Malchus}; accessed
2026-07-17. Used for Jerome's literary programs and report-frames, not as
uncorroborated event history of their subjects.
\item Adomn\'an. \emph{De locis sanctis} 2.5, trans. Denis Meehan,
\sourceurl{https://celt.ucc.ie/published/T201090.html}{CELT edition}; accessed
2026-07-17. Used for Arculf's late-seventh-century report of a Jerome tomb at
Bethlehem, not a burial or relic-custody chain.
\item John Moschus, \emph{Spiritual Meadow} 107; the ninth-century Jerome life
\emph{Plerosque nimirum}; and Jacobus de Voragine, \emph{Golden Legend}, life
of Jerome, in William Caxton's public-domain rendering,
\sourceurl{https://ccel.org/ccel/voragine/goldleg5/goldleg5.xxix.html}{online
text}. Transmission was checked through Filomena Giannotti, ``Saint Jerome's
Posthumous Life: Aspects of His Reception in the Twentieth Century,''
\emph{Clotho} 3, no.~2 (2021): 115--127,
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.4312/clotho.3.2.115-127}{article DOI};
accessed 2026-07-17. Used to show that the \emph{Golden Legend} transmitted
rather than invented the Jerome lion cycle; the proposed name-confusion route
remains a reconstruction. The modern analysis is paraphrased; no reuse license
is inferred from online access.
\item Sulpicius Severus. \emph{Dialogue} 1.8--9,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/35031.htm}{public-domain NPNF
translation}; accessed 2026-07-16. Used for a visitor's praise of the Bethlehem
scholar, not a neutral work diary.
\item Palladius. \emph{Lausiac History} 36.6--7 and 41.2. Trans. W. K.
Lowther Clarke (London: SPCK, 1918),
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/palladius_lausiac_02_text.htm}{public-domain
English text}; accessed 2026-07-16. Used as hostile Origenist counter-witness,
not transparent access to Jerome's motives.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Official Catholic reception actually inspected}

\begin{itemize}
\item Benedict XVI.\ General Audiences,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20071107.html}{7 November
2007} and
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2007/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20071114.html}{14 November
2007}; accessed 2026-07-15. These catecheses are M and themselves preserve a
419--420 death range.
\item Francis. Apostolic Letter
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/apost_letters/documents/papa-francesco-lettera-ap_20200930_scripturae-sacrae-affectus.html}{\emph{Scripturae
Sacrae affectus}} (30 September 2020); accessed 2026-07-16. Used for current
Catholic reception, its exact ancient loci, collaborative emphasis, official
420 commemoration, and explicit identification of cardinal dress as
anachronistic. Vatican English remains third-party wording and is paraphrased.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modern critical work actually used}

\begin{itemize}
\item Houghton, H. A. G. \emph{The Latin New Testament: A Guide to Its Early
History, Texts, and Manuscripts}. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2016,
pp.~31--35. Open-access edition under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0;
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198744733.001.0001}{book DOI}.

G. Salvesen and Timothy Michael Law, eds., \emph{The Oxford Handbook of the

\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199665716.013.21}{chapter DOI}.
The chapter was inspected on 2026-07-15 for Jerome's prologues and the larger
Greek/Latin canon setting; copyrighted prose is not reproduced.
\item Canellis, Aline. ``L'h\'eritage du latin chr\'etien: la r\'eception de
J\'er\^ome \`a la Renaissance,'' teaching handout (\'Ecole de N\'eolatin,
2016),
\sourceurl{https://neolatinlyon.sciencesconf.org/conference/neolatinlyon/pages/A._Canellis_Neolatin_2016_Exemplier.pdf}{PDF}.
Accessible chronology consulted 2026-07-16 only for the explicitly qualified
347/419--420 scheme and late-418/early-419 placement of Eustochium's death.
\end{itemize}
