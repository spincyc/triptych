# Saint Jerome — chronology audit

## Method

Dates are Common Era. “Attested” identifies an event or relative sequence; it
does not make an NPNF heading, a modern civil date, or a later feast an ancient
datum. **A** marks Jerome's own retrospective or contemporary writing, **N**
external near-contemporary evidence, **E** early reception, **M** official
Catholic reception, and **K** modern reconstruction. Where only the broad
sequence is controlled, the table declines to manufacture an exact age.

| c. 415–417 | Writes the *Dialogue against the Pelagians* and draws closer to Augustine in the grace controversy. | dialogue; later correspondence | Editorial chronologies vary within this range; literary interlocutors are not neutral transcripts of Pelagius. |

## Chronological questions deliberately left open

1. Whether the birth belongs around 331 or around 347, and the exact text and
   force of Prosper's chronicle entry.
2. Stridon's exact site and the limits of late Roman border terminology.
3. Exact dates of Roman schooling, baptism, Trier/Aquileia travel, Antioch,
   Chalcis, ordination, and Constantinople.
4. The fine sequence and textual descent of Psalter revisions and individual
   Old Testament books.
5. Letter-by-letter dating, circulation, and lost intermediaries in the
   Augustine exchange.
6. Exact procedural and legal characterization of Jerome's 385 departure.
7. Full reciprocal chronology of the John/Rufinus/Origenist controversies.
8. Exact responsibility, reported casualty details, proceedings, and aftermath
   of the 416 attack.
9. Eustochium's precise death date and Jerome's death year.
10. Exact witnesses for any burial, relic, lion, Gerasimus, visual, place, or
    patronage history before any event or date is added to this chronology.
