# Saint Peter — research scope

## Publication identity

- **Publication:** *Saint Peter: Apostle, Witness, and Martyr*
- **Provider / collection / leaf:** `gpt / biographies / saint-peter`
- **Genre:** sustained historical and hagiographic biography
- **Subject:** Simon Peter / Symeon / Cephas, Jewish disciple of Jesus, apostle,
  ecclesial leader, and martyr
- **Primary geography:** Bethsaida, Capernaum, Galilee, Jerusalem, Samaria,
  Lydda, Joppa, Caesarea, Antioch, and Rome
- **Primary life period:** first century AD, with birth unknown and death
  probably under Nero, AD 64–68
- **Reception period:** late first century through 15 July 2026
- **Rite / ecclesial frame:** principally Roman Catholic doctrine and liturgy,
  while treating Peter as a figure of the whole Christian tradition
- **Language:** English; ancient names and technical terms supplied only where
  they clarify the evidence
- **Research and mutable-fact cutoff:** 15 July 2026
- **Governing guidance:** `guidance/editorial.md`, `guidance/repository.md`, and
  `guidance/biographies.md`

Peter and Paul are separate publications. Their shared Roman cult is discussed
in each only where it bears directly on the subject; neither life is an appendix
to the other.

## Controlling question

What can responsibly be said about Peter as a first-century Jewish follower of
Jesus, Easter witness, apostolic leader, missionary across Jewish–Gentile
boundaries, bearer of the Catholic Petrine office, and martyr remembered at
Rome, when the sources differ sharply in date, genre, proximity, and purpose?

The publication is meant for serious general readers, clergy, students, and
researchers who want a readable life without a silent Gospel harmony or an
ungraded accumulation of legends. It is a study aid, not an archaeological
authentication, a new critical edition, an ecumenical settlement, or a
substitute for liturgy and magisterial teaching.

## Evidence architecture

The publication and audits use the biography profile's codes exactly:

- **A — autobiographical or subject-authored:** no surviving work supplies an
  uncontested direct Petrine autobiography. First and Second Peter are treated
  as canonical works attributed to him whose direct literary authorship must be
  argued rather than presumed.
- **N — near-contemporary external witness:** principally Paul's undisputed
  letters and the distinct canonical narrative witnesses, read according to
  date and genre rather than harmonized as transcripts.
- **E — early received tradition:** First Clement, Ignatius, Papias, Irenaeus,
  Gaius, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria, Origen as preserved by Eusebius,
  and other ancient reception.
- **L — later hagiographic or devotional tradition:** late apocrypha when used
  for their developed scenes, medieval sacred geography, exact episcopal
  tenures, relic itineraries, and popular motives.
- **M — magisterial or official ecclesial reception:** conciliar and papal
  teaching, the Catechism, liturgy, and official shrine interpretation.
- **K — modern critical reconstruction:** identified scholarly arguments about
  chronology, source relations, authorship, Roman Christianity, archaeology,
  and memory.
- **S — source-grounded project synthesis:** explicit conclusions drawn by the
  publication from named, differently weighted evidence.

Canonical narrative is not made a separate evidence code. Material evidence
is described as material evidence and assessed through its excavation and
interpretive history; an official shrine claim is **M**, while a modern
archaeological conclusion is **K**.

## Source corpus in scope

### Canonical witnesses

- The four Gospels, with parallel scenes kept distinct.
- Acts 1–15, including the literary and theological character of its speeches.
- Paul's undisputed letters, especially 1 Corinthians 1, 9, and 15 and
  Galatians 1–2.
- First and Second Peter, with canonical status and direct-authorship questions
  explicitly separated.

All short Scripture quotations use the *New American Bible, Revised Edition*
(NABRE) as published by the USCCB. Summary wording is a checked project
paraphrase, not a generated Bible translation. NABRE text and notes remain
outside the project's license.

### Ancient and material witnesses

- First Clement 5–6.
- Ignatius, *Romans* 4.3, middle recension.
- Papias, Dionysius of Corinth, Gaius of Rome, Clement of Alexandria, and
  Origen as preserved at exact loci in Eusebius.
- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.1.1 and 3.3.2–3.
- Tertullian, *Prescription* 36 and *Scorpiace* 15.
- Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 for Neronian context only.
- The late-second-century *Acts of Peter*, especially 35–39, for the *Quo
  vadis* and head-down martyrdom traditions.
- Gaius's reported `tropaion`; the official Vatican tomb and necropolis
  presentation; and G. W. Bowersock's checked publisher excerpt for the
  proposed second-century monument and Constantinian alignment. Primary
  excavation, stratigraphic, custody, and osteological reports were not
  inspected and are outside the evidentiary core of this version.

### Ecclesial and modern controls

- *Lumen gentium* 18–23; *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 552–553 and
  880–882; *Nostra aetate* 4; the Pontifical Biblical Commission on the Jewish
  Scriptures.
- Official Vatican basilica presentations of the tomb and necropolis.
- G. W. Bowersock's checked essay excerpt and the checked Oxford Cult of Saints
  record are the modern interpretive sources actually used. Publisher and
  bibliographic pages for work by Pheme Perkins, Markus Bockmuehl, Martin
  Hengel, Helen Bond and Larry Hurtado, James D. G. Dunn, David Eastman, and
  Peter Lampe were used only to identify possible specialist follow-up; their
  unseen arguments do not support claims in this version.

## Required biographical coverage

The publication covers:

1. source problem, method, names, geography, and chronological limits;
2. Galilean Jewish world, household, marriage, fishing economy, language, and
   education limits;
3. distinct canonical call portraits;
4. discipleship, confession, correction, Passion failure, restoration, and
   Easter witness;
5. Jerusalem leadership, preaching, signs, discipline, imprisonment, Samaria,
   and the Cornelius episode;
6. relationships with Andrew, the Twelve, James, John, Mark, and Paul;
7. the Antioch conflict and Gentile inclusion without contempt for Torah or
   supersession of Israel;
8. Catholic doctrine of Petrine service without importing a modern curial job
   description into the first century;
9. teaching and authorship tiers for First and Second Peter, plus the Mark
   tradition and apocryphal attributions;
10. final years, Roman convergence, Neronian setting, manner and date of death;
11. burial, memorial, archaeology, relic and bone limits;
12. cult, liturgy, iconography, patronage, sacred geography, and institutional
    legacy;
13. a dedicated tradition audit and a candid contested legacy; and
14. final synthesis, detailed timeline, and full references.

## Controlling conclusions

- Peter was a married Jewish fisherman associated with Bethsaida and a
  household in Capernaum; his birth date, age, wealth, formal education, and
  wife's name are unknown.
- He belonged to Jesus' earliest Galilean circle, occupied a singular place
  among the Twelve, confessed Jesus as Messiah, resisted the Passion, denied
  Jesus, and became a primary named Resurrection witness.
- Paul's early references independently establish Cephas as a leading apostle,
  a Jerusalem contact, one of the pillars, a missionary associated with the
  circumcised, a married or accompanied apostle, and the object of a real
  public rebuke at Antioch.
- Acts' canonical portrait establishes Peter's theological role in Jerusalem
  proclamation and Gentile inclusion but is not used as a stenographic or
  exhaustive itinerary.
- Catholic doctrine concerning Peter's commission and the Roman succession is
  stated positively as **M**, while the first-century form of his ministry is
  not redescribed through medieval or modern administrative structures.
- A Roman final ministry and martyrdom under Nero are probable by cumulative
  **E/N/S** evidence. The sources converge more strongly on Rome and martyrdom
  than on exact year or manner.
- Crucifixion has early third-century or c. 200 explicit support; head-down
  crucifixion has ancient but later support. The exact date, 29 June as death
  day, same-day execution with Paul, and popular humility motive are not
  contemporary data.
- The checked official and modern presentations support an ancient Petrine
  memorial at the Vatican site and long architectural continuity. This study
  did not inspect the primary archaeological or osteological reports and does
  not authenticate a particular human bone as Peter's.

## Anti-supersessionist control

Peter remains Jewish throughout the evidence. Temple prayer, Israel's
Scriptures, dietary observance, mission to the circumcised, Cornelius, Acts 15,
and the Antioch dispute are not stages in a story in which Judaism becomes
contemptible or Israel is discarded. Gentile admission without adopting the
full disputed yoke does not make Jewish Torah observance hypocrisy. Paul's
charge concerns a withdrawal that denied shared life to Gentiles. Passion and
Acts polemic may not be used to assign collective or perpetual guilt to Jews.
The publication is controlled explicitly by *Nostra aetate* 4 and the
Pontifical Biblical Commission's account of the enduring relation between the
Church and Israel's Scriptures.

## Exclusions and restraint

- No invented childhood, dialogue, appearance, psychological diagnosis, or
  continuous itinerary.
- No claim that Peter changed religions in the modern sense.
- No claim that a seven-year Antiochene or twenty-five-year Roman tenure is
  established chronology.
- No assumption that Romans' silence disproves every later Roman presence.
- No literal-first-founder reading of Irenaeus that contradicts Christians in
  Rome before Paul's arrival.
- No treatment of First or Second Peter as uncontested autobiography.
- No use of the *Acts of Peter*, *Quo vadis*, Mamertine legends, Petronilla,
  or later relic labels as first-century proof.
- No forensic authentication of the Vatican bones.
- No images. The biography's depth rests on text, evidence, chronology, and
  reception history.

## Live uncertainties requiring review

- Dating of Jesus' ministry and Paul's visits; relationship of Galatians 2 to
  Acts 11 and 15.
- Direct literary authorship, date, setting, and secretarial role in First
  Peter; authorship and date of Second Peter.
- Extent of Peter's Antiochene and Roman ministry and timing of arrival.
- Whether First Peter's “Babylon” can be used independently as Rome.
- Whether Peter died in the immediate aftermath of the fire of 64 or later in
  Nero's reign; exact method and burial process.
- Interpretation of `tropaion`, the monument's stratigraphy, the excavation and
  custody history of Vatican human remains, and claimed relic pathways.
- Origins and intervening transmission of the now-controlled ancient 29 June
  entry, and whether its Catacombs notice implies bodily translation.

These questions are disclosed rather than silently decided. The publication
does not claim independent archaeological, linguistic, theological, or
ecumenical review.

## Rights and publication maturity

The project contributes original selection, paraphrase, synthesis, structure,
and prose. Scripture, patristic and official translations, modern scholarship,
institutional web content, and source editions retain their own rights. Only
short necessary quotations are used. Nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century
public-domain translations are identified individually; a public-domain
ancient work does not make every modern translation public domain.
