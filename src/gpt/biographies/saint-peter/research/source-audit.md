# Saint Peter — source audit

All online sources below were accessed 15 July 2026. “Used for” describes the
claim actually carried by the source in the publication; it is not a list of
everything the source might support. The evidence codes follow
`guidance/biographies.md` exactly. Canonical narrative is identified by genre
within **N** rather than assigned a new code. Archaeological evidence is
identified in words and its interpretation assigned **K** or **M** as
appropriate.

## Scripture edition and rights boundary

The Scripture edition used for every short direct quotation is the *New
American Bible, Revised Edition* (NABRE), copyright © 2010, 1991, 1986, 1970
Confraternity of Christian Doctrine, Washington, DC, as published by the
USCCB. Wording introduced as summary is a checked project paraphrase, not a
generated Scripture translation. The biblical text, introductions, and notes
remain outside the project's license. Short quotation is limited to study,
criticism, identification, and exposition.

## Claim-to-source checkpoints

| Consequential claim | Required sources | Audit result |
|---|---|---|
| Peter was married | Mark 1:29–31; 1 Cor 9:5 | Secure canonical convergence; wife's name and fate not inferred. |
| Cephas was a primary Easter witness | 1 Cor 15:3–5; Luke 24:34; John 21 | Earliest formula given priority; Gospel scenes kept distinct. |
| Peter held a singular Catholic office | Matt 16; Luke 22; John 21; *Lumen gentium*; CCC | Canonical basis and magisterial reception distinguished from first-century institutional reconstruction. |
| Peter learned Gentile inclusion yet faltered in practice | Acts 10–15; Gal 2 | Sources are juxtaposed without forcing them into one timeline; both concern the terms of shared Jewish–Gentile life. |
| Peter died at Rome under Nero | 1 Clem. 5; Ignatius *Rom.* 4; Gaius/Dionysius; Tacitus; Tertullian | Probable cumulative synthesis. No single witness made to prove every element. |
| Peter was crucified | John 21 as foreshadowing; Tertullian; later acts | Early received and plausible, but not a contemporary narrated fact. |
| Peter was crucified upside down | *Acts of Peter* 37–38; Origen/Eusebius 3.1.2 | Ancient but late; motive and exact setting separately graded. |
| Vatican is an early Petrine memorial | Gaius/Eusebius; checked Bowersock excerpt; official basilica | Supports ancient reception and architectural continuity; primary excavation and osteological reports were not audited. |
| Peter and Paul died on 29 June together | Dionysius/Eusebius; *Depositio martyrum*; current liturgy in Benedict XVI's 2008 homily | Not established; the ancient and current calendars confess joined memory but supply no execution date. |

## Review and preservation notes

- Exact Greek and Latin philology is reported only through identified texts and
  translations; no independent textual collation was performed.
- No copyrighted modern translation is reproduced at length.
- No source scan, bulk OCR, image, or archaeological photograph is vendored.
- No claim of independent historical, theological, archaeological, or
  ecclesiastical review is made.
- The publication's references and this audit must be updated together if a
  quotation, source tier, or archaeological claim changes.
