\subsection{Early Christian and Roman witnesses}

\begin{itemize}
\item Clement of Rome / the Roman church. \emph{First Epistle to the
  Corinthians}, 5.5--7. Translated by John Keith in \emph{Ante-Nicene
  Fathers}, vol.~9, edited by Allan Menzies (New York: Christian Literature
  Company, 1896). Public-domain English translation at
  \sourceurl{https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers/Volume_IX/The_Epistles_of_Clement/The_First_Epistle_of_Clement_to_the_Corinthians/Chapter_5}{Wikisource, chapter 5},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Under the conventional witness sequence, whose
  precise dating was not independently audited here, this is the earliest
  located external account of Paul's completed witness; ``limit of the West''
  remains geographically disputed.

\item Ignatius of Antioch. \emph{Letter to the Romans}, 4.3. Precise dating
  and recensional history were not independently audited. In
  \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~1. Public-domain patristic
  translation in revised web presentation at
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0107.htm}{New Advent}, reviewed
  16 July 2026. The line establishes early paired authority at Rome, not an
  itinerary.

\item Irenaeus of Lyons. \emph{Against Heresies} 3.1.1 and 3.3.2--3. In
  \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~1. Public-domain English translation at
  \sourceurl{https://ccel.org/ccel/irenaeus/against_heresies_iii/anf01.ix.iv.iv.html}{CCEL, 3.3},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used for Roman apostolic and corpus reception, not the
  claim that no Christians preceded the apostles at Rome.

\item Eusebius of Caesarea. \emph{Ecclesiastical History} 2.22.2--8
  (reported release and renewed preaching); 2.25.5--8 (Dionysius and Gaius);
  3.1.1--3 (Origen on Paul under Nero). Translation by Arthur Cushman
  McGiffert, in \emph{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers}, second series, vol.~1
  (1890), public domain. Online text:
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm}{book 2} and
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm}{book 3}, reviewed
  16 July 2026. Eusebius preserves earlier witnesses but writes centuries
  after Paul.

\item Tertullian. \emph{The Prescription Against Heretics}, 36; and
  \emph{Scorpiace}, 15. In \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~3 (1885),
  public-domain English translations. Online text:
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0311.htm}{Prescription} and
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0318.htm}{Scorpiace}, reviewed
  16 July 2026. Used for the early explicit Roman beheading tradition.

\item \emph{Acts of Paul and Thecla}, especially the opening portrait and the
  trial sequences, within the ancient \emph{Acts of Paul}; precise dating was
  not independently audited.
  Translation by Alexander Walker, in \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.~8
  (Buffalo: Christian Literature Publishing, 1886), public domain. Revised
  web presentation at
  \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0816.htm}{New Advent},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used for ancient reception of Paul's appearance,
  women, celibacy, and mission; not as a contemporary life.

\item Tacitus. \emph{Annals} 15.44. Translation by Alfred John Church and
  William Jackson Brodribb (1876), public domain. Online at
  \sourceurl{https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Annals_(Tacitus)/Book_15}{Wikisource, book 15},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Tacitus supplies the Neronian persecution context and
  does not name Paul.
\end{itemize}

The nineteenth-century \emph{Ante-Nicene Fathers} and \emph{Nicene and
Post-Nicene Fathers} translations were treated as public-domain editions;
modern web presentation and editorial material may retain separate rights.
No apocryphal narrative or modern translation was reproduced at length.

Jerome's witness that a Paul--Seneca correspondence was widely read was checked
at \emph{De viris illustribus} 12 in Ernest Cushing Richardson's public-domain
NPNF translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm}{New Advent}, reviewed
16 July 2026. The letters themselves and a modern critical edition were not
collated, so they support reception history only and no claim of friendship.

\subsection{Magisterial, liturgical, and institutional sources}

\begin{itemize}
\item Second Vatican Council. Declaration \emph{Nostra aetate} (28 October
  1965), 4, and Dogmatic Constitution \emph{Lumen gentium} (21 November 1964),
  19--20. Vatican English texts:
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html}{Nostra aetate} and
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/wss/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html}{Lumen gentium},
  reviewed 16 July 2026.

\item \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, especially 442, 787--795,
  1987--2029. Vatican English edition at
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en.html}{Catechism index},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used for ecclesial reception of Paul's confession,
  the body of Christ, and justification; not as an independent chronology.

\item Pontifical Biblical Commission. \emph{The Jewish People and Their Sacred
  Scriptures in the Christian Bible} (2001), especially I.A and III.B.
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/pcb_documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20020212_popolo-ebraico_en.html}{Vatican English text},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used to control anti-supersessionist interpretation.

\item Benedict XVI, general audiences ``Life of Saint Paul before and after
  Damascus,'' 27 August 2008, and ``St Paul's `Conversion','' 3 September
  2008. Vatican English texts:
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2008/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20080827.html}{27 August} and
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2008/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20080903.html}{3 September},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Official theological synthesis is distinguished from
  direct ancient evidence.

\item Benedict XVI, homily at First Vespers closing the Pauline Year, 28 June
  2009. Vatican English text at
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/homilies/2009/documents/hf_ben-xvi_hom_20090628_chiusura-anno-paolino.html}{Vatican},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. This is the located public source for the sarcophagus
  probe and radiocarbon announcement; it is not a full scientific report and
  was not used to audit the wider Pauline Year program.

\item United States Conference of Catholic Bishops. \emph{Liturgical Calendar
  for the Dioceses of the United States of America 2026}, entry for 29 June.
  \sourceurl{https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026cal.pdf}{USCCB calendar},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used only to establish the current U.S. solemnity,
  not a universal calendar or the date or origin of the ancient cult.

\item Papal Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls. ``The Tomb of the
  Apostle.'' Official institutional presentation at
  \sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/various/basiliche/san_paolo/en/basilica/tomba.htm}{Vatican basilica site},
  reviewed 16 July 2026. Used for the shrine's official identification and
  architectural presentation; its traditional execution and burial details
  were not treated as independent first-century proof.
\end{itemize}

Official English translations and web presentations retain their own rights.
They were paraphrased or quoted only briefly and remain outside the project's
license.

\subsection{Modern bibliography consulted only as research leads}

The following complete bibliographic descriptions were used to map questions
for later specialist review. Only publisher metadata, tables of contents, or
limited previews were available in this generation; the books were not
inspected at the pages needed to support publication claims. Accordingly no
argument or factual assertion in this biography depends on them. They remain
listed as an explicit research boundary, not as authority silently borrowed.

\begin{itemize}
\item Barclay, John M.~G. \emph{Paul and the Gift}. Grand Rapids: Eerdmans,
  2015.

\item Campbell, Douglas A. \emph{Framing Paul: An Epistolary Biography}.
  Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 2014.

\item Fredriksen, Paula. \emph{Paul: The Pagans' Apostle}. New Haven: Yale
  University Press, 2017.

\item Gaventa, Beverly Roberts. \emph{Our Mother Saint Paul}. Louisville:
  Westminster John Knox, 2007.

\item Meeks, Wayne A. \emph{The First Urban Christians: The Social World of
  the Apostle Paul}. 2nd ed. New Haven: Yale University Press, 2003.

\item Murphy-O'Connor, Jerome. \emph{Paul: A Critical Life}. Oxford: Clarendon
  Press, 1996.

\item Sanders, E.~P. \emph{Paul and Palestinian Judaism: A Comparison of
  Patterns of Religion}. Philadelphia: Fortress, 1977.

\item Eastman, David L. \emph{The Ancient Martyrdom Accounts of Peter and
  Paul}. Writings from the Greco-Roman World 39. Atlanta: SBL Press, 2015; and
  \emph{The Many Deaths of Peter and Paul}. Oxford: Oxford University Press,
  2019.

\item Lampe, Peter. \emph{From Paul to Valentinus: Christians at Rome in the
  First Two Centuries}. Translated by Michael Steinhauser. Minneapolis:
  Fortress, 2003.
\end{itemize}

These modern works are copyrighted and were not quoted. The Acts--letters
chronology, Gallio inscription, authorship spectrum, Roman social world, and
martyr tradition still require independent specialist review against critical
editions.
