# Saint Paul — chronology audit

This chronology distinguishes Paul's relative intervals, Acts' narrative
sequence, modern orientation ranges, early reception, and later tradition.
Dates are AD unless stated. Online loci were reviewed 16 July 2026. The primary
Gallio inscription was not independently collated; its range is reported only
through the identified papal source.

## Synchronization decisions

- No exact birth year, age at Stephen's death, or age at martyrdom is supplied.
- The call is ranged rather than assigned to a recovered day. The 25 January
  observance was not controlled by an inspected calendar in this generation.
- Galatians' “three” and “fourteen” years are not converted into exact calendar
  appointments.
- Galatians 2:1–10 is not silently identified with Acts 11 or Acts 15.
- Antioch is not forced before or after every Acts Gentile-inclusion scene.
- Acts' three circuits remain Acts' literary orientation, not an exhaustive
  count of travel.
- The Gallio range is kept at 51–53 through the inspected papal report; the
  primary inscription requires specialist collation.
- Letter dates remain ranges; prison letters are not automatically Roman.
- Acts' two years do not become a “first” imprisonment without stating the
  release hypothesis.
- Spain is a plan in Romans and a possible reading of early reception, not a
  fixed station.
- AD 64–68 is the controlling martyrdom range, bounded by the post-fire context
  and the end of Nero's reign. AD 67, 29 June, same-day death,
  Tre Fontane, and a named sentence remain uncollated or unverified.
- Gaius's memorial, its conventional dating, a fourth-century basilica, and a
  first-century burial are separate claims.
- A first-to-second-century radiocarbon range is not a personal identifier.

## Review limit

No independent Pauline chronology, epigraphic, papyrological, Roman legal,
archaeological, or radiocarbon review has occurred. Specialist review should
prioritize the Gallio inscription, Galatians–Acts mapping, Corinthian and
prison-letter chronology, Pastorals and post-Acts itinerary, First Clement's
western phrase, Gaius's memorial, ancient calendar history, and the complete
sarcophagus testing record.
