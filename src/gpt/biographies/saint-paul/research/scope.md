# Saint Paul — research scope

## Publication identity

- **Publication:** *Saint Paul: Apostle to the Nations*
- **Provider / collection / leaf:** `gpt / biographies / saint-paul`
- **Stable publication identifier:** `biographies/saint-paul`
- **Genre:** sustained historical and hagiographic biography
- **Subject:** Saul / Paul, Jewish Pharisee, persecutor of the Church, witness
  called by the risen Jesus, apostle among the nations, letter writer, and
  martyr received at Rome
- **Primary geography:** Tarsus and Jerusalem according to Acts; Damascus,
  Arabia, Syria, Cilicia, Antioch, Galatia, Cyprus, Macedonia, Achaia, Asia,
  Judea, Malta, and Rome
- **Primary life period:** first century AD; birth unknown, probably in the
  first decade; death probably at Rome under Nero, responsibly ranged AD 64–68
- **Reception period:** late first century through 16 July 2026
- **Rite / ecclesial frame:** principally Roman Catholic doctrine, liturgy,
  and shrine reception, while treating Paul as a figure of the whole Christian
  tradition
- **Language:** English; Greek and Latin terms appear only where they control a
  historical or interpretive claim
- **Research and mutable-fact cutoff:** 16 July 2026
- **Governing guidance:** `guidance/editorial.md`, `guidance/repository.md`, and
  `guidance/biographies.md`

Peter and Paul are separate publications. Their relationship, Antioch
conflict, common Roman reception, and joint solemnity appear in both only from
the subject's own evidentiary horizon. The shared feast does not create one
combined itinerary or martyrdom scene.

## Controlling question

What can responsibly be said about Paul as a first-century Jew whose own
letters remember persecution, revelation, mission, conflict, suffering, and
care for communities, when Acts supplies a distinct later canonical narrative
and early and later Christian reception progressively fills the open ending of
his life?

The intended readers are serious general readers, clergy, students, and
researchers who need a coherent life without treating Acts as stenography,
every Pauline attribution as undisputed autobiography, or hagiographic detail
as contemporary fact. The publication is a study aid, not a critical edition,
archaeological authentication, scientific relic report, complete Pauline
theology, moral manual, or independent ecclesiastical judgment.

## Governing thesis

Paul is best known from a layered but unusually rich record. The seven
undisputed letters supply the direct autobiographical core, though each is
occasional and rhetorical. Acts supplies a canonical theological history with
its own sequence and speeches. Disputed Pauline letters reveal canonical
Pauline reception but cannot silently prove post-Acts travels. Early external
witnesses make a Roman martyrdom under Nero probable and establish an Ostian
memorial in Gaius's received report, whose precise date was not independently
audited. Beheading is an early received tradition; release,
Spain, a second arrest, the exact year and day, and personal bone identity
remain differently graded; Tre Fontane was not collated as a publication
claim.

Paul's Jewish identity is continuous through the evidence. The call radically
reorders his life and his account of Torah, righteousness, and the nations; it
does not turn Judaism into a rejected religion of merit or Israel into a
discarded people. His mission is networked rather than solitary and requires
women and men co-workers, patrons, household hosts, enslaved and free
believers, delegates, messengers, and communities to remain visible.

## Evidence architecture

The publication and records use the biography profile's codes exactly:

- **A — autobiographical or subject-authored:** Romans, First and Second
  Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, First Thessalonians, and Philemon. Their
  broad direct-authorship consensus does not make them neutral memoirs.
- **N — near-contemporary external witness:** principally Acts as a distinct
  canonical theological history. The Gallio anchor is used only through the
  checked modern official report; the Greek inscription and a modern
  epigraphic edition were not independently collated.
- **E — early received tradition:** First Clement, Ignatius, Dionysius and
  Gaius as preserved by Eusebius, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Origen as preserved by
  Eusebius, and the early fixed Roman memorial.
- **L — later hagiographic or devotional tradition:** the *Acts of Paul and
  Thecla*, developed martyr narratives, Paul–Seneca correspondence, exact
  itineraries, horse, springs, local prisons, physical portrait, relic labels,
  and simplified popular motifs.
- **M — magisterial or official ecclesial reception:** conciliar and papal
  teaching, the Catechism, the current USCCB 29 June calendar entry, papal
  reporting of the 2009 sarcophagus investigation, and the official basilica
  presentation.
- **K — modern critical reconstruction:** identified judgments about
  chronology, direct authorship, social setting, and material evidence. In
  this generation, claims rely on inspected canonical introductions and
  official or primary loci; publisher-only monographs remain leads and do not
  support claims.
- **S — source-grounded project synthesis:** explicit editorial conclusions
  from named evidence, particularly the probability and limits of Roman
  martyrdom, the Acts–letters relationship, and the grading of traditions.

Canonical narrative is not assigned an invented eighth code. Material evidence
is named in words and its interpretation classified as **M**, **K**, or an
unresolved lead. The current U.S. calendar entry and a claimed ancient
calendar origin are not the same claim.

## Source corpus actually inspected and used

### Canonical witnesses

- The seven undisputed letters at exact loci listed in `source-audit.md` and
  the publication references.
- Acts 7:54–9:31; 11:19–30; 13–28, with chapters 9, 22, and 26 retained as
  three literary tellings of the call.
- Second Thessalonians, Colossians, Ephesians, First and Second Timothy, and
  Titus only for classified canonical reception and authorship questions.
- Hebrews for the history of Pauline attribution, not autobiography; Second
  Peter 3:15–16 for early reception of a Pauline letter collection.

All short direct Scripture quotations use the *New American Bible, Revised
Edition* (NABRE), as published by the USCCB. Summary wording is a checked
project paraphrase and not a generated Bible translation. The NABRE text,
introductions, and notes remain outside the project's license.

### Early and later Christian witnesses

- First Clement 5.5–7 for completed witness and the ambiguous western limit.
- Ignatius, *Romans* 4.3, for paired authority at Rome. Precise date and
  recensional history were not independently audited.
- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.1.1 and 3.3.2–3, for Roman and corpus
  reception.
- Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* 2.22.2–8; 2.25.5–8; 3.1.1–3, preserving
  claims about release, Dionysius, Gaius, and Origen.
- Tertullian, *Prescription* 36 and *Scorpiace* 15, for explicit Roman
  beheading reception. Both exact loci were inspected on 16 July 2026.
- Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 for Neronian context only; he does not name Paul.
- *Acts of Paul and Thecla*, opening portrait and trial narratives, in
  Alexander Walker's public-domain ANF translation, for later reception.
- Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 12, for the fact that a Paul–Seneca
  correspondence was read in the late fourth century; the letters and a
  critical edition were not collated.

### Official and institutional controls

- *Nostra aetate* 4, *Lumen gentium* 19–20, *Catechism of the Catholic Church*
  442, 787–795, 1987–2029, and the Pontifical Biblical Commission's 2001
  document on the Jewish Scriptures.
- Benedict XVI's audiences of 27 August and 3 September 2008. The former is
  the inspected modern official locus reporting the Gallio/Delphi range as
  AD 51–53; this research did not inspect the primary inscription.
- Benedict XVI's homily of 28 June 2009, the exact public locus reporting a
  probe through a small opening in the sarcophagus, textile/incense material,
  blinded-provenance carbon-14 testing of minute bone fragments, and a
  first-to-second-century result.
- Official Vatican presentation of the tomb beneath the papal altar. Its
  inherited execution and burial narrative is official shrine reception, not
  independent first-century proof.
- USCCB 2026 calendar entry for the current U.S. solemnity, and the
  *Depositio martyrum*, MGH *Chronica minora* 1, pp. 71–72, for the
  fourth-century 29 June pairing and distinct Catacombs/Ostian locations.
  Neither establishes a joint death date or bodily translation.
- The 25 January and 18 November observances, Pauline Year program, pallium and
  ecumenical practices, patronage catalogues, and art-historical conventions
  remain uncollated leads rather than publication claims.

Complete bibliographic descriptions for work by John Barclay, Douglas

## Required biographical coverage

The publication covers:

1. evidence method, names, Jewish identity, geographic limits, and chronology;
2. the seven undisputed letters, Acts, disputed letters, and Hebrews in
   separate authorship tiers;
3. Pharisaic formation, diaspora and Roman world, work, citizenship, language,
   education, and appearance limits;
4. persecution, revelation and call, the three Acts accounts, baptism,
   Arabia, Damascus, and Jerusalem contacts;
5. Antioch, Barnabas, Acts' three circuits, Macedonia, Achaia, Ephesus, the
   collection, travel, signs, suffering, and communities;
6. relationships with Peter, James, John, Barnabas, Silas, Timothy, Titus,
   Mark, women co-workers, patrons, enslaved people, rivals, and wounded
   communities;
7. cross and Resurrection, grace and justification, Torah and Israel, body of
   Christ, gifts, office, eschatology, bodies, marriage, and sexuality;
8. difficult texts concerning Judaism, women, slavery, sexuality, civic
   authority, and apostolic power, with later reception retained as a future
   research question where no reception-history corpus was inspected;
9. Jerusalem arrest, Caesarean custody, final voyage, Acts' open ending,
   release, Spain, second-arrest hypotheses, and martyrdom;
10. Ostian memorial, basilica, sarcophagus, 2009 announcement and scientific
    limit, the current U.S. 29 June solemnity, and explicit deferral of
    uncollated relic, iconographic, patronage, and sacred-geography leads;
11. a dedicated tradition review; and
12. a final synthesis, detailed chronology, and references.

## Controlling conclusions

- Paul was a Jew, Israelite, Benjaminite, and Pharisee who used Greek and
  operated across Roman provincial systems. Acts alone explicitly supplies
  Tarsus, Gamaliel, citizenship, and the named trade.
- Saul and Paul are coexisting names. No canonical account says Jesus renamed
  him on the Damascus road.
- Paul violently persecuted the Church and experienced the risen Christ as
  revelation, appearance, and call to proclaim among Gentiles. Acts' three
  accounts preserve a common center and differing narrative detail; none has
  a horse.
- The seven undisputed letters are the autobiographical core. Direct
  authorship of Second Thessalonians, Colossians, Ephesians, and the Pastorals
  is differently disputed. Hebrews is anonymous and is not Paul's fourteenth
  autobiographical letter.
- Paul's mission depended on communities and co-workers. Women are historically
  indispensable; enslaved persons and slaveholding structures must be named.
- Paul remained Jewish. His argument against requiring Gentile circumcision
  does not establish contempt for Torah or replacement of Israel.
- The evidence contains both women praying, prophesying, hosting, funding,
  laboring, and bearing offices and restrictive texts with complex authorship
  and transmission. Neither side is suppressed.
- Philemon applies brotherhood within slavery but does not record abolition or
  Onesimus's legal outcome. Later pro-slavery and abolitionist reception was
  not collated and remains a future research question.
- Acts ends without verdict. Release, Spain, renewed eastern work, and a second
  arrest are possible but not secure.
- Roman martyrdom under Nero is probable by cumulative **E/S** evidence.
  Beheading is explicit in Tertullian and plausible; exact date, same-day death
  with Peter, citizen sentence, and execution site are not contemporary data.
- Gaius's report establishes an Ostian memorial in the received witness
  sequence; its precise date was not independently audited. The official shrine
  and basilica preserve received location; this does not prove an untouched
  grave.
- The 2009 papal announcement reports first-to-second-century carbon-14
  consistency. No full laboratory report, calibration dataset, complete
  custody audit, genetic comparator, or unique personal identifier was located.

## Anti-supersessionist control

Paul never becomes a non-Jew in the evidence. His Scriptures, Messiah,
prophetic call language, kinship grief, olive-tree warning, and hope for Israel
remain Jewish. ``Works of law'' is not expanded into a universal definition of
Judaism as self-salvation. Gentiles are warned against boasting over Jewish
branches. Acts' synagogue conflicts and Paul's polemic may not be generalized
into collective Jewish guilt. *Nostra aetate* 4 and the Pontifical Biblical
Commission's 2001 document govern Catholic reception in this publication.

## Exclusions and restraint

- No invented childhood, family, wife, dialogue, continuous itinerary,
  psychological diagnosis, or medical diagnosis of the thorn.
- No claim that Tarsian birth proves elite philosophical schooling.
- No use of ``young man'' or Philemon's ``old man'' to calculate an exact birth
  year.
- No assumption that Acts' speeches are verbatim transcripts.
- No forced equation of Galatians 2 with Acts 11 or 15.
- No claim that Acts' three journeys exhaust Paul's travel.
- No use of the disputed Pastorals as certain proof of release or a second
  imprisonment.
- No promoted Muratorian Spain claim; no critical edition was inspected.
- The ancient 29 June entry is verified; its origin, intervening transmission,
  and any Appian bodily translation remain unresolved.
- No historical claim from uncollated art, patronage, Tre Fontane, Mamertine,
  local relic, exact-last-words, or detailed martyr-act leads.
- No forensic authentication of sarcophagus contents.
- No images. Depth is carried by evidence, sustained narrative, chronology,
  controversy, and reception history.

## Live uncertainties requiring specialist review

- Absolute chronology; primary publication and interpretation of the Gallio
  inscription; relation of Galatians visits to Acts.
- Destination and date of Galatians; Corinthian visit/letter sequence; prison
  location for Philippians and Philemon; possible compositional layers.
- Direct authorship and setting of all six disputed Pauline letters; ancient
  production norms and ethics of pseudonymity.
- Paul's education, social status, citizenship, trade, language profile,
  illness, and the thorn.
- Identity and views of Paul's opponents, especially where only polemic
  survives.
- Interpretation and reception of texts on women, enslaved persons, sexuality,
  Israel, and governing authority.
- Release, Spain, later eastern work, number of Roman imprisonments, exact date
  and manner of death.
- Critical text of First Clement's western phrase and ancient martyr acts.
- Ostian stratigraphy, Gaius's `tropaion`, calendar origin, Appian Way
  commemoration, sarcophagus custody, test laboratory, calibration, and full
  scientific publication.

## Rights boundary

The project contributes original selection, structure, paraphrase, synthesis,
and prose. Scripture, patristic and official translations, web presentations,
modern scholarship, liturgical texts, shrine claims, and scientific reporting
retain their own rights. Only short necessary quotations appear. Public-domain
translations are identified by translator and edition; the age of an ancient
work does not make every modern translation public domain. No prayer, new
Scripture translation, image, scan, bulk OCR, or modern book extract is
vendored.
