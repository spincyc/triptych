# Saint Paul — tradition audit

This record asks what the earliest located witness actually says, what later
reception adds, and what status the publication may claim. “Late” does not mean
useless, and “edifying” does not upgrade chronology. Online loci were reviewed
16 July 2026.

## Traditions intentionally not promoted into the historical core

- an exact birth year, childhood, parental occupation, elite Tarsian degree,
  formal rabbinic ordination, or Sanhedrin seat;
- a horse, divine name change, exact Damascus calendar date, or merged companion
  transcript;
- a named wife, complete medical diagnosis, or recovered physical portrait;
- a complete three-journey itinerary, certain prison for Philippians/Philemon,
  or certain post-Acts circuit;
- Spain as achieved fact, a second Roman imprisonment as certainty, or a
  Muratorian claim not collated;
- a Paul–Seneca friendship, Thecla as contemporary companion, or apocryphal
  dialogue as Paul's words;
- exact Neronian judge, trial sentence, last words, year 67, same-day death,
  Mamertine cell, Tre Fontane springs, or Appian relic route;
- every sword, chain, footprint, cloth, head relic, earth sample, sarcophagus
  fragment, or bone labeled Pauline.

## Audit conclusion

The strongest Roman conclusion does not depend on the picturesque details.
First Clement, Ignatius, Dionysius, Irenaeus, Gaius, Tertullian, the Neronian
context, and the Ostian memorial make Roman martyrdom probable and beheading
an early plausible tradition. The inspected Thecla narrative and Jerome's
notice of the Seneca letters remain evidence for their own reception settings.
Other later stories require an exact witness, date, genre, and provenance
before this publication can describe their cultural or devotional work.
