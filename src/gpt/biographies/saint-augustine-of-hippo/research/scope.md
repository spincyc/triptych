## Reader and question

The intended reader is a serious non-specialist willing to follow exact
patristic loci and evidence limits. The central question is how Augustine's
remembered conversion, common life, pastoral government, writing, and
controversy formed the sanctity received by the Church—and how that reception
can remain candid about coercion, polemical asymmetry, social limits, and later
legend.

## Governing thesis

Augustine's sanctity is not exhausted by a garden scene or by a timeless
system called Augustinianism. It is historically embodied in repeated
conversion: intellectual and sacramental reorientation, common life,
preaching, administration, argument, correction, and penitence. His defense
of coercive law against Donatists is neither erased by his sanctity nor allowed
to erase his whole pastoral and theological life.

## Included

- childhood and education as retrospectively narrated;
- unnamed partner, Adeodatus, Monnica, Alypius, Valerius, Possidius, Jerome,
  women correspondents, clergy, and episcopal networks;
- Manichaean adherence and criticism;
- Milanese intellectual and moral conversion, Cassiciacum, baptism, Ostia,
  return, and common life;
- priesthood, episcopacy, preaching, arbitration, charity, and institutions;
- Manichaean, Donatist, and Pelagian controversies;
- writings embedded in chronology rather than a free-standing doctrine digest;
- Vandal crisis, death, burial, early memory, and carefully bounded later
  relic, liturgical, iconographic, patronage, and institutional leads;
- dedicated review of late, transferred, unsupported, and decontextualized
  traditions.

## Excluded or bounded

- no attempt at a complete intellectual history of every work;
- no new critical edition or translation;
- no independent medical judgment on reported healings;
- no archaeological or scientific authentication of remains;
- no resolution of modern racial identity by inference from art or geography;
- no assumption that later Augustinian institutes were juridically founded by
  Augustine;
- no independent judgment of sanctity or ecclesiastical approval.

## Evidence classes

- **A:** Augustine's works, sermons, and letters, with *Confessions* treated as
  prayerful retrospective self-presentation.
- **N:** Possidius and any separately verified proceedings, laws, or
  correspondence external to Augustine's unilateral control.
- **E:** received late-antique and early-medieval memory, with individual
  chronicle and relic witnesses used only after direct collation.
- **L:** later legend, iconography, quotation culture, and institutional memory.
- **M:** Catholic liturgical and papal reception.
- **K:** identified critical scholarship actually consulted at a reproducible
  locus; publisher-only book records do not qualify.
- **S:** project synthesis explicitly supported by the above.

## Principal corpus used at the cited loci

- Augustine, *Confessions* 1–13; *De beata vita* 1.6; *De magistro*;
  *De doctrina christiana*; *De Trinitate*; *De civitate Dei*;
  *Retractationes*; Letters 21, 93, 130, 166–167, 185, and 213;
  *In epistulam Ioannis ad Parthos* 7.8; relevant anti-Manichaean,
  anti-Donatist, and anti-Pelagian works.
- Possidius, *Life of Augustine*, especially 4–8, 9–19, 22–31.
- Prosper, *Chronicle*, MGH AA 9, p. 473; *Gesta collationis
  Carthaginiensis*, BnF lat. 1546 and CSEL 104; *Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.38;
  Augustine, Letter 211; and Paul the Deacon, *History of the Lombards* 6.48.
- Benedict XVI's audience of 9 January 2008 as official reception, not neutral
  critical biography.

The *Confessions* Latin base and Possidius edition are identified in the
publication. Other Augustinian works are cited by conventional work and locus;
their edition-level limits remain explicit in the source audit.

## Located leads not used as evidentiary support

- the uncollated portions of the 411 proceedings and wider imperial legal
  sequence beyond *Codex Theodosianus* 16.5.38;
- Bede, the first Africa-to-Sardinia relic witness, and current institutional
  or scientific sources beyond Paul the Deacon's Sardinia–Pavia report;
- textual priority and adaptation within the Rule families, and the earliest
  witnesses for the child-and-shell and *Te Deum* traditions;
- Sermons 355–356, the Divjak letters, and the Dolbeau sermons at edition
  level; and
- books by Peter Brown, Erika Hermanowicz, James O'Donnell, Serge Lancel,
  Catherine Conybeare, Robert Markus, Brent Shaw, Paula Fredriksen, and Jennifer
  Ebbeler, for which only bibliographic or publisher records were controlled.

These leads may guide later review. They do not carry a published claim in this
revision and are not represented as though their complete arguments were read.

## Material uncertainties

- precise ancestry, phenotype, and the applicability of modern ethnic labels;
- exact dates of some schooling, travel, Adeodatus's death, the garden
  conversion, return to Africa, and episcopal consecration/succession;
- exact location of Cassiciacum;
- names of Augustine's partner and sister;
- reconstruction of opponents where the archive is Catholic and polemical;
- textual development and priority within the Rule tradition;
- degree and use of Greek and Punic;
- exact chain from Hippo burial through Sardinia to Pavia;
- attribution and dating of individual sermons and letters.
