# Saint Robert Bellarmine — source audit

## Method and source hierarchy

This audit records what each witness can establish, its date and genre, and
the limit that must travel with the claim. Access date for online sources:
**2026-07-20**.

Bellarmine's works and 1613 autobiography are primary evidence for his
arguments, actions he reports, and retrospective self-understanding. They are
not neutral institutional minutes. Curial, Jesuit, episcopal, diplomatic, and
trial records can externally control events, but an inquisitorial or
canonization record has its own legal and adversarial purpose. The first lives
are close to the subject yet promote a cult and cause. Modern reconstruction
is used only at inspected loci. Official Catholic acts establish beatification,
canonization, Doctor status, feast, or current commemoration; their praise is
not substituted for critical biography.

Codes follow `guidance/biographies.md`: **A** subject-authored; **N**
contemporary or near-contemporary external; **E** early received; **L** later
tradition; **M** official ecclesial reception; **K** named modern critical
reconstruction; **S** source-grounded project synthesis.

## Source-family limits and negative results

### Autobiography and APUG material

APUG 2743 is the strongest continuous A witness but is a retrospective,
selective spiritual and institutional self-account completed in 1613. Its
Vulgate, Index, cardinalate, Capua, conclave, charity, and providence claims
must be paired with external records. The GATE transcription is explicitly
marked “To be proofread.” APUG 1460 and 1461 are later copies; APUG 1461
contains an appendix absent from 2743. The deathbed addendum and notarial
recognition are distinct documents and must not be attributed to Bellarmine's
autobiographical voice.

### Printed works and editions

The Fèvre *Opera omnia* (Paris, 1870–1874) and early scans are useful
public-domain Latin witnesses, not modern critical editions. The consequential
*De laicis* III.21 claim was separately checked at printed page 495 in the
1587 Ingolstadt scan. *Recognitio librorum omnium* is an authorial correction
and reception witness, not proof that every surviving printing incorporates
the correction. Work title, book, chapter, edition, page, language, and project
translation must accompany every quotation. Modern commercial English
translations were not used as hidden source text.

### Holy Office, Bruno, and Galileo

No complete Bruno trial dossier survives. The lost eight propositions and the
surviving summary prevent a complete reconstruction of votes and motives. The
Mercati edition was located bibliographically but not collated in full. The
Galileo file is much richer but internally contested: the warning, precept,
certificate, Index decree, later recollections, and 1633 use of the 1616 record
must remain distinct. Bellarmine's 1621 death excludes participation in 1633
but not responsibility for his documented 1616 act.

### Early lives, cult, and official reception

The 1622 and 1624 lives establish early cult-building and may preserve close
testimony, but each anecdote requires its own source chain. Canonization and
Doctor acts competently establish the later ecclesial judgments and their
stated objects. They do not independently authenticate every childhood scene,
charity story, exhumation interpretation, or miracle in modern historical or
medical terms. The Gregorian University's exact *malleus haereticorum* and
Bernini pages control only the posthumous reception and object claims for which
they are cited.

### Negative and unresolved searches

No competent act was located that universally designates Bellarmine patron of
catechists, catechumens, canonists, lawyers, or another class. This negative
does not displace Paul VI's exact 1967 act for the Archdiocese of Capua. No
independent scientific authentication of the remains at Sant'Ignazio was
located. No secure contemporary portrait was established. No exact early
witness was controlled for the “walls do not catch cold” saying or similar
detached charity maxims. These claims belong in `tradition-audit.md` as
unresolved, not in the narrative as fact.

## Edition, translation, and rights notes

- Bellarmine's early-modern Latin and Italian and nineteenth-century editions
  are public-domain textual candidates. Scan, photograph, IIIF, catalog, and
  repository terms remain separately controlling.
- APUG/GATE manuscript images and metadata must be cited and not redistributed
  without checking the site's terms. Its unfinished transcription is a finding
  aid, not quotation authority.
- Motta, modern scholarship, Baldini–Coyne, Mayer, Finocchiaro, Vatican
  Observatory English, Treccani and Gregorian prose, Vatican and Dicastery
  website text, and modern editorial apparatus and translations retain their
  respective rights. They may be cited and newly paraphrased within the
  project's limits; no sustained wording is copied.
- Any brief Bellarmine quotation must be checked in Latin or Italian against a
  named edition or manuscript image and newly translated or credited. No
  unattributed protected English translation is used.
- No third-party image, facsimile, manuscript page, or portrait is authorized
  for publication merely because it is viewable online.

## Known audit limits

The identified source architecture supports a qualified working biography,
but publication-level review must still collate the complete Bellarmine corpus
used, Le Bachelet's document editions, the Mercati Bruno summary, the complete
Galileo dossier and recent scholarship, Capuan synodal and visitation acts,
the original Capuan appointment and consecration acts, *de auxiliis*
memorials, reciprocal Venetian and English controversial
literature, conclave documentation, and the cause and relic files. Each
retained tradition, quotation, count, and current institutional claim still
requires item-level verification in the exact future prose snapshot.
