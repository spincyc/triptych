\subsection{Bellarmine's works and authorial records}

\begin{itemize}
\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{Vita Roberti card. Bellarmini ab ipso
conscripta Anno MDCXIII}, Archivio della Pontificia Università Gregoriana,
APUG 2743, fols.~1r--12v,
\sourceurl{https://gate.unigre.it/mediawiki/index.php/Index\%3AAPUG_2743.pdf}{GATE
index and manuscript images}; accessed 2026-07-20.  This is the controlling
continuous authorial witness.  The site's transcription is marked ``To be
proofread,'' so no unchecked transcription controls a quotation.  The later
deathbed declaration and notarial material adjoining the life are treated as
separate documents.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{Institutiones linguae Hebraicae} (Rome,
1578).  Used as bibliographic and authorial evidence of Hebrew study; no claim
of modern philological competence is inferred.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{Disputationes de controversiis christianae
fidei}, first printed in volumes at Ingolstadt, 1586--1593, especially
\emph{De Romano Pontifice} V, chs.~6--7, on direct and indirect power, and
\emph{De membris Ecclesiae militantis}, book III, \emph{De laicis}, ch.~21,
on temporal punishment of heretics.  The latter claim was checked in the 1587
Ingolstadt printing of \emph{Liber tertius de laicis sive de secularibus},
printed p.~495 (scan page 71), Zentralbibliothek Zürich, Rs~355,
\sourceurl{https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-23569}{e-rara record and scan};
accessed 2026-07-20.  The page image and Latin determination were inspected;
the publication paraphrases rather than reproduces it.  Broader working corpus
control also used the Fèvre \emph{Opera omnia} (Paris, 1870--1874),
\sourceurl{https://openlibrary.org/books/OL26396532M/Opera_omnia}{public-domain
volume record}; accessed 2026-07-20.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{Dottrina christiana breve} (1597) and
\emph{Dichiarazione più copiosa della dottrina christiana} (1598).  Used for
the catechetical sequence and pastoral purpose; no uncontrolled count of
editions or translations is repeated.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{Recognitio librorum omnium}, located
Ingolstadt printing (1608); \emph{Responsio Matthaei Torti} (1608); and
\emph{De potestate Summi Pontificis in rebus temporalibus adversus
Gulielmum Barclaeum} (1610).  Used as bibliographic authorial controls for
correction, the English oath controversy, and indirect power.  A reported Rome
1607 \emph{Recognitio} remains unlocated.  The exact 1606 Venetian writings
were not collated; Motta controls that episode's broad sequence.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  Letter to Paolo Antonio Foscarini, 12 April 1615,
in Antonio Favaro, ed., \emph{Le Opere di Galileo Galilei}, Edizione
Nazionale, vol.~XII, no.~1110, pp.~171--172.  The event is also indexed in
\sourceurl{https://galileoteca.museogalileo.it/GTArchivi/QCrono?anno=1615\&lang=en}{Museo
Galileo's 1615 chronology}; accessed 2026-07-20.  The publication newly
paraphrases the source rather than borrowing a modern English translation.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  Autograph draft of the certificate to Galileo, 26
May 1616, Archivum Romanum Societatis Iesu, \emph{Opera nostrorum} 243 I,
fol.~243r.  Edition and study: Ugo Baldini and George V. Coyne,
\emph{The Louvain Lectures (Lectiones Lovanienses) of Bellarmine and the
Autograph Copy of his 1616 Declaration to Galileo} (Vatican City, 1984),
pp.~25--26; contextual resource through the
\sourceurl{https://www.vaticanobservatory.org/resources/galileo/bellarmine-and-the-autograph-of-his-1616-declaration-to-galileo/}{Vatican
Observatory}; accessed 2026-07-20.  The modern edition, presentation,
facsimile, and translation retain their rights.

\item Bellarmine, Robert.  \emph{De ascensione mentis in Deum} (1615),
\emph{De aeterna felicitate} (1616), \emph{De gemitu columbae} (1617),
\emph{De septem verbis} (1618), \emph{De officio principis christiani}
(1619), and \emph{De arte bene moriendi} (1620).  Used for the annual late
spiritual sequence; individual edition histories remain to be collated.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contemporary process and early reception}

\begin{itemize}
\item Roman Holy Office, surviving summary tradition of the proceedings
against Giordano Bruno.  Edition lead: Angelo Mercati, \emph{Il sommario del
processo di Giordano Bruno} (Vatican City, 1942).  The complete edition was not
collated for this study, so this bibliographic lead does not independently
carry the January 1599 sequence reconstructed in the next item; the lost
propositions and dossier are not reconstructed by conjecture.

\item Gatti, Hilary.  ``Giordano Bruno,'' \emph{Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy}, section~1, revised 2024,
\sourceurl{https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bruno/}{online article}; accessed
2026-07-20.  Used as the inspected source-based modern control for the process
sequence, lost record, collective setting, and limits on a cosmology-only
account.

\item Roman Inquisition dossier for 24--26 February and 5 March 1616, used
through Maurice A. Finocchiaro, \emph{The Galileo Affair: A Documentary
History} (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1989), especially the
sequence around pp.~146--153.  Warning, acquiescence, stronger precept, and
Index decree are not silently fused.

\item Mayer, Thomas F.  ``The Roman Inquisition's Precept to Galileo
(1616),'' \emph{British Journal for the History of Science}, 2009,
\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-for-the-history-of-science/article/abs/roman-inquisitions-precept-to-galileo-1616/056497F7D37EDF0BE8096DA87AE73B11}{publisher
record}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used to preserve the modern dispute about the
precept's authenticity, legality, and relation to Bellarmine's warning.

\item D'Amato, Francesco Maria, published under the name Marcello Cervini.
\emph{Adumbrata imago} (1622); and Giacomo Fuligatti, \emph{Vita del
Cardinale Roberto Bellarmino} (Rome, 1624),
\sourceurl{https://bibliotecavirtualmadrid.comunidad.madrid/bvmadrid_publicacion/en/consulta/registro.do?id=395097}{digital
catalog record}; accessed 2026-07-20.  These are early, close, edifying, and
cause-promoting witnesses, not neutral modern biographies.

\item Pontifical Gregorian University.  ``Unlocking Bellarmine,'' especially
the
\sourceurl{https://www.unigre.it/it/archivio-storico/museion/unlocking-bellarmine/cronologia-una-selezione/}{selected
chronology} and the
\sourceurl{https://www.unigre.it/it/archivio-storico/museion/unlocking-bellarmine/le-vite-del-bellarmino/dalle-vite-alle-biografie/}{history
of the early lives}; the
\sourceurl{https://www.unigre.it/it/archivio-storico/museion/unlocking-bellarmine/malleus-haereticorum/}{early
\emph{malleus haereticorum} reception}; and the
\sourceurl{https://www.unigre.it/it/archivio-storico/museion/unlocking-bellarmine/iconografia-bellarminiana/il-busto-di-g-l-bernini/}{Bernini
bust and monument history}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used at identified item level
for institutional chronology, manuscript and reception orientation, and
object history.  Exhibit prose is paraphrased and occasional metadata errors
are not allowed to override competent acts.

\item Italian Episcopal Conference, BeWeB.  ``Diocesi di Capua,''
\sourceurl{https://www.beweb.chiesacattolica.it/diozesen/diozese/170/Diocesi\%2Bdi\%2BCapua}{diocesan
institutional history}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used to preserve its distinction
among election on 25 March 1602, consecration on 21 April, probable private
arrival on 1 May, and formal entry on 4 May.  Its dates are reported beside,
not silently preferred to, the conflicting official and modern records.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Official Catholic and current custodial reception}

\begin{itemize}
\item Pius XI.  \emph{Lux illa}, canonization decretal, 29 June 1930,
\emph{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 22 (1930), 593--602, especially 598 on
beatification and the 1923 transfer and 601--602 on the dispositive act (with
Bellarmine's 17 September clause on p.~601),
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-22-1930-ocr.pdf}{official
PDF}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used for the official acts and their stated
objects, not as sole critical evidence for the life or independent medical
review of miracles.

\item Pius XI.  \emph{Providentissimus Deus}, 17 September 1931,
\emph{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 23 (1931), 433--438, dispositive text at 437,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-23-1931-ocr.pdf}{official
PDF}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Controls the Doctor declaration and extension of
the then 13 May Mass and Office; it does not approve every proposition or
curial act.

\item Paul VI.  \emph{Roma altera}, apostolic letter, 29 September 1967,
\emph{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 60 (1968), 12--13,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/la/apost_letters/documents/hf_p-vi_apl_19670929_roma-altera.html}{Holy
See text}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Controls Bellarmine's formal designation as
equal principal heavenly patron of the Archdiocese of Capua with Saint Stephen
Protomartyr; it establishes no universal professional or devotional class.

\item Dicastery for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments.
``Calendarium Romanum,''
\sourceurl{https://www.cultodivino.va/en/formazione/pubblicazioni/libri-liturgici/aliae/calendarium-romanum.html}{official
publication record}; and Italian Episcopal Conference, \emph{Liturgia delle
Ore}, vol.~III,
\sourceurl{https://liturgico.chiesacattolica.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2017/09/21/Liturgia-delle-Ore-III-ULN-web.pdf}{current
General Roman Calendar table}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Together they control the
current 17 September optional memorial and its general-calendar scope;
particular calendars may differ.

\item Bertone, Tarcisio.  Homily for Saint Robert Bellarmine, 22 May 2007,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/card-bertone/2007/documents/rc_seg-st_20070522_s-roberto-bellarmino_it.html}{Holy
See text}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used as official reception explicitly
distinguishing the former 13 May observance from the calendar reform's return
to 17 September, and as a bounded institutional claim that Bellarmine is
patron of the Gregorian university family.

\item Dicastery for the Causes of Saints.  ``San Roberto Bellarmino,''
\sourceurl{https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/roberto-bellarmino.html}{official
record}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used for identity, office anchors, beatification,
canonization, and current 17 September memorial.  Its life chronology is later
official concurrence, not an independent contemporary act; the mutable page is
not used to create anachronistic institutional membership or universal
patronage.

\item Benedict XVI.  General Audience, 23 February 2011,
\sourceurl{https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2011/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20110223.html}{Holy
See English}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used as modern official Catholic reception
of Bellarmine as saint, Doctor, pastor, theologian, and spiritual writer, not
as a critical source for disputed sixteenth-century events.

\item Church of Sant'Ignazio, Rome.  Current art and chapel description,
\sourceurl{https://chiesasantignazio.it/opere-arte-chiesa/}{official church
page}; accessed 2026-07-20.  Used only for the church's present claim to hold
Bellarmine's body.  It is not independent biological authentication or a
complete custody history.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modern critical reconstruction}

\begin{itemize}
\item Motta, Franco.  ``Bellarmino, Roberto,'' \emph{Dizionario Biografico
degli Italiani}, 2016,
\sourceurl{https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/santo-roberto-bellarmino_\%28Dizionario-Biografico\%29/}{Treccani
online}; accessed 2026-07-20.  This is the principal inspected modern control
for formation, office chronology, print sequence, French legation, Sixtine
Index, Vulgate revision, \emph{de auxiliis}, Capua, the conclaves, political
controversies, late writings, and death.  Interpretations of motive are named
as reconstruction rather than documentary fact.

\end{itemize}
