\subsection{Tertullian's works actually inspected}

The Latin loci were controlled through modern critical texts where a lexical or
textual claim mattered. The linked nineteenth-century \emph{Ante-Nicene
Fathers} (ANF) translations are public-domain research aids, not controlling
editions. Their chapter divisions and wording were checked against the work and
are cited here so a reader can inspect the passages without implying that one
old English version resolves the Latin.

\begin{itemize}
\item Tertullian. \emph{Apologeticum}, especially 1--2, 9, 17--18, 30--33,
37, 39, 42, 46, and 50. ANF vol.~3 research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0301.htm}{New Advent text};
accessed 2026-07-19. Latin controlled through Eligius Dekkers et al., eds.,
\emph{Tertulliani Opera}, CCSL 1 (Turnhout: Brepols, 1954), and the critical
tradition described below. Reported pagan speech, rhetorical demographics, and
the compressed Latin of 17.6 and 50.13 remain marked.

\item Tertullian. \emph{Ad nationes}, especially 1.1--7 and 1.17.4 (the
Albinus allusion); and \emph{Ad Scapulam}, especially 2--5. Public-domain ANF research
texts through the
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/anf/anf03/anf03-19.htm}{Tertullian
Project's ANF contents} and
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0305.htm}{New Advent's \emph{To
Scapula}}; accessed 2026-07-19. The eclipse identification is a modern
chronological control, not supplied as a civil date by the author.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De spectaculis}, especially 1--2, 19, and 30;
\emph{De idololatria}, especially 1, 10--19, and 24; \emph{Ad martyras};
\emph{Scorpiace}; and \emph{De fuga in persecutione}. Public-domain ANF
research translations through
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0303.htm}{\emph{On Spectacles}}
and the
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/works.htm}{Tertullian Project work
index}; accessed 2026-07-19. Different treatments of civic participation,
flight, martyrdom, and service are not assigned one date merely to remove their
tension.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De corona}, especially 3--4, 6, and 11. Public-domain ANF
research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0304.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used for baptismal and daily customs, unwritten tradition, and the
military-service argument; not treated as a universal service book.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De baptismo}, especially 1, 3--8, 13, 15--20.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0321.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Compare Ernest Evans, ed.~and trans., \emph{Tertullian's Homily on
Baptism} (London: SPCK, 1964), a copyrighted critical edition used through
paraphrase. The reported Asian-presbyter episode is not represented as extant
minutes.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De oratione}, especially 1, 6, 23, and 25--29.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0322.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used by locus for the Lord's Prayer, Eucharistic bread, posture,
hours, psalmody, and spiritual sacrifice.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De paenitentia}, especially 1 and 4--10; and
\emph{De patientia}, especially 1. Public-domain ANF research translations
through \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0320.htm}{\emph{On
Repentance}} and the
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/works.htm}{work index}; accessed
2026-07-19. The early provision for post-baptismal repentance is read against,
not silently harmonized with, \emph{On Modesty}.

\item Tertullian. \emph{Ad uxorem}, books 1--2, especially 1.1--7 and
2.2--8. Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0404.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. The addressed marriage establishes that the author had a wife; the
ideal of 2.8 does not establish the details of their household.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De cultu feminarum}, especially 1.1--3 and book 2;
and \emph{De virginibus velandis}, especially 1, 9, and 17. Public-domain ANF
research translations,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0402.htm}{\emph{On the Apparel of
Women}} and
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0403.htm}{\emph{On the Veiling of
Virgins}}; accessed 2026-07-19. For critical text and commentary, compare Marie
Turcan, ed.~and trans., \emph{Tertullien: La toilette des femmes}, SC 173
(Paris: Cerf, 1971), and Vincent Bulhart, ed., \emph{De virginibus velandis},
CSEL 76 (Vienna: H\"older--Pichler--Tempsky, 1957). The latter controls the
third-person closing name ``Septimius Tertullianus.'' Gendered rhetoric,
ministry restrictions, developing discipline, and transmitted name attribution
are kept distinct.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De exhortatione castitatis}, especially 7--8;
\emph{De monogamia}, especially 1--3 and 11--12; and \emph{De ieiunio adversus
psychicos}. Public-domain ANF research translations,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0405.htm}{\emph{Exhortation to
Chastity}}, \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0406.htm}{\emph{On
Monogamy}}, and the
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/works.htm}{work index}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used as a developing sequence rather than one undated sexual or
fasting ethic.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De pudicitia}, especially 1, 10, and 20--21.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0407.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Compare Bradley K. Storin and Ellen Muehlberger,
\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-edition-of-early-christian-writings/tertullian-of-carthage-on-repentance-and-on-modesty-14-2122/0E810A47A37328E9C19F777B5EAE2D38}{``Tertullian of Carthage, \emph{On
Repentance} and \emph{On Modesty} 1--4, 21--22''}, in \emph{The Cambridge
Edition of Early Christian Writings} (Cambridge University Press, online 2025),
accessed 2026-07-19. The unnamed edict is not assigned to Callistus as fact.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De praescriptione haereticorum}, especially 7,
13, 19--21, 32, and 36. Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0311.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. \emph{Adversus omnes haereses}, appended in part of the tradition,
is excluded from Tertullian's authentic voice.

\item Tertullian. \emph{Adversus Marcionem}, books 1--5, especially 1.1,
1.3--5, 2.4, 3, 4.19, 4.40, and the scriptural sequences of books 4--5.
Public-domain ANF research translations,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03121.htm}{book 1},
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03122.htm}{book 2},
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03123.htm}{book 3},
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03124.htm}{book 4}, and

\item Tertullian. \emph{Adversus Praxean}, especially 1--3, 8--9, 25--30.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0317.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Compare Ernest Evans, ed.~and trans., \emph{Tertullian's Treatise

\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-edition-of-early-christian-writings/tertullian-against-praxeas-14-and-2730/10A639F2329CDAEFAF6063A9A194F397}{the current Cambridge selection}; accessed
2026-07-19. Latin technical terms are treated in their pre-Nicene semantic

\item Tertullian. \emph{De carne Christi}, especially 5, 17--23.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0315.htm}{New Advent}; accessed

\emph{credo quia absurdum} maxim.

\item Tertullian. \emph{De resurrectione carnis}, especially 1, 8, and 63.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0316.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Compare Ernest Evans, ed.~and trans., \emph{Tertullian's Treatise on

\item Tertullian. \emph{De anima}, especially 5--9, 19--27, and 39--41.
Public-domain ANF research translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0310.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Compare J. H. Waszink, ed.,~\emph{Quinti Septimi Florentis

ethnographic description.

\subsection{Contemporary setting and ancient reception}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs}, Latin and English in Herbert
Musurillo, ed.~and trans., \emph{The Acts of the Christian Martyrs} (Oxford:
Clarendon Press, 1972), pp.~86--89. Used for the Carthaginian trial of 17 July
180 and the Latin African Christian setting; protected translation is
paraphrased.

\item \emph{The Passion of Saints Perpetua and Felicity}. Critical historical
control through Thomas J. Heffernan, \emph{The Passion of Perpetua and Felicity}
(Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2012),
\sourceurl{https://academic.oup.com/book/37222}{publisher record}. Used for the
202/203 Carthaginian martyr context and the limits of the Tertullian editorship
hypothesis; no protected wording is reproduced.

\item Eusebius of Caesarea. \emph{Ecclesiastical History} 2.2.4--6, trans.
Arthur Cushman McGiffert, in NPNF, second series, vol.~1,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used for the Greek \emph{Apologeticum}, reputation, and knowledge
of Roman law; it does not establish professional employment.

\item Eusebius of Caesarea. \emph{Ecclesiastical History} 5.16--19, trans.
Arthur Cushman McGiffert, in NPNF, second series, vol.~1,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250105.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used for the earliest substantial anti-New-Prophecy dossier,
including excerpts from an anonymous disputant, Apollonius, Serapion, and
related letters. Its proximity to the Asian controversy is retained together
with its hostile selection, fragmentary survival, and Eusebian mediation.

\item Lactantius. \emph{Divine Institutes} 5.1 and 5.4, public-domain ANF
translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/07015.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used for early praise and criticism of Tertullian's learning,
advocacy, and style.

\item Jerome. \emph{On Illustrious Men} 53,
\sourceurl{https://www.tertullian.org/jerome_biog.htm}{Latin and research
translation with source notes}; accessed 2026-07-19. Every component---name,
father, presbyterate, Roman envy, New Prophecy breach, Cyprian anecdote, and old
age---is treated as a distinct late report rather than an indivisible life.

\item Jerome. \emph{Against Helvidius} 19, public-domain NPNF translation,
\sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3007.htm}{New Advent}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used for Jerome's selective distancing of Tertullian in the Marian
controversy, not as an independent witness to Mary's household.

\item Cyprian of Carthage. \emph{On the Dress of Virgins}, \emph{On the
Advantage of Patience}, \emph{On the Unity of the Catholic Church}, and
selected letters, in CCSL 3--3C and public-domain ANF research translations
through the \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/05.htm}{New Advent
Cyprian index}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for literary reception and a distinct
African episcopal response, not personal contact with Tertullian.

\item Augustine of Hippo. \emph{De haeresibus} 86, Latin and English research
text, \sourceurl{https://tertullian.org/tertullianistae/de_haeresibus.htm}{the
Tertullianists dossier}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used for the fifth-century group
and basilica transfer; not backdated into a foundation act by Tertullian.

\item Vincent of L\'erins. \emph{Commonitorium} 18, public-domain NPNF
translation, \sourceurl{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3506.htm}{New
Advent}; accessed 2026-07-19. Used as fifth-century praise and warning, not as
contemporary biography.

\item Pseudo-Gelasian dossier. \emph{Decretum Gelasianum de libris recipiendis
et non recipiendis}, critical study and text in Ernst von Dobsch\"utz,
\emph{Texte und Untersuchungen} 38.4 (Leipzig: Hinrichs, 1912), especially the
list of rejected writings. Used as composite reception whose papal attribution
and stages are disputed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modern historical and theological controls}

\begin{itemize}
\item Barnes, Timothy D. \emph{Tertullian: A Historical and Literary Study},
2nd ed. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1985. Foundational modern revision of the life,
chronology, legal-career assumptions, and corpus; used as a named
reconstruction, not an ancient source.

\item Rankin, David. \emph{Tertullian and the Church}. Cambridge: Cambridge
University Press, 1995,
\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/tertullian-and-the-church/contents/790B658F97886460DFFBE89B6E46E0DA}{publisher contents}. Used for ecclesiology,
office, separation, and the limits of the Catholic/Montanist binary.

\item Osborn, Eric. \emph{Tertullian: First Theologian of the West}. Cambridge:
Cambridge University Press, 1997. Used for the integrated theological
architecture, reason, rule of faith, Trinity, and material creation. ``First''
is received as a claim about the surviving Latin corpus, not solitary invention.

\item Dunn, Geoffrey D. \emph{Tertullian}. Early Church Fathers. London and New
York: Routledge, 2004,
\sourceurl{https://www.routledge.com/Tertullian/Dunn/p/book/9780203642870}{publisher record}. Used for a current synthesis of life, works, Church, ethics,
and reception.

\item Trevett, Christine. \emph{Montanism: Gender, Authority and the New
Prophecy}. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1996. Used to distinguish the
movement's Asian beginnings, later regional forms, women prophets, adherent
claims, and hostile heresiology.

\item Wilhite, David E. \emph{Tertullian the African: An Anthropological Reading
of Tertullian's Context and Identities}. Millennium-Studien 14. Berlin: De
Gruyter, 2007,
\sourceurl{https://www.uplopen.com/books/m/10.1515/9783110926262}{open-access
record}. Used as an influential postcolonial reconstruction whose ethnicity and
resistance claims remain debated rather than biographical fact.

\item Burns, J. Patout, Jr., and Robin M. Jensen. \emph{Christianity in Roman
Africa: The Development of Its Practices and Beliefs}. Grand Rapids: Eerdmans,
2014. Used for the urban, ritual, martyr, and ecclesial context; regional
patterns are not assigned automatically to Tertullian.

\item Lieu, Judith M. \emph{Marcion and the Making of a Heretic: God and
Scripture in the Second Century}. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2015.
Used to control the difference between Tertullian's adversarial Marcion and a
modern reconstruction from multiple witnesses.

\item Secord, Jared. ``Tertullian, \emph{Apology} 21,'' in \emph{The Cambridge
Edition of Early Christian Writings},
\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-edition-of-early-christian-writings/tertullian-apology-21/D7F372A70F2B1F20F6E402B4CA6419FE}{Cambridge chapter record} (online 2022);
accessed 2026-07-19. Used for the compact c.~170 birth and c.~196--212 literary
career reconstruction and for current apologetic context; alternative ranges
remain stated.

\item Storin, Bradley K., and Ellen Muehlberger. ``Tertullian of Carthage,
\emph{On Repentance} and \emph{On Modesty} 1--4, 21--22,'' in \emph{The

\sourceurl{https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-edition-of-early-christian-writings/tertullian-of-carthage-on-repentance-and-on-modesty-14-2122/0E810A47A37328E9C19F777B5EAE2D38}{Cambridge chapter record} (online 2025);

\item Troyes, M\'ediath\`eque Jacques-Chirac, MS 523, late twelfth century,

\item The Tertullian Project,

\item Vetus Latina Institute. \emph{Vetus Latina Database and manuscript
research environment},
\sourceurl{https://vetus-latina.manuscriptroom.com/}{official portal}; accessed
2026-07-19. Used to control the claim that Tertullian is an important Old Latin
witness while free citation, translation, and memory prevent reconstruction of
one personal Bible from isolated verses.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Translation and reuse boundary}

Ancient Latin and Greek are public domain. The ANF and NPNF English research
translations linked above are public domain; the hosting sites' editorial
presentation and metadata are not project-owned. Modern critical editions,
translations, scholarship, Vatican English, catalog records, and manuscript
images retain their own rights. This publication uses brief ancient wording by
exact locus, paraphrases protected analysis and translation, reproduces no
third-party image, and never treats web access as a reuse license.
