# Tertullian — research scope

## Document identity

- **Publication ID:** `biographies/tertullian`
- **Title:** *Tertullian: The Rhetorician of Carthage and the Cost of Rigor*
- **Genre:** source-first historical biography of an early Christian author
  under `guidance/biographies.md`; not a critical edition, saint's Life,
  doctrinal manual, or canonization dossier
- **Subject:** Tertullian of Carthage, whose transmitted third-person closing
  attribution in *On the Veiling of Virgins* 17 names Septimius Tertullianus;
  Latin Christian apologist, moralist, exegete, polemicist, and theologian
- **Controlled range:** `fl. c. 196/197–212 or later`. Birth is unknown and is
  placed only in a broad modern range of c. 155–170; death occurred after 212
  but its year, place, and cause are unknown. The customary c. 220/225 endpoint
  combines relative work chronology with Jerome's much later old-age report.
- **Principal place and setting:** Carthage in Roman Africa Proconsularis under
  the Severans. Rome matters to several arguments and traditions but is not a
  securely documented residence; Phrygia and Asia belong to the New Prophecy's
  history, not to an established journey by Tertullian.
- **Language and ecclesial frame:** the surviving corpus is Latin. Tertullian
  reports Greek works now lost and engages Greek Scripture and learning. His
  biblical wording must be controlled as Old Latin evidence, possible authorial
  rendering, and transmitted text rather than as one recoverable personal
  Bible. He was a married convert; presbyterate is a late and disputed report.
- **Mutable-claim cutoff:** online institutional, manuscript, publisher, papal,
  and scholarship records were checked through 2026-07-19. No current shrine,
  cult, relic, feast, or patronage claim is inferred from an older notice.

## Reader and governing question

The intended reader is a serious general reader, cleric, student, or researcher
who needs a sustained life without a fabricated private chronology. The
governing question is how a married Christian intellectual in Carthage made
Latin prose carry an integrated account of Church, body, truth, worship, and
discipline, and why the same habits of exactness, adversarial boundary-making,
and escalating rigor led him toward isolation.

## Governing thesis and boundary

Tertullian's theological achievement and estrangement grew from related habits:
material and linguistic exactness, refusal of divided allegiance, insistence
that belief take bodily form, and confidence that the Paraclete demanded a
stricter discipline. His writings supplied durable Latin theological resources,
yet their developing occasions resist division into a simple Catholic period
followed by one dated “Montanist conversion.” Catholic reception later selected
particular arguments and formulations while also making his rigor a warning.

This is a historical synthesis, not an ecclesiastical verdict. It does not
decide Tertullian's eternal state, canonize him, make “Father” or “saint” a
juridical status, identify every unnamed opponent, reconcile every change of
position, reconstruct Marcion's lost edition from one hostile witness, or turn
selective Catholic citation into approval of the whole corpus.

## Included

- the Roman and African setting, prior martyr records, Carthaginian Christian

- name, education, conversion, baptism, marriage, household, occupation,
  ministry, and chronology, including every material uncertainty;
- the apologetic, catechetical, moral, anti-heretical, exegetical, doctrinal,
  disciplinary, and New Prophecy writings as developing genres rather than a
  neutral autobiography;
- public Christianity, imperial loyalty, coercion, martyrdom, civic life,
  military service, assembly, poor relief, and the limits of rhetorical totals;
- baptism, prayer, Eucharistic passages, custom, Scripture, rule of faith,
  apostolic custody, and Old Latin evidence at controlled loci;
- flesh, resurrection, soul, freedom, marriage, gender, Mary, Trinitarian and
  Christological language, and pre-conciliar semantic limits;
- the developing New Prophecy allegiance, breach with those called
  *psychici*, disputed Roman-bishop and absolution identifications, and absence

- work revision, lost Greek writings, spuria, manuscript families, early print,
  critical editions, and the limits of work and manuscript counts;
- Cyprianic, Lactantian, Eusebian, Jeromian, Augustinian, Vincentian, Catholic,

- memorable biographical, quotation, ecclesial, cultic, burial, relic, and
  foundation claims reviewed as traditions rather than silently admitted.

## Excluded or deliberately bounded

- no line-by-line commentary on every authentic treatise or complete inventory
  of every biblical citation, lexical coinage, work title, lost work, fragment,
  manuscript, early edition, or modern translation;
- no complete collation of the *Apologeticum* tradition, Cluniacense,
  Agobardinum, Trecense, Corbeiense, Ottobonian excerpts, or work-specific
  textual families;
- no reconstruction of a single personal Bible or full Vetus Latina apparatus
  from Tertullian's citations;
- no complete independent reconstruction of Marcion, Praxeas, Hermogenes,
  Valentinian schools, the New Prophecy, the unnamed bishop in *On Modesty*,
  or every African and Roman ecclesial institution;
- no whole-corpus lexical or ethical census of gendered, anti-Jewish, coercive,
  or adversarial rhetoric; publication-level judgments stay with identified
  passages and stated scholarly controls;
- no archaeological identification of home, church, basilica, grave, relic,
  school, scriptorium, or library, and no item-level survey of iconography,
  patronage, local devotion, or calendars; and
- no independent patristic, theological, rights, ecclesiastical, imprimatur,
  or canonization judgment.

## Evidence classes

- **A — subject-authored evidence:** Tertullian's works, controlled by exact
  locus, genre, audience, adversarial purpose, relative date, revision, and
  manuscript transmission. Self-reference is literary self-presentation, not a
  neutral personnel record.
- **N — contemporary or near-contemporary external witness:** especially the
  African martyr records and early literary reception. Proximity to Tertullian's
  world does not make a witness an eyewitness to his life.
- **E — early received report:** especially Lactantius, Eusebius, Jerome,
  Augustine, and Vincent of Lérins. It establishes ancient reception and may
  preserve lost reports without becoming contemporary biography.
- **L — later tradition:** medieval paratext, manuscript and print transmission,
  popular quotation, occupation, devotional, visual, local, and cult claims;
  evidence first for the transmitting period.
- **M — official ecclesial reception:** limited to the object and force of the
  particular act, such as a Catechism citation or papal general audience.
  Neither canonizes Tertullian nor approves his entire corpus.
- **K — named modern critical reconstruction:** used only to its inspected
  premises and alternatives, including chronology, identity, status, opponent
  identification, attribution, and manuscript relationship.
- **S — source-grounded project synthesis:** editorial inference from identified
  evidence, never placed in an ancient witness's voice.

## Principal corpus and transmission controls

The principal subject-authored corpus inspected comprises *To the Nations*,
*Apologeticum*, *To Scapula*, the martyr and civic-life writings, *On the
Crown*, *On Baptism*, *On Prayer*, *On Repentance*, *On Patience*, *To His
Wife*, *On the Apparel of Women*, *On the Veiling of Virgins*, *Exhortation to
Chastity*, *On Monogamy*, *On Fasting*, *On Modesty*, *Prescription against
Heretics*, *Against Marcion*, *Against Praxeas*, *On the Flesh of Christ*, *On
the Soul*, and *On the Resurrection of the Flesh*. Other authentic works are
used only at identified loci. *Against the Jews* is qualified because the unity
and authorship of its later portion are disputed. *Against All Heresies* and
verse and prose spuria are not silently merged into Tertullian's voice.

External controls are the Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs, *Passion of Perpetua
and Felicity*, Eusebius's hostile and excerpted New Prophecy dossier in HE
5.16–19, Lactantius, Eusebius's other Tertullian notices, Jerome, Cyprian's
literary reception, Augustine, Vincent of Lérins, and the composite
Pseudo-Gelasian dossier.
Official Catholic reception is limited to the Catechism's actual citations and
Benedict XVI's 30 May 2007 catechesis. Modern controls include Barnes, Rankin,
Osborn, Dunn, Trevett, Wilhite, Burns and Jensen, Lieu, Secord, Storin and
Muehlberger, Binder, work-specific critical editions, institutional catalogs,
and Vetus Latina resources.

No manuscript earlier than the late-eighth-century *Against the Jews* fragment
in BnF lat. 13047 was located. The *Apologeticum* has a substantially separate
tradition of more than three dozen witnesses. Other works travel unevenly in
the Cluniacense, Agobardinum, Trecense, Corbeiense, and excerpt collections;
Codex Agobardinus preserves thirteen surviving works from a larger collection,
and Troyes 523 is the sole extant representative of the Trecense family and
carries five Tertullian works. That family-level fact does not make it the sole
witness to each work. Lost Corbie witnesses remain partly available through
fragments, collations, and early editions.
Tertullian himself reports revision of *Against Marcion* and lost Greek works.
Every lexical or textual claim therefore remains work-specific rather than
assuming one uniform 1954 collation or an authorial autograph.

## Material uncertainties

- exact name beyond the third-person closing attribution “Septimius
  Tertullianus”; birth year and place;
  ancestry, ethnicity, family, social and legal status, teachers, and education;
- Jerome's centurion-father report and whether the title itself represents a
  historically recoverable office;
- conversion motive, date, place, teacher, baptism, and the details of the
  marriage or household beyond the addressed wife;
- Roman residence, professional advocacy, formal legal employment, and identity
  with any similarly named jurist in the *Digest*;
- ordination, ministry office, and how to relate Jerome's presbyterate to
  Tertullian's lay self-placement in *Exhortation to Chastity* 7.3 and *On
  Monogamy* 12.2;
- fine chronology and revision of most writings; the institutional stages of
  New Prophecy allegiance; and any exact “Montanist conversion” date;
- identity of the Roman bishop in *Against Praxeas* 1, identity of the bishop
  and edict in *On Modesty* 1, the Callistus hypothesis, and any formal
  excommunication or transfer act;
- editorship of the *Passion of Perpetua and Felicity*;
- unity and later chapters of *Against the Jews*, reconstruction of opponents,
  exact Latin biblical sources, and scope of lexical originality;
- precise contents, ancestry, and lost exemplars of every manuscript family,
  and exact totals of authentic, doubtful, spurious, and lost works; and
- final communion, death year, cause and place, burial, grave, relics, cult,
  feast, iconographic type, patronage, and any institutional continuity from
  Tertullian to Augustine's fifth-century Tertullianists.

## Located leads not used beyond their inspected scope

- The Tertullian Project supplied specialist manuscript, edition, work, and
  chronology finding aids. It is not an institutional catalog or critical
  edition; item-level claims were paired with the official BnF records and
  Gallica scans for Latin 13047 and Latin 1622, and with the BnF CCFr and
  IRHT/CNRS ARCA records for Troyes 523.
- Secord's Cambridge chapter supplied the inspected argument for a possible
  c. 170 birth and compact c. 196–212 literary career. It does not convert
  competing wider ranges into errors or supply a missing birth record.
- Publisher records and accessible portions of Binder, Storin and Muehlberger,
  and other current scholarship support only the claims actually inspected;
  unseen pages are not hidden support.
- The complete Vetus Latina database, all CCSL and Sources Chrétiennes
  apparatuses, all manuscript images, reciprocal opponent corpora, Roman and
  African epigraphy, and local cult or property archives remain research leads
  rather than unreported evidence.

## Rights boundary

Project-created prose and organization are intended for CC BY 4.0. Ancient
Latin and Greek wording is public domain. Nineteenth-century ANF and NPNF
English was used as a public-domain research aid and not as a controlling
critical translation. Modern critical establishment, apparatus, translations,
scholarship, Cambridge/Oxford/Routledge and other publisher content, Vatican
English, catalog metadata, and manuscript images retain their respective
rights. Protected analysis and translation are paraphrased; only brief ancient
wording is used at exact loci. No third-party image is reproduced, and online
access is not treated as a reuse license.
