# Tertullian — source audit

## Method and source hierarchy

This audit records what each source can establish, not merely where it was
mentioned. Tertullian's own works are primary evidence for his arguments,
literary self-presentation, addressed relationships, revision notices, and
developing discipline. They are not neutral minutes of events or a private
diary. Contemporary African martyr texts establish context but do not narrate
his life. Lactantius, Eusebius, Jerome, Augustine, and Vincent establish early
reception at increasing chronological distance. Medieval manuscripts and
paratext establish transmission first. Official Catholic texts establish only
their own selective reception. Named modern scholarship supplies controlled
reconstruction, never a replacement ancient witness.

Codes are those of `guidance/biographies.md`: A subject-authored; N
contemporary or near-contemporary external; E early received; L later
tradition; M official ecclesial reception; K named modern reconstruction; S
source-grounded synthesis.

## Consequential negative results

The search covered the reasonably accessible authentic corpus, contemporary
African martyr evidence, ancient literary reception, official Catholic acts,
critical and manuscript finding aids, institutional catalog records, and named
current professional scholarship appropriate to each claim. It located no:

- contemporary biography or civil birth record;
- secure exact birthplace, teacher, conversion scene, baptism date or sponsor;
- independent record for Jerome's father, Roman-envy, or presbyterate reports;
- Roman residence or professional employment record, or secure identification
  with a jurist in the *Digest*;
- ordination act, office list, named excommunication, papal act against
  Tertullian, or dated institutional transfer to a “Montanist church”;
- source naming the bishop in *Against Praxeas* 1 or the edict issuer in *On
  Modesty* 1;
- authorial or contemporary notice establishing Tertullian as editor of the
  *Passion of Perpetua and Felicity*;
- contemporary death, deathbed, cause, place, final-communion, burial, grave,
  relic, translation, miracle, feast, cult, patronage, or canonization record;
- secure institutional chain connecting Tertullian personally to Augustine's
  fifth-century Tertullianists or their basilica;
- securely contemporary portrait or authenticated physical likeness; or
- exact ancient source for the expanded “blood of the martyrs” maxim or
  *credo quia absurdum* as commonly quoted.

These are bounded negative results, not proof that no lost or unexamined local
record ever existed. The audit did not exhaust every manuscript, epigraphic and
archaeological catalog, municipal archive, local calendar, image, or oral
tradition.
