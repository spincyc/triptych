## Reader and governing question

A serious nonspecialist who meets the FSSP in news reporting, in a
parish, or in argument about *Traditionis custodes*, and wants to know
what the Church's acts actually constituted and what has kept it in
being.

Governing question: what, exactly, did the acts of 1988 constitute, and
what has kept that constitution in being through four subsequent changes
in the Roman law of the liturgy? Subordinate: under what authority did a
commission for reconciliation erect an institute; what the erection
decree granted and in what terms; what happened in 1999–2000; what the
2003 constitutions settled; what *Traditionis custodes* did to a body
erected by the Commission and what the decree of 11 February 2022 did
about it; and what the position is at the cutoff.

## Thesis

The acts of 1988 gave the Fraternity two legally distinct things and made
only one of them provisional. The rescript *Quia peculiare munus* (AAS 82
[1990] 533–534) gave the Commission the faculty to erect the Fraternity
with the marks of *Ecclesia Dei* 6 a, and separately, in its sixth number
alone, the exercise of the Holy See's authority over such bodies *donec
aliter provideatur*. Every Roman act since that has moved the Fraternity
has moved the supervisor and not the erection; the 2019 motu proprio
cites n. 6 by volume, fascicle, page and number. The 1999–2000 crisis
marks the outer limit of the constitution: the Holy See refused to let
the patrimony become a personal obligation binding each member against
the universal law, and enforced the refusal through canon 593 without
touching the erection, the object or the liturgical concession. The
position at the cutoff is two-tiered: a constitution no located act has
altered, and a faculty whose only public witness is the beneficiary.

## Evidence hierarchy and viewpoint rules

1. Acts printed in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* control the act they record.
2. Official Holy See web texts and press bulletins control acts not in
   the gazette.
3. The 1983 Code in the Holy See's Latin web presentation controls
   current-law statements; the English pages are a reading aid only.
4. Documents published by the Fraternity establish the Fraternity's own
   positions, self-description, internal events and self-reported
   numbers — never a contested fact against or in the absence of an
   official act.
5. Roman acts of 1999–2000 that the Holy See never published are used
   only from named unofficial renderings hosted by named interested
   parties, with the corroboration stated. The decree of 12 October 2000
   is corroborated by nothing else and no conclusion rests on it alone.
6. Peer-reviewed canonical scholarship is used for original-language
   texts it quotes, for printed loci not reached, and for its own
   analysis, always with its author's institutional position stated.
7. News reporting is used at one remove and only where no Holy See
   channel carries the text.
8. Negative results are recorded with their corpus and date and phrased
   as correctable.

## Translation and quotation rule

No text in any language is translated by this project. English
quotations come from the Holy See's own English versions where they
exist, or from the Fraternity's own English texts where the document is
the Fraternity's or is published by it. Latin, German, French and
Italian acts without an official English version are paraphrased with
short original-language phrases quoted.
