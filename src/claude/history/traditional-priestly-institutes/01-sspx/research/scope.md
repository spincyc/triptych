## Reader and governing question

A serious nonspecialist — Catholic or not — who meets the SSPX in news
reporting, canonical discussion, or parish conversation and wants to
know what actually happened and what the Church's acts actually say.

Governing questions: who erected the Society, under what law, with what
effect; what the acts of 1975–1976 decided and what the public record
leaves unresolved; what the excommunications of 1988 attached to and to
whom; what the remission of 2009 restored and did not restore; the exact
scope of the 2015–2017 confession and marriage faculties; and what the
Holy See decreed and the Society answered in July 2026.

## Thesis

The history is a history of acts, and each act is narrower than the claim
usually built on it. Two questions treated as settled by both parties are
not settled on the public record: the procedural regularity of the 1975
suppression, and the effect of the recourse lodged on 11 July 2026. The
account states what each act says, names who said it, and preserves what
authority has left open.

## Included

- Lefebvre's ecclesiastical career as recorded in *Acta Apostolicae
  Sedis* (1947 Dakar vicariate; 1948 apostolic delegation; 1962 Tulle;
  1962 Synnada);
- the erection of 1 November 1970 by Bishop François Charrière and
  Cardinal Wright's letter of 18 February 1971;
- the 1974 visitation and the Declaration of 21 November 1974;
- the Cardinals' Commission judgment of 6 May 1975, the suppression, and
  the four questions the record leaves open;
- 1976: the consistory allocution of 24 May, the ordinations, the
  suspension *a divinis*, the papal letters of 15 August and 11 October,
  and the audience of 11 September;
- 1977–1987: institutional growth, *Quattuor abhinc annos* (1984), the
  succession problem, and the Gagnon canonical visitation;
- 1988: the protocol of 5 May, the information note of 16 June, the
  canonical warning of 17 June, the consecrations of 30 June, the
  declaratory decree of 1 July, and *Ecclesia Dei* of 2 July;
- 1991–2007: internal succession, the 1996 note on formal adherence,
  the Jubilee contacts, and *Summorum Pontificum* as characterized by
  *Ecclesiae unitatem*;
- 2009: the request of 15 December 2008, the decree of 21 January, the
  Secretariat of State note of 4 February, Benedict XVI's letter of
  10 March, and *Ecclesiae unitatem* of 2 July;
- 2009–2012: the eight doctrinal meetings, the Doctrinal Preamble, the
  judgments of March and June 2012, and the personal-prelature draft;
- 2015–2017: the Jubilee confession faculty, *Misericordia et misera* 12,
  and the marriage letter of 27 March 2017;
- 2026: the announcement of 2 February, the February exchange, Cardinal
  Fernández's declaration of 13 May, Leo XIV's letter of 29 June, the
  consecrations of 1 July, the DDF decree and explanatory note of 2 July,
  and the recourse of 11 July;
- a terminal statement of the present situation as the acts state it.

## Excluded or bounded

- general histories of Vatican II, the liturgical reform, French
  traditionalism, sedevacantism, or the wider traditionalist world;
- the distinct histories of the FSSP, the ICKSP, and other institutes;
- biographies of superiors, bishops, or dissidents beyond institutional
  need; the 1983 United States separation and the 1986 Assisi
  declaration are mentioned as background only and carry no claim;
- any liturgical-law study of *Summorum Pontificum* or *Traditionis
  custodes*;
- any independent census or financial audit of the Society;
- any adjudication of the 1975 suppression, the state-of-necessity
  defence, the validity of any penalty, the effect of the 2026 recourse,
  or any individual's culpability;
- any pastoral advice about attending the Society's chapels or receiving
  sacraments there;
- claims about the contents of the Gagnon report, the 1988 protocol, or
  the 2011 Doctrinal Preamble, none of which is published.

## Evidence hierarchy and viewpoint rules

1. Acts printed in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* control the act they record.
2. Official Holy See web texts and press bulletins control acts not (or
   not yet) in the gazette.
3. The 1983 Code with Book VI as revised in 2021 controls current-law
   statements; the 1917 Code governs the acts of 1976 on their own terms.
4. Documents published by the Society establish the Society's own
   positions, self-description, internal events, and self-reported
   numbers — never a contested fact against or in the absence of an
   official act.
5. Negative results are recorded with their corpus and date and phrased
   as correctable.

"The Holy See declared" and "the Society stated" preserve actor and
authority throughout. No canonical question left open by competent
authority is decided.

## Translation and quotation rule

No text in any language is translated by this project. English
quotations come from the Holy See's own English versions where they
exist, or from the Society's own English texts where the document is the
Society's. Latin, Italian and French acts without an official English
version are paraphrased with short original-language phrases quoted.

## Material uncertainties preserved

Terms and canonical figure of the 1970 erection; text, author and
procedure of the 1975 suppression and the fate of any recourse;
membership and juridical form of the Cardinals' Commission; date and
signatory of the 1976 suspension; contents of the Gagnon report; texts of
the 1988 protocol, the 16 June 1988 information note, the decree
*Dominus Marcellus Lefebvre*, and the 2011 Doctrinal Preamble; whether
and where a Holy See statement of 12 February 2026 was published; the
effect of the 11 July 2026 recourse; and the present status of the 2016
and 2017 sacramental faculties.
