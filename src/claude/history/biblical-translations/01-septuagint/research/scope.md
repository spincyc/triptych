## Reader and governing question

A serious nonspecialist who meets "the Septuagint" in biblical, Catholic,
Orthodox, Jewish, or text-critical discussion. Governing questions: how the
Greek Torah came to be; how one translation became a corpus; what the
Greek–Hebrew differences witness; and how successive communities adopted,
revised, abandoned, defended, and reconstructed the texts.

## Thesis

"Septuagint" names several linked historical objects, not one thing: the
Ptolemaic Greek Torah; a book-by-book accumulation of Old Greek translations
and Greek compositions; the working Bible of Hellenistic Judaism and the
apostolic Church; a revised and recensed late-antique corpus; the great
codices; and modern editorial reconstructions. The origin legend itself has
a traceable growth from sober committee (Aristeas) to miracle (Philo,
Irenaeus, Epiphanius, the Talmudic baraita) and must be told as reception
history.

## Included

- Alexandria's setting and the Jewish community's language shift;
- the Letter of Aristeas at its own date: exact claims (Torah-only scope,
  collaborative procedure, communal reception), pseudonymity, disputed date,
  purpose;
- the legend's growth: Philo, Josephus, Justin, Irenaeus, ps.-Justin,
  Epiphanius, Augustine, b. Megillah 9a–9b, Soferim 1.7;
- expansion beyond the Torah anchored in the Sirach prologue;
- textual character/diversity and the Judean Desert evidence (4Q119–120,
  Fouad 266, Rylands 458, 4QJer-b/d, 8ḤevXIIgr, kaige);
- Hellenistic Jewish use; NT usage with bounded claims (Heb 10:5; Acts
  15:16–17; Matt 1:23);
- Jewish withdrawal; Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion and the Theodotion
  problem; rabbinic notices; Novella 146;
- Origen's Hexapla and its afterlife;
- codices, canon lists (Melito, Athanasius), Jerome–Augustine dispute,
  Trent;
- Orthodoxy's received text; modern editions (Rahlfs/Rahlfs-Hanhart,
  Göttingen, NETS, IOSCS); Dei Verbum 22; CCC 120–123.

## Excluded or bounded

- no project translation of any ancient text; no verse-level commentary;
- no complete per-book histories, daughter versions, lectionaries, or
  citation inventories;
- no theological judgment on inspiration or any communion's canon; Catholic
  canon reception reported as acts, not adjudicated;
- no NT-citation percentages; no manuscript images; no choice of one
  edition as "the original."

## Terminology controls

Septuagint/LXX (Torah when origins in view; received corpus loosely
elsewhere, flagged); Old Greek (editorial construct); Vorlage; Masoretic
Text (medieval tradition, not automatically any translator's source);
revision/recension (demonstrated deliberate alteration only); canon (named
community/authority only); deuterocanonical vs apocrypha usage noted at
Trent.

## Material uncertainties preserved

Exact date/personnel/procedure/patronage/motive of the Torah translation;
Aristeas's date and the meaning of §30's "careless" copies; dates/places of
post-Pentateuchal translations; kaige boundaries and "Theodotion"; Aquila's
and Symmachus's biographies; Hexapla configuration/purpose; absence of any
attested first-century canon list matching a printed Septuagint; no
Ptolemaic documentary record; no ancient decree of Jewish rejection.
