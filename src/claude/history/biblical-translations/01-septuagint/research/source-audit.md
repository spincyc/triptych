## Jewish tradition (T) — checked

- b. Megillah 9a–9b: baraita of Ptolemy, 72 elders, 72 rooms, God-given
  counsel, identical texts; list of changes (incl. "God created in the
  beginning"; the hare/Ptolemy's wife); mishnaic frame (R. Simeon
  b. Gamaliel: Greek only; R. Yohanan: halakha follows him). Checked via
  the Sefaria API text of the William Davidson (Steinsaltz) English,
  2026-07-24; CC BY-NC; paraphrased only.
- y. Megillah 1:9: Aquila the proselyte translated before R. Eliezer and
  R. Joshua; praised with Ps 45:3. Checked via Sefaria API (Guggenheimer
  translation); paraphrased only.
- Soferim 1.7: five elders wrote the Torah in Greek for Ptolemy; day as
  ominous as the golden calf, "since the Torah could not be accurately
  translated." Checked via Sefaria API (Soncino minor tractates, Cohen
  1965); paraphrased only; post-Talmudic compilation.
- Justinian, Novella 146 (553): synagogue reading permitted in Greek
  (Septuagint commended, Aquila allowed); deuterosis forbidden. Identity
  and provisions checked via the University of Wyoming Blume translation
  listing and scholarly summaries located by search 2026-07-24; used only
  for the bounded statement in §7 and the timeline.

## Official ecclesial acts (M) — checked

- Trent, Session IV (8 Apr 1546): canon list incl. Tobias, Judith, Wisdom,
  Ecclesiasticus, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees; "entire with all their parts";
  anathema; "old and vulgate edition" authentic for public use. Checked at
  papalencyclicals.net/councils/trent/fourth-session.htm.
- Dei Verbum 22: exact English sentence on the Septuagint quoted from
  vatican.va (hist_councils .../vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html).
- CCC 120–123: canon (46 OT books incl. deuterocanon), apostolic
  Tradition, permanent value of OT, rejection of Marcionism. Checked at
  vatican.va/content/catechism/en/... iv_the_canon_of_scripture.html.
  Negative check: the Catechism's article on Scripture (101–141) does not
  name the Septuagint; bounded to those paragraphs.

## Source families searched, and negative results

- No Ptolemaic documentary/epigraphic record of the translation was
  located in any witness consulted; stated as a bounded negative.
- No ancient decree of Jewish rejection of the Greek Bible was located;
  the withdrawal is presented as inference from versions, polemics, and
  rabbinic memory.
- CCC 101–141 checked for a named mention of the Septuagint: none found;
  bounded to those paragraphs.
- New Advent pages fathers/3007.htm (Against Helvidius, not the Vulgate
  prefaces) and fathers/3002.htm (prefaces to other works only) were
  fetched and found not to contain the Pentateuch preface; the Edgecomb
  translation was used instead. Negative results preserved here.
