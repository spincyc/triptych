## Rubrical dependencies

- Sunday use requires the Gloria and the Creed (*Tempus per annum* rubric 4; GIRM 53, 68).
- Because Week XXVI has no proper Preface, *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 and GIRM 365 govern: one of the eight Prefaces of the Sundays in Ordinary Time is used unless the selected Eucharistic Prayer has its own. GIRM 365(d) binds Eucharistic Prayer IV to its invariable Preface and admits it on Sundays in Ordinary Time; GIRM 365(b) lets Eucharistic Prayer II take its own Preface or another. The Preface and the Eucharistic Prayer are therefore one coupled choice, and this formulary selects neither.
- *Tempus per annum* rubric 6 describes the pair of Communion antiphons, the first from the psalms and the other mostly from the Gospel, lets either be chosen, and prefers one that happens to agree with the Gospel of the Mass. In this week the second antiphon is drawn from 1 John, not from a Gospel. Whether either agrees with a cycle Gospel is resolved, if at all, in each consuming leaf.
- The formulary is proper to the Twenty-sixth Sunday and is additionally *available* on an eligible Ordinary Time feria under *Tempus per annum* rubric 3(b) and GIRM 363. Availability is not appointment: a ferial use is a recorded selection, imports no Sunday reading, and does not carry the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.

## Findings internal to this edition

1. **The Entrance antiphon is a composite printed without *Cf.*** Its locator lists five verses out of order, 31, 29, 30, 43 and 42, all within the Prayer of Azariah that the Vulgate numbers as Daniel 3:24–90. Against the tracked Clementine Vulgate (`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`) the Missal condenses v. 31's two parallel object clauses into one and adds *Dómine*; renders the confession of vv. 29–30 in different words, with *peccávimus tibi* and *mandátis tuis non obœdívimus* where the Clementine has *peccavimus … recedentes a te* and *præcepta tua non audivimus*; takes from v. 43 only the plea for glory to God's name; and takes from v. 42 only the plea for mercy, without its first clause and without *juxta mansuetudinem tuam*. The antiphon is therefore an adaptation, not a quotation, whatever the absence of *Cf.* It is not established here which Latin text the vocabulary of vv. 29–30 follows. The verse numbers are also a hazard. Bibles that print Daniel from the Aramaic alone, such as the tracked King James Version (30 verses in chapter 3), lack these verses entirely, and their Daniel 3:29–30 are a different text, the king's decree. Any tool resolving this locator must use the Vulgate or Greek Daniel numbering, or its equivalent Prayer of Azariah numbering.
2. **Communion antiphon A is cited in Vulgate numbering and marked *Cf.*** The locator *Cf. Ps 118, 49-50* is Ps 119:49–50 in Hebrew numbering. Against the tracked Clementine the Missal reads *Meménto* for *Memor esto*, adds *Dómine*, and ends before the last clause of v. 50, on God's word giving life. The finding-aid index `src/sources/calendars/postconciliar/propers.yaml` declares Vulgate numbering for this entry and resolves it correctly, but it omits the *Cf.*, marks the antiphon as unread, and gives both antiphons the note that the Missal prints them after *Vel*, which is true only of the second. This record controls; the index belongs to its own owner and is unchanged here.
3. **Communion antiphon B agrees with the Clementine word for word.** Apart from punctuation, the Missal's text of 1 John 3:16 is the tracked Clementine's, including *Dei* after *caritatem*. It adds no *dicit Dominus* and needs no *Cf.* Whether the Nova Vulgata reads the same was not checked.
4. **The conclusions differ by element.** The Collect has the long conclusion *Per Dominum*; the Prayer over the Offerings the short *Per Christum*; the Prayer after Communion, whose last clause speaks of the Son, *Qui vivit et regnat in sǽcula sæculórum*. These are recorded as printed.
5. **The registered passage record for this formulary is mislocated.** `passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-tertia-2002.dominica-xxvi-per-annum` gives artifact pp. 302–303 and printed pp. 487–488. In the same hash-matched artifact pp. 302–303 carry the end of the Solemnity of Christ the King and the heading *Ordo Missæ*, and the artifact's own index puts the Most Holy Trinity at printed p. 485 and the Body and Blood of Christ at p. 489, so no Ordinary Time Sunday can stand at printed pp. 487–488. The Week XXVI heading is at physical p. 289. `tools/tpt source-library uses` shows the record bound only by another provider's 1962 leaf, as reception evidence for an oration. This audit relies on its own reading and not on that record, which belongs to its owner and is unchanged here.

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the ICEL antiphon excerpt in element order, antiphon assignment, alternative structure, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation-list silence is a bounded negative check: it shows no *listed* change to Week XXVI and is not evidence of page-level identity between the 2002 and 2008 printings.

**Leads not verified here.** The tracked `ot-26` rows of `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml` record the Collect as verbatim in the Hadrianum edited by H. A. Wilson (1915), with *Corpus orationum* no. 1952, and record no ancient ancestor for the Prayer over the Offerings within that inventory's declared corpus. Neither statement was checked against its witness in this audit. They are leads for the research stage, not evidence of either prayer's history.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and the short Latin phrases needed by the findings above. It reproduces no complete oration or antiphon in either language and no approved English wording at all. The rights records that govern any display of these texts are `guidance/liturgical-text-publication-policy.md`, `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml` and `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml`; their `ot-26` rows were read as leads and are not restated.

## Additions from the research stage of `PC-S52-A` (2026-09-24)

These are bounded results on the questions this record left open. Each states
how far it was checked. None changes an element, a locator, a boundary or a
finding above.

1. **Re-collation and passage records.** The 2002 artifact, the 2008
   *Notitiae* issue and the ICEL excerpt were fetched again whole over verified
   TLS, matched to the hashes stated above, and re-read at the loci this record
   names: the Week XXVI formulary at physical PDF pp. 289–290 (text layer), the
   *Tempus per annum* rubrics at pp. 267–268, the table of liturgical days and
   no. 60 of the *Normae universales* at pp. 70–71, 27 September in the
   *Calendarium Romanum generale* at p. 79, the variation list at journal
   p. 371, and the excerpt at printed p. 79. Nothing differs from the tables
   above. The formulary now has a correctly located passage record,
   `passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-tertia-2002.dominica-xxvi-per-annum-289-290`,
   and the excerpt page has
   `passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.2010-english-icel-antiphonary.ordinary-time-twenty-sixth-sunday`.
   The mislocated record of finding 5 belongs to its consumer and is
   unchanged; the new record names the difference in its notes.
2. **Collect: two early witnesses read.** The Collect stands word for word, as
   far as spelling and punctuation allow, in the Hadrianum edited by H. A.
   Wilson (1915), printed p. 172, under *Dominica .XI. post Pentecosten*, read
   in a page image of the tracked complete scan
   (`artifact.catholic-church.sacramentarium-gregorianum-hadrianum.wilson-1915.ia-pdf-96fd93c7`,
   PDF p. 230). It stands also in the Old Gelasian edited by Wilson (1894)
   as the first prayer of an *Item alia Missa* in the Sunday series, read only
   in the registered remote OCR
   (`…gelasianum-vetus.wilson-1894.ia-djvu-ocr-libersacramentorum`, fetched
   whole and hash-matched), with the one variant *honorum* for *bonorum*,
   which may be the OCR's. The translations inventory's lead is confirmed for
   the Hadrianum. No claim is made about the prayer's route into the 1970
   Missal. Thomas Aquinas quotes the prayer as something "said of God" in
   *Summa theologiae* I q. 25 a. 3 arg. 3 and answers it in ad 3 (Leonine
   vol. 4, pp. 293–294); the Leonine margin identifies the source as the
   Collect of the Tenth Sunday after Pentecost of the books the editors used.
   That is reception of the text, recorded in the consuming leaf's research;
   nothing of that other Mass enters this formulary.
3. **Prayer after Communion: first clause ancient, ending not found.** Its
   first clause, *Sit nobis, Domine, reparatio mentis et corporis caeleste
   mysterium*, stands in the Hadrianum at printed p. 171 (text layer of the
   same tracked scan) and in the Old Gelasian (OCR) with a different ending, *ut cuius exsequimur actionem
   sentiamus effectum*, and in the Verona sacramentary (Feltoe's edition,
   registered remote OCR, fetched and hash-matched) with the same older
   ending. The 2002 ending, on being co-heirs in glory with him whose death
   is proclaimed and whose sufferings are shared, was found in none of the
   three sacramentary texts searched. Its words *coheredes* and *compatimur*
   stand together in Romans 8:17 and *mortem … annuntiando* recalls
   1 Corinthians 11:26 in the Clementine; that is a textual observation, not
   an identified source, and neither verse is a locator the Missal prints.
4. **Prayer over the Offerings: bounded negative.** The phrases *fons omnis
   benedictionis* and *haec nostra tibi oblatio* were searched literally in
   the Hadrianum text layer and in the whole OCR of the Old Gelasian (two
   registered deliveries) and the Verona sacramentary (two registered
   deliveries), and not found. OCR damage, inflection and every book not
   searched are outside this result, which is not evidence that the prayer has
   no antecedent.
5. **Entrance antiphon: earlier use and wording.** Ildefonso Schuster, *The
   Sacramentary*, English translation, vol. 3 (1927), pp. 174–175, read in the
   tracked public-domain OCR
   (`artifact.ildefonso-schuster.the-sacramentary.burns-oates-washbourne-english-1927.ia-volume-3-ocr-4d8c8988`,
   physical lines 10902–10960), treats the antiphon as an Introit taken from
   Daniel 3 and says that "the actual words of the text are not quoted". That
   is a liturgical historian's statement about the element and agrees with
   finding 1. The OCR's list of Daniel verses differs from the Missal's in its
   last figure; the printed page was not collated. Jerome's lemma of Daniel
   3:29 in his commentary (Migne PL 25, 1845, col. 509, page image) agrees
   with the Clementine, not with the antiphon. Which Latin text the antiphon's
   vocabulary follows remains **not established**.
6. **Communion antiphon A: an older Latin form.** Hilary's lemma at the head of
   the letter Zain (*Tractatus in Ps.* 118, CSEL 22, p. 418, page image) reads
   *Memento uerbi tui seruo tuo, in quo mihi spem dedisti*, with Zingerle's
   apparatus reporting *memor esto* in two manuscripts; Augustine's lemma
   (*Enarr. in Ps.* 118, s. 15.1, Migne PL 37, col. 1540, page image) reads
   *Memento verbi tui servo tuo, in quo spem dedisti mihi*. The Missal's
   *Meménto* (finding 2) is therefore an older Latin psalter reading, not a
   Missal innovation, and its word order agrees with Hilary's lemma. Which
   psalter the compilers followed was not determined; the *Nova Vulgata* was
   not consulted for this verse.
