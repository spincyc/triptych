## Appointed elements

Only identifiers, printed locators, short Latin incipits, and original analytical summaries are retained. The complete Latin formulary and the approved English texts are not reproduced here.

The two Communion antiphons are a genuine either/or in the Missal, printed with *Vel:*. The formulary prints no Offertory antiphon, no proper Preface, no proper Eucharistic Prayer insert, no Sequence, no prayer over the people, and no solemn blessing.

## Rubrical dependencies

- Sunday use requires the Gloria and the Creed (*Tempus per annum* rubric 4; GIRM 53, 68).
- Because Week XXV has no proper Preface, *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 and GIRM 365 govern: one of the eight Prefaces of the Sundays in Ordinary Time is used unless the selected Eucharistic Prayer has its own. GIRM 365(d) binds Eucharistic Prayer IV to its invariable Preface; GIRM 365(b) lets Eucharistic Prayer II take its own Preface or another. The Preface and the Eucharistic Prayer are therefore one coupled choice, and this formulary selects neither.
- *Tempus per annum* rubric 6 describes the pair of Communion antiphons, the first from the psalms and the other mostly from the Gospel, lets either be chosen, and prefers one that happens to agree with the Gospel of the Mass. Whether either agrees depends on the cycle Gospel and is resolved, if at all, in each consuming leaf.
- The formulary is proper to the Twenty-fifth Sunday and is additionally *available* on an eligible Ordinary Time feria under *Tempus per annum* rubric 3(b) and GIRM 363. Availability is not appointment: a ferial use is a recorded selection, imports no Sunday reading, and does not carry the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.

## Findings internal to this edition

1. **The Entrance antiphon carries no scriptural locator.** The 2002 Latin prints *Ant. ad introitum* with no citation, and the ICEL excerpt prints none in its citation margin. The excerpt's compiler adds a bracketed remark that the antiphon "is based on" Ps 36[37]:39–40 and offers Ps 77[78]:1–7 (with a *Graduale Romanum* page pointer) or Ps 36[37]:27–40 as its psalm. That is the compiler's assignment, not the Missal's citation. The antiphon is therefore treated as a composed liturgical text with an identified scriptural basis still to be tested, not as a quotation of any verse.
2. **Communion antiphon A is cited in Vulgate numbering.** The printed locator *Ps 118, 4-5* is Ps 119:4–5 in Hebrew numbering; the Douay–Rheims (Challoner) at Ps 118:4–5 corresponds to the antiphon's Latin clause for clause. The finding-aid index `src/sources/calendars/postconciliar/propers.yaml` carries the same digits, `Psalm 118:4-5`, under a file that declares Hebrew numbering, so `tools/tpt mass-propers show --mass ot-25 --bible douay-rheims` resolves it to Vulgate Ps 117:4–5, a different passage. The index entry's own note marks that antiphon as unread. This record controls: the appointment is Vulgate Ps 118:4–5, Hebrew Ps 119:4–5. The index belongs to its own owner and is unchanged here. *(As of 2026-09-19. The index was corrected on 2026-09-25; see the dated correction below.)*
3. **Both non-oration texts that speak in Christ's or God's voice add *dicit Dóminus*.** The Entrance antiphon and Communion antiphon B each carry the phrase; in antiphon B it stands inside a text otherwise printed without *Cf.*

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the ICEL antiphon excerpt in element order, antiphon assignment, alternative structure, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation-list silence is a bounded negative check: it shows no *listed* change to Week XXV and is not evidence of page-level identity between the 2002 and 2008 printings.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and two short Latin phrases needed by the findings above. ## Additions from the research stage of `PC-S51-A` (2026-09-19)

These are bounded results on the questions this record left open. Each states
how far it was checked. None changes an element, a locator or a boundary above.

1. **Earlier Roman use of the Entrance antiphon.** Ildefonso Schuster, *The
   Sacramentary*, English translation, vol. 3 (1927), p. 171, read in the
   tracked public-domain OCR
   (`artifact.ildefonso-schuster.the-sacramentary.burns-oates-washbourne-english-1927.ia-volume-3-ocr-4d8c8988`,
   physical lines 10700–10760), treats *Salus populi* as the Introit of the
   Nineteenth Sunday after Pentecost and of the Thursday after the Third Sunday
   of Lent, both with the station at Saints Cosmas and Damian, and says the
   text "emanates perhaps from the Itala". That is a named liturgical
   historian's statement about the antiphon's earlier use and a conjecture
   about its textual source; it identifies no verse. The OCR was read; the
   printed page was not collated. It is recorded as the antiphon's history and
   imports nothing from any other Missal into this formulary.
2. **The stated psalm basis, tested.** Against the tracked Clementine Vulgate,
   Psalm 36:39–40 shares two words with the antiphon and no clause. Psalm 34:3,
   Psalm 33:18 and Psalm 90:15 each share a clause-level phrase. None is a
   quotation. Finding 1 stands: a composed text rather than a quotation or
   adaptation, with Ps 36[37]:39--40 retained as the Antiphonary compiler's
   identified scriptural basis.
3. **Prayer after Communion, one earlier occurrence.** The wording *Quos tuis,
   Domine, reficis sacramentis, continuis attolle benignus auxiliis* through *et
   mysteriis capiamus et moribus* occurs as an *Oratio post Communionem* in the
   *Missale Ambrosianum* (Milan, 1712), tracked public-domain OCR
   (`artifact.archdiocese-of-milan.missale-ambrosianum.milan-de-sirturis-1712.missale-ambrosianum-1712-text-a1319e20`,
   physical lines 66599–66603), within a section of votive orations whose
   exact title the damaged OCR does not yield. The Ambrosian text reads *tuae
   redemptionis effectum*. It was not compared word for word with the 2002
   Latin, which the research stage did not re-open. OCR only; no facsimile
   was collated; no claim is made about the prayer's origin or its route into
   the 1970 Missal.
4. **Collect and Prayer over the Offerings: bounded negative.** A literal,
   case-insensitive search of every tracked file under `src/sources` for
   *legis omnia constituta*, *sacrae legis* and *tuae plebis propitiatus*
   returned only the postconciliar finding-aid index. This is a raw-line
   search of held text. It cannot see inflected, abbreviated or OCR-damaged
   forms or any book the library does not hold, and it is not evidence that
   the prayers lack antecedents. No sacramentary edition and no published
   source list for the 1970 orations was consulted.

5. **Re-collation and registration (research re-entry, 2026-09-19).** The 2002
   artifact, the 2008 *Notitiae* issue and the ICEL excerpt were fetched again
   whole over verified TLS, matched to the hashes stated above, and re-read at
   the loci this record names: the Week XXV formulary at physical PDF pp.
   288–289 (text layer, with p. 288 also in a rendered page image), the
   *Tempus per annum* rubrics at pp. 267–268, the eight Sunday Preface headings
   at pp. 325–330, the variation list at journal pp. 371–372, and the excerpt
   at printed p. 79. Nothing differs from the tables above. The Week XXV locus
   now has a registered passage record,
   `passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-tertia-2002.dominica-xxv-per-annum`,
   and the excerpt page has
   `passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.2010-english-icel-antiphonary.ordinary-time-twenty-fifth-sunday`.
   GIRM nos. 48, 51, 53, 61–64, 68, 74, 87 and 363–365 were re-read in
   responses registered as new dated states, with passage records for the
   paragraphs that had none. All are bound in the consuming leaf's
   `research/source-bindings.toml`.
6. **Prayer after Communion compared with the Ambrosian occurrence.** With the
   2002 Latin re-opened, the two were compared. The Ambrosian OCR reads *ut tuae
   redemptionis effectum* and ends with the long conclusion; the 2002 text has
   no *tuae* and the short conclusion. The wording is otherwise the same as far
   as the damaged OCR shows. No facsimile was collated and no claim is made
   about the route between them.

## Correction of 2026-09-25

Finding 2 is kept as it was made on 2026-09-19; this note records what has
changed since. On 2026-09-25 the `ot-25` entry of
`src/sources/calendars/postconciliar/propers.yaml` was corrected. Communion

`tools/tpt mass-propers show --calendar postconciliar --mass ot-25 --bible douay-rheims`

had called the first antiphon the second, now reads that the Missal prints
two Communion antiphons, the second after *Vel*, and that *Tempus per annum*
rubric 6 lets one or the other be chosen. Finding 2's description of the index
is therefore history. The appointment it states, Vulgate Ps 118:4–5, Hebrew
Ps 119:4–5, is unchanged.
