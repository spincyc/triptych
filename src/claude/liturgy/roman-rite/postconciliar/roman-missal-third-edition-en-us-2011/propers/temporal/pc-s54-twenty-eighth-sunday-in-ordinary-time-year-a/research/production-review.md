### What the study contains

An opening on the Sunday's question, with the standing notice on the study
English where Scripture first appears, the *Ordo*'s three titles and a
twelve-row map of the appointed elements, both forms of the Gospel and both
Communion antiphons shown as alternatives; the appointed Scripture in the
Douay–Rheims (Challoner), with the words the Lectionary does not appoint set in
brackets (the Moab clause of Isaiah 25:10, the psalm title within Psalm 22:1,
the first half of Psalm 22:6 beside the response, the rest of Psalm 129:4 and
the first half of 1 John 3:2 beside the antiphons) and the omission of
Philippians 4:15–18 marked; the two Gospel forms printed separately; the
Entrance Antiphon, both Communion antiphons and the Alleluia verse by Latin
incipit and description with their departures from the Vulgate stated and their
source verses printed as sources, not as their words; the three orations by
incipit and description; an element-by-element section giving each text its
literary, textual and liturgical setting, including the Latin forms of Isaiah
25, the Psalm 22 response, the wish and promise of Philippians 4:19, the
recasting of the acclamation, the text and context of Matthew 22:1–14, and the
early witnesses of the Collect, the Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer
after Communion; three interpretations of the whole formulary, each carrying
all twelve element keys, naming its difficulties and closing with its own four
senses; a comparison; a terminal Scriptural Date and Location sheet on the
generated chronology projection, the reviewed home of the chronology the
concise page 2 will reprint; the Liturgical Resolution appendix with the twelve
branch IDs of the manifest; the Scope and Qualifications appendix; and
References. One `\label{proper-<element-key>}` stands at each of the twelve
appointed texts.

### Bounds observed

- Every claim resting on Matthew 22:11–14 is marked as holding only in the
  longer form; every four-senses clause resting on one Communion antiphon is
  marked with that antiphon.
- Gregory's formulation of the wedding is quoted as *Apertius ergo atque

- No English wording of any oration or antiphon stands in quotation marks; the
  dated USCCB page is described, not quoted; no claim rests on the wording of

  clause of the scope appendix.
- No date figure is printed outside the generated Date cells. The omitted
  memorial of the date is not named, since its rank rests on a witness that was
  not read (`research/context.md`).

## Author-study, iteration 1

Revised 8 October 2026 in the same run at iteration 1, after the study review
returned five blocking findings (STU-001 to STU-005) and five advisories
(STU-006 to STU-010). No research evidence record was changed.

### Blocking findings

- **STU-001, lane openings.** Each interpretation now opens by crediting every
  named witness with his own clause only. The first names Gregory (the Church
  joined to the Son, in the present Church), Hilary (the sacrament of glory at
  the resurrection), Jerome (Isaiah's feast for the nations after the Passion)
  and Aquinas alone for joining Isaiah's feast to the king's dinner, and states
  the one-gift reading across both readings as the editor's synthesis, which
  no witness holds as stated. The second states the claim its witnesses share
  (below). The third names Gregory, Hilary, Chrysostom, Aquinas and Jerome on
  the excuses, says that Augustine and Bellarmine comment only on the psalm
  verse of the first Communion antiphon, and gives the whole as the editor's.
- **STU-002, the garment.** The second interpretation's controlling claim is
  now what its witnesses share: a free call to all, and a garment required of
  those gathered, to be kept and answered for when the king comes in; those
  who say where it comes from (Chrysostom, Augustine, Gregory, Hilary) say it
  is given. Charity is attributed to Augustine and Gregory, with Aquinas
  counting it among the ways of putting on Christ. The interpretation leaves
  the identification open; the claim of neutrality that contradicted an
  adoption of charity, the exclusion rationale for Hilary and the phrase
  "carrying witnesses" are removed from the prose, and the opening map, the
  allegorical sense and the comparison follow. The manifest's
  `carrying_authors` for `wedding-garment` is unchanged, following
  `research/interpretations.md`; Hilary now shares the controlling claim in
  the prose, and the reviewer may judge whether the research record's
  rationale for leaving him out still stands.
- **STU-003, Matthew 22:14.** Augustine's reading is reported from *Sermo*
  90.4 in the bound NPNF text, read again at its locus at this iteration:
  the one cast out is many, and those many far surpass the good, who are many
  in themselves and few by comparison; the saying shows who at the present
  feast are to be brought to the other. Hilary's *raritas in electis* is
  reported as a statement about the chosen. The claims that no witness reads
  the saying numerically are withdrawn; Gregory and Aquinas are named as the
  two who do not weigh the numbers; the remaining caution against a count of
  the saved is the editor's, stated beside Augustine's and Hilary's
  comparison. `research/scope.md` (sections 3.1 and 7) and
  `research/interpretations.md` (Reading 2) summarise Augustine as counting
  the few only beside the bad and direct that the verse not be read as a
  statement about how many are saved; that summary omits *those many in
  numbers far surpassed the number of the good*, and is reported here for the
  reviewer to route.
- **STU-004, the shorter Gospel.** The first interpretation's join of the
  present and the final feast now holds in both forms through the resurrection
  (Hilary, Chrysostom, Jerome and Aquinas with Isaiah 25:8), with the
  judgment at the king's coming in (22:11) marked as a second link in the
  longer form only; under the shorter form the two feasts stand without the
  judgment between them. The second Communion antiphon answers the King of
  22:2 (Aquinas's *cum videbitur sicuti est, tunc erit Rex*) in both forms and
  the king's coming in only in the longer. The comparison table, its
  disputes paragraph and its closing paragraph now say what the first
  interpretation loses under the shorter form.
- **STU-005, other Masses.** The element section no longer names the other
  Masses of the present Missal that use the Prayer over the Offerings or the
  Prayer after Communion, nor speculates on the 1970 Missal's placing. The
  Gregorian and Verona sacramentary notes remain as history of the texts.

Revised 8 October 2026 in the same run at iteration 2, after the study review

### Blocking finding

- **STU-003, Matthew 22:14.** The sentences saying that two witnesses read the
  saying as a comparison of numbers and that Gregory and Aquinas do not weigh
  the numbers are withdrawn. The subsection *Many are called* now reports,
  beside Augustine (*Sermo* 90.4) and Hilary (22.7): Gregory's passage from the
  one cast out, *in quo videlicet omne malorum corpus exprimitur* (38.14);
  Gregory's *quod in Ecclesia et multi mali et pauci sunt boni*, with the ark
  narrowing to one cubit, Matthew 7:13–14 and the threshing floor's few grains
  and great heaps of chaff (38.8); Aquinas's reason, *quia quidam nolunt
  venire, quidam non habent vestem nuptialem*, with its continuation joining
  the saying to Matthew 7:14 (p. 282); and Irenaeus's comparison with the
  former covenant, "with many of them was He not well pleased" (IV.36.6;
  1 Corinthians 10:5). Each witness's unlimited call is kept beside his
  judgement on the few. Jerome's *non initia, sed finis* stands as his
  different reading, and Gregory's humility and hope in mercy (38.14, 16) as
  what he draws for his hearers. The lane's closing limit now states the
  difference between the one guest cast out and the saying, names how each
  witness reads the few, and says that the saying itself names no number; it
  no longer describes the study's restraint. The four senses add the few who
  find the strait way (Gregory, Aquinas) to the anagogical sense.
- `research/interpretations.md` (Reading 2, the closing caution: "Augustine
  counts the few against the bad, Gregory draws only humility from it") and
  `research/scope.md` carry the same omission of Gregory 38.8 and 38.14,
  Aquinas's Matthew 7:14 and Irenaeus IV.36.6 on v. 14. The study now departs
  from that summary on the evidence of the bound loci; the research records are
  reported here for the reviewer to route.
