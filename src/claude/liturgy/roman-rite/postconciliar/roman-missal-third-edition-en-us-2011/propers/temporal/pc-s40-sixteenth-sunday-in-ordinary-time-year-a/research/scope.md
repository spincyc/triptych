## Historical orientation

- **Psalms.** Psalms 54 and 86 carry Davidic headings; Psalm 54's heading names the Ziphite betrayal (1 Samuel 23:19; 26:1). Psalm 111 is an alphabetic acrostic of praise with no individual author named. Inherited attribution is preserved and is not converted into a dated autograph; final forms are uncertain and are stated as such.
- **Wisdom.** The book speaks in Solomon's persona, survives only in Greek, and is commonly placed among Alexandrian Jews in the last century BC. Author and exact date are unknown. Its argument at 12:3–11 looks back to the occupation of Canaan.
- **Matthew.** Ancient tradition names the apostle Matthew; the modern majority dates the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, often c. AD 80–90, with Antioch a common but unproved proposal. Matthew 11 and Matthew 13 are distinct narrated moments; Matthew 13 itself moves from the shore to a house.
- **Romans.** Paul writes from Corinth about AD 56–58 to mixed Jewish and Gentile house churches at Rome; the letter names Tertius as scribe (16:22).
- **Revelation.** Written on Patmos to seven churches of Roman Asia; a date late in Domitian's reign (c. AD 95) is traditional and widely used, and an earlier date is argued. Revelation 3:20 stands inside the message to Laodicea.

## Branch and pastoral limits

- The two Gospel forms are mutually exclusive; the guide treats both and narrates neither as enacted.
- The shorter form omits mustard seed, leaven, the fulfilment citation, the move indoors, the explanation, the angels, the furnace, and the shining of the just. Every claim depending on that material is marked longer-form only.
- One psalm response is printed for the day; no alternative refrain is appointed.
- The permitted Sunday sprinkling is a ritual substitution, not a proper text.
- Music, Preface, Eucharistic Prayer, and Communion antiphon selection are unknown; the Preface and Eucharistic Prayer branches are coupled by rubric.
- Ferial use of the Week XVI formulary is permitted (*Tempus per annum* rubric 3(b); General Instruction 363) but imports neither the Sunday readings nor the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface. The weekdays around this Sunday follow the independent ferial cycle II.
- The parable forbids servants from anticipating the Master's final judgment. It does not forbid safeguarding, reporting abuse, removing dangerous persons from access, proportionate ecclesiastical discipline, medical treatment, or resisting injustice; those are governed by other texts and by law, and the guide says so where the reception history is discussed.
- "Children of the evil one" is a role inside the Lord's own explanation and is not a licence to declare any living person beyond repentance or fit for mistreatment.

## Rights boundary

It carries citations, boundaries, short Latin incipits and argument-bearing Latin phrases, original summaries, and public-domain Douay–Rheims quotations named at each use. Any English rendering of a Latin phrase not taken from a named public-domain translation is a working gloss of the guide and is never offered as a translation for prayer or recitation.
