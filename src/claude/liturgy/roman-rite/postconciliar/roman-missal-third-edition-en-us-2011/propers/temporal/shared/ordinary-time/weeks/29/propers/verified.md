## Rubrical dependencies

- Sunday use requires the Gloria and the Creed (*Tempus per annum* rubric 4; GIRM 53, 68).
- Because Week XXIX has no proper Preface, *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 and GIRM 364–365 govern: one of the eight Prefaces of the Sundays in Ordinary Time is used unless the selected Eucharistic Prayer has its own. GIRM 365 d binds Eucharistic Prayer IV to its invariable Preface and admits it on Sundays in Ordinary Time; GIRM 365 b lets Eucharistic Prayer II take its own Preface or another. The Preface and the Eucharistic Prayer are therefore one coupled choice, and this formulary selects neither.
- *Tempus per annum* rubric 6 describes the pair of Communion antiphons, the first from the psalms and the other mostly from the Gospel, lets either be chosen, and prefers one that happens to agree with the Gospel of the Mass. In this week the second antiphon is drawn from a Gospel, Mark 10:45. Which, if either, agrees with a given cycle's Gospel is resolved, if at all, in each consuming leaf.
- The formulary is proper to the Twenty-ninth Sunday and is additionally *available* on an Ordinary Time feria under *Tempus per annum* rubric 3 b and GIRM 363. Availability is not appointment: a ferial use is a recorded selection, imports no Sunday reading, and does not carry the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.

## Findings internal to this edition

1. **The Entrance antiphon is an adaptation, marked *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine Vulgate (`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`) the antiphon gives Psalm 16:6 without the Clementine's *mihi* after *inclina aurem tuam*, passes over v. 7, and gives v. 8 without its opening phrase *A resistentibus dexterae tuae*, adding the vocative *Dómine* after *Custódi me*. In the tracked King James Version, translating the Hebrew, the phrase about those who rise up stands at the end of v. 7, so the antiphon's v. 8 begins where the Hebrew verse does; the verse numbers 6 and 8 otherwise coincide in both numberings. The same antiphon, with the same locator, is also the Entrance antiphon of Tuesday of the Third Week of Lent (physical p. 144), and Psalm 16:6 alone, as Cf. Ps 16, 6, is Communion antiphon A of Week IX (physical p. 275). These are recorded as editorial facts of this edition; what the repetition means is left to research.
2. **Communion antiphon A is cited in Vulgate numbering and marked *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine the Missal reads *timéntes* where the Clementine has *metuentes*; the rest of vv. 18–19 agrees. Vulgate 32:18–19 is Hebrew 33:18–19, and the tracked King James Version's verse numbers coincide. Any tool resolving this locator must still declare which numbering it uses.
3. **Communion antiphon B is an adaptation printed without *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine the Missal omits the opening *Nam et* and the whole contrast *non venit ut ministraretur ei, sed ut ministraret*, so that the Son of Man *venit, ut daret animam suam redemptionem pro multis*. The same words, printed there as *Cf. Mc 10, 45*, are a Communion antiphon of the Common of Pastors, for one founder of a church (physical pp. 569–570). The absence of *Cf.* in Week XXIX is recorded as printed.
4. **Conclusions and repeated wording.** The Collect has the long conclusion *Per Dóminum*; the Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion the short *Per Christum*. A literal search of the whole 2002 artifact's text layer found the Collect, the Prayer over the Offerings, the Prayer after Communion and Communion antiphon A only at p. 292. The Prayer over the Offerings' final clause, *ut, tua purificánte nos grátia, iísdem quibus famulámur mystériis emundémur*, also closes the Prayer over the Offerings of Week IX (physical p. 275), whose opening clauses differ. These are editorial facts of this edition; what they mean for either prayer's history is left to research.
5. **No passage record yet.** The source library has no passage record for *Dominica XXIX per annum* in the 2002 artifact, and none for printed p. 81 of the ICEL excerpt. Registering them belongs to the research stage.

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the ICEL antiphon excerpt in element order, antiphon assignment, alternative structure, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation-list silence is a bounded negative check: it shows no *listed* change to Week XXIX and is not evidence of page-level identity between the 2002 and 2008 printings.

**Leads not verified here.** The tracked `ot-29` rows of `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml` record the Collect as verbatim in the Old Gelasian sacramentary edited by H. A. Wilson (1894) and the Prayer over the Offerings as nearly verbatim (match coverage 0.9474) in the Verona sacramentary edited by C. L. Feltoe (1896), each from a remote OCR match. Neither statement was checked against its witness in this audit. They are leads for the research stage, not evidence of any prayer's history.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and the short Latin phrases needed by the findings above. It reproduces no complete oration in either language and no approved English wording at all. The Latin antiphons are compared with the public-domain Clementine only phrase by phrase. The rights records that govern any display of these texts are `guidance/liturgical-text-publication-policy.md`, `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml` and `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml`; their `ot-29` rows were read as leads and are not restated.

2. **Collect: early witness read.** The Collect stands word for word in the
   Old Gelasian sacramentary as edited by H. A. Wilson (Oxford, 1894), as the
   first prayer of Book I, LXI, *Quinta Dominica post clausum Paschae*
   (printed p. 105), read on a rendered page image of the Internet Archive PDF
   of that edition (item `LiberSacramentorumRomanaeEcclesiae`, 13,625,474
   bytes, SHA-256 `006a38a3…`, fetched whole on 2026-10-16, not registered and
   not retained); Wilson's margin gives the concordant witnesses and "III. ix
   infra". It stands again, word for word, as the second prayer of Book III,
   IX, *Item alia Missa* (printed p. 229, text layer of the same PDF), and in

   III, XVI (text layer only). The registered OCR of the same edition
   (`artifact.catholic-church.sacramentarium-gelasianum-vetus.wilson-1894.ia-djvu-ocr-libersacramentorum`,
   fetched whole and hash-matched) carries the first two at physical lines
   17460–17463 and 29276–29279. The translations inventory's lead of a
   verbatim Old Gelasian witness is confirmed. No claim is made about the
   prayer's route into the 1970 Missal or about why it stands in Week XXIX.
3. **Prayer over the Offerings: early witness read.** The prayer stands in the
   Verona sacramentary as edited by C. L. Feltoe (Cambridge, 1896), April
   section, Mass XXXVIII *Item alia* (printed p. 17), read on a rendered page
   image of the Internet Archive PDF of that edition (item
   `sacramentariumle00cath`, 19,868,448 bytes, SHA-256 `3b991b13…`, fetched
   whole on 2026-10-16, not registered and not retained): *Tribue nos Dñe
   quaesumus donis tuis libera mente servire ut purificante nos gratia tua
   iisdem quibus famulamur mysteriis emundemur per.* The Missal's clause order
   *tua purificante nos gratia* differs from Feltoe's *purificante nos gratia
   tua*; the rest agrees. The Mass is one of the April Masses of martyrs, and
   its preface speaks of the martyrs' blood shed in testimony to the truth.
   A different prayer of the same April section (printed p. 8, OCR only) opens
   *Tribue quaesumus Dñe donis tuis libera nos mente servire* and continues
   with the intercession of the saints; it is recorded as a lead only. The
   registered OCR of the same edition
   (`artifact.catholic-church.sacramentarium-veronense.feltoe-1896.ia-djvu-ocr-sacramentariumle00cath`,
   fetched whole and hash-matched) carries them at physical lines 1788–1790
   and 1283–1285. Finding 4's shared closing clause with Week IX is not
   explained by this witness, and nothing here asserts a reason.
4. **Prayer after Communion: early witness read, not a lead of the
   inventories.** The Verona sacramentary (Feltoe), September section,
   XXVIIII *In natale episcoporum*, Mass III *Item alia* (printed p. 126),
   read on a rendered page image of the same PDF, ends with *Gaudeat Dñe
   quaesumus populus tua semper benedictione confisus et caelestium rerum
   frequentatione proficiat ut et temporalibus beneficiis adiuvetur et
   erudiatur aeternis per.* From *caelestium rerum frequentatione* to
   *aeternis* the Missal's prayer agrees with it, recast in the first person
   plural (*proficere … adiuvemur … erudiamur*); its opening *Fac nos,
   quaesumus, Domine* is not in this prayer. The registered OCR carries it at
   physical lines 8145–8147. The Mass belongs to an anniversary of a bishop's
   ordination. No claim is made about the Missal's source for the opening, or
   about the route by which the clause reached the 1970 Missal.
5. **Antiphons against the *Nova Vulgata* (findings 1–3).** In the Holy See's
   web delivery of the *Nova Vulgata* Psalms (269,126 bytes, SHA-256
   `e508ed1c…`, fetched whole on 2026-10-16, not registered, not retained),
   Psalm 17 (16):6 reads *Ego ad te clamavi, quoniam exaudis me, Deus;
   inclina aurem tuam mihi*, and v. 7 ends *sperantes in dextera tua*, so that
   v. 8 opens *Custodi me ut pupillam oculi*; the Missal's antiphon follows the
   Clementine's *Ego clamavi, quoniam exaudisti me*, not the *Nova Vulgata*,
   and its vocative *Domine* stands in neither. Psalm 33 (32):18 reads *super
   metuentes eum, in eos, qui sperant*, so the Missal's *timentes* stands in
   neither Latin psalter. In the *Nova Vulgata* Mark (82,944 bytes, SHA-256
   `46e2598d…`, same terms) 10:45 reads as the Clementine does, *nam et Filius
   hominis non venit, ut ministraretur ei, sed ut ministraret et daret animam
   suam redemptionem pro multis*; the antiphon's omission of the clause of
   service is the Missal's.
6. **The Mark antiphon and the Year B acclamation.** The *Ordo lectionum
   Missae* (1981) at no. 146, read on the page image of printed p. 79, gives
   Mark 10:45 as the Year B Alleluia verse in the form *Filius hominis venit
   ut ministraret, et daret animam suam redemptionem pro multis*, keeping the
   clause of service that Communion antiphon B omits. This is recorded as an
   edition fact; it says nothing about why the antiphon was chosen.
