### What the study contains

An opening on the parable's question, with the standing notice on the study
English where Scripture first appears, the *Ordo*'s three titles, and a
twelve-row map of the appointed elements (both forms of the second reading and
both Communion antiphons as alternatives); the appointed Scripture in the
Douay–Rheims (Challoner), with the words the Lectionary does not appoint set in
brackets (Psalm 24:4a, the rest of v. 6 in the response) and the last clause of
Psalm 24:5 footnoted as possibly outside the United States strophe; the two
forms of the second reading printed separately; the Entrance Antiphon and
Communion antiphon A by Latin incipit and description with the Missal's
departures stated and their source verses printed as sources, not as the
antiphons' words; the three orations by incipit and description; an
element-by-element section giving each text its literary, textual and
liturgical setting, including the textual state of Matthew 21:29–31 and the
early witnesses of the Collect and the Prayer after Communion; three
interpretations of the whole formulary, each carrying all twelve element keys,
naming its strongest difficulty and closing with its own four senses; a
comparison; a terminal Scriptural Date and Location sheet on the generated
chronology projection, the reviewed home of the chronology the concise page 2
will reprint; the Liturgical Resolution appendix, on a fresh page, with the
twelve branch IDs of the manifest; the Scope and Qualifications appendix; and
References. One `\label{proper-<element-key>}` stands at each of the twelve
appointed texts; a trial conversion with `tools/web-edition` into the lane's
scratch space produced all twelve `proper-<key>` anchors.

The interpretations are those of `research/interpretations.md`, under its lane
keys, each in its own component:

The manifest's `authors` for each lane name every author the section develops,
with a comment giving the supporting and contrary roles; `carrying_authors` are
those the research record names, and the checker resolves them to Fathers and
Doctors in `src/sources/inventories/author-standing-v1.toml`.

Differences carried where the research records them: Hilary's reversal of the
sons, with its textual basis (the answer "the younger") beside Jerome's
*Novissimus* and "true copies"; Augustine and Aquinas on John 10:26, each in his
own terms; Jerome and Aquinas on the justices of one who falls away, as a
development and not a disagreement about Ezekiel; Jerome and Schuster on the
speaker of the Entrance Antiphon; and the complementary relation of the first
two interpretations. No witness is credited with a reading of this Mass. Every
cross-element join that no witness makes is marked in the prose as the
editor's, and the one join a witness makes (Augustine citing Ezekiel 18:22 on
Psalm 118:49) is attributed to him. Schuster and the Leonine margin's Sundays
are not narrated; one clause of the scope appendix says that Schuster's Masses
differ in their other elements.

**Research-review advisories.** RES-008: the study reports Augustine on John
10:26 as "predestined to everlasting destruction" and Aquinas as "not
predestined to believe … foreknown to eternal ruin", never as "the same gloss",
and gives the "obeying" gloss of hearing and the "imitation" gloss of following
to Aquinas alone. RES-009: the study attributes to Augustine only his claim that
the froward call God's way not right because he makes whole those who confess
their sins and humbles those who seek their own righteousness; the likeness to
Christ's path is marked as the editor's, and "the way of humility" is not
attributed to him. Both advisories remain open in the research records, which
this stage did not change.

### Source limits

Those of `research/scope.md`, restated to the reader in the scope appendix. The
points that shaped the prose: the psalm, the Entrance Antiphon's verses and
Communion antiphon B each rest on one ancient witness; the first reading's and
Communion antiphon B's later witness is doctrinal use; the Entrance Antiphon's

manuscript of Matthew was examined; no commentary on the Prayer over the
Offerings or the Prayer after Communion was found; and the named corpora of
scope section 4 were not opened, so no priority, dependence or common view is
asserted.
