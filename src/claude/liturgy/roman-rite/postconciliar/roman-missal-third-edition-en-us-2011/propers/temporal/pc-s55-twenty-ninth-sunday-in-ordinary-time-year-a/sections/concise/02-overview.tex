\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Flattered with true words,
Jesus calls the questioners hypocrites, makes them name the census coin's
image, and bids them repay Caesar his own and God his. The Lord grasps
the hand of Cyrus, who does not know him, that all may know there is no other
God. God's due is tithes and offerings (Jerome) or body, soul and will
(Hilary); tribute is rendered as a debt, not given (Chrysostom).\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ is the image
of God and bears no image of Caesar (Ambrose), come to restamp the coin worn
smooth (Augustine); the King who began to reign by faith among the gentiles
(Bellarmine); the Truth, truthful by union with God (Aquinas), who reads
hearts as only God does (Hilary, Chrysostom). Where the second Communion
antiphon is sung, the Son of Man gives his life as a ransom.\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Render yourself as a vow
(Augustine), the will devoted and the heart undivided (Collect). Pay civil
dues (Aquinas), but refuse what harms godliness (Chrysostom), as soldiers
under Julian refused incense (Augustine). Be single and believe no flatterer
(Aquinas).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The image restored is
seen when ``we shall see Him as He is'' (Augustine). The Lord comes to judge
the peoples with justice, without regard of persons (Bellarmine); the rod of
the wicked is ``for a season'' (Augustine); nothing hidden within men is
hidden from God (Hilary).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
