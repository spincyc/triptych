\section{The Appointed Texts}
\label{sec:appointed-texts}

The Missal's texts for this Sunday are those of \latin{Dominica XXV ``per
annum''} in the \work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica tertia (2002), which the
\work{Roman Missal}, Third Edition, renders for the dioceses of the United
States; the readings, psalm and Alleluia verse are those of no.~133 of the
\work{Lectionary for Mass}, which follows the \work{Ordo lectionum Missae} of
1981. The Latin of the Missal, the approved English of the Missal and the
approved English of the Lectionary are all under copyright, and none of them
is reproduced here. Each biblical element is given at its canonical verses in
the Douay--Rheims version as revised by Bishop Challoner, which translates the
Clementine Vulgate and numbers the psalms as the Vulgate does. That English is
a study text and is not the wording proclaimed at Mass. Words printed in
brackets and italics belong to the verse but not to the appointed text. Each
oration, and the Entrance Antiphon, is identified by the opening words of its
Latin and described, and no English is supplied for them.

\subsection{Entrance Antiphon}
\label{proper-entrance-antiphon}

\properlocus{\latin{Salus populi ego sum, dicit Dominus}. The Missal prints no
scriptural reference.}

\noindent The antiphon is an oracle in the first person. The Lord names
himself the salvation of the people, promises to hear them from whatever
tribulation they cry out of, and declares himself their Lord for ever. It is a
composed liturgical text and not a verse of Scripture, so that no biblical
English stands for it, and none is printed.

\subsection{Collect}
\label{proper-collect}

\properlocus{\latin{Deus, qui sacrae legis omnia constituta}. Long conclusion.}

\noindent The address says that God has made everything the sacred law lays
down rest on the love of himself and of neighbour. The petition asks that
those who keep his precepts may come to eternal life. No English is printed.

\subsection{First Reading}
\label{proper-first-reading}

\properlocus{Isaiah 55:6--9. Lectionary no.~133.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Isa 55:6--9}
\textsuperscript{6}Seek ye the Lord, while he may be found: call upon him,
while he is near. \textsuperscript{7}Let the wicked forsake his way, and the
unjust man his thoughts, and let him return to the Lord, and he will have
mercy on him, and to our God: for he is bountiful to forgive.
\textsuperscript{8}For my thoughts are not your thoughts: nor your ways my
ways, saith the Lord. \textsuperscript{9}For as the heavens are exalted above
the earth, so are my ways exalted above your ways, and my thoughts above your
thoughts.
\end{englishwitness}

\subsection{Responsorial Psalm}
\label{proper-responsorial-psalm}

\properlocus{Psalm 145 (144):2--3, 8--9, 17--18; the response is taken from
v.~18a. Lectionary no.~133.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 144:18, first half, as the response}
The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him\notread{: to all that call
upon him in truth}.
\end{englishwitness}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 144:2--3, 8--9, 17--18}
\textsuperscript{2}Every day will I bless thee: and I will praise thy name for
ever; yea, for ever and ever. \textsuperscript{3}Great is the Lord, and
greatly to be praised: and of his greatness there is no end.

\textsuperscript{8}The Lord is gracious and merciful: patient and plenteous in
mercy. \textsuperscript{9}The Lord is sweet to all: and his tender mercies are
over all his works.

\textsuperscript{17}The Lord is just in all his ways: and holy in all his
works. \textsuperscript{18}The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him:
to all that call upon him in truth.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The psalm is sung in three strophes of two verses each. The verse
numbers are the same in the Hebrew and the Vulgate numbering of this psalm.

\subsection{Second Reading}
\label{proper-second-reading}

\properlocus{Philippians 1:20c--24, 27a. Lectionary no.~133.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Phil 1:20--24, 27}
\textsuperscript{20}\notread{According to my expectation and hope; that in
nothing I shall be confounded: but with all confidence, as always, so now
also,} shall Christ be magnified in my body, whether it be by life or by
death. \textsuperscript{21}For to me, to live is Christ: and to die is gain.
\textsuperscript{22}And if to live in the flesh: this is to me the fruit of
labour. And what I shall choose I know not. \textsuperscript{23}But I am
straitened between two: having a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ,
a thing by far the better. \textsuperscript{24}But to abide still in the flesh
is needful for you. \textsuperscript{27}Only let your conversation be worthy
of the gospel of Christ\notread{: that, whether I come and see you, or, being
absent, may hear of you, that you stand fast in one spirit, with one mind
labouring together for the faith of the gospel}.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The reading enters verse~20 at the clause in which Christ is
magnified in Paul's body, passes over verses 25 and~26, and takes from
verse~27 only its opening command. ``Conversation'' in the Douay--Rheims means
manner of life.

\subsection{Alleluia Verse}
\label{proper-gospel-acclamation}

\properlocus{\latin{Aperi, Domine, cor nostrum}; cf.\ Acts 16:14b. Lectionary
no.~133.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Acts 16:14, the verse on which the acclamation is formed}
\notread{And a certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple, of the city of
Thyatira, one that worshipped God, did hear:} whose heart the Lord opened to
attend to those things which were said by Paul.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Lectionary's ``cf.''\ marks an adaptation. Luke's sentence is
narrative in the third person: the Lord opened Lydia's heart to heed what Paul
said. The verse sung before the Gospel turns it into a petition in the first
person plural, asking the Lord to open our heart to heed the words of his Son.
The words of Acts are printed as the basis of the verse and are not the verse
itself, for which no English is printed.

\subsection{Gospel}
\label{proper-gospel}

\properlocus{Matthew 20:1--16a. Lectionary no.~133. No shorter form is given.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Mt 20:1--16}
\textsuperscript{1}The kingdom of heaven is like to an householder, who went
out early in the morning to hire labourers into his vineyard.
\textsuperscript{2}And having agreed with the labourers for a penny a day, he
sent them into his vineyard. \textsuperscript{3}And going out about the third
hour, he saw others standing in the marketplace idle. \textsuperscript{4}And
he said to them: Go you also into my vineyard, and I will give you what shall
be just. \textsuperscript{5}And they went their way. And again he went out
about the sixth and the ninth hour, and did in like manner.
\textsuperscript{6}But about the eleventh hour he went out and found others
standing, and he saith to them: Why stand you here all the day idle?
\textsuperscript{7}They say to him: Because no man hath hired us. He saith to
them: Go ye also into my vineyard.

\textsuperscript{8}And when evening was come, the lord of the vineyard saith
to his steward: Call the labourers and pay them their hire, beginning from the
last even to the first. \textsuperscript{9}When therefore they were come that
came about the eleventh hour, they received every man a penny.
\textsuperscript{10}But when the first also came, they thought that they
should receive more: And they also received every man a penny.
\textsuperscript{11}And receiving it they murmured against the master of the
house, \textsuperscript{12}Saying: These last have worked but one hour, and
thou hast made them equal to us, that have borne the burden of the day and the
heats.

\textsuperscript{13}But he answering said to one of them: friend, I do thee no
wrong: didst thou not agree with me for a penny? \textsuperscript{14}Take what
is thine, and go thy way: I will also give to this last even as to thee.
\textsuperscript{15}Or, is it not lawful for me to do what I will? Is thy eye
evil, because I am good? \textsuperscript{16}So shall the last be first and
the first last. \notread{For many are called but few chosen.}
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The appointed Gospel ends with the first sentence of verse~16. The
second sentence stands in the Clementine Vulgate, which the Douay--Rheims
translates, and is not read at this Mass. In the \work{Ordo lectionum Missae}
the passage opens with a liturgical incipit that addresses the parable to the
disciples.

\subsection{Prayer over the Offerings}
\label{proper-prayer-over-offerings}

\properlocus{\latin{Munera, quaesumus, Domine, tuae plebis}. Short conclusion.}

\noindent God is asked to accept his people's gifts with favour, so that what
they profess by devout faith they may lay hold of through the heavenly
sacraments. No English is printed.

\subsection{Communion Antiphon, First Option}
\label{proper-communion-antiphon-a}

\properlocus{\latin{Tu mandasti mandata tua custodiri nimis}. Psalm 119
(118):4--5; the Missal cites it in the Vulgate numbering, as Ps 118, 4--5.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 118:4--5}
\textsuperscript{4}Thou hast commanded thy commandments to be kept most
diligently. \textsuperscript{5}O! that my ways may be directed to keep thy
justifications.
\end{englishwitness}

\subsection{Communion Antiphon, Second Option}
\label{proper-communion-antiphon-b}

\properlocus{\latin{Ego sum pastor bonus, dicit Dominus}. John 10:14. Printed
in the Missal after \latin{Vel}.}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Jn 10:14}
I am the good shepherd: and I know mine, and mine know me.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal prints this antiphon without ``cf.'', yet it is not the
bare verse. It inserts \latin{dicit Dominus} after the opening clause, as the
Entrance Antiphon also does, and it supplies the noun \latin{oves}, which the
Clementine Vulgate leaves unexpressed at this verse. The two Communion
antiphons are alternatives, and one of them is sung or said.

\subsection{Prayer after Communion}
\label{proper-prayer-after-communion}

\properlocus{\latin{Quos tuis, Domine, reficis sacramentis}. Short conclusion.}

\noindent Those whom God restores with his sacraments are commended to his
continual help, so that they may possess the effect of redemption,
\latin{redemptionis effectum}, both in the mysteries and in their manner of
life, \latin{et mysteriis \ldots\ et moribus}. No English is printed.
