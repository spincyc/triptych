\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & A people whose sanctuary is
trodden down confesses its sin and asks God to come down as he came down to
free Israel from Egypt (Aquinas). Jesus tells four disciples, and all through
them, to stay awake for an unknown hour. Paul rests a faulty church's hope on
God's fidelity.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Having prayed ``come
down'', the people say in the Latin ``thou didst come down'': the Word made
flesh (Jerome). The son of man at God's right hand is Christ (Augustine,
Bellarmine), and the salvation asked in the Alleluia verse is Christ
(Augustine). The man gone abroad is Christ ascended and the doorkeeper the
order of pastors (Bede).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & The hour is hidden so that the
disciples may live each day as if to be judged the next (Jerome). Each guards the door of
his heart (Bede) and keeps by works what he believes (Gregory). ``Except Thou
convert us, we shall not be converted'' (Augustine): the Collect asks God for
the will itself.\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & Christ comes twice,
unseen like rain on a fleece and openly in glory (Cyril); given as man, he will
be given as God (Augustine). The world's last day takes each as his own last
day finds him (Augustine). The fellowship of the Son, had now by grace, becomes
a sharing in glory (Aquinas).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
