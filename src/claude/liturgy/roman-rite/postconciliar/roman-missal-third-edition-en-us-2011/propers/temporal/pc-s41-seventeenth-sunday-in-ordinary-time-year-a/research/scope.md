## Question and material exclusions

The question is what the Roman Rite actually appoints for this celebration in this edition-locale on this date, what each appointed text says in its own context, how the tradition received it, and what may and may not be claimed about the relations among the parts. Excluded by design: the Year B and Year C targets under this parent; any weekday; any Ritual, Votive or Various Needs Mass; music; and every local calendar below the national level.

## Texts and contexts studied

- 1 Kings 3:1–15 read whole, with the appointed 3:5, 7–12 boundary and the non-proclamation of v. 6 kept explicit, and with vv. 13–14 and 16–28 read as unproclaimed context.
- Psalm 119 read as a complete alphabetic poem, with each appointed verse located by stanza and position; the response verse 97a located in a stanza none of whose other verses is sung.
- Romans 8:18–39 read whole; 8:28–30 retained as the appointed semi-continuous unit.
- Matthew 13:1–58 read whole, with the boat-to-house shift at 13:36 and both authorized Gospel boundaries kept distinct; Matthew 5:1–12 and 11:25–30 read for the antiphon and acclamation sources.
- Psalms 67 and 102 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering) read for the Entrance and first Communion antiphons, in Latin and English.
- The complete Latin Week XVII formulary read in the exact 2002 artifact, together with the 2002 Order of Mass preparation blessings, the First Sunday of Advent Post-Communion, the Lenten Prayer over the People sharing this Collect's address, and the Ritual Mass for the Dedication of a Church.

## Historical orientation retained

- **1 Kings.** The book names no author. Modern accounts commonly place Kings inside a longer Deuteronomistic history reaching its present shape in or after the exile; no single reconstruction is made a premise. The narrated event is Solomon's dream at Gibeon, conventionally about 970–960 BC.
- **Psalms.** Psalm 119 is anonymous, alphabetic and Torah-centred; Psalms 67 and 102 carry inherited Davidic headings that orient reception without settling compositional history. All three keep their Israelite horizon.
- **Romans.** Pauline authorship is undisputed; written from Corinth or Cenchreae about AD 56–58 to congregations Paul had not met.
- **Matthew.** Ancient tradition, reported by Papias through Eusebius, names Matthew the apostle; the modern majority places the canonical Greek Gospel after AD 70, often c. AD 80–90, with Antioch proposed and unproved. Matthew 5, 11 and 13 are distinct narrated moments.

## Search method and corpus boundary

All reception was reached by opening complete works or complete sections at exact loci. Quotation aggregators were not used as evidence. The corpora actually searched on 25 July 2026 were:

1. standard English translations of Chrysostom, Origen, Ambrose, Augustine and Cassian in the Ante-Nicene and Nicene and Post-Nicene collections;
2. public Latin transcriptions of the Migne text of Jerome and Hilary on Matthew, Gregory the Great's Gospel homilies, Augustine's *Enarratio in Psalmum CXVIII* (PL 37) and Ambrose's *Expositio Psalmi CXVIII* (PL 15);
3. the Latin *Super Matthaeum*, *Super Romanos* and the complete four parts of the *Summa theologiae*, with the English *Summa* for wording;
4. the Holy See's texts of *Sacrosanctum concilium*, *Gaudium et spes* and the *Catechism*, and the published texts of Orange II and Trent Session VI;
5. the complete extracted Latin text of the 2002 *Missale Romanum* artifact, searched for every intratextual claim made in the guide; and
6. public-domain literary texts at Project Gutenberg, Wikisource and the Internet Archive for the afterlife gallery.

Every "not located" statement in the guide is bounded by this list and this date. Repository-wide web search quota was exhausted before this pass began, so all sources were reached by direct URL construction; some material discoverable only by search may therefore have been missed.

## Branch, occurrence and pastoral limits

- The two Gospel forms are mutually exclusive; so are the two Communion antiphons. Neither pair is resolved.
- The Sunday sprinkling rite may replace the Penitential Act; it brings its own texts and is not a proper of this formulary.
- Preface and Eucharistic Prayer are a coupled unresolved choice under GIRM 365(b) and 365(d).
- An approved chant may replace either Missal antiphon under GIRM 48 and 87.
- The Memorial of Saints Joachim and Anne is omitted on 26 July 2026 without commemoration or transfer; it creates no branch and contributes no text.
- Week XVII ferias in 2026 take the independent Cycle II course. The Thursday Gospel that week is Matthew 13:47–53; this is a coincidence of two arrangements, not a completion of a shortened Sunday reading.
- Romans 8:28 must never be used to pressure a harmed person into silence, to discourage lament, or to displace safeguarding, medical care, reporting, restitution, and civil or ecclesiastical justice.
- The treasure and the pearl do not teach that grace can be bought, that property is intrinsically evil, or that anyone may abandon dependants or just obligations. The net's separation belongs to divine judgment alone.

**Rejected leads and negative results.** The police sense of *dragnet* has no documented dependence on Matthew 13:47: the word is attested in the fishing sense from the 1540s and in the police sense by 1894, and it predates the translations that use it in that verse. *Candide*'s "best of all possible worlds" targets Leibniz, named explicitly in the text, not Romans 8:28. The idiom of "Solomonic judgment" depends on 1 Kings 3:16–28, which is not appointed. A search of some twenty-seven public-domain novels found no use of "pearl of great price" outside Hawthorne, and no Bunyan locus exists. The Latter-day Saint collection *The Pearl of Great Price* was considered and set aside as closer to a bare title than to a semantic turn, although its official manual does state the derivation. Bare titles, mottoes, hymn settings and straight devotional repetition were excluded throughout.

## Rights, review and source-role limits

The United States Lectionary text, the Psalm response, and the approved English orations and antiphons are protected and are reproduced nowhere in the guide or in these records. The Latin *editio typica* is likewise protected; the guide retains short incipits and five short Latin phrases on which stated philological or intratextual arguments turn, and reproduces no complete oration in any language. Scriptural wording quoted in the guide is public-domain Douay-Rheims or Clementine Vulgate, named as such at the point of use and expressly distinguished from the text proclaimed. Third-party pages, transcriptions and translations retain their own rights; citation places nothing under this project's licence.

The following remain **outstanding ceilings** and are stated in the guide's terminal scope appendix: liturgical-text permission for any protected text; collation of a named United States altar-book printing and of 2008 Latin reprint page images; work-specific rights review; independent specialist, theological, canonical or ecclesiastical review; and collation of any chant book. Internal checking is not independent review, and production quality is not approval. Nothing here carries an imprimatur or a *nihil obstat*.
