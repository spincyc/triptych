The formulary prints one Communion antiphon and no alternative, no Offertory antiphon, no proper Eucharistic Prayer insert, no Sequence and no prayer over the people. It carries three rubrics: *Non dicitur Glória in excélsis* after the Entrance antiphon, *Dicitur Credo* after the Collect, and, after the Prayer after Communion, *Adhiberi potest formula benedictionis sollemnis, p. 606*.

## Rubrical dependencies

- The Gloria is omitted and the Creed is said, by the formulary's own rubrics. The United States General Instruction agrees: no. 53 has the Gloria on Sundays outside Advent and Lent, and no. 68 the Creed on Sundays (read in the states listed in the consuming leaf's context record).
- The Preface is appointed. The formulary's rubric names *Præfatio I de Adventu*, and that Preface's own rubric (no. 33, physical p. 311) assigns it to Masses of the season from the First Sunday of Advent to 16 December and to other Masses of that time without a proper Preface. *Præfatio II de Adventu* (no. 34) belongs to the days from 17 December. The Eucharistic Prayer remains a choice, coupled to this Preface: the United States General Instruction no. 365 c lets Prayer III take any Preface, no. 365 b lets Prayer II take another Preface than its own, and no. 365 d admits Prayer IV, with its invariable Preface, only when a Mass has no Preface of its own and on Sundays in Ordinary Time. How the consuming leaf records that choice is its own matter.
- The solemn blessing is optional. The formulary says it *may* be used, and the section's own opening rubric (physical p. 370) makes the solemn blessings usable at the priest's discretion at the end of Mass.
- Under the Universal Norms printed in the artifact, the Sundays of Advent stand at place 2 of the table of liturgical days (no. 59) and take precedence over every feast of the Lord and every solemnity; a solemnity falling on one is transferred to the following Monday (no. 5). Advent begins with Evening Prayer I of the Sunday falling on or closest to 30 November (no. 40), and its Sundays are named the First to Fourth Sundays of Advent (no. 41).

## Findings internal to this edition

1. **The Entrance antiphon is an adaptation, marked *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine Vulgate (`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`), Psalm 24:1–3, the antiphon lacks the vocative *Domine* after *Ad te*, and in v. 3 reads *qui te exspectant* where the Clementine has *qui sustinent te*; the rest agrees, and the psalm's title, which stands at the head of v. 1 in the Clementine, is not part of it. Vulgate 24:1–3 is Hebrew 25:1–3. These are recorded as editorial facts of this edition; what they mean is left to research.
2. **The Communion antiphon is printed without *Cf.* but trims its verse.** Against the tracked Clementine, Psalm 84:13, the antiphon omits the opening *Etenim*; the rest agrees. Vulgate 84:13 is Hebrew 85:13. The same antiphon, with the same locator, is the Communion antiphon of a Mass for Various Needs, no. 27 *In agris conserendis* (physical p. 703). These are editorial facts; what the repetition means is left to research.
3. **Conclusions and repeated wording.** The Collect has the long conclusion *Per Dóminum*; the Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion the short *Per Christum*. A literal search of the whole 2002 artifact's text layer finds the Collect and the Entrance antiphon only on p. 82. The Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion of this Sunday stand again, whole, in the formulary of Monday of the First Week of Advent on p. 83; the opening of the Prayer over the Offerings is found again on pp. 88 and 93, and its closing clauses and the Prayer after Communion on pp. 85, 88, 90, 93 and 95 (p. 96 for one clause), all within the Advent weekday formularies. Which weekdays these are, and what the repetition means, is left to research.
4. **No passage record for the Sunday's orations.** The source library holds passage records for this formulary's Communion antiphon (`passage.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-tertia-2002.historical-antiphon-advent-1-main-communion-antiphon-1`, artifact p. 83) and its Prayer over the Offerings (`…english-completion-20261029-postconciliar-phase3-temporal-advent-1-main-prayer-over-the-offerings-all-1-comparison`, artifact pp. 82–83), made by other productions and read here as records only. It holds none for the formulary as a whole, for the Collect, the Prayer after Communion, the Preface or the blessing, and none for printed p. 3 of the ICEL excerpt. Registering what the leaf relies on belongs to the research stage.

*Added by the research stage of the `PC-S01-B` production (run `99f04ef5cf9b711b`), 2026-11-26.* Findings 5–8 rest on the witnesses named in each, read on that date; the consuming leaf's `research/source-bindings.toml` binds them and its `research/scope.md` section 3.7 gives the bounds. They are textual observations: agreement of wording shows that a text existed in that form, not how or when the Missal's compilers used it.

5. **The antiphons against the *Nova Vulgata*.** The *Nova Vulgata* psalter (the Holy See's web state, byte-identical to the registered `artifact.catholic-church.nova-vulgata.vatican-official-web.liber-psalmorum-e508ed1c`) reads Psalm 25 (24):1–3 with the vocative *Domine* after *Ad te*, with *Neque exsultent super me inimici mei* in v. 2 and *qui sustinent te* in v. 3. The Entrance antiphon departs from it in all three places: it has no vocative, it keeps the Clementine's *Neque irrideant me inimici mei*, and it reads *qui te exspectant*, which neither the *Nova Vulgata* nor the Clementine has. The antiphon is therefore not a *Nova Vulgata* text. The registered record of Augustine's *Enarratio in Psalmum 24* on augustinus.it (`…augustinus-it-ps024-614fe703`, not re-read: a request on 2026-11-26 returned 4,608 bytes that are not that page) records that his psalter reads *qui te exspectant*; that is a lead to the antiphon's older Latin, not a verified source. The *Nova Vulgata* reads Psalm 85 (84):13 *Etenim Dominus dabit benignitatem, et terra nostra dabit fructum suum*, as the Clementine does; the Communion antiphon omits only *Etenim* (finding 2). Augustine's psalter reads *suavitatem* for *benignitatem* (his *Enarratio* 84.15, Latin, tracked).
6. **The Collect and the Old Gelasian.** The Old Gelasian sacramentary (Vat. Reg. lat. 316) in H. A. Wilson's edition (Oxford, 1894), printed p. 218, read on the page image (PDF p. 311 of the registered scan `artifact.catholic-church.sacramentarium-gelasianum-vetus.wilson-1894.internet-archive-pdf-006a38a3`, fetched whole and matched to its SHA-256 before reading), gives as the *Postcommunio* of the fourth Advent *Missa* (book II, LXXXIII, *Item alia missa*): *Da, quaesumus, omnipotens Deus, cunctae familiae tuae hanc voluntatem in Christo Filio tuo, Domino nostro venienti in operibus iustis aptos occurrere: et eius dexterae sociati regnum mereantur possidere caeleste.* The Missal's Collect has *hanc tuis fidelibus voluntatem* for *cunctae familiae tuae hanc voluntatem*, *ut, Christo tuo venienti iustis operibus occurrentes* for the infinitive construction, and the same closing clause. Wilson's note (e) on the same page records the prayer in the books he abbreviates R., S. and Gerb. as that of a *Missa in quotidianis diebus*, in Men. as the *super populum* for the third Sunday before Christmas, and in the book he abbreviates Miss. Gall. as a *Benedictio populi* in the first Advent *Missa*; those witnesses were not read, and the abbreviations are left as Wilson prints them. In the Gelasian it is a prayer after Communion of an Advent Mass; in the 2002 Missal it is this Sunday's Collect.
7. **The Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion in the Verona sacramentary.** C. L. Feltoe's edition of the Verona (Leonine) sacramentary (Cambridge, 1896), read on page images of the registered tracked scan `artifact.catholic-church.sacramentarium-veronense.feltoe-1896.internet-archive-pdf-3b991b13` (fetched whole on 2026-11-26 because the checkout lacks the payload; SHA-256 matched): (a) printed p. 74 (PDF p. 98), *Mense Iulio*, XXVIIII *Item alia*, marginal no. 372, ll. 5–7, has the Prayer over the Offerings whole, differing only in *temporalis* for the Missal's *temporali*; this confirms the inventories' lead. (b) The Prayer after Communion has no single antecedent in what was read, but its two halves agree with two Verona prayers: printed p. 134 (PDF p. 158), in the September section, XV *Item [alia]*, ll. 27–28, *Prosint nobis Domine frequentata mysteria quae nos a cupiditatibus terrenis expediant et instituant amare caelestia*; and printed p. 20 (PDF p. 44), *Mense Maio*, VIIII *Preces in Ascensa Domini*, ll. 23–24, *Da nobis Domine non terrena sapere sed amare caelestia et inter praetereuntia constitutos iam nunc inhaerere mansuris*. The Missal's prayer joins the first prayer's opening to the second prayer's close. Neither Verona context is Advent.
8. **The Preface's closing clause in the Verona sacramentary.** On the same p. 20 (PDF p. 44), ll. 16–19, among the *Preces in Ascensa Domini*: *Exaudi nos misericors Deus et da mentibus nostris quo Redemptor noster conscendit attolli ut secundo Mediatoris adventu manifesto munere capiamus quod nunc audemus sperare promissum.* Preface I of Advent closes with *ut, cum secundo venerit in suae gloria maiestatis, manifesto demum munere capiamus, quod vigilantes nunc audemus exspectare promissum*. The words *secundo … manifesto munere capiamus quod … nunc audemus … promissum* agree; where the Verona prayer has *sperare*, the Preface has *vigilantes … exspectare*. The first half of the Preface's embolism (the first coming in humble flesh) was not found in what was read. The antecedent of the solemn blessing was not searched.

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the ICEL antiphon excerpt in element order, antiphon assignment, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation list's Advent subsection names no page of this formulary: that is a bounded negative check, showing no *listed* change, and is not evidence of page-level identity between the 2002 and 2008 printings.

**Leads not verified here.** The tracked `advent-1` rows of `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml` record the Prayer over the Offerings as agreeing, except for one inflection (*temporalis* against the Missal's *temporali*), with a prayer of the Verona sacramentary in C. L. Feltoe's edition (1896), section XXVIIII, *Item alia*, marginal no. 372; and they record that no ancient ancestor of the Prayer after Communion was found within that inventory's declared corpus. They record no antecedent for the Collect. None of this was checked against a witness in this audit. They are leads for the research stage, not evidence of any prayer's history. *Research, 2026-11-26:* finding 7 checks the Verona lead for the Prayer over the Offerings against its witness and confirms it; findings 6–8 record what was found for the Collect, the Prayer after Communion and the Preface.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and the short Latin phrases needed by the findings above. It reproduces no complete oration, Preface or blessing in either language and no approved English wording at all. The Latin antiphons are compared with the public-domain Clementine only phrase by phrase. The rights records that govern any display of these texts are `guidance/liturgical-text-publication-policy.md`, `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml` and `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml`; their `advent-1` rows were read as leads and are not restated.
