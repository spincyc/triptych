## Rubrical dependencies

- Sunday use requires the Gloria and the Creed (*Tempus per annum* rubric 4; IGMR 53, 68).
- Because Week XXVIII has no proper Preface, *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 and IGMR 364–365 govern: one of the eight Prefaces of the Sundays in Ordinary Time is used unless the selected Eucharistic Prayer has its own. IGMR 365 d binds Eucharistic Prayer IV to its invariable Preface and admits it on Sundays in Ordinary Time; IGMR 365 b lets Eucharistic Prayer II take its own Preface or another. The Preface and the Eucharistic Prayer are therefore one coupled choice, and this formulary selects neither.
- *Tempus per annum* rubric 6 describes the pair of Communion antiphons, the first from the psalms and the other mostly from the Gospel, lets either be chosen, and prefers one that happens to agree with the Gospel of the Mass. In this week the second antiphon is drawn from 1 John, not from a Gospel. Whether either agrees with a cycle Gospel is resolved, if at all, in each consuming leaf.
- The formulary is proper to the Twenty-eighth Sunday and is additionally *available* on an Ordinary Time feria under *Tempus per annum* rubric 3 b and IGMR 363. Availability is not appointment: a ferial use is a recorded selection, imports no Sunday reading, and does not carry the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.

## Findings internal to this edition

1. **The Entrance antiphon is an adaptation printed without *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine Vulgate (`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`) the antiphon reproduces Psalm 129:3 and the first colon of v. 4, *Quia apud te propitiatio est*, and then departs from both: it omits the rest of v. 4, *et propter legem tuam sustinui te, Domine*, and ends with the vocative *Deus Israel*, which stands nowhere in the Clementine's Psalm 129. The psalm names Israel only in vv. 6 and 8, in the third person. The antiphon is therefore an adaptation, not a quotation, whatever the absence of *Cf.* Which Latin text supplies its ending is not established here. The Hebrew verse numbers for vv. 3–4 coincide with the Vulgate's in the tracked King James Version, but the Hebrew v. 4 reads differently in its second half; this record makes no judgment on that difference.
2. **Communion antiphon A is cited in Vulgate numbering and marked *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine the Missal reads *quæréntes* for *inquirentes*; the rest of v. 11 agrees. The numbering is a hazard in two ways. Vulgate 33:11 is Hebrew 34:11 in the count that numbers the title, and 34:10 in English Bibles that do not, such as the tracked King James Version. And the subject differs between traditions: the tracked King James Version, translating the Hebrew, has young lions where the Clementine and the Douay–Rheims have the rich. Any tool resolving this locator must declare which numbering it uses; no judgment on the textual difference is made here.
3. **Communion antiphon B is an adaptation printed without *Cf.*** Against the tracked Clementine the Missal omits the opening *Scimus quoniam* of the clause it quotes and supplies an explicit subject, *Dóminus*, for *apparuerit*, which in the Clementine has none expressed; it omits the first half of the verse, *Carissimi, nunc filii Dei sumus: et nondum apparuit quid erimus*. The supplied subject is an interpretive choice by the Missal, recorded here as printed. It adds no *dicit Dominus*.
4. **Conclusions and repeated orations.** The Collect has the long conclusion *Per Dóminum*; the Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion the short *Per Christum*. A literal search of the whole 2002 artifact's text layer found the Prayer over the Offerings printed also, with the same body, for the Seventh Sunday of Easter (physical p. 260) and the Monday of the Seventh Week of Easter (physical p. 261), and the Prayer after Communion printed also for the Saturday of the Fifth Week of Lent (physical pp. 163–164). The Collect and the three antiphons occur only on p. 291. These are recorded as editorial facts of this edition; what the repetition means for either prayer's history is left to research. The Prayer after Communion's closing petition uses the words *divinæ naturæ … consórtes*, which stand in 2 Peter 1:4 in the tracked Clementine; the Missal prints no locator for any oration, and that is a textual observation, not an identified source.
5. **No passage record yet.** The source library has no passage record for *Dominica XXVIII per annum* in the 2002 artifact, and none for printed p. 80 of the ICEL excerpt. Registering them belongs to the research stage.

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the ICEL antiphon excerpt in element order, antiphon assignment, alternative structure, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation-list silence is a bounded negative check: it shows no *listed* change to Week XXVIII and is not evidence of page-level identity between the 2002 and 2008 printings.

**Leads not verified here.** The tracked `ot-28` rows of `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml` record the Collect as verbatim in the Hadrianum edited by H. A. Wilson (1915) and the Prayer after Communion as verbatim in the Verona sacramentary edited by C. L. Feltoe (1896), each from a remote OCR match; and they record an English antecedent for the Prayer over the Offerings from a nineteenth-century lay missal. None of these statements was checked against its witness in this audit. They are leads for the research stage, not evidence of any prayer's history, and the last is an antecedent of another form of the rite whose text this production does not import.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and the short Latin phrases needed by the findings above. It reproduces no complete oration in either language and no approved English wording at all. The Latin antiphons are compared with the public-domain Clementine only phrase by phrase. The rights records that govern any display of these texts are `guidance/liturgical-text-publication-policy.md`, `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml` and `src/sources/inventories/postconciliar-proper-translations-v1.toml`; their `ot-28` rows were read as leads and are not restated.
