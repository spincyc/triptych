\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & A householder hires labourers
at five hours of one day, pays the last the denarius he agreed with the first,
and asks the murmurer, ``Is thy eye evil, because I am good?'' Isaiah calls
the wicked back to a God ``bountiful to forgive'', merciful and just in the
psalm; Paul, in chains, stays at his work for the Philippians.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The vineyard is the
Church from Abel to the last of the elect (Gregory); its hours are the ages of
the world (Gregory; Augustine) or of one life (Chrysostom; Jerome). The
householder who ``went out'' is Christ made known to the nations (Augustine).
The wage is the king's image and likeness (Jerome).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Come when called, for the reward
is certain and the day is not (Augustine). Know the shepherd by deed and not
by belief alone (Gregory). To grieve at goodness is the evil eye (Aquinas). Boast of no
merit, and ask of God what he commands (Augustine).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The denarius is eternal
life: all the just receive the resurrection together, alike in living for
ever and unlike in brightness (Augustine). God will give himself to those who
call on him in truth (Augustine, of the psalm); the pasture of the elect is
his present face (Gregory, of the shepherd's sheep).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
