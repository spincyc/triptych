\section*{Note on Sources and Delivery}

{\small

\textbf{Audience and occasion.} An adult parish assembly, at the Mass of the
Twenty-sixth Sunday in Ordinary Time, Year~A: \latin{Dominica XXVI ``per
annum''} of the \work{Roman Missal}, Third Edition, for the dioceses of the
United States, with no.~136 of the \work{Lectionary for Mass}, for the
occurrence of 27~September 2026. The words hold under either form of the
second reading. Philippians 2:3 and~5 stand in both forms; 2:6--8 are quoted
as what Paul goes on to write after 2:5, and not as words the assembly has
heard. Of the two Communion antiphons, the second is named as one of the pair
the Missal gives.

\textbf{Length and pace.} The spoken body runs to 1,553 words, counted as
whitespace-separated words once comments and markup are removed: about twelve
minutes at an unhurried 130 words a minute, a little under twelve and a half
at 125, and a little under thirteen at 120. That is arithmetic on
the word count; no speaker has delivered these words and no delivery has
been timed. The prose was read through in full for sense, sentence length
and ease of speech, silently and not aloud.

\textbf{Relation to the reviewed interpretations.} The argument preached is
the first of the expansive study's three interpretations, \textit{Afterward
He Repented: Each Is Judged Where He Is Found}. God judges each where he is
found, so no sinner may despair of turning and no just man may rest on his
past, and the turning is urgent because no one is promised tomorrow. Jerome
on Ezekiel, Chrysostom and Aquinas on the parable, and Augustine on delay, on
the psalm and on 1~John 3:16--17 carry it. It is joined to the third,
\textit{The Mind That Was in Christ Jesus: The Son Who Obeyed unto Death},
for the second reading, Chrysostom on Philippians 2:8, Augustine's lowly
God, Schuster on the Collect and Augustine's table of the ruler. From the
second, \textit{The Publicans Go Before You}, come only the parable's first
hearers as Matthew names them, Chrysostom's ``go before you'' as a hope held
out to them, and Aquinas's application of the second son to clerics and
religious. The comparison in the study calls the first two complementary and
the third different in kind, and all three put the deed before the word; the
combination preached asserts nothing any of the three denies. The
identification of the two sons with two peoples is not preached, and the
application falls on the baptized.

Each Father is credited only with what he says of his own passage:
Chrysostom's charge, his hope and his two warnings of the parable, his
obedient Son of Philippians 2:8; Jerome's sentence of Ezekiel 18; Aquinas's
``today'', his professed just and his double sin of Matthew 21; Augustine's
tomorrow of the promise in Ezekiel, his lowly God of the washing of the feet,
his table of the ruler of 1~John 3:16 read with a proverb, and his words to
the beginner in charity and to the one who has the name without the deeds of
1~John 3:16--17; Schuster's condescension of the Collect, glossed in his own
sense as God's descent into the abyss to draw the sinner out. The psalm heard
as the prayer of one who has wandered and cannot find the way back unless the
Lord comes out to meet him is Augustine's reading of Psalm 24, spoken in the
preacher's words without his name. The following connections are the
homily's own, drawn from the wording of the texts, and no Father makes them:
Jerome's rule applied to the two sons' first word; Chrysostom's saying that
God does not reproach the past set beside the psalm verse as what the psalm
asks; the Amen of the baptized as the second son's answer; the Son of
Philippians~2 set beside the two sons as the one who says yes and goes; the
gifts as the turned heart of the first son, taken into the Son's offering;
the Collect's power in mercy read in the descent of the lowly God;
Augustine's sentence on the name without the deeds set beside the second son;
the Prayer after Communion as the mending of a turning begun; and Aquinas's
``today'' read with Augustine's ``tomorrow''.

Left to the studies: Jerome and Aquinas on whether the good works of one who
falls away help him when he returns (the homily makes only the claim both
hold, that no one may plead his past); Hilary's reversal of the sons and the
divided text of Matthew 21:31; Augustine and Aquinas on John 10:26, with the
Alleluia verse; the Entrance Antiphon and the prayer of Azarias; the first
Communion antiphon and Augustine's joining of it to Ezekiel 18:22; and the
early history of the Collect and the Prayer after Communion.

\textbf{Texts quoted, and their route.} Scripture is quoted in the
Douay--Rheims version as revised by Bishop Challoner, a public-domain study
translation, at Mt 21:28--32 (with 21:23--27 narrated), Ezek 18:2, 23 and
25--27 (with 1:1 and~3 for the captives), Ps 24:7 (Psalm 25 as the
Lectionary numbers it), Phil 2:5--8 and 1~Jn 3:16. It is not the English
proclaimed at Mass, which is that of the \work{Lectionary for Mass for Use
in the Dioceses of the United States of America} and is not reproduced. The
Collect, the Prayer over the Offerings and the Prayer after Communion are
described after the study's descriptions of the Latin of the \work{Missale
Romanum}; the descriptions are not translations and are not the prayers'
words. Chrysostom's homilies on Matthew and on Philippians and Augustine's
sermon and tractates are quoted in the English of the Nicene and Post-Nicene
Fathers, and Schuster in the English of 1927; each of those quotations was
read in the tracked text at the locus below. Augustine's exposition of the
psalm and his beginning of charity in giving to a brother in need are followed
in that English without quotation marks. Jerome and Aquinas are reported
without quotation marks, in English that renders the Latin the study prints;
that Latin was read on page images at the research stage.

\textbf{Exact loci.} Chrysostom, ``For an evil thing it is not at the first
to choose the good \ldots'' and ``the word, `go before you,' \ldots'':
\work{Homilies on Matthew} 67.3; ``Let no man then of them that live in vice
despair \ldots'' and ``God is not as man \ldots'': 67.4; ``He became obedient
as a Son to His Father'', with the honour this act gives the Father:
\work{Homilies on Philippians} 7. Jerome, \latin{Unusquisque enim in quo
invenietur, in eo iudicabitur}: \work{In Hiezechielem} VI, on Ezek
18:21--25, PL 25, col.~180. Aquinas, \latin{Hodie, quasi per totum tempus
vitae tuae}; the second son turned on clerics and religious, who profess
justice; and \latin{duplex peccatum; peccatum inobedientiae, et transgressio
voti}: \work{Super Matthaeum} c.~21, pp.~275--276. Augustine, ``who hath
promised thee a to-morrow?'' and the raven and the dove: \work{Sermo} 82.14;
the psalm as the prayer of the wanderer who cannot return unless the Lord
meets him: \work{Enarrationes in Psalmos} 24.5; ``proud man would have
perished eternally \ldots'': \work{Tractates on John} 55.7; ``what is the
table of the ruler \ldots'': 84.1; ``Do not too soon despair of thyself'',
charity begun in giving to a brother in need, and ``Thou hast the name, and
hast not the deeds'': \work{Homilies on the First Epistle of John} 5.12.
Schuster, ``requires, so to speak, a condescension and powerful energy
\ldots'', and God's descent into the abyss to draw the sinner out: \work{The
Sacramentary}, vol.~3, pp.~121--122.
