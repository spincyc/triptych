## Rubrical dependencies

- Sunday use requires the Gloria and the Creed (*Tempus per annum* rubrics; GIRM 53, 68).
- Because Week XVII has no proper Preface, the Preface is chosen under *Tempus per annum* rubric 5 and GIRM 365: an authorized Preface of Sundays in Ordinary Time is used unless the selected Eucharistic Prayer carries its own. GIRM 365(d) binds Eucharistic Prayer IV to its own inseparable Preface, which therefore excludes a Sunday Preface; GIRM 365(b) leaves Eucharistic Prayer II free to take its own Preface or another that qualifies.
- The Missal's own index places *Tempus per annum* at printed p. 450 and the Solemnity of the Most Holy Trinity at printed p. 485. A Week XVII locus at printed pp. 466–467 is arithmetically consistent with thirty-four roughly one-page formularies in that span, but the registered artifact carries no folio numbers and this audit did not read a printed page. The 2008 official notice states that the reprint's pagination is unchanged from the 2002 edition except in the concluding matter and the indexes, so a 2002 locus may be reported as a 2008 locus; the same notice records that page layout was improved in rare cases, which is why direct page-image inspection remains outstanding.
- The formulary is proper to the Seventeenth Sunday and is additionally *available* on an eligible Ordinary Time feria under *Tempus per annum* rubric 3(b) and GIRM 363. Availability is not appointment: a ferial use is a recorded selection, imports no Sunday reading, and does not carry the Gloria, Creed, or Sunday Preface.

## Findings internal to this edition

These observations were made by reading the exact 2002 artifact and are recorded here because they are properties of the reusable formulary rather than of any one cycle.

1. **The Collect's address is not unique.** *Protéctor in te sperántium, Deus* opens two different prayers in the 2002 Missal: this Collect, and an optional *Oratio super populum* appointed for Thursday of the Fourth Week of Lent. The two prayers diverge immediately after the address. The invocation is therefore a formulaic Roman address, and no argument may treat it as a signature of Week XVII alone.
2. **The Collect's petition was revised in the postconciliar redaction.** The 1962 Missal's Collect for *Dominica III post Pentecosten* shares the whole first half word for word, and then reads, in the community transcription checked, a petition to *pass through* temporal goods so as not to *lose* the eternal. The 2002 Week XVII text instead asks so to *use* passing goods *now* that we may *already* cleave to those that abide. The change of verbs — from transit and non-loss to use and adherence — and the doubled temporal adverb are the substantive difference. Because the 1962 side rests on a community transcription rather than a collated printing, this audit records the comparison as a strong but secondary-witness finding.
3. **The Collect's closing idiom recurs in Advent.** The exact phrase *inhærére mansúris* appears eight times in the 2002 Missal: once in this Collect, and seven times in the Post-Communion prayer of the First Sunday of Advent and of the Mondays and Thursdays of the Advent weeks, where it is paired with *inter prætereúntia ambulántes*. Week XVII's *bonis transeúntibus* is the only occurrence of that exact phrase. The passing/abiding contrast is thus a shared redactional idiom of this typical edition, not an isolated conceit of one Sunday.
4. **The Entrance antiphon's Latin follows neither Vulgate recension.** The Missal reads *unánimes in domo*. The Clementine Vulgate at Ps 67:7 reads *unius moris*; the Nova Vulgata at Ps 68:7 reads *desolatos in domo*. The Missal's wording matches the older Latin psalter form carried in the chant repertory, and the official Antiphonary keys this antiphon to *Graduale Romanum* p. 310. This audit did not collate a *Graduale Romanum* page, so the chant-book derivation is recorded as the best-supported explanation of a verified divergence rather than as a collated fact.
5. **The same Entrance antiphon serves the Dedication of a Church.** The 2002 Missal appoints *Deus in loco sancto suo* (`Cf.` Ps 67:6.7.36) as the Entrance antiphon of the Ritual Mass *In dedicatione ecclesiae*, with an alternative from Ps 121:1. The Week XVII antiphon therefore carries an established ecclesial and architectural register within this same book.

## Variants, discrepancies, rights, and disposition

No formula-level discrepancy was found between the 2002 Latin text and the official English Antiphonary in element order, antiphon assignment, alternative structure, or printed scriptural locators. The 2008 variation-list silence at Week XVII is reported as a bounded negative check only: the list's two *Tempus per annum* entries at printed pp. 457 and 471 bracket the Week XVII locus without touching it, which is evidence of no *listed* change, not evidence of page-level identity.

**Rights.** The Latin *editio typica* and the approved English translation are both under active copyright (Libreria Editrice Vaticana for the Latin; ICEL and the USCCB for the English). This record retains citations, short Latin incipits, and a small number of short Latin phrases required by the philological findings above. It reproduces no complete oration in either language, and it reproduces no approved English antiphon or oration at all.
