\section{Daily Readings for Advent}\label{sec:daily}

The readings in these tables are those of the \textit{Lectionary for Mass} for use in the dioceses
of the United States. They are cited as that Lectionary cites them, by the chapters and verses of
the New American Bible, with the psalms numbered as in the Hebrew Bible. The number in brackets
beside a day's readings is the Lectionary's own number for them. Where the Lectionary allows a
choice, the choices are joined by ``or''.

Read the passages in your own Bible. A Sunday has a first reading, a psalm, a second reading and
the Gospel, and the Sundays follow a cycle of three years: Advent 2026 begins Year~B, Advent 2027
Year~C and Advent 2028 Year~A. A weekday has a first reading, a psalm and the Gospel, and the
weekdays of Advent are the same every year. Until 16 December a weekday is found by its week: the
Tuesday of the second week has the same readings whatever its date. From 17 December the readings
go by the date, whatever the day of the week.

Each day also has its Office of Readings, the Hour of the Liturgy of the Hours in which a passage
of Scripture is followed by a second reading from a Father of the Church, a saint, a council or
another spiritual writer; the tables name its author and work. On the Sundays, and on the
solemnities and feasts, the Office of Readings ends with the hymn of praise \latin{Te Deum}.

What the Lectionary and the Office appoint is what the Church reads that day, at the altar and in
the Hours. The last column of the weekday table is of another kind. A word to keep is one verse
from that day's first reading or Gospel, to learn in the morning and say again at night; it is
offered for home, and no book of the liturgy appoints it. These verses are quoted from the
Douay-Rheims Bible, under the usual names of the books and with the Douay's own chapter and
verse numbers, which in places differ from the
Lectionary's. Where a Father's commentary on a psalm is named, the number in brackets is that
psalm's number in his Latin Bible.

\subsection{Finding the Day}

Advent begins with Evening Prayer~I of the Sunday that falls on or nearest 30 November (Universal
Norms on the Liturgical Year, n.~40). The First Sunday therefore falls between 27 November and 3~December
, and the Fourth between 18 and 24 December. The season lasts from twenty-two to
twenty-eight days and ends on the evening of 24 December, before Evening Prayer~I of Christmas.

The first and second weeks of Advent always have all their weekdays. The third week has Monday to
Friday only, and it stops wherever it meets 17 December: in the longest Advent all five days are
read, in the two shortest none.

A Sunday between 17 and 24 December has its own readings, and the weekday readings of that date are
not read at Mass. The \textit{Ordo lectionum Missae} (1981), the Latin edition of the Lectionary,
in a note before the Masses of 17 December, allows readings passed over in this way to be read on
another day of the week, earlier or later, especially in place of a weekday reading that repeats
one heard on the Sunday.

When 24 December is itself the Fourth Sunday, as in 2028, the morning belongs to the Sunday, with
its own readings and its own verse before the Gospel in place of \latin{Oriens}, the Dawn; in the
evening Advent ends at Evening Prayer~I, and the Vigil Mass of Christmas is celebrated. In Year~A,
as in 2028 and 2034, the Sunday Gospel (Mt 1:18--24) and the Vigil Gospel (Mt 1:1--25 or 1:18--25)
both tell of Joseph's dream. The Sunday and Christmas are each days of obligation; follow your
parish's schedule of Masses for both.

A saint's day, or a feast of our Lady, that falls on a Sunday of Advent gives way to the Sunday and
is not kept that year. The Immaculate Conception alone is moved. When 8 December is a Sunday the
solemnity is kept on Monday 9 December, St Juan Diego is not kept that year, and the obligation to
attend Mass does not pass to the Monday. The next such year is 2030.

In 2026 the First Sunday is 29 November and the Sundays read Year~B. St Andrew's feast falls on
Monday 30 November, the Immaculate Conception, a holy day of obligation, on Tuesday 8 December, and
Our Lady of Guadalupe on Saturday 12 December; on each of these days the feast's readings replace
the weekday's. St Nicholas (6 December) and St Lucy (13 December) fall on the Second and Third
Sundays and are not kept. The Fourth Sunday is 20 December, so the weekday readings of that date
give way to the Sunday, whose Gospel is the same Annunciation (Lk 1:26--38). On Thursday 24~December
 the morning's first reading, 2~Sam 7, repeats the Sunday's, so the permission of the
\textit{Ordo lectionum Missae} fits the case: Is 7:10--14, passed over on 20 December, may be read
in its place. Christmas Day is a Friday.

\subsection{The Sundays}

Each Sunday has three sets of readings, one for each year of the cycle. The Office of Readings is
the same every year and is given beneath each Sunday. On the Third Sunday of Year~B the psalm is
the Magnificat, Mary's canticle in Luke. The Fourth Sunday has no second reading of its own in the
Office of Readings and takes that of its date.

\subsection{17 to 24 December}

In these days the Gospels tell what came before the Lord's birth: his genealogy; the angel's
message to Joseph, to Zachary and to Mary; the Visitation and Mary's Magnificat; the birth of
John and his father's song. The verse sung before the Gospel is one of the great O antiphons,
shortened. Evening Prayer sings the same antiphons whole, with the
Magnificat, in a different order. Each is named by its first Latin words.
