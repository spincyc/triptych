\subsection{The Holy Night}\label{sec:holy-night}

The first people to hear of the birth were awake. St Luke says that there were
``shepherds watching and keeping the night watches over their flock,'' and that
the glory of the Lord shone round about them:

\begin{quote}
And the angel said to them: Fear not; for, behold, I bring you good tidings of
great joy that shall be to all the people: For, this day is born to you a
Saviour, who is Christ the Lord, in the city of David.\par
{\smallskip\noindent\small ---Luke 2:10--11\par}
\end{quote}

\noindent The Church has kept the watch for four weeks. On the night of Christmas
she hears the Gospel the shepherds heard, and in the morning, in the Office of
Readings of the Nativity, Pope St Leo the Great speaks to her as he spoke to the
people of Rome:

\begin{quote}
Our Saviour, dearly-beloved, was born to-day: let us be glad. For there is no
proper place for sadness, when we keep the birthday of the Life, which destroys
the fear of mortality and brings to us the joy of promised eternity. No one is
kept from sharing in this happiness. \ldots{} Let the saint exult in that he
draws near to victory. Let the sinner be glad in that he is invited to pardon.
Let the gentile take courage in that he is called to life.\par
{\smallskip\noindent\small ---St Leo the Great, Sermon 21\par}
\end{quote}

The grace of that night can reach a single soul. St Thérèse of Lisieux, telling
her own story, wrote of the Christmas of 1886, when she still longed to be
admitted to the Carmel. She had been at Midnight Mass with her family, and she
knew that at home her shoes would be waiting in the chimney-corner, filled with
presents, as when she was little. On the stairs she heard her father, vexed for once, say that it was
all too babyish for a big girl and that he hoped it would be the last year. Her
sister Céline, who knew how easily she cried, whispered to her to wait before
going down. Instead she ran down, picked up the shoes, and pulled out the
presents gaily, and her father laughed. She gave the change to the Child of
that night:

\begin{quote}
On that blessed night the sweet Infant Jesus, scarce an hour old, filled the
darkness of my soul with floods of light. By becoming weak and little, for love
of me, He made me strong and brave; He put His own weapons into my hands, so
that I went from victory to victory, beginning, if I may say so, ``to run as a
giant.'' The fountain of my tears was dried up, and from that time they flowed
neither easily nor often.\par
{\smallskip\noindent\small ---St Thérèse of Lisieux, \emph{Story of a Soul}, chapter V\par}
\end{quote}

\noindent Advent began with a command to go out and meet the Lord. On Christmas
morning Leo tells the Church who she is, now that he has come:

\begin{quote}
Christian, acknowledge thy dignity, and becoming a partner in the Divine nature,
refuse to return to the old baseness by degenerate conduct. Remember the Head
and the Body of which thou art a member. Recollect that thou wert rescued from
the power of darkness and brought out into God's light and kingdom. By the
mystery of Baptism thou wert made the temple of the Holy Ghost: do not put such a
denizen to flight from thee by base acts, and subject thyself once more to the
devil's thraldom: because thy purchase money is the blood of Christ, because He
shall judge thee in truth Who ransomed thee in mercy, who with the Father and the
Holy Spirit reigns for ever and ever. Amen.\par
{\smallskip\noindent\small ---St Leo the Great, Sermon 21\par}
\end{quote}
