# Cold review

Two independent reviewers read the complete draft on 26 November 2026: the
body, the daily readings, the prayers and About This Book. Neither had seen
the drafting. The reader (lane R) read only the editorial standard, the two
seasonal-handbook profiles and the section files in order, and planned four
days with nothing else. The accuracy reviewer (lane Q) read the draft against
the evidence pack, the five research dossiers, the writers' ledgers and the
library records. The coordinating session dispositioned their findings; the
body's writer and the daily-readings writer applied them in one fix pass, and
the coordinator applied the findings that fell on the prayers appendix (whose
text a script generates from the records) and on About This Book. A further
source-acquisition lane registered the witnesses that four findings needed.

## Lane R: the reader

Verdict: accept with corrections. Checked as sound: all seventeen
cross-references; no use of the polemical names, and no grading of one form of
the Roman Rite against another; no audit vocabulary in the body; every reading
of the appendix against the reader's knowledge of the U.S. Lectionary; the
start-date table recomputed for 2023–2044, with every Sunday letter; law given
with body, territory and date, and counsel plainly counsel; the Fathers given
room to speak.

Use tests, with the book alone: the First Sunday of 2026, 8 December 2026 and
21 December all worked. The overlap year (24 December a Sunday) was found by
the book's rules (2028, 2034) and confirmed with `datetime`; the morning and
evening were clear, but the question of one Mass and two obligations was not
answered (R11).

## After the fix pass

The quotation script was run again over every section file against a freshly
built corpus: no fragment fails. Ten lines differ only in capitals set in the
ordinary way (eight in the prayers appendix, and the Douay's emphatic capitals
in Numbers 24:17, quoted twice), and thirty are the book's own renderings after
Latin or Nahuatl, each read against its original. The prayers appendix is
regenerated by its script, whose self-check finds every printed line in its
record.

The coordinator read the two passages added for R15 against their sources.
The account of St Thérèse's Christmas agrees with the checked transcription,
and her quoted paragraph is exact. The paraphrase of *Admirabile signum* was
reworded where it ran close to the Vatican English ("our littleness"; the
poverty the Son of God "took upon himself"; "the humble and the poor"); a test
for runs of five words shared with that English now finds only the letter's
dating clause. The same test, run over the whole book against the Holy See's
English of *Sacrosanctum Concilium*, *Marialis cultus*, the *Directory on
Popular Piety and the Liturgy*, the Loreto decree and the Dicastery's note of
2025, found four more paraphrases that kept the official English's word order
(*Sacrosanctum Concilium* nn. 100 and 107; the *Directory* nn. 98 and 103).
They were rewritten, from the Latin where the Latin is held, and now share only
titles and set phrases with it.

## Collation

Building the source audit from the final text surfaced sixteen claims with no
library record, and four places where the wording went beyond its record. The
four were corrected in the text: the present Office keeps the call to go out to
meet him, not the whole Latin refrain; St Hilary's "fuel" is the consciousness
of good works the alms buy, as the Catena gives him; Amalarius wrote "or", not
"and"; and Chrysostom's homily is on John 1:41–42. The John of the Cross ballads
are now named by their printed titles, and *Ineffabilis Deus* is called an
apostolic constitution, as its record has it. The sixteen claims went to a second
research pass under the editorial guidance. It registered a witness for each:
the General Instruction of the Liturgy of the Hours and the daily Office for the
shape of the present Hours; the U.S. liturgical calendar of 2026 for the
alternative readings and the short Gospels; the Missal's rubric for the hour of
the Vigil Mass; the Martyrology of 1902, Gifford's life of St Cyril, Coleridge's
life of St Francis Xavier, the *Patrologia Latina* and other held books for the
descriptions of persons; and the Breviary's general rubrics for the *Te Deum*.
One search ended without result: no public Latin text of the General Instruction
of the Liturgy of the Hours was found, so its English of 1974 serves, and the
book paraphrases it.

## Reconciliation with main, 27 November 2026

The branch was rebuilt on main, which had added four rules this book was not
written to. The maintainer asked that all four be applied lightly: the
harmonization pass, published English where it exists, the canticles collated
against a printed Douay, and the Lasance conclusions checked against the Latin.

*Harmonization pass* (editorial guidance). A fresh agent read the whole book
once for consistency. Its changes:

- titles of works in italics (the *Catena Aurea*, *City of God*, *Against
  Heresies* and others);
- the usual names of the biblical books in the prose and the tables, with the
  Douay's own chapter and verse numbers kept and the Douay's name given where
  it differs (Sirach, "Ecclesiasticus in the Douay");
- "our Lady" in ordinary use, and "Our Lady of" in the titles of feasts (the
  coordinator made the two generated headnotes of Appendix B agree);
- a few attributions shortened.

Divine pronouns are lowercase in the book's own prose, and quotations keep
their editions' capitals, as About This Book says. The agent also cut the
*Rorate* Introit and the Angelus prayer from the Fourth Sunday's chapter
because Appendix B prints them, which would have undone the disposition of R1.
The maintainer chose to keep them in both places, so that cut was reverted
and the rest of the chapter's changes kept.

*Published English* (source guidance). Six of the book's own renderings were
replaced by public-domain English of the same text:

- Bright's *Ancient Collects* (1887) for the Gelasian *Excita*, the Gelasian
  prayer behind the 21 December Collect and the Veronese clause on the second
  coming of the Mediator;
- Bute's Breviary for St Gregory on Bethlehem (Christmas, seventh lesson) and
  on the leaps and *Trahe nos* (Octave of the Ascension, eighth lesson).

The other renderings stay the book's own. `scope.md` records what was found,
what was set aside and why, and where the search could not reach. About This
Book and the References now name Bright, the Spring volume of Bute and the
Baltimore Douay of 1914.

*Canticles.* The Benedictus and Magnificat of Appendix B had been copied from
the Gutenberg e-text. They were collated against the page images of the
Douay printed at Baltimore in 1914 and now follow that printing:

- the words agree with the e-text;
- the punctuation differs in eighteen places;
- the e-text's "for thou shalt, go before" (Luke 1:76) is its own error.

The appendix's script reads the three new records, and its self-check finds
each of the 150 printed lines in a record.

*Lasance.* The rule touches no printed text: the one Lasance English the book
prints is the *Rorate* Introit, an antiphon without a conclusion. The Lasance
Collects consulted for *Festina* and *Concede* were read against the Tours
Latin, and none of their conclusions was faulty. The book does not print them.

After these changes the quotation script, run over every section against a
freshly built corpus, finds:

- no failing fragment in the body;
- the same ten capital-only differences as before;
- twenty-six renderings after Latin, down from thirty.

The References still show the one title that the script reads as a quotation
(the Felix Just finding aid) and one hymn first line in display capitals. The
content preflight passes, with 463 bindings, each fingerprinted.
