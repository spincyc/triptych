# Research Scope

## Identity

- **Provider:** Claude (Anthropic).
- **Collection and genre:** Roman-rite liturgy; edition-specific calendar reference. This Claude
  edition is scoped as a **norms and temporal-cycle reference**, not as a complete normative
  inventory of the fixed calendar.
- **Rite:** postconciliar Roman Rite.
- **Typical-book base:** *Missale Romanum*, editio typica tertia (2002), carrying the *Normae
  universales de anno liturgico et de calendario*, the *Institutio generalis Missalis Romani*, the
  *Calendarium Romanum Generale*, the rubrics of Ordinary Time and the *Tabella temporaria*; with
  the *Ordo lectionum Missae*, editio typica altera (1981), for the Lectionary rules.
- **Territorial implementation:** the dioceses of the United States of America, under the Roman
  Missal, Third Edition (2011), whose Latin base is the 2008 *reimpressio emendata*.
- **Normative cutoff:** 2026-07-25.
- **Language:** normative Latin throughout. Every English rendering in the publication is a labelled
  project working rendering; no approved English translation of a norm is quoted.
- **Governing profiles:** `guidance/liturgy/roman-calendar-references.md` governs the leaf;
  `guidance/liturgy/calendar-computation.md` owns the recurring arithmetic this publication states
  and applies; `guidance/liturgy/postconciliar-propers.md` governs edition and territorial
  discipline where applicable.

## Question and intended reader

For a reader who must know what the postconciliar Roman calendar actually requires, and at which
locus: how the year is structured, how the four grades of celebration differ and what each entails,
how the table of liturgical days resolves an occurrence, what transfer and omission mean and do not
mean, how the movable anchors are derived, how the weeks of Ordinary Time are numbered and how one
of them comes to be omitted, how the Sunday and weekday Lectionary cycles are fixed and why they are
independent, and how national, diocesan and church calendars are composed from the general one.

The work is the reference spine that other guides in this collection cite for a rule. It is not an
*Ordo*, not a liturgical book, and not an act of any authority.

## Included

1. The Universal Norms, nn. 1-61, read complete, with the Table of Liturgical Days in full Latin.
2. The articles of the General Instruction that give the grades their content and govern the choice
   of Mass and readings: 53, 68, 353-355, 357-358, 363, 372, 374-378.
3. The Missal's own rubrics for Ordinary Time, the formularies heading weeks I and XXXIV, the rubric
   at the Baptism of the Lord, the heading of the Christmas Time weekday Masses, the Epiphany rubric
   on announcing movable feasts, and the *Tabella temporaria* for 2000-2023.
4. The *Praenotanda* of the Order of Readings at nn. 65, 66 with note 102, 69, 103 and 104 with
   notes 115-116, and its two decrees of promulgation.
5. The instruction *Calendaria particularia* (1970) on the composition of particular calendars.
6. Canon 1246 and *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 102-111 with the appended calendar declaration.
7. Three liturgical years, 2026, 2027 and 2028, computed end to end and checked against the
   Secretariat of Divine Worship's published calendars; and a twelve-year computed table.
8. A dated timeline of the governing acts, each marked read or reported.

## Excluded, deliberately

- The dated entries of the General Roman Calendar and the recurring United States national overlay.
  Individual entries appear only to illustrate a rule. **No completeness is claimed for any list of
  celebrations in the publication, and the term "complete calendar" is nowhere used of it.** The
  profile's definition of a complete calendar is therefore not asserted here; a reader who needs the
  fixed inventory must go elsewhere.
- Diocesan, parish, cathedral, titular, dedication, patronal and religious-family calendars.
- Prayers, readings, antiphons, chants, prefaces and formularies.
- A resolved calendar for any year offered for use.
- The Liturgy of the Hours beyond the point at which the Universal Norms themselves extend a rule to
  it; the *Institutio generalis de Liturgia Horarum* was not read.
- The history of the reform as narrative.

## Terminology and method

The four postconciliar grades - solemnity, feast, memorial, optional memorial - are used in the
sense of Universal Norms 10 and 14. No term of the 1960/1962 system is treated as a synonym for any
of them. Latin is reproduced with the orthography of the witness used: the 2002 Missal's ae/oe
ligatures, the 1981 Order of Readings' separated letters.

The order of authority applied is: promulgating act; Latin typical edition; universal instruction;
approved territorial calendar; and last, computation. Computation is never its own witness. Every
dated result was computed from the norms and then checked against a dated official witness, and the
outcome of each check is published.

## Material uncertainties

1. No licensed altar book was collated. The Latin Missal text was read in a digitally typeset
   secondary reproduction whose first appendix has no contents and whose *Tabella temporaria* has
   lost its column structure. Page-level identity with the printed edition is not claimed.
2. A systematic one-day error was found in the February dates of five leap-year rows of that
   *Tabella temporaria*. Whether it stands in the printed typical edition, was corrected in 2008, or
   was introduced in the reproduction was not determined; the publication states the balance of
   probability as a labelled project judgment and does not rest any other claim on it.
3. The 2008 *reimpressio emendata* and the 2011 United States English edition were not examined; no
   claim is made about what either prints.
4. The 1969 first typical edition of the *Ordo lectionum Missae* was not examined. Its 1981
   promulgating decree records that the *Praenotanda* were enlarged, so no claim is made that any
   cited number or footnote stood in 1969.
5. The *Institutio generalis de Liturgia Horarum* was not read; the gap is named in the publication
   where it bears on an argument.
6. No ecclesiastical statement of the Easter computus was located. The rule is published as an
   inherited rule of the Gregorian calendar. This is a bounded negative result of the searches
   actually run.
7. The original Claude research reported the 1998 notification on the Immaculate Heart (Prot. n.
   2671/98/L) and the decrees inserting post-2002 additions into the General Roman Calendar from the
   territorial calendar after the Holy See's portal did not serve them at the paths tried on
   2026-07-25. An independent subsequent check on 2026-08-26 read the complete notification in the
   official Vatican Latin delivery and its official publication at *Notitiae* 35 (1999), 157. That
   later verification does not retroactively attribute access to the Claude sessions. The inserting
   decrees remain outside the later check.
8. The United States complementary norm to canon 1246 §2 was not read at its own source; the
   Conference's page returned an access refusal on 2026-07-25. Its effect is reported from the
   Secretariat's 2026 calendar.
9. No amendment to the Table of Liturgical Days after 1969 was located. That is a bounded negative
   result over the sources consulted, not a proof that none exists.

## Rights and review state

The publication reproduces no prayer, reading, antiphon, chant, preface or formulary. The ICEL
English of the Missal and the English *Lectionary for Mass* are not quoted or paraphrased as
substitutes; where their content matters it is described and cited by locus. The Secretariat of
Divine Worship's annual calendars carry an express reservation of rights and are used only as
factual witnesses to dates, week numbers, cycle labels and territorial decisions. The Latin
normative texts quoted are official acts of the Church quoted for study at named loci; the exact
artifacts through which they were read are registered under `src/sources/works/` with their rights
status, and none of their bytes is redistributed by this publication.

Internal source and computation review is complete as of 2026-07-26: every Latin quotation was read
at the recorded locus in the identified artifact; the passages from the 1981 Order of Readings were
additionally collated against page images rendered at 190-200 dpi; the *Tabella temporaria* was
recomputed row by row; and the three worked years were checked entry by entry against the
Secretariat's calendars.

That work was then audited by a second agent session which redid it rather than accepting it: both
needed artifacts were re-acquired and hashed byte-identical, every published Latin passage from the
Missal artifact was re-read at its locus, and every computation — the twelve-year table, the three
worked years, the 1900-2100 range results and all twenty-four *Tabella temporaria* rows — was
reproduced from an independently written implementation of the norms. Three defects in published
claims were found and corrected; they are itemised in `source-audit.md`. The two-pass build is
warning-free at 39 pages, generation metadata validates, the source library validates, the web
edition converts with both contribution records visible, and every one of the 39 pages was
inspected at full size. Repository-wide metadata and web-edition gates were failing on another
agent's in-flight leaf during this session, so both were run scoped to this leaf.

Independent liturgical, canonical, territorial-law, Latin-philological and specialist review remain
outstanding, as does work-specific rights review. No ecclesiastical approval of any kind is claimed
or implied. No distribution authorization has been requested or granted for this leaf.
