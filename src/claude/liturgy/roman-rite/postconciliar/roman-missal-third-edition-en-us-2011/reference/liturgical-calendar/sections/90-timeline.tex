\section{Dated modification timeline}
\label{sec:timeline}

The following are the dated acts on which the rules stated in this reference depend, through the
cutoff of 25 July 2026. Selection criterion: an act is listed only if it established, amended or
authentically restated a norm of the liturgical year, the table of liturgical days, the composition
of calendars, or the Lectionary cycles, or if it fixed the edition in which such a norm is read for
the dioceses of the United States. Entries are marked \emph{read} where the act's own text was
examined for this reference, and \emph{reported} where it is known only through another source named
in the row.

\begin{actstable}
\textbf{4 December 1963} & \emph{Sacrosanctum Concilium} promulgated. Articles 102--111 establish the
principles of the year's revision; the appended \latin{declaratio de calendario recognoscendo}
addresses the date of Easter and the perpetual calendar. Acta Apostolicae Sedis 56 (1964) 97--138.
\emph{Read} in the Latin Acta and in the Holy See's Latin web presentation.\\
\textbf{14 February 1969} & Paul VI, apostolic letter \latin{motu proprio} \emph{Mysterii Paschalis},
approving the Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year and the new General Roman Calendar, to take
force from 1 January 1970. \emph{Read} in the Latin reprint in the 2002 typical edition.\\
\textbf{3 April 1969} & Paul VI, apostolic constitution \emph{Missale Romanum}, approving the Order
of Readings, as recited in the promulgating decree below. \emph{Reported} from that decree.\\
\textbf{25 May 1969} & Sacred Congregation for Divine Worship, decree protocol 106/69, promulgating
the \latin{Ordo lectionum Missae} with force from 30 November 1969 and naming series~B and series~II
for the first liturgical year. \emph{Read} in the reprint in the 1981 second typical edition.\\
\textbf{1970} & \latin{Missale Romanum}, \latin{editio typica}, carrying the Universal Norms, the
table of liturgical days and the General Roman Calendar in a Missal for the first time.
\emph{Reported}; not examined for this reference.\\
\textbf{24 June 1970} & Sacred Congregation for Divine Worship, instruction \emph{Calendaria
particularia}, on the revision of particular calendars and propers. Acta Apostolicae Sedis 62 (1970)
651--663. \emph{Read} in the Latin Acta.\\
\textbf{1975} & \latin{Missale Romanum}, \latin{editio typica altera}. \emph{Reported}; its Masses
for various needs are noted as a source of new Lectionary indications in the 1981 decree below.\\
\textbf{25 April 1979} & John Paul II, apostolic constitution \emph{Scripturarum thesaurus},
establishing the \latin{Nova Vulgata} as typical for liturgical use, as recited in the 1981 decree.
\emph{Reported} from that decree.\\
\textbf{21 January 1981} & Sacred Congregation for the Sacraments and Divine Worship, decree protocol
CD 240/81, promulgating the \latin{Ordo lectionum Missae}, \latin{editio typica altera}, and
recording that the text of the \latin{Praenotanda} has been enlarged. \emph{Read} in the edition's
front matter.\\
\textbf{25 January 1983} & Code of Canon Law promulgated; canon 1246 lists the days of precept and
empowers a conference of bishops, with the prior approval of the Apostolic See, to abolish some or
transfer them to a Sunday. \emph{Read} in the Holy See's Latin web presentation of Book~IV.\\
\textbf{8 December 1998} & Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments,
notification protocol 2671/98/L: the obligatory memorial of the Immaculate Heart of the Blessed
Virgin Mary becomes optional in years in which it conflicts with another obligatory memorial.
\emph{Reported} from the Secretariat of Divine Worship's calendar for 2026; the Congregation's own
text was not reached.\\
\textbf{2000, 2002} & Decree on the third typical edition of the \latin{Missale Romanum}, and the
publication of that edition, which carries the Universal Norms, the table of liturgical days, the
General Roman Calendar, the \latin{Tabella temporaria} for 2000--2023, and the rubrics of Ordinary
Time used throughout this reference. \emph{Read} in a digital reproduction identified in the source
records.\\
\textbf{2008} & \latin{Missale Romanum}, \latin{editio typica tertia emendata}, the emended reprint
that is the Latin base of the United States English edition. \emph{Reported}; the exact 2008 book was
not examined here.\\
\textbf{2011} & \emph{The Roman Missal, Third Edition} for the dioceses of the United States of
America, based on the 2008 emended reprint. \emph{Reported} from the Secretariat of Divine Worship's
calendar, which names it as the source of its saints' name forms.\\
\textbf{After 2002} & Universal additions and rank changes made to the General Roman Calendar after
the printed 2002 base --- including the obligatory memorial of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of the
Church, inscribed on the Monday after Pentecost --- appear in the current territorial calendars.
\emph{Reported} from the Secretariat's calendars; the individual decrees were not reached for this
reference, and no claim is made here about their dates, protocol numbers or complete enumeration.\\
\textbf{Annually} & \emph{Liturgical Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America},
published by the Secretariat of Divine Worship. Editions for 2026, 2027 and 2028 were \emph{read} and
are the territorial control for Section~\ref{sec:years}. An annual calendar witnesses a year's resolution; it does
not promulgate the recurring calendar.\\
\end{actstable}

Two absences in this timeline are themselves results and are recorded as such. No act was found that
amends the table of liturgical days after 1969; every provision quoted in Section~\ref{sec:table} is read in the
2002 printing of a text promulgated in 1969, and no amendment to it was located, which is a bounded
negative result and not a proof that none exists. And no act was found that states the ecclesiastical
computation of Easter; the search for one is described in the terminal appendix.
