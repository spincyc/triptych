\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,topsep=0.05em]
\item \latin{Missale Romanum}, editio typica tertia (Civitas Vaticana: Typis Vaticanis, 2002),
consulted in the registered secondary digital reproduction (restricted; SHA-256
\texttt{0b458944\ldots}; 828 pages). Re-acquired publication-locally on 2026-07-25 and found
byte-identical to the registered artifact. Pages inspected for this study: the front matter
carrying the Latin constitution \latin{Missale Romanum} and the decrees; the \latin{Institutio
Generalis} entire; and the \latin{Ordo Missae} nn.\ 1--146 with the following solemn blessings.
Not the printed altar book; claims are bounded to the inspected pages and to unambiguous
readings.
\item \latin{Missale Romanum}, editio typica tertia, \latin{reimpressio emendata} (Civitas
Vaticana, 2008). No exact artifact obtained; the 2008 state is controlled by the two \emph{Notitiae}
records below.
\item \latin{Missale Romanum}, editio typica (Civitas Vaticana: Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis,
1962), consulted in the registered facsimile (remote; SHA-256 \texttt{648fdb8f\ldots}). Canon
pages read at rendered page images on 2026-07-25; the chalice narrative and consecratory form at
printed p.\ 307 (marginal nn.\ 1103--1105) newly registered as a verified passage.
\item \latin{Institutio Generalis Missalis Romani}, Latin text of the third typical edition, in
the same reproduction.
\item \emph{General Instruction of the Roman Missal}, United States English state emended
through 2021, Chapter II (nn.\ 27--90),
\sourceurl{https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/the-mass/general-instruction-of-the-roman-missal/girm-chapter-2}{usccb.org},
read 2026-07-25 (new source record). Chapters III and IV were not obtainable from the same host
during this review; no claim rests on them.
\item \emph{The Roman Missal, Third Edition, for use in the Dioceses of the United States of
America} (2011). Under copyright; not reproduced. Cited by locus and described only.
\item \latin{Ordo lectionum Missae}, editio typica altera (1981). Identified as the Missal's own
authority at \latin{IGMR} 56, 57, and 61; not examined for this study, and its text is nowhere
reproduced.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection*{Ancient and patristic witnesses}

{\fontsize{8.6}{9.6}\selectfont
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,topsep=0.05em]
\item Justin Martyr, \emph{First Apology} 65--67, in \emph{The Ante-Nicene Fathers}, vol.\ 1
(Buffalo: Christian Literature Publishing, 1887), Dods--Reith translation; registered edition
with facsimile and OCR witnesses. Chapter 67 used for the second-century Roman sequence.
\item The church order conventionally called \latin{Traditio apostolica}, Latin of the Verona
palimpsest, in Edmund Hauler, \emph{Didascaliae apostolorum fragmenta Veronensia Latina}
(Leipzig: Teubner, 1900), pp.\ 106--107; page images read 2026-07-25 (new source records:
work, edition, two artifacts, two verified passages). Authorship, date, place, and redactional
unity of the underlying work are disputed; no attribution to Hippolytus is made here.
\item Ambrose of Milan (or the author of \latin{De sacramentis}), \latin{De sacramentis} IV, 5,
21--23 and IV, 6, 26--27, in \emph{Patrologiae cursus completus, series Latina}, tomus XVI
(Parisiis: apud Garnier Fratres et J.-P. Migne successores, 1880), coll.\ 462--464; page images
read 2026-07-25 (new source records: work, edition, two artifacts, two verified passages). The
Maurist apparatus on those columns records the variant readings noted in this study.
\end{itemize}}
