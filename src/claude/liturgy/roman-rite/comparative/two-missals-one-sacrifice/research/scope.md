# Two Missals, One Sacrifice — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; Roman Rite comparative liturgy,
  `liturgy/roman-rite/comparative/`.
- **Governing profiles.** `guidance/articles.md` governs the argument, its
  epistemic order, its authority calibration and its terminal apparatus; the
  work is a discursive study with a stated judgement. `guidance/liturgy/ordinary-expositions.md`
  governs every direct comparison of liturgical editions, texts and rubrics, and
  supplies the required records (`scope.md`, `edition-manifest.md`,
  `sequence-inventory.md`, `source-audit.md`). Canon-law-adjacent claims — the
  discipline of the 1962 Missal — follow the mixed-article canon-law rules in
  `guidance/articles.md`, including the `Legal Scope and Currentness` block.
  Neither propers profile's template is imposed: this is not a proper guide, and
  it has no page-1 map, no page-2 date/location sheet and no four-senses table.
- **Series relationship.** An independent Claude edition of the leaf
  `liturgy/roman-rite/comparative/two-missals-one-sacrifice`. It shares the leaf
  and subject territory with the GPT edition but is an independent work:
  research, argument order, section structure, examples, comparison tables and
  prose were produced without reading the GPT edition's sections, main file or
  PDF; only that edition's `research/scope.md` was consulted, for topic parity.
  The Claude edition deliberately narrows the shared territory. It does not
  contain a community census, a seven-sacrament validity matrix, a Eucharistic
  discipline survey, a pastoral decision framework, a pre-Tridentine history, or
  an orientation timeline. Its distinctive contribution is the locus-by-locus
  textual comparison the GPT edition's scope record does not undertake at this
  depth: a complete Order of Mass inventory classified as retained, altered,
  dropped or newly composed; the offertory sequences set out entire; an
  enumeration of the Canon's gestural rubrics; the preface counts; and the
  falsifiable synthesis.
- **Sibling de-confliction within this provider.** The Claude article
  `articles/faith/council-missal-and-crisis` treats the conciliar mandate, the
  reform's execution and the postconciliar crisis narrative, and expressly
  excludes prayer-by-prayer Missal comparison. The present study is the
  complement: it does the textual comparison and declines the causal and
  statistical questions. Neither reopens the other's territory.
- **Intended reader.** A serious reader, Catholic or not, who wants to know what
  the two books contain and what the acts governing them legislate.
- **Governing question.** At the loci where each matter is actually settled, what
  does each Missal say; what do the promulgating and governing acts actually
  legislate; and which claims commonly made about the two books do not survive
  contact with them?
- **As-of date.** 2026-07-25 for the discipline; the two books' contents are not
  date-sensitive in the same way.

## Argument order

1. **Two books on one desk.** The two objects identified exactly; the three
   working rules (a locus or nothing; keep demonstrable and contested apart; take
   each side at its strongest); the two disclaimers about what a textual
   comparison cannot reach.
2. **What the governing acts legislate.** *Quo primum* in four parts with its
   two-hundred-year exception and the addressees of its shielding clause; the
   internal refutation supplied by Clement VIII and Urban VIII in the same book;
   *Missale Romanum* (1969) and the three typical-edition decrees; *Summorum
   Pontificum*; *Universae Ecclesiae* 24–26 and 28; *Traditionis custodes* with
   two precisions; the *Responsa* and the 2023 rescript.
3. **The Order of Mass.** The complete inventory in a three-column table; four
   observations from it; the newer book's structure of options; and the
   correction that the 1962 book is itself a reform product (general rubrics 64,
   503, 513(f), 523).
4. **The offertory.** Both sequences entire; the strong accusation fails, the
   weak one succeeds; the water-and-wine prayer's partial survival; the *Lavabo*
   substitution; the audibility hinge.
5. **The Roman Canon and the three added Prayers.** What is identical; the three
   classes of difference; the *De defectibus* finding on *mysterium fidei*; the
   gestural count; the brackets; the prefaces; the four Prayers' shapes; and the
   correction that the new Prayers did not remove propitiation.
6. **The lectionary.** Plenary Missal against Missal-plus-Lectionary; the two
   provisions; two corrections and one concession from *Praenotanda* 76–77; what
   the comparison cannot settle; what *Traditionis custodes* 3 §3 has done to the
   older provision.
7. **Calendar and ranks.** The two incommensurable schemes; what each says about
   itself; where the newer is more conservative; three real losses.
8. **The priest.** What the postconciliar books state in terms; what the 1962
   rubrics imply; three genuine differences; ministries; concelebration; and the
   orientation finding.
9. **Language.** Trent, *Quo primum*, the 1962 book's own vernacular provision,
   *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 36, the three postconciliar loci, and the reversal in
   the current discipline; two failing claims, one on each side.
10. **Synthesis.** Both cases at full strength in official and papal
    formulations; the labelled `Project synthesis` block with judgement,
    counterargument at full strength and falsifying test; three refusals; a
    closing observation on the shared phrase.

## Claim classification

- **Verified text.** Wording checked at an identified witness and locus. All
  Latin quotations. Collation method (page image or text layer) stated in each
  endnote.
- **Checked description.** A summary of what a locus says, verifiable there.
  Most of the comparison tables.
- **Source-grounded synthesis.** The four observations in section 3; the four
  findings in section 4; the three classes of difference in section 5; the
  reversal described in section 9. Each is a conclusion from checked evidence and
  is presented as such.
- **Project judgement.** Confined to the `Project synthesis` block in section 10
  and to the passages that explicitly announce a judgement (the studybox in
  section 7 and the closing refusals). Attributed to no source.
- **Not made.** No claim about the merits of either book; no causal claim about
  the postconciliar period; no claim about the interior dispositions or canonical
  situation of any person; no adjudication of the disagreement between the 2007
  and 2021 frameworks.

## Bounded negative results (correctable, publication-local)

1. The complete text of *Quo primum*, read at artifact pp. 3–4 of the registered
   1962 facsimile, contains no norm about the language of the Mass.
2. Trent session XXII chapter VIII and canon IX, read at leaves n127 and n128 of
   the registered Tauchnitz page images, do not define Latin as required for the
   Mass; the chapter states an inexpediency and the canon condemns an exclusive
   claim.
3. No proposition of Trent session XXII's doctrine on the sacrifice of the Mass
   was found to lack a locus in the 2002 book. Tested against the four
   Eucharistic Prayers, the *Institutio generalis* Proœmium, and the loci named
   in the synthesis. Bounded to those loci.
4. The Dicastery for Divine Worship document archive, read 2026-07-25, lists no
   act later than the 20 February 2023 rescript.

## Quantitative findings and their ceilings

- **Signs of the cross in the 1962 Canon: 25 over the offerings, at 8 places.**
  Enumerated from the printed gestural rubrics; reproducible. The priest's sign
  upon himself in *Supplices* is excluded and named separately. The postconciliar
  Canon prescribes one.
- **Prefaces: 15 plus the preface of the dead in the 1962 Order of Mass; 50 in
  the corresponding section of the 2002 Order of Mass.** Counted by heading. The
  2002 figure is a floor: prefaces printed with particular formularies elsewhere
  in the book are additional.
- **Old Testament lesson incipits in the 1962 Missal: approximately 183, against
  approximately 206 Pauline.** Counted programmatically over the facsimile's OCR
  text layer by matching printed incipit formulas. OCR error and repeated
  formularies make the figures approximate. Component tallies are recorded in
  `source-audit.md`. The count establishes presence and rough proportion only; no
  argument turns on the exact numbers.

## Excluded scope

Not a merit verdict, a ceremonial manual, a parallel Missal, a history of the
reform's execution, a study of the statistical record, or an authorisation to
celebrate with either book. Excluded: the Requiem Mass and the *Dies irae*; the
Holy Week rites and the 1955 reform of them; the Divine Office; the other
sacraments and their rituals except as the *Responsa* touch them; vernacular
translation history in any language; music beyond the two books' own norms; the
question of who may serve at the altar; the 1969 *Institutio generalis* and its
article 7; the 2008 *reimpressio emendata*; Eastern Catholic liturgy and law;
other Latin uses and their books.

## Rights boundary

Latin *editiones typicae* are quoted at the loci the argument requires. The ICEL
English of the Roman Missal and the Lectionary pericopes are never reproduced.
The Lectionary's *Praenotanda* are quoted in Latin from the typical edition and
the copyrighted approved English translation of them is not quoted. Holy See
portal texts are quoted briefly with attribution and their restrictive legal
notes are recorded in the artifact records. Restricted artifacts — the 2002
reproduction and the 1981 Lectionary scan — are not retained. Public-domain
English for the 1962 formularies is registered in the source library and was not
needed, the comparisons being made in Latin. No text is offered for recitation
and no translation is composed; English renderings of Latin are labelled working
glosses.
