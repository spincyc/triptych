## Claim families

### 1. What the governing acts legislate

Controlled by the acts themselves. *Quo primum* and the briefs of Clement VIII
and Urban VIII were read in the 1962 Missal's front matter, the first two at
rendered page images. The publication's four-part analysis of *Quo primum*
(commission, imposition, faculty, shielding clause), its two-hundred-year
exception, and the identification of the shielding clause's addressees are all
readings of the printed sentence and can be checked at artifact pages 3–4.

The claim that *Quo primum* was understood by later popes as alterable rests on
two witnesses printed in the same book — Clement VIII's `Qui etsi multis
propositis poenis severissime caverit … tamen, progressu temporis` and Urban
VIII's account of restoring degenerated rubrics — and is corroborated by the
preamble to *Summorum Pontificum*, which names both popes in a list of those who
updated the books. No secondary literature is used for the point.

The postconciliar acts are quoted from official English deliveries whose bytes
matched the registered artifacts. The publication's two precisions about
*Traditionis custodes* — that art. 1 does not state an abrogation of the 1962
Missal, and that art. 3 §3 requires vernacular readings where *Summorum
Pontificum* art. 6 permitted them — are readings of the printed articles and are
verifiable at those articles.

### 2. The Order of Mass inventory

Built movement by movement from the two printed Orders and recorded in
`sequence-inventory.md`. Classification of a movement as retained, altered,
dropped or newly composed is a textual observation, not a judgement.

Load-bearing Latin was collated at page images for the 1962 *Confiteor*
(artifact p. 297), the offertory prayers (artifact pp. 301–302) and the
institution narrative (artifact pp. 387–388). The 2002 wording rests on the
registered digital reproduction only; that ceiling is stated in the publication.

### 3. The 1962 book as a reformed book

Four provisions are cited: *Rubricae generales* 64 (octaves reduced to three),
503 (the *Confiteor* before the faithful's Communion suppressed within Mass),
513(f) (the celebrant omits what sacred ministers or a lector proclaim) and 523
(the celebrant may sit during *Kyrie*, *Gloria*, sequence and *Credo*). Number
513 was collated at a rendered page image; the others at the text layer. The
corroborating rubric in *Ritus servandus* VI, 4 (`quam celebrans sedens
auscultat`) was read at the text layer.

### 4. The Roman Canon

The collation of 1962 against 2002 was made prayer by prayer. The count of
twenty-five signs of the cross over the offerings was made by enumerating the
1962 Canon's own gestural rubrics at eight places: *Te igitur* 3, *Quam
oblationem* 5, the two institution narratives 1 each, *Unde et memores* 5,
*Supplices* 2, *Per quem haec omnia* 3, the doxology 5. The priest's sign upon
himself in *Supplices* is excluded from the count and named separately. The
count is reproducible from the printed rubrics.

The *mysterium fidei* finding — that the 1962 book's own *De defectibus* V
includes the phrase in what it calls the form of the sacrament — was collated at
a rendered page image of artifact p. 69. The accompanying distinction between a
private subtraction and a determination by competent authority rests on the same
paragraph's own clause and on Trent session XXI chapter II, read at leaf n115.

### 5. The lectionary

Structural claims rest on *Praenotanda* 65–69 and the two books' own contents.
The two corrections and the one concession in the publication rest on
*Praenotanda* 76 and 77, read visually at printed p. XXXV of the Latin typical
edition. The copyrighted approved English translation was consulted to locate
the articles and check sense and is not quoted.

The Old Testament distribution claim is supported by a programmatic count over
the 1962 facsimile's OCR text layer, matching the printed incipit formulas. The
tallies were: *Lectio Epistolae beati Pauli Apostoli* about 206; Old Testament
incipits about 183 in total, of which the sapiential books supply roughly 90,
Isaias 27, and Kings, Jeremias, Daniel, Ezechiel, Leviticus, Exodus,
Deuteronomy, Machabees, Genesis, Judith, Esther, Job, Numbers, Esdras and Tobias
the remainder; *Lectio Actuum Apostolorum* 24 and *Lectio libri Apocalypsis* 12
are counted separately as New Testament. OCR error and repeated formularies make
these approximate, and the publication states the figures as approximate. The
count establishes presence and rough proportion; it is not an edition statistic
and no argument turns on the exact numbers.

### 6. Calendar and ranks

The 1962 scheme is taken from *Rubricae generales* 8–18 and 63–70. The
postconciliar scheme is taken from the *Normae universales* 3–35 and the table
of liturgical days, and from *Institutio generalis* 394. The observation that
the third typical edition's own apparatus cites `Missale Romanum [ed. typ.
1962]` for two collects rests on the notes to *Mysterii paschalis* at artifact
p. 63.

### 7. The priest and the ministries

Postconciliar claims rest on *Institutio generalis* 4, 27, 30–33, 91, 93, 107,
199, 254 and 299. The 1962 claims rest on *Ritus servandus* I, III, V–VII and
*Rubricae generales* 511–514. The orientation finding — that neither book
legislates the direction of celebration as a general rule, that *Ritus
servandus* V, 3 contemplates an altar *ad orientem, versus populum*, and that
*Institutio generalis* 299 legislates about the altar — is a reading of those two
loci and nothing else.

### 8. Language

Trent session XXII chapter VIII and canon IX were read visually at leaves n127
and n128. *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 36 was quoted from the registered official
English delivery. The observation that *Institutio generalis* 11–12 quotes both
Trent passages and reads them as prudential is a reading of those two articles.
The 1962 vernacular provision at *Rubricae generales* 84–85 was read at the text
layer.

## Bounded negative results

- **No language provision in *Quo primum*.** The complete bull was read at
  artifact pp. 3–4; it contains no norm about the language of the Mass. The
  result is bounded to that text.
- **No Trent definition of Latin.** Session XXII chapter VIII expresses a
  judgement of inexpediency and canon IX condemns an exclusive claim; neither
  defines Latin as required. Bounded to the two loci read.
- **No proposition of Trent session XXII found absent from the 2002 book.** The
  falsifiable test stated in the publication's synthesis was run against the four
  Eucharistic Prayers, the *Institutio generalis* Proœmium, and the loci named
  there. Every proposition tested has a locus. The search is bounded to those
  loci and is correctable; it is not a claim of universal negation.
- **No universal act later than the 2023 rescript located.** The Dicastery for
  Divine Worship archive index was read on 2026-07-25 and lists the rescript as
  its most recent item. Bounded to the collections consulted.

## Material leads not used

- The 1969 first edition of the *Institutio generalis* and the 1970 revision of
  its article 7. No primary witness was examined, so the publication says
  nothing about it and uses the 2002 text throughout.
- The 2008 *reimpressio emendata* of the third typical edition. Registered
  through the *Notitiae* variation records; not collated here. Any point at
  which 2008 differs from 2002 is outside this study.
- Principal non-official critical literature on the reform (works by identified
  authors rather than acts of authority). Not examined in an identified edition;
  the critical case is therefore presented in official and papal formulations
  only, and the publication records that as a limitation on its synthesis.
- The question of who may serve at the altar, on which the 1962 *De defectibus*
  X and the current law both speak. The current law was not researched, so the
  point is omitted rather than half-treated.
- The Requiem Mass and the *Dies irae*. A genuine locus for the propitiatory
  question, deliberately outside this study's scope.

## Rights audit

- The Latin *editiones typicae* of both Missals are quoted at the loci the
  argument requires. The 2002 witness is a restricted secondary reproduction;
  its bytes are not retained.
- The approved English of the postconciliar Missal and the Lectionary pericopes
  are nowhere reproduced.
- The Lectionary's *Praenotanda* are quoted in Latin from the typical edition;
  the copyrighted English translation is not quoted.
- Holy See portal texts are quoted briefly with attribution; the portal's
  restrictive legal notes are recorded in each artifact record.
- Trent is quoted from a pre-1930 printing whose page images carry a
  public-domain rights basis for the United States.
- No text is offered for vocal prayer or recitation, and no translation is
  composed by this study; English renderings of Latin are labelled working
  glosses or third-person description.

## Outstanding

Canonical fingerprints were computed on 2026-07-26, once the shared source
library validated, and the bindings now carry reviewed states on the exact
hashed witnesses: the CMAA facsimile, the 2002 reproduction and its *Institutio
generalis* segment, the three Trent leaf images, the Latin and English
*Ordo lectionum* files, and the *Sacrosanctum Concilium* delivery, together with
the fourteen registered passages. Edition-level bindings remain catalog-level
by design, since an edition identity is not an exact witness.

`verified` is claimed only where a page image was rendered and read visually or
an authoritative delivery was re-fetched and compared byte for byte. It is
deliberately not claimed for anything reached only through the secondary digital
reproduction of the 2002 third typical edition. Collating the 2002 wording
against a printed altar book or a page-image facsimile remains the study's
principal outstanding source obligation.

Independent liturgical, historical, canonical and theological review remains
outstanding. Internal checking is not independent review.
