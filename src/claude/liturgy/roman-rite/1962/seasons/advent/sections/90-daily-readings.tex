\section{Daily Readings for Advent}\label{sec:daily}

These tables give a reading for each day of Advent in any year. The first is keyed by week and
weekday, from the First Sunday to the Friday of the fourth week; the second is keyed by date, from
17 to 24 December. Between them they give, for each day, the lesson from Isaiah that the Breviary
reads at Matins, named by its chapter and opening words; a short passage from the Fathers; a word
on the day's theme; the meditation St Alphonsus de Liguori assigns to the day in \textit{The
Incarnation, Birth and Infancy of Jesus Christ}; and, from 17 December, the great O antiphon of
the evening.

A quarter of an hour is enough to use them. Read the chapter of Isaiah in a Bible, or as much of it
as time allows; read the passage from the Fathers twice; and, if you have Alphonsus's book, read his
meditation. On a day that has no Isaiah, read the Gospel of the day's Mass instead. The tables are
counsel for private reading.

\medskip\noindent\textit{Which row to use.} Up to 16 December, use the first table alone. From 17
December the O antiphon and Alphonsus's novena go by date, in the second table, every day; the
Isaiah lesson still goes by week and weekday, in the first table, on every day that has one; and
the reading from the Fathers comes from the first table on a Sunday or an Ember day and from the
second on any other day. The Vigil takes everything from the second table, also when it falls on a
Sunday. Alphonsus begins his novena on 16 December, whatever its weekday: on that day read his
first novena meditation, ``God has given us his only Son to save us'', in place of the meditation
the first table gives.

\medskip\noindent\textit{The Office.} On Sundays the 1962 Office reads two lessons of Isaiah and
then the opening of St Gregory's homily on the Gospel (nn.~162\,a, 220). On the Ember days and the
Vigil all three lessons are of a homily on the day's Gospel, and Matins reads no Isaiah
(n.~222\,a). The feasts of the saints are not shown, because their weekdays change with the year.
On a feast of the third class the first two lessons are still those of the occurring Scripture
(nn.~169, 221\,a). When a feast of higher rank impedes the day's Isaiah, the lesson is omitted, not
moved to another day (n.~213); so it is in every year on 8 December, the Immaculate Conception,
which is of the first class. The chapters are those the Breviary of 1888 assigns to each week and
weekday.

\medskip\noindent\textit{Shorter and longer years.} The First Sunday falls between 27 November and
3 December (n.~20), so Advent has from twenty-two to twenty-eight days before Christmas. In the
shortest year the First Sunday is 3 December; 17 December is then the Third Sunday, and the Fourth
Sunday falls on 24 December, where the Vigil takes its place (n.~30). In the longest
year the First Sunday is 27 November; the Ember Wednesday falls on 14 December and the Thursday of
the third week on 15 December, a day for which Alphonsus has no meditation, and the fourth week has
weekdays from Monday 19 to Friday 23 December, which take their Isaiah from the last rows of the
first table. Alphonsus labels his eighteen meditations by week and weekday, from the First Sunday
to the Third Wednesday; since his novena begins on 16 December, the last of them are not reached
in most years.

\medskip\noindent\textit{Worked year: Advent 2026.} The First Sunday falls on 29 November, and the
season has twenty-six days. Week I runs from 29 November to 5 December and week II from 6 to 12
December; the Immaculate Conception falls on the Tuesday of week II, so the Isaiah of that day,
chapter 14, is not read. The Third Sunday is 13 December, and the Ember days fall on 16, 18 and 19
December. The Fourth Sunday is 20 December, and O clavis David is sung at its Vespers; the other
six O antiphons fall on weekdays. The fourth week has weekdays from Monday 21 to Wednesday 23
December, and the Vigil falls on Thursday 24 December. Alphonsus's novena begins on the Ember
Wednesday, so his eighteenth meditation is not reached.
