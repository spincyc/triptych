\section{Daily Readings for Advent}\label{sec:daily}

All through Advent the Church reads the prophet Isaiah at Matins, the Office of the night, a
chapter or part of one each day. The tables give that chapter for every day of the season, with a
passage from the Fathers and a verse of Scripture.

A quarter of an hour is enough. Read the chapter slowly in your Bible, or as much of it as you have
time for. Read the Father's words twice. Keep the verse with you through the day, and say it again
before you sleep.

To find the day:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Isaiah} is always found by the week, in the first table.
\item \textit{Until 16 December} the first table has everything: Isaiah, the Father and the verse.
\item \textit{From 17 December} the second table goes by the date. It has the great antiphon sung
that evening at Vespers before and after Our Lady's song, the Magnificat, with the verse of
Scripture the antiphon takes up and a passage from the Fathers. Sundays and Ember Days keep their
own row in the first table and take only the antiphon from the date; on the Ember Days and on
Christmas Eve, when Matins has no Isaiah, read the Gospel of the day's Mass.
\end{itemize}

\noindent So if 18 December is a Thursday, read that Thursday's Isaiah in the first table, and the
antiphon \latin{O Adonai}, its verse and the Father in the second. If it is the Ember Friday, read
the Ember Friday's row and take only the antiphon from the date.

St Alphonsus Liguori's novena for Christmas, in his \textit{The Incarnation, Birth and Infancy of
Jesus Christ}, begins on 16 December.

\subsection{From year to year}

The First Sunday of Advent falls between 27 November and 3 December, so the season before Christmas
lasts from twenty-two to twenty-eight days, and the two tables meet at a different point each year.
In the shortest Advent the First Sunday is 3 December: the Third Sunday is then 17 December, the
Ember Days fall on 20, 22 and 23 December, and the Fourth Sunday falls on Christmas Eve, which takes
its place; use the row for 24 December. In the longest the First Sunday is 27 November: the Ember
Wednesday is then 14 December, and the fourth week has all its weekdays, from Monday 19 to Friday 23
December. In every year the Ember Days are the Wednesday, Friday and Saturday after the Third
Sunday.

When a greater feast falls on a day of Advent, Matins is of the feast and that day's chapter of
Isaiah is not read in the Office, nor moved to another day. The Immaculate Conception, on 8
December, is such a feast every year, and it is kept even when it falls on a Sunday. On the lesser
saints' days the Isaiah is still read. At home, the chapter may be read on any day.

In 2026 the First Sunday is 29 November, and Advent has twenty-six days. The Immaculate Conception
falls on the Tuesday of the second week, so chapter 14 is not read at Matins. The Third Sunday is 13
December and the Ember Days are 16, 18 and 19 December. On Thursday 17 December, then, read Isaiah
33 from the first table and take the rest from the date; on the Ember Friday and Saturday and on
the Fourth Sunday, 20 December, keep to the first table and take the evening antiphon from the
date. Christmas Eve falls on a Thursday.
