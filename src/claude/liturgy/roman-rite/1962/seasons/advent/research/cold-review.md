# Cold review

Two independent reviewers read the cold-written draft on 2026-11-18. Neither
had seen the drafting; each read only the series profile, the editorial
standard, the plan, the draft and the evidence records. Their findings were
dispositioned by the coordinating session and applied in one fix pass.

## Lane Q: quotations, attributions, claims and law

Verdict: revise. Checked as sound: 253 quotations matched to their bytes by
script and about 60 by hand (all of Bernard, Cyril, Augustine, Leo, the
Catena, Bute, Trent, de Sales, the USCCB statement, the councils and the
medieval Latin); the seven prayer blocks of Appendix B byte-exact, each from
a single witness; the law passages against the law table, with the 1917 and
present law kept apart; all 26 dates of the worked year recomputed; 48
entries of Appendix D against their records.

Verdict: accept with corrections. Checked as sound: order and headings;
all 41 cross-references; appendices lettered A–D; body length; tables

## Fix pass

Every accepted finding was applied in one pass on 2026-11-18 by a fresh
session working only from the dispositions, the review reports and the
evidence records. A script compared every block and inline quotation with
its source bytes after the pass, and all matched (allowing TeX quotation
marks, V./R. and Bute's large initials); two punctuation slips it caught
were corrected. The body went from 17,646 to 16,650 words.

The pass added: the 1983 Code cc. 1246–1247 as the cited law of Sunday and
holy-day Mass (rows `s13-sunday-holy-day-*`); Jerome on Matthew 1:19, as the
Catena gives it, for the Vigil (row `s09-jerome-1-19`); rows `sA-n-213` and
`sA-n-221-a` for the daily table's rules; thirteen whole-verse quotations of
the Douay-Rheims (Challoner); and an Appendix A in two tables, by week and
weekday and then by date from 17 December, with 2026 as a worked year.

Left as the records leave them: no held 1962 record gives the class of St
Andrew or St Thomas, so the daily table gives no saints' classes; the 1960
rubrics' treatment of an O antiphon on a feast is not stated, so the Vigil
section cites only n. 109's privileged commemoration; that the 1962
Breviary's Vigil homily is Jerome's rests on the 1888 Breviary and
n. 222 a, and the text says so.
