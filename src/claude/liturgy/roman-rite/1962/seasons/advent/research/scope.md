## Reader and purpose

A Catholic who assists at the 1962 Mass, or wants to, and means to keep Advent
well. The reader may be new to the older calendar. The primer prepares the
reader before the season begins and accompanies the reader through it: what
the season is and where it came from, what each Sunday and the Ember Days
show, what the Fathers teach about the Lord's coming, and how to use the four
weeks to renew prayer, fasting, almsgiving and confession.

It is deeply scriptural and patristic: the Fathers, Doctors and the liturgy
itself carry the argument, quoted at identified loci from held witnesses
(`guidance/editorial.md`, "Speaking from within the tradition").

## Boundary with the proper guides

The proper guides for rows `01`–`06` (planned) own each formulary: its full
texts, component-by-component exegesis, four senses, chronology and homily.
The primer owns the season. For each Sunday it gives the station, the
governing text or two (incipit-level), the Sunday's place in the season's
movement, and one patristic reading of it, and stops there. It prints no
complete proper and no component inventory. The Four Last Things essays
(`library/faith.md`) are cross-referenced where the season's old preaching
custom is discussed, not restated.

## Law and custom

Under the 1962-horizon law (1917 *Codex Iuris Canonici*), Advent itself carried
no general fast: the Advent Ember Days and the Vigil of the Nativity did.
Current law (1983 Code, with any conference norms) appoints no Advent fast.
The primer states each discipline with its body of law, territory and date,
and presents the older Advent fast (St Martin's Lent; the Eastern Nativity
Fast) as history and as a voluntary practice, never as an obligation. Every
practical proposal is labelled as the primer's own counsel. All of these
facts are to be verified in phases 2–3; none is settled by this plan.

## Cold-review fixes (2026-11-18)

Two cold reviews (lanes V and Q) were dispositioned by the coordinator and
applied to the held leaf. In summary:

- **Appendix A** rebuilt as above. The O-antiphon count corrected: in 2026
  O clavis David falls on the Fourth Sunday and six antiphons on weekdays.
- **Law.** The Mass cell of the rule of life now cites cc. 1246-1247 (1983
  Code, Latin Church, current 2026); Section 5 states the universal canons
  and leaves each conference's holy-day calendar unstated; the scope
  appendix's exclusion was narrowed to match. Ages are given as "completed";
  every present-law "now" is scoped by body of law and territory.
- **Votive Masses.** The Rorate custom (no witness) is dropped; Section 11
  states nn. 384 and 387 and leaves n. 384 for an Advent feria unsettled.
- **Section 9** carries Jerome's Vigil homily (as the Catena gives it; BR88
  p. 174 for the Breviary's use) and ends with counsel; its repetitions of
  Sections 1 and 3 were cut.
- **Scripture.** Key verses are quoted whole from the Douay-Rheims
  (Challoner) verse texts instead of paraphrased.
- **Latin collects** are captioned as Triptych's editorial projection, named
  in Appendix D, and its normalisations are listed in Appendix C.
- **Voice.** Repeated quotations, maxim closers, credited inferences and the
  book-as-subject restraint sentences were cut; each repeated point is kept
  once and cross-referenced.
- **Apparatus.** Edition dates and imprints follow the edition records
  (Buffalo 1886; [1921]; 2nd ed., copyright 1886; Waterworth's undated
  reprint; the 1958 instruction without a day of the month); the omitted
  Appendix D entries were added.
