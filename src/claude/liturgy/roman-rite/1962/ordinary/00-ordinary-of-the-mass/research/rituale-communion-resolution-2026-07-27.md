# Rituale communion formula resolution

## Question

The sequence inventory says that Missal rubric n. 502 refers specified
circumstances to the *Rituale Romanum*, “whose formula this study does not
reproduce.” This note determines what that reference reaches and how far its
provenance can safely be stated.

## Direct findings

The controlling 1962 Missal remains the authority for the study:

- n. 502 concerns Communion distributed immediately before or after Mass and
  refers the manner of distribution to the *Rituale Romanum*;
- n. 503 separately prescribes Communion within Mass: after the celebrant's
  Communion, `Ecce Agnus Dei` and the threefold `Domine, non sum dignus` are
  said, but the confession and absolution are omitted; and
- the cue after n. 1129 locates Communion of the faithful after the celebrant's
  reception of the chalice.

The 1925 typical *Rituale Romanum*, title IV, chapter 2, supplies the historical
procedure behind that cross-reference. Number 3 reads:

> `Ecce Agnus Dei, ecce qui tollit peccata mundi.`
>
> `Domine, non sum dignus, ut intres sub tectum meum, sed tantum dic verbo, et
> sanabitur anima mea`, repeated a second and third time.

The same chapter distinguishes three settings. Numbers 1–10 govern Communion
outside Mass. Number 12 adapts that rite within Mass and still directs the
minister to say the `Confiteor` and the priest to say `Misereatur vestri`.
Number 13 directs that Communion immediately before or after a private Mass
follow the outside-Mass manner, with stated omissions.

Thus “formula” is too imprecise if it suggests that n. 502 merely imports
`Ecce Agnus Dei` and `Domine, non sum dignus`. The cross-reference imports a
ritual manner for the before-or-after-Mass cases. For Communion within Mass,
n. 503 itself names the retained formulas and expressly removes the confession
and absolution found in the older Ritual procedure.

## Edition and provenance limit

The exact title and locus checked here are *Rituale Romanum Pauli V Pontificis
Maximi jussu editum, aliorumque Pontificum cura recognitum atque auctoritate
Ssmi D.N. Pii Papae XI ad normam Codicis Juris Canonici accommodatum*, typical
edition of 1925, title IV, chapter 2, nn. 1–13. The inspected artifact is a
2007 electronic resetting, PDF pp. 68–70, not a facsimile.

The source library also catalogs the 1952 typical edition, but no exact 1952
artifact is registered. Therefore this audit does **not** claim direct
verification of the 1952 wording, nor does it treat the 1925 resetting as a
witness to 1952. The 1925 evidence establishes an earlier form of the
procedure; the 1962 Missal establishes the later rule that controls this
publication. Any claim about continuity of exact wording from 1925 through
1952 requires a direct 1952 witness.

## Rights and source accuracy

The underlying 1925 Latin official text is public domain in the United States
by publication date. The exact 2007 electronic resetting asserts copyright,
so its bytes remain untracked and restricted. The displayed pages and embedded
text agree at the cited locus, but a modern resetting can contain
transcription errors; it supports the bounded historical comparison above,
not typography, punctuation history, or an edition-wide negative claim.

## Disposition

Preserve the study and its controlling 1962 claim. At the next content-edit
stage, replace “whose formula this study does not reproduce” with language
that distinguishes the imported before-or-after-Mass procedure from n. 503's
in-Mass prescription. No section text was changed in this research-only pass.
