# Second-Pass Book-History and Canon Review — 2026-07-27

This review tests four narrow questions against the current publication and its
bound evidence. It does not alter the publication and is not an independent
specialist approval.

## 1. Typography and the physical book

### Exact local control

- The controlling 1962 Vatican facsimile, `Canon Missae`, printed pp. 299–311
  (artifact PDF pp. 380–392; marginal nn. 1088–1112), directly establishes
  only what this artifact prints: a separate Canon gathering, Crucifixion
  plate, red rubrics, enlarged consecratory forms, and the displayed hierarchy
  described in the article.
- The consecratory forms are controlled more narrowly at artifact PDF
  pp. 387–388 (printed pp. 306–307; nn. 1101–1105).
- The 1861 Cummiskey hand missal, printed pp. xxxix–xlii (artifact pp. 50–53),
  is a second identified printing. It supports comparison of its own display,
  not a claim about every nineteenth-century or 1962 altar missal.

### Book-history lead

D. B. Updike, “Some Notes on Liturgical Printing,” states that manuscript and
printed missals commonly set the Canon in substantially larger type, and
connects the typographic size-name “canon” with that practice. This is useful
book-history evidence that visual hierarchy predates the collated 1962
artifact. It does not establish sacramental causality, the date of any
particular convention, or uniformity among all 1962 impressions.

The article's present boundary is therefore correct: typography can identify
the book's practical and visual emphasis, but cannot by itself identify the
theological instant at which sacramental efficacy occurs. Before Updike is used
in publication, an exact edition, page range, and rights-qualified artifact
should be registered and inspected; the available web transcription is a lead,
not yet publication evidence.

## 2. The typical edition and amended impressions

The 23 June 1962 decree printed in the Missal establishes the typical edition's
promulgation. The Sacred Congregation of Rites decree *De S. Ioseph nomine
Canoni Missae inserendo*, 13 November 1962, `AAS` 54 (1962) 873, orders the
insertion after the Virgin's name and makes it effective on 8 December 1962.
The controlling facsimile prints the resulting seven-word clause at ordinary
`Communicantes` n. 1090 and in the common continuation following the proper
forms, artifact PDF pp. 383–386 (printed pp. 302–305).

Those facts require a distinction:

1. the typical edition was promulgated before the Saint Joseph decree;
2. the text legally in force was amended during 1962;
3. the collated facsimile contains the amendment; but
4. neither the clause nor the digitized title matter dates that particular
   physical impression.

A contemporary bibliographic record identifies at least one non-Vatican 1962
issue: *Missale romanum*, “1a ed. iuxta typicam,” Sumptibus et typis Mame,
Rome, 1962, OCLC 977624814. The record proves an issue identity, not whether
the catalogued copy incorporates the December amendment. The later Sodi–Toniolo
anastatic edition (LEV, ISBN 9788820979652; XVII + 1096 pp.) identifies itself
as a facsimile of the 1962 typical edition, but its catalog description does
not identify which 1962 impression supplied the reproduced sheets.

Accordingly, “amended 1962 text” is supportable; “second edition,” “revised
typical edition,” “printed after 8 December,” or a specific impression date is
not supportable from the evidence currently inspected. Resolving the physical
history would require title-page, colophon, quire, and Saint-Joseph-clause
collation of multiple authenticated Vatican and *iuxta typicam* copies.

## 3. Canon audibility

The exact governing loci are the 1962 *Rubricae generales*:

- n. 511 lists what is said `clara voce`; between the `Sanctus` and the Canon's
  closing dialogue it names `Nobis quoque peccatoribus`, while the remaining
  matter is `secreto`;
- n. 512 defines `secreto`: the priest is to pronounce the words so that he
  hears himself and those standing around do not;
- n. 513(e) provides that at solemn Mass the celebrant says secretly the other
  parts that are aloud at Low Mass;
- n. 514 applies n. 513 to sung Mass without sacred ministers.

The corresponding `Ordo Missae` loci are n. 500 for the Canon generally and
n. 1110 for `Nobis quoque peccatoribus`, whose first words are said with the
voice raised somewhat before the rest continues secretly.

Thus “silent Canon” is acceptable only as conventional shorthand. “Inaudible”
or “unspoken” is inaccurate. The positive prescription is low vocalization
audible to the celebrant but not to bystanders. The article's current
formulation is source-exact.

## 4. Historical textual changes

The evidence supports three different classes and they should not be merged:

- **Substantive amendment within the 1962 edition's operative life:** the Saint
  Joseph clause, established by `AAS` 54 (1962) 873 and the amended facsimile.
- **Orthographic difference between identified printings:** `expecto` /
  `exspecto`, `quotidianum` / `cotidianum`, and comparable `I`/`J` or
  `ae`/`oe` forms in the 1861 and 1962 witnesses. These are printing and
  orthographic differences, not independently established changes in the
  prayer's substance.
- **Long historical development before 1962:** Fortescue's identified pages
  and the late-fourth-century Milanese parallels in *De sacramentis* establish
  selected antecedents and changes. They do not constitute a critical
  stemma of the Roman Canon.

Fortescue, *The Mass* (1922), p. 172 records the tradition that Gregory was the
last to touch the Canon and Benedict XIV's dictum; p. 305 concerns Gregory's
placement of the Lord's Prayer. These are attributed historical judgments, not
proof of invariance. Trent, session XXII, chapter IV, states the Canon's
received authority and constituent kinds; it does not claim that every word
was fixed apostolically or that no later competent authority could amend it.

The postconciliar removal of `mysterium fidei` from the chalice form and
placement of the phrase as an acclamation cue is a direct cross-edition textual
question. It should remain in the separately controlled comparative or
postconciliar study unless this leaf binds the later typical edition directly.

## Result

The current corrections on all four questions should stand. The only plausible
expansion is a tightly bounded book-history note using Updike after exact source
registration. Claims about the date or sequence of amended 1962 impressions
remain unproved and should not be restored without physical-copy collation.
