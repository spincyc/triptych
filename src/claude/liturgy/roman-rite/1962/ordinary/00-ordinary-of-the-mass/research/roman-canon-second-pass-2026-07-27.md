# Roman Canon second-pass research — 2026-07-27

## Question

This pass tests the corrected draft's deliberately narrow claims about the
history of the Roman Canon, its verbal parallels with *De sacramentis* IV,
5--6, and comparisons sometimes framed as an epiclesis. It does not attempt a
reconstruction of the Canon or adjudicate sacramental causality.

## Witness hierarchy reached

1. The publication's exact PL 16 page-image passages at cols. 462--464 remain
   the direct textual controls actually inspected.
2. Otto Faller's critical edition, *Ambrosius, Explanatio symboli, De
   sacramentis, De mysteriis*, CSEL 73 (Vienna, 1955), is the critical control
   that should govern any future manuscript or text-history claim. The official
   CSEL catalog identifies *De sacramentis* in this volume; BiblIndex likewise
   identifies CSEL 73 and Bernard Botte's *Sources Chrétiennes* 25bis as modern
   editions.
3. Botte's revised SC 25bis text substantially follows Faller. A contemporary
   review notes that the revised edition aligned *De sacramentis* and *De
   mysteriis* almost continuously with Faller, while discussing the few
   divergences in critical notes.
4. A modern historical synthesis by Enrico Mazza, “L'antique prière
   eucharistique romaine et les autres témoins de cette tradition,” supplies a
   useful scholarly comparison among the Roman tradition, Milan,
   Alexandrian/Markan material, Gelasian material, and Hispanic witnesses. It is
   orientation, not a substitute for the primary texts or their critical
   editions.

## Claims the present evidence supports

### *De sacramentis* parallels

The narrowed draft may say that the late-fourth-century Milanese witness has:

- four adjectives corresponding in order to the Roman Canon's
  *adscriptam, ratam, rationabilem, acceptabilem*;
- a recognizably related institution narrative with material verbal
  differences;
- an anamnesis naming Passion, Resurrection, and Ascension before
  *offerimus*;
- a single sequence that parallels material later distributed across
  *Unde et memores*, *Supra quae*, and *Supplices*; and
- Abel, Abraham, and Melchisedech in the same order, followed by an ascent-to-
  the-heavenly-altar petition through plural angelic hands rather than the
  Roman Canon's singular *sancti Angeli tui*.

These are direct collation findings from the identified PL witness. They support
“close structural parallel with nonidentical wording.” They do **not** support
identity of the Milanese prayer with the received Roman Canon, direct textual
descent, a Roman provenance for every clause, or the antiquity of the received
Roman wording as a whole.

The sentence reporting the Maurist note about *ratam* is supportable only as a
report of that edition's apparatus. Until Faller's apparatus is acquired and
inspected, it should not be expanded into a current claim about the full
manuscript tradition or the earliest recoverable reading.

### History of the Roman Canon

The evidence supports a deliberately modest chronology:

- *De sacramentis* is a late-fourth-century Milanese witness to a related Latin
  Eucharistic-prayer pattern.
- Trent XXII.4 describes the received Canon as composed from the Lord's words,
  apostolic traditions, and pious institutions of holy pontiffs. This is a
  conciliar description of the received Canon's sources and soundness, not a
  critical dating of its individual prayers.
- The 1962 Missal and the 1861 hand missal can establish exact bounded states
  and the Saint Joseph difference already recorded; they cannot date the
  formation of the ancient prayer units.

The revised draft is therefore right not to date individual Canon sections,
assign authors, or turn one parallel into a reconstruction. No stronger
section-by-section dating claim was secured in this pass.

### Epiclesis comparisons

The Roman text permits two observations without resolving the historical or
theological controversy:

- *Quam oblationem* asks the Father to make the offering blessed, enrolled,
  ratified, reasonable, and acceptable so that it may become Christ's Body and
  Blood; it does not explicitly name the Holy Spirit.
- *Supplices* asks that the offered gifts be borne to the sublime altar so that
  communicants may be filled with heavenly blessing and grace; it likewise
  contains no explicit invocation of the Holy Spirit.

These prayers may be compared functionally with pre- and post-institution
epiclesis material, but “is the Roman epiclesis,” “contains no epiclesis,” and
“the angel is the Holy Spirit” are rival interpretations, not results supplied
by the Roman text alone. The revised draft's refusal to adjudicate the question
is supported.

Any future Eastern or Alexandrian comparison must bind the exact critical text
and edition of each anaphora. A generic appeal to “Eastern epicleses” would
collapse different prayer structures. In particular, Serapion, the
Alexandrian/Markan tradition, and the church-order text commonly called
*Apostolic Tradition* must each be handled as separate, edition-identified
witnesses.

## Publication judgment

The prior narrowed claims can stand if they remain at the level now stated:
exact verbal/structural comparison, explicit difference, and refusal to infer
identity, descent, section dates, an author, or an epiclesis solution. The PL
collation is sufficient for those bounded observations.

Before adding any manuscript-history claim—especially one built on the Maurist
note—the project should acquire and inspect the relevant pages and apparatus in
Faller's CSEL 73 or Botte's revised SC 25bis and register exact reusable
passages. Before adding comparative epiclesis claims, it should do the same for
each compared anaphora.
