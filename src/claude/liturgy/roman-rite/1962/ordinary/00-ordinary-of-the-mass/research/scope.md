# Research scope

## Question

What does the 1962 Vatican typical edition of the *Missale Romanum* actually
print as the Ordinary of the Mass; what do its own rubrics require of each unit;
where did each unit come from and when; and what does each unit say when its
Latin is read closely? The Canon is the place where the fourth question carries
the study, and it is read clause by clause in its own Latin.

## Controlling object

The `Ordo Missae` and `Canon Missae` of that edition, marginal numbers 1013 to
1139, together with the `Praefationes` at 1043 to 1087 and the front-matter
`Rubricae generales Missalis romani` and `Ritus servandus in celebratione
Missae` so far as they govern those numbers. "Ordinary" here does not mean the
sung Ordinary and does not mean the Missal.

## Included

- Every appointed text printed in the `Ordo Missae` and `Canon Missae`, in the
  book's own order and under the book's own marginal numbers.
- The rubrics printed with those texts, and the general rubrics that condition
  them: nn. 424-432, 466-476, 480-482, 487-499, 500-503, 507-510, 511-514.
- The five proper `Communicantes` (nn. 1093-1097) and the one proper `Hanc
  igitur` (n. 1099), described but not printed entire.
- The dated development of each unit, so far as identified witnesses establish
  it.
- One documented alteration to the Canon made inside the life of this edition:
  the insertion of Saint Joseph's name, decreed 13 November 1962 and in force
  from 8 December 1962.

## Excluded, and why

- The temporal and sanctoral propers. They are a different genre with their own
  profile; they appear here only as context at the introit, collect, lessons,
  offertory antiphon, secret, Communion antiphon and postcommunion, each of
  which is named as a proper slot rather than expounded.
- The Holy Week ordo. Its Masses have their own `Ordo Missae` variations and
  cannot be treated as conditional cases of the ordinary sequence.
- Ritual, votive, conventual and pontifical Masses, except where the printed
  rubric of the `Ordo Missae` itself names them.
- The ceremonial of solemn Mass beyond the points the `Ordo Missae` prescribes.
  The `Ritus servandus` was consulted at those points and not read entire.
- The music. Chant settings are counted and their rubrics reported; no musical
  claim is made.
- The present canonical discipline governing the use of the 1962 books. That is
  a dated question of current law about a different subject.
- Comparison with the reformed Order of Mass, except at the two points where
  the collation of this edition supplies evidence. A sibling Claude study
  treats the postconciliar Order of Mass on its own sources; this study does
  not duplicate or contradict it.

## Principal questions carried through the exposition

1. Is the Ordinary one composition or a deposit of unlike strata, and what
   turns on the answer?
2. Where does the 1962 book's own presentation (numbering, interleaved rubric,
   rule of silence) do work that the texts themselves do not do?
3. How much of what looks old in this book is in fact the code of 1960?
4. How large was the one change this edition made to the Canon, and what does
   its size show?

## Deliberate non-decisions

The study does not date the sections of the Canon severally, assign an author
to any of them, adjudicate the epiclesis question, identify the `sanctus
Angelus tuus` of `Supplices`, or explain Andrew's presence in `Libera nos`. The
last two are recorded as unresolved rather than filled by inference.
