### What the study contains

- An opening on the Sunday's occurrence (its precedence over the Maternity of
  the Blessed Virgin Mary, the commemoration in low and conventual Masses and
  its omission in a sung Mass that is not conventual, green, Gloria, Credo,
  Trinity Preface), the formulary's movement and the three questions it
  raises, with a ten-row map of the appointed elements.
- The complete appointed Latin of the ten elements from the 1962 typical
  edition, with the Douay–Rheims (Challoner) at the canonical verses for the
  scriptural elements and the 1861 Cummiskey English for the Introit antiphon
  and the three orations, and a note at every place where the Missal's form
  leaves the Douay or the 1861 English departs from the Latin; then the three
  commemorated Maternity orations with the 1945 Lasance English, marked as
  belonging to the low or conventual Mass of this date and not to the
  formulary. No English is composed for the Missal's divergent forms.
- An element-by-element section giving each text its literary context,
  textual facts and liturgical place, with the reception that has no fuller
  home in the readings (Chrysostom, Hilary and Cassiodorus on Ps 144;
  Cassiodorus and Bellarmine on Ps 107/56; Aquinas and Schuster on the Epistle;
  Rupert, Durandus and St Anthony on the Gospel; Schuster and Fromage on the
  orations).
- Three readings of the whole formulary, each carrying all ten element keys and
  closing with its own four senses, recorded in the manifest as the lanes
  `word-at-a-distance`, `songs-of-sion-in-babylon` and
  `israel-and-the-nations`.
- A comparison of the three readings with a comparison table.
- A terminal Scriptural Date and Location appendix on the generated chronology
  projection (the reviewed home of the chronology the concise study's page 2
  will print), the scope appendix, and References.

The three readings are those of `research/interpretations.md`:

- `word-at-a-distance` — *The Word That Heals from Afar: Faith Taught to Rest
  on Christ's Word*. Carried by Gregory the Great (*Hom. in Ev.* 28, 1–3; 4, 3)
  and John Chrysostom (*Hom. in Io.* 35, 2–3), with Augustine (*Tract. in Io.*
  16, 3–4; *Enarr.* 144 on vv. 15–17; 118 s. 15 on v. 49), Cyril (*In Io.*
  II.5), Aquinas (*Super Io.* c. 4 l. 7; *Super Eph.* c. 5 l. 6, described),
  Hilary (*Aleph* 2), Cassiodorus (118.1 and, from iteration 1, 107.2,
  described), Bellarmine (118:49 and, from iteration 1, 56:8), St Anthony
  (described), Schuster and Fromage; from iteration 1 also Augustine (*Enarr.*
  56 on v. 8) and Schuster (p. 176) on the Alleluia. Exegetical question: what faith
  Christ asks of the father and why he heals by a word without going down.
- `songs-of-sion-in-babylon` — *The Song of the Exiles: Confession, Humility
  and Hope by the Rivers of Babylon*. Carried by Augustine (*Enarr.* 136; 144 on
  v. 17; 118 s. 1, 1 and s. 15 on v. 50; 56 on v. 8) and Hilary (*Tract.* 136,
  including 136.14; 118 *Aleph* 1–3 and *Zain*), with Jerome (*In Dan.* on 3:26,
  29, 37, 49; *In Eph.* III on 5:16, 19, 20), Chrysostom (*Hom. in Eph.* 19;
  *Expos. in Ps.* 136, described), Bellarmine (118:1; 136), Cassiodorus (136,
  described), Gregory (*Hom.* 28, 3), and Sicard and Durandus for the shared
  chants only. Exegetical question: how a people that has sinned and lives in
  exile is to pray, sing and spend its time.
- `israel-and-the-nations` — *The Dying Son and the Two Peoples: Israel's
  Confession and the Nations' Hope*. Carried by Augustine (*Tract. in Io.* 16,
  3–7) and Aquinas (*Super Io.* c. 4 l. 7, the allegory of the Fathers and the
  people, described), with Cyril (*In Io.* II.5, the fullness of the Gentiles
  and the salvation of all Israel), Jerome (*In Dan.* on 3:29, 37), Chrysostom
  (*Expos. in Ps.* 136, described), Cassiodorus (118 preface; 107.4,
  described), Bellarmine (118:49), and Rupert, Honorius and Fromage for the
  shared chants only. Exegetical question: whose story the healing at
  Capharnaum tells and whose voices the Mass lets us hear.

### Iteration 1: repair of the first study review

The study's first cold review returned two blocking findings and one advisory.
The stage re-entered at iteration 1, same model and effort, and repaired:

- **STU-001** (the first reading gave the Alleluia no place). The reading's
  opening statement now names the Alleluia's contribution, a heart made ready
  to accept God's will, beside the Epistle's understanding of it. Its
  subsection on the Introit, Epistle and Collect, retitled *The way, the will
  of God, and a heart made ready*, gains a paragraph on *Paratum cor meum*:
  Augustine on Ps 56:8 (*Enarr.* 56, the prepared heart accepts the will of
  God; "shall I not prepare heart to endure?"), Bellarmine on Ps 56:8 ("ready
  to take anything cheerfully from your hand"), Schuster on *gloria mea* (God
  the soul's glory as "the just judge of our merits", p. 176), and, with the
  Collect, Cassiodorus on Ps 107:2 (the verse said rightly by those who trust
  their sins forgiven by his gift; described, not quoted). Each witness is
  credited only with what he says of the verse; each join to the father, to
  Chrysostom's thanks "even if they obtain it not", to Gregory's rebuke of
  rank and to the Collect is marked as the editor's. The second reading's
  treatment of the singing verbs and Augustine's example of St Paul are not
  repeated. The moral sense gains the ready heart (Augustine; Bellarmine) and
  the anagogical sense Schuster's God as author of eternal glory. The
  opening's statement that every element has a place in each reading is now
  true of all three.
- **STU-002** (the comparison claimed common ground that one reading lacks).
  The comparison's first paragraph now claims for all three only the
  Postcommunion's *oboedire mandatis* answering the Introit's *non
  oboedivimus*, a beginning from the Introit's confession heard three ways,
  and a place for the Communion's hope in God's word, each stated as each
  reading holds it. The Gospel's word as hinge and the agreement that the
  healing was by a word are credited to the first and third readings;
  Augustine's order of prayer to the first and second. The second reading's
  use of the Gospel is described as the supporting place it gives it. The
  closing paragraph no longer gives the second reading an account of *et
  credidit ipse, et domus eius tota*: it says that reading does not take up
  those words, reports what it does say of the Gospel (Gregory's world that
  sends us to God; Jerome's *sermo divinus*), and names its own answer. Two
  further sentences were brought into line: the second reading's "full
  weight" is now confined to the two verbs of singing, and the setting the
  second reading gives the others is stated as the comparison's observation,
  not a claim of that reading.
- **STU-003** (advisory; scope appendix's clause on the medieval
