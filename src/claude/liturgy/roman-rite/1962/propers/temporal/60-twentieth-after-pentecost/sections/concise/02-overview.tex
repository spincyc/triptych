\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Azarias confesses in the
furnace that God's judgments on his people were true and asks mercy. At Cana a
ruler asks Jesus to come down to his dying son, is rebuked for a faith that
waits on signs, believes the word ``Thy son liveth'', and learns that the fever
left the boy at that hour. Captives weep by Babylon's rivers; St Paul bids the
Ephesians redeem the time and sing in their hearts.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ, absent
nowhere, heals by command what he created by his will (Gregory). Babylon is the
world whose loves flow past, Sion the heavenly mother of the exiled faithful
(Augustine; Hilary). Christ's own country figures the people of the Jews, who
saw signs and scarcely believed, and the Samaritans the nations (Augustine);
the ruler figures the Fathers praying for their people (Aquinas).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Believe the word before you see,
and grow in faith on the road (Aquinas); do not wait for miracles
(Chrysostom). Confess God's justice before asking his gifts (Jerome;
Augustine); sit by Babylon's rivers, not in them (Augustine); sing with the
heart (Jerome). Stand humbly as the grafted branch before the root, the
patriarchs (Augustine).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The road between the
word and its proof ends in sight: the blessed enjoy the truth that sustains us
``without any veil, face to face'' (Schuster); captivity passes, and we
triumph with our King (Augustine); when the fullness of the Gentiles has come
in, all Israel will be saved (Cyril, after Rom 11:25--26).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
