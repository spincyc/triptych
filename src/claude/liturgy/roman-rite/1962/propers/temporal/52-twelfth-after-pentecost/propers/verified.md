## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1552

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 69, 2-3`
(Vulgate numbering; Hebrew-numbering Ps. 70:2–3). The psalm verse is cited
`Ps. ibid., 4`.

> Deus, in adiutórium meum inténde: Dómine, ad adiuvándum me festína:
> confundántur et revereántur inimíci mei, qui quærunt ánimam meam.
> *Ps. ibid., 4* Avertántur retrórsum, et erubéscant: qui cógitant mihi mala.
> ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 392, left column, below the
formulary heading; PDF p. 473. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Notes on the text-form.** The antiphon and verse follow the chant psalter
(the *Psalterium Romanum* tradition of the Roman Gradual), not the Gallican
psalter of the Clementine Vulgate, and diverge from the Clementine at two
places, both verified as printed:

1. the antiphon reads `confundántur et revereántur **inimíci mei**, qui
   quærunt ánimam meam`, where Clementine Ps. 69:3 has no `inimici mei`
   (`Confundantur, et revereantur, qui quaerunt animam meam`);
2. the verse reads `qui **cógitant** mihi mala`, where Clementine Ps. 69:4
   reads `qui **volunt** mihi mala`.

The verse takes only the first half of v. 4; the Clementine's second half
(`avertantur statim erubescentes…`) is not appointed. The Venice 1570 Missal
prints the verse with the Gallican `volunt` (OCR-level observation recorded
under Rights below), so the 1962 book's `cógitant` is an edition-identified
reading of the 1962 text, not an assumption that every predecessor Missal read
so.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation,
or reference issue.

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1553

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Omnípotens et miséricors Deus, de cuius múnere venit, ut tibi a fidélibus
> tuis digne et laudabíliter serviátur: tríbue, quǽsumus, nobis; ut ad
> promissiónes tuas sine offensióne currámus. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 392, left column; PDF p. 473.
**Checked:** 2026-08-19, with the 600-dpi crop confirming the comma after
`venit`, the colon after `serviátur`, and the semicolon after `nobis`.

**Note on punctuation.** The semicolon after `nobis` separates the petition
(`tríbue, quǽsumus, nobis`) from its purpose clause (`ut ad promissiónes tuas
sine offensióne currámus`), and the comma after `venit` marks the subject
clause `ut tibi … serviátur` as the content of the gift: worthy and laudable
service is itself what comes `de cuius múnere`.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1554

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Corínthios.` · `2 Cor. 3, 4-9`.

> Fratres: Fidúciam talem habémus per Christum ad Deum: non quod sufficiéntes
> simus cogitáre áliquid a nobis, quasi ex nobis: sed sufficiéntia nostra ex
> Deo est: qui et idóneos nos fecit minístros novi testaménti: non líttera,
> sed spíritu: líttera enim occídit, spíritus autem vivíficat. Quod si
> ministrátio mortis, lítteris deformáta in lapídibus, fuit in glória; ita ut
> non possent inténdere fílii Israël in fáciem Móysi, propter glóriam vultus
> eius, quæ evacuátur: quómodo non magis ministrátio Spíritus erit in glória?
> Nam si ministrátio damnatiónis glória est: multo magis abúndat ministérium
> iustítiæ in glória.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 392, left and right columns;
PDF p. 473. **Checked:** 2026-08-19, with the 600-dpi crop confirming
`Israël` with diaeresis and the semicolon after the first `in glória`.

**Notes.** The liturgical incipit `Fratres:` is the missal's, not Paul's, and
it displaces the connective of Clementine 2 Cor. 3:4, `Fidúciam **autem**
talem habémus`: the lesson begins as address what Paul wrote as continuation.
The pericope ends at v. 9; v. 10 (`Nam nec glorificátum est…`) is outside the
appointed lesson. The missal prints `ministrátio` three times but `ministérium
iustítiæ` in the final clause, following the Clementine's own change of noun
at v. 9.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1557

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Lucam.` · `Luc. 10, 23-37`.

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Beáti óculi, qui vident quæ
> vos vidétis. Dico enim vobis, quod multi prophétæ et reges voluérunt vidére
> quæ vos vidétis, et non vidérunt: et audíre quæ audítis, et non audiérunt.
> Et ecce quidam legisperítus surréxit, tentans illum, et dicens: Magíster,
> quid faciéndo vitam ætérnam possidébo? At ille dixit ad eum: In lege quid
> scriptum est? quómodo legis? Ille respóndens, dixit: Díliges Dóminum Deum
> tuum ex toto corde tuo, et ex tota ánima tua, et ex ómnibus víribus tuis, et
> ex omni mente tua: et próximum tuum sicut teípsum. Dixítque illi: Recte
> respondísti: hoc fac, et vives. Ille autem volens iustificáre seípsum, dixit
> ad Iesum: Et quis est meus próximus? Suscípiens autem Iesus, dixit: Homo
> quidam descendébat ab Ierúsalem in Iéricho, et íncidit in latrónes, qui
> étiam despoliavérunt eum: et plagis impósitis abiérunt, semivívo relícto.
> Accidit autem ut sacérdos quidam descénderet eádem via: et viso illo
> præterívit. Simíliter et levíta, cum esset secus locum, et vidéret eum,
> pertránsiit. Samaritánus autem quidam iter fáciens, venit secus eum: et
> videns eum, misericórdia motus est. Et apprópians alligávit vúlnera eius,
> infúndens óleum et vinum: et impónens illum in iuméntum suum, duxit in
> stábulum, et curam eius egit. Et áltera die prótulit duos denários, et dedit
> stabulário, et ait: Curam illíus habe: et quodcúmque supererogáveris, ego
> cum redíero, reddam tibi. Quis horum trium vidétur tibi próximus fuísse
> illi, qui íncidit in latrónes? At ille dixit: Qui fecit misericórdiam in
> illum. Et ait illi Iesus: Vade, et tu fac simíliter.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Credo.` — printed at the right-hand end of
the Gospel's final line, not on a line of its own.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed pp. 392–393; PDF pp. 473–474.
**Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Notes.** The liturgical incipit `In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis
suis:` displaces Luke's own narrative frame, Clementine 10:23 `Et convérsus ad
discípulos suos, dixit:` — the evangelist's "turning to his disciples" is not
part of the appointed text. The pericope is complete from v. 23 through
v. 37; v. 38 (Martha) begins a new episode outside the lesson. The lowercase
`quómodo legis?` after `In lege quid scriptum est?` is as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue in the Vatican typical
witness. See the discrepancy table for the v. 34 comma.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1558

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Offertorium` ·
`Exodi 32, 11, 13 et 14`, the reference printed at the head of the text line.

> Precátus est Móyses in conspéctu Dómini Dei sui, et dixit: Quare, Dómine,
> irásceris in pópulo tuo? Parce iræ ánimæ tuæ: meménto Abraham, Isaac, et
> Iacob, quibus iurásti dare terram fluéntem lac et mel. Et placátus factus
> est Dóminus de malignitáte, quam dixit fácere pópulo suo.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, left column, below the
`Credo` rubric; PDF p. 474. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi for
`Dómini Dei sui` (no comma between `Dómini` and `Dei`) and `Isaac, et Iacob`
(comma before `et`), both confirmed in the Benziger images as well.

**Note on the chant's text-form.** This is a narrative Offertory whose text is
an adaptation, not a quotation, of the Vulgate. Collated word for word against
Clementine Ex. 32:11–14:

1. `Precátus est Móyses in conspéctu Dómini Dei sui, et dixit` condenses
   v. 11's `Moyses autem orabat Dominum Deum suum, dicens`;
2. `Quare, Dómine, irásceris in pópulo tuo?` reshapes `Cur, Domine, irascitur
   furor tuus contra populum tuum…?` — the verb becomes second person, and the
   clause `quem eduxisti de terra Aegypti, in fortitudine magna, et in manu
   robusta` is dropped;
3. `Parce iræ ánimæ tuæ` carries the matter of **v. 12** — `quiescat ira tua,
   et esto placabilis super nequitia populi tui` — although the printed
   citation names only vv. 11, 13 and 14; the phrase's shape (`ira animae
   tuae`, the anger *of thy soul*) matches the Septuagint's τῆς ὀργῆς τοῦ
   θυμοῦ σου rather than any Vulgate wording;
4. `meménto Abraham, Isaac, et Iacob` replaces v. 13's `Recordare Abraham,
   Isaac, et Israël servorum tuorum` — `meménto` for `recordare`, `Iacob` for
   `Israël`, `servorum tuorum` dropped;
5. `quibus iurásti dare terram fluéntem lac et mel` replaces the oath's
   content in v. 13 (`per temetipsum… Multiplicabo semen vestrum sicut stellas
   caeli; et universam terram hanc… dabo semini vestro`) with the
   milk-and-honey formula of Ex. 3:8 and 33:3 — and in the accusative form
   `lac et mel` (the Septuagint's γῆν ῥέουσαν γάλα καὶ μέλι), where the
   Clementine has the ablative `terram fluentem lacte et melle`;
6. `Et placátus factus est Dóminus de malignitáte, quam dixit fácere pópulo
   suo` renders v. 14 in the Septuagint's shape (ἱλάσθη Κύριος περὶ τῆς κακίας
   ἧς εἶπε ποιῆσαι…), against the Clementine's `Placatusque est Dominus ne
   faceret malum quod locutus fuerat adversus populum suum`.

The chant is therefore an Old-Latin (Septuagint-shaped) centonisation of Ex.
32:11–14 that antedates and ignores the Vulgate; the 1962 book prints it
exactly as above, and this guide treats the differences as features of the
appointed text, verified as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1559

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Hóstias, quǽsumus, Dómine, propítius inténde, quas sacris altáribus
> exhibémus: ut, nobis indulgéntiam largiéndo, tuo nómini dent honórem. Per
> Dóminum nostrum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, left column; PDF p. 474.
**Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Notes.** The typical edition prints the conclusion cue `Per Dóminum
nostrum.`; Benziger prints `Per Dóminum.` The comma pair around `nobis
indulgéntiam largiéndo` is the typical edition's (see the discrepancy table);
it marks the gerund clause as the means: by lavishing pardon on us, the
offerings give honour to God's name.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.` — the Preface
of the Most Holy Trinity, printed with `Ssma` under a tilde abbreviation for
`Sanctissima`. Expanded here from the same book's own abbreviation practice.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1560

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Communionem` ·
`Ps. 103, 13 et 14-15` (Vulgate numbering; Hebrew-numbering Ps. 104:13–15).
The unusual citation form `13 et 14-15` is as printed, and it is exact: the
antiphon takes v. 13's second clause, skips v. 14's first clause, and then
runs continuously to the end of v. 15.

> De fructu óperum tuórum, Dómine, satiábitur terra: ut edúcas panem de terra,
> et vinum lætíficet cor hóminis: ut exhílaret fáciem in óleo, et panis cor
> hóminis confírmet.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, left column, foot; PDF
p. 474. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Notes.** Against Clementine Ps. 103: the antiphon inverts v. 13b's word
order (`de fructu operum tuorum satiabitur terra` becomes `De fructu óperum
tuórum … satiábitur terra`) and inserts the vocative `Dómine`, which is not in
the verse; v. 14a (`producens fœnum iuméntis…`) is omitted, exactly as the
printed citation declares; from `ut edúcas` to the end the antiphon follows
vv. 14b–15 word for word.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion — marginal no. 1561

**Printed heading:** `Postcommunio`.

> Vivíficet nos, quǽsumus, Dómine, huius participátio sancta mystérii: et
> páriter nobis expiatiónem tríbuat, et munímen. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, top of the right column;
PDF p. 474. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** The comma before `et munímen` is printed; the prayer's two fruits —
`expiatiónem … et munímen`, cleansing and defence — hang from one verb
`tríbuat` with `páriter` binding them as equal and simultaneous.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

Nothing here is quoted from either witness; they are read as finding aids for
the presence of the texts.

The project claims no rights in the received liturgical text and has not
composed, translated, modernised, conflated, or adapted any of it. Both
digitisations used to verify the 1962 text are third-party artifacts whose own
rights status the repository's source records list as unresolved; neither is
redistributed here, and only focused extracts and citations are kept.

Every English rendering of an appointed text in this guide comes from one of
two registered public-domain witnesses, named at the point of use:

- **Scriptural elements** — the Douay-Rheims as revised by Bishop Challoner
  (1749–1752), edition
  `edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
  which is the English of the Clementine Vulgate this missal prints. Loci
  quoted: Ps. 69:2–4; 33:2–3; 87:2; 103:13–15; Ex. 32:11–14; Lk. 10:23–37;
  2 Cor. 3:4–9.
- **Orations** — the anonymous English of the 1861 Philadelphia Cummiskey
  *Roman Missal … for the use of the laity*, edition
  `edition.eugene-cummiskey.roman-missal-english-laity.philadelphia-1861`,
  filed by 1962 formulary as `post-pentecosten-12` (printed pp. 420–423) and
  confirmed against its neighbouring Sundays so that the filing cannot slip by
  one: the row before it renders the Eleventh Sunday's `abundantia pietatis
  tuae` and the row after it the Thirteenth Sunday's `fidei, spei et caritatis
  augmentum`.

**Places where the registered English does not answer the Latin above.** They
are declared in the guide and are not filled in by the project.

1. The Introit antiphon's `inimíci mei` has no counterpart in Clementine
   Ps. 69:3 and none in the Douay ("Let them be confounded and ashamed that
   seek my soul").
2. The Introit verse sings `qui cógitant mihi mala` ("who devise evils against
   me"); the Clementine, and therefore the Douay, reads `qui volunt mihi mala`
   ("that desire evils to me").
3. The Communion's inserted vocative `Dómine` is not in Clementine
   Ps. 103:13, and the Douay's v. 13 renders the verse's own order, not the
   antiphon's inversion.
4. The Offertory is an Old-Latin adaptation, so the Douay of Ex. 32:11, 13 and
   14 answers its Vulgate base and not its exact wording: the Douay has "Moses
   besought the Lord his God" where the chant has `in conspéctu`, "Israel, thy
   servants" where the chant sings `Iacob`, the stars-and-seed oath where the
   chant sings `terram fluéntem lac et mel`, and nothing at all for `Parce iræ
   ánimæ tuæ`, whose matter is the uncited v. 12. The four Douay verses are
   printed whole with the correspondences and gaps marked; no English join is
   composed.
5. The Collect's 1861 English renders `digne et laudabíliter serviátur` as
   "worthily serve thee," giving no separate word for `laudabíliter`, and
   turns `ad promissiónes tuas … currámus` into "run on … to the obtaining the
   effects of thy promises."

The liturgical incipits `Fratres:` (which also displaces Clementine 2 Cor.
3:4's `autem`) and `In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis:` (which
displaces Clementine Lk. 10:23's `Et convérsus ad discípulos suos, dixit`) are
the missal's own and are likewise not translated.
