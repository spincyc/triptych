# Fifteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical full research edition at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/55-fifteenth-after-pentecost` and its
mechanical `-synthesis` PDF companion. The canonical edition prints the complete
Latin below beside the identified English; the synthesis companion omits the
appointed-text component.

  formulary it happens twice — **`fidei`** at Gal. 6:10 (p. 396) and

  read at native resolution and enlarged: the `f`'s hood closes over a dotless
  `i` with nothing above it, while `vídua` on the very next line of p. 397 and
  `sticos` on the same line as `fidei` carry their dots and acutes plainly. Words
  whose accent falls elsewhere keep it across the ligature: `Lætífica`,
  `Puríficent` (the Fourteenth Sunday's, one line above this formulary),
  `confitéri`, `deficiámus`, `deficiéntes`, `efficiámur`, `magnificábant`,
  `efféctus`. A capital `F` does not ligate, which is why `Fílium tuum` is
  accented in both the Secret and the Postcommunion. **This record prints
  `fidei` and `filius` unaccented, as the book does. Any later stage that adds an
  acute to either is departing from the controlling witness.**

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1582

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 85, 1 et 2-3`
(Vulgate numbering). The psalm verse is cued `Ps. ibid., 4`. Read off the tracked
Douay psalm-numbering concordance rather than computed: Vulgate Ps. 85 answers
**English Ps. 86** and **Masoretic Ps. 86** alike, `english_offset = 0` and
`english_offset_uniform = yes`, because the Clementine's inscription `Oratio ipsi
David.` is counted inside v. 1 in all three series. So the antiphon is English
Ps. 86:1, 2–3 and the verse English Ps. 86:4.

> Inclína, Dómine, aurem tuam ad me, et exáudi me: salvum fac servum tuum, Deus
> meus, sperántem in te: miserére mihi, Dómine, quóniam ad te clamávi tota die.
> *Ps. ibid., 4* Lætífica ánimam servi tui: quia ad te, Dómine, ánimam meam
> levávi. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, left column, below the
formulary heading; PDF p. 477. **Checked:** 2026-09-05, with the drop-cap `I`,
the reference `Ps. 85, 1 et 2-3`, the cue `Ps. ibid., 4` and every accent re-read
at 500 ppi.

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** This is a centonisation, not a
quotation: v. 1a as far as `exáudi me`, then v. 2b from `salvum fac`, then v. 3
entire, with the joins pointed by colons. Two of its words are not the
Clementine's — the added `ad me` at v. 1 and `mihi` for `mei` at v. 3 — and both
are recorded in the divergence table below rather than emended. The psalm verse
is v. 4 whole, with `quia` for the Clementine's `quoniam`.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation or
reference issue. No repetition cue is printed after `℣. Glória Patri.`

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1583

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Ecclésiam tuam, Dómine, miserátio continuáta mundet et múniat: et quia sine te
> non potest salva consístere; tuo semper múnere gubernétur. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, left column, below the
Introit; PDF p. 477. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi for the colon after
`múniat`, the semicolon after `consístere`, and every accent.

**Note on the pointing, and on the short conclusion.** The semicolon is the
prayer's hinge and is not a comma: it divides the causal clause (`et quia sine te
non potest salva consístere`) from the single petition it grounds
(`tuo semper múnere gubernétur`), so that the two verbs of the first limb
(`mundet et múniat`) and the one verb of the second stand on either side of it.
All three older witnesses opened by this audit print a comma there instead — the
Pustet 1862, the Venice 1570 and the Vatican typica 1604 all read `consistere,
tuo semper` — while the Benziger 1962 prints the semicolon, so it is the 1962
revision's pointing and is recorded as such. The conclusion is the short
`Per Dóminum.`, against the long form printed at the Secret and the
Postcommunion of this same formulary.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1584

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Gálatas.` · `Gal. 5, 25-26; 6, 1-10`. The two-part reference marks the chapter
break; the lesson is continuous and skips nothing.

> Fratres: Si spíritu vívimus, spíritu et ambulémus. Non efficiámur inánis
> glóriæ cúpidi, ínvicem provocántes, ínvicem invidéntes. Fratres, et si
> præoccupátus fúerit homo in áliquo delícto, vos, qui spirituáles estis,
> huiúsmodi instrúite in spíritu lenitátis, consíderans teípsum, ne et tu
> tentéris. Alter altérius ónera portáte, et sic adimplébitis legem Christi. Nam
> si quis exístimat se áliquid esse, cum nihil sit, ipse se sedúcit. Opus autem
> suum probet unusquísque, et sic in semetípso tantum glóriam habébit, et non in
> áltero. Unusquísque enim onus suum portábit. Commúnicet autem is qui
> catechizátur verbo, ei qui se catechízat, in ómnibus bonis. Nolíte erráre:
> Deus non irridétur. Quæ enim semináverit homo, hæc et metet. Quóniam qui
> séminat in carne sua, de carne et metet corruptiónem: qui autem séminat in
> spíritu, de spíritu metet vitam ætérnam. Bonum autem faciéntes, non
> deficiámus: témpore enim suo metémus, non deficiéntes. Ergo dum tempus
> habémus, operémur bonum ad omnes, máxime autem ad domésticos fidei.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, foot of the left column and
the whole upper two thirds of the right column; PDF p. 477. **Checked:**
2026-09-05, read line by line at native resolution, with `catechizátur` /
`catechízat`, the two `spíritu` of v. 25, and the final `fidei` enlarged.

**Note on the two `Fratres`.** The word stands twice, and only the first is
liturgical. `Fratres:` at the head is the lesson's added address, prefixed to
Gal. 5:25 and displacing nothing, since v. 25 begins `Si Spiritu vivimus` in the
Bible text too. `Fratres,` in the middle is Paul's own vocative at Gal. 6:1 and
belongs to the text. A reading that treats the second as a liturgical repetition
has misread the pericope.

**Note on `fidei`, and on `spíritu`.** The lesson's last word is printed
**without** an accent, because the accent would fall on the `i` of the `fi`
ligature; the rule and its evidence are under Provenance above, and the word is
reproduced bare, as printed. Separately, v. 25's two occurrences of `spíritu` are
printed in **lower case**, where the tracked Clementine capitalises `Spiritu`
both times. Nothing about the words changes; the case is the book's and is tabled
below.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 4. Gradual — marginal no. 1585

**Printed heading and reference:** `Graduale` · `Ps. 91, 2-3` (Vulgate
numbering). Read off the tracked concordance: Vulgate Ps. 91 answers **English
Ps. 92** at `english_offset = 1`, the inscription `Psalmus cantici, in die
sabbati` standing as Vulgate v. 1 and being left unnumbered in English Bibles, so
Vulgate 91:2–3 is **English Ps. 92:1–2** and **Masoretic Ps. 92:2–3**.

> Bonum est confitéri Dómino: et psállere nómini tuo, Altíssime. ℣. Ad
> annuntiándum mane misericórdiam tuam et veritátem tuam per noctem.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, right column, below the
Epistle; PDF p. 477. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi, and the line ending
`misericórdiam tuam` re-read a second time and enlarged, because the missing
comma there is a reading against every other witness this audit opened.

**Note.** The wording is the Clementine's exactly, both verses, with nothing
added, dropped or transposed. What differs is entirely pointing: a colon for the
Clementine's comma after `Dómino`, a full stop and the versicle sign for its
colon after `Altíssime`, a capital `Ad` where the Bible text runs on in lower
case, the comma after `misericórdiam tuam` dropped, and a full stop for the
comma at the end, v. 3 being cut off from v. 4.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 5. Alleluia — marginal no. 1586

**Printed form and reference:** no separate heading; the reference `Ps. 94, 3`
(Vulgate numbering; English and Masoretic alike **Ps. 95:3**, `english_offset =
0`, the Clementine folding this psalm's title into v. 1) is printed inline after
the versicle sign.

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 94, 3* Quóniam Deus magnus Dóminus, et Rex magnus
> super omnem terram. Allelúia.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, right column, below the
Gradual; PDF p. 477. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi.

**Note, and it is the largest textual finding in the chant of this formulary.**
The chant reads `super omnem terram` where the tracked Clementine's Ps. 94:3
reads `super omnes deos`. This is not a cut, an addition or an orthographic
variant: it is a different predicate, and it makes the verse say that the great
King is great over the whole earth rather than above all gods. The same reading
stands in the Pustet 1862, in the Venice 1570 and in the Vatican typica 1604, so
it is the Roman chant tradition's and not a 1962 alteration; **no derivation from
any particular older psalter is asserted here**, no *Psalterium Romanum* witness
having been collated by this audit. The consequence for the English is recorded
under Rights below: the Douay renders the Clementine and therefore does not
answer this line.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1587

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Lucam.` · `Luc. 7, 11-16`. The cross is set as a display initial at the head of
the two heading lines.

> In illo témpore: Ibat Iesus in civitátem, quæ vocátur Naim: et ibant cum eo
> discípuli eius, et turba copiósa. Cum autem appropinquáret portæ civitátis,
> ecce defúnctus efferebátur filius únicus matris suæ: et hæc vídua erat: et
> turba civitátis multa cum illa. Quam cum vidísset Dóminus, misericórdia motus
> super eam, dixit illi: Noli flere. Et accéssit, et tétigit lóculum. (Hi autem,
> qui portábant, stetérunt). Et ait: Aduléscens, tibi dico, surge. Et resédit qui
> erat mórtuus, et cœpit loqui. Et dedit illum matri suæ. Accépit autem omnes
> timor: et magnificábant Deum, dicéntes: Quia prophéta magnus surréxit in nobis:
> et quia Deus visitávit plebem suam.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, foot of the right column,
continuing to printed p. 397, left column, PDF pp. 477–478. The page break falls
inside `copió-`/`sa`. **Checked:** 2026-09-05, with `filius`, `Aduléscens` and
the closing `stetérunt).` enlarged.

**Notes.** `filius` at v. 12 is printed **without** an accent, the `fi` ligature
again; see Provenance. `Aduléscens` is printed with `u`, not the `Adolescens` of
the Clementine and of the older Missals, and is reproduced as printed. The
parenthesis at v. 14 closes **before** its full stop, `stetérunt).`, against the
Clementine's `steterunt.)`. The incipit `In illo témpore: Ibat Iesus` replaces
the Bible text's `Et factum est: deinceps ibat` and supplies the subject the
pericope would otherwise lack; it is the missal's own and is tabled below.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1588

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Offertorium` · `Ps. 39, 2, 3 et 4`
(Vulgate numbering). Read off the tracked concordance: Vulgate Ps. 39 answers
**English Ps. 40** at `english_offset = 1`, the inscription `In finem. Psalmus
ipsi David.` standing as Vulgate v. 1 and being left unnumbered in English
Bibles, so Vulgate 39:2, 3, 4 is **English Ps. 40:1, 2, 3** and **Masoretic
Ps. 40:2, 3, 4**.

> Exspéctans exspectávi Dóminum, et respéxit me: et exaudívit deprecatiónem
> meam: et immísit in os meum cánticum novum, hymnum Deo nostro.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 397, left column, below the
`Credo` rubric; PDF p. 478. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi.

**Note, and it is the second large textual finding.** Three of this antiphon's
phrases are not the Clementine's. Where the Bible text has `et intendit mihi`,
the antiphon has `et respéxit me`; where it has `Et exaudivit preces meas`, the
antiphon has `et exaudívit deprecatiónem meam`; and where it has `carmen Deo
nostro`, the antiphon has `hymnum Deo nostro`. All three stand in the Pustet
1862, the Venice 1570 and the Vatican typica 1604 as well, so they are the Roman
chant tradition's and not 1962 alterations. Everything between — the whole of
v. 3 from `et eduxit me de lacu miseriæ` to `direxit gressus meos` — is cut, and
so is v. 4b. As at the Alleluia, no derivation from a particular older psalter is
asserted here.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1589

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Tua nos, Dómine, sacraménta custódiant: et contra diabólicos semper tueántur
> incúrsus. Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et
> regnat in unitáte.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 397, left column, below the
Offertory; PDF p. 478. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi for the colon after
`custódiant` and for the accent on `Fílium`, which the capital `F` leaves room
for.

**Note on the long conclusion.** The typical edition prints the conclusion out to
`in unitáte.` and stops there, leaving the celebrant to complete it. Both the
Pustet 1862 and the Vatican typica 1604 print the short `Per Dominum.` at this
Secret, and so does the Benziger 1962; the long form here is the typical
edition's own choice at this place, and it is not a different prayer.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1590

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Communionem` · `Io. 6, 52`. The
tracked Clementine and the tracked Douay both number this verse 52, so the
missal's reference and the Bible texts this guide quotes agree without
adjustment; editions that number it 51 are not used here.

> Panis, quem ego dédero, caro mea est pro sǽculi vita.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 397, left column, below the
Preface rubric; PDF p. 478. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi.

**Note, and it is the third large textual finding.** The antiphon quotes the
second half of Jn. 6:52 and departs from it twice. The Clementine reads `et panis
quem ego dabo, caro mea est pro mundi vita`; the antiphon reads `dédero` for
`dabo` — a future perfect for a simple future, *the bread that I shall have
given* — and `pro sǽculi vita` for `pro mundi vita`. Both readings stand in the
Pustet 1862, the Venice 1570 and the Vatican typica 1604. The first half of the
verse (`Si quis manducaverit ex hoc pane, vivet in ætérnum: et`) is not sung, and
`Panis` is capitalised where the Bible text has it mid-sentence.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion — marginal no. 1591

**Printed heading:** `Postcommunio`.

> Mentes nostras et córpora possídeat, quǽsumus, Dómine, doni cæléstis
> operátio: ut non noster sensus in nobis, sed iúgiter eius prævéniat efféctus.
> Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in
> unitáte.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 397, foot of the left column;
PDF p. 478. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 500 ppi for the absence of a comma after
`nostras`, the commas around `quǽsumus`, and the colon after `operátio`.

**Note.** The subject of `possídeat` is `doni cæléstis operátio`, held back to the
end of its clause behind two objects and a vocative, and the prayer then asks
that its `efféctus` — not `noster sensus` — should `prævenire` in us. The
pointing carries that: no comma after `nostras`, so `Mentes nostras et córpora`
is one object pair, and the colon after `operátio` divides the petition from the
purpose clause. The conclusion is the long form, as at the Secret.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

**One negative result belongs with the table, and it is the Epistle's.** After
its liturgical address, the whole of Gal. 5:25–6:10 matches the tracked
Clementine word for word: the alignment reports exactly five difference runs
across those twelve verses, and they are rows 9 to 12 — two capitalisations, both
folded into row 9, one word division, one i/j orthography and one comma. Not a
syllable of the lesson's substance departs from the Bible text. The Gospel is
nearly as clean: six runs, rows 20 to 25, of which five are the incipit, one i/j
orthography and pointing, and exactly one — row 25 — is a difference of word
form. **The three elements that do depart substantively are the Alleluia,
the Offertory and the Communion** — rows 19, 26–29 and 31–32 — and in every one of
those places the departure is shared by the Pustet 1862, the Venice 1570 and the
Vatican typica 1604, so it is inherited and not a 1962 alteration.

**Two classes are declared here rather than tabled row by row**, because each is
the arithmetic of quoting continuously rather than a departure the missal makes.
First, sentence boundaries at the joins: the Introit runs `exáudi me: salvum fac`
and `in te: miserére` where the Clementine ends a sentence or a colon-clause, and
the Gradual runs `Altíssime. ℣. Ad annuntiándum` where it has a colon; rows 3, 4
and 14 describe these as cuts and continuations, and they are named again here so
that the exhaustiveness claim above covers them explicitly. Second, accentuation:
the tracked Clementine carries no accents at all, so no row below is about an
accent, and this record's accentuation rests wholly on the page images of the
controlling witness.

**A caveat that governs rows 6, 9, 17 and 18.** Capitalisation in a modern
electronic Clementine is that e-text's convention, and capitalisation in the 1962
missal is that book's. Neither is evidence of what the Latin intends, and no
argument should be built on the difference alone.

**Declared places where the registered English does not answer the Latin above.**
They must be declared in the guide and are not to be filled in by the project.

1. **The Alleluia.** The Douay renders the Clementine's Ps. 94:3, "For the Lord is
   a great God, and a great King **above all gods**," which is not what the missal
   sings; the missal has `super omnem terram`. There is no Douay sentence for the
   missal's reading. The guide should print the Douay verse and say plainly that
   the chant reads otherwise. Noted for the authoring stage and **not** adopted
   here: the 1861 Cummiskey prints its own English of this versicle at p. 428,
   `℣. For the Lord is a great God, and a great King **over all the earth**.
   Alleluia.`, beside the Latin `Quoniam Deus magnus Dominus, et Rex magnus super
   omnem terram`, so an identified public-domain English of the missal's own
   reading does exist. Whether to use a hand-missal English for a *scriptural*
   proper is a question for the repository's translation rule and is not settled
   by this record.
2. **The Offertory.** Three phrases have no Douay equivalent, because the Douay
   renders the Clementine: "he was attentive to me" for `et respéxit me`, "he
   heard my prayers" for `et exaudívit deprecatiónem meam`, and "a song to our
   God" for `hymnum Deo nostro`. The 1861 hand missal is only half a help here:
   at p. 429 it prints "with expectation I have waited for the Lord, and he was
   attentive to me: and he heard my prayer, and he put a new canticle into my
   mouth: a hymn to our God" — following the Douay at the first, taking the
   Douay's verb with the missal's singular at the second (`deprecationem meam`
   is singular where the Clementine's `preces meas` is plural, and the Douay
   reads "he heard my prayers"), and the missal at the third. The Douay's Ps. 39:2–4 also continues past what is sung;
   the antiphon stops at `Deo nostro`.
3. **The Communion.** The Douay's Jn. 6:52 reads "and the bread that **I will
   give** is my flesh, for the life of **the world**," which answers neither
   `dédero` nor `sǽculi`. The guide should print the Douay verse whole with the
   sung portion marked, and supply no rendering of its own.
4. **The Introit's added `ad me` and its `miserére mihi`.** The Douay's Ps. 86:1
   is "Incline thy ear, O Lord, and hear me," with no "to me"; its 86:3, "Have
   mercy on me, O Lord," happens to answer `mihi` as well as `mei` and so needs no
   declaration. The antiphon also sings only halves of vv. 1 and 2; the Douay
   renders both whole.
5. **The Epistle's incipit `Fratres:`.** It supplements Gal. 5:25 rather than
   replacing anything, so the Douay's verse is complete without it; the address
   itself is the missal's and is not translated.
6. **The Gospel's incipit `In illo témpore: Ibat Iesus`.** It replaces Luke's
   `Et factum est: deinceps ibat`, which the Douay renders "And it came to pass
   afterwards that he went"; the missal's opening is its own and is not
   translated.
7. **`Aduléscens`.** The Douay's "Young man" answers the word whichever way it is
   spelled, so nothing is missing; the spelling difference is a Latin matter only
   and is recorded at row 25 above.
