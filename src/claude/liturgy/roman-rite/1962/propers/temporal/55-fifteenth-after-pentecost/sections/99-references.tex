\subsection*{The Missal and the liturgical witnesses}

\begin{itemize}
\item \work{Missale Romanum ex decreto Sacrosancti Concilii Tridentini
restitutum, Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum}, editio typica (Vatican City:
Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis, 1962), printed pp.~396--397, marginal nos.\
1582--1591, with the \latin{Rubricae generales} at printed pp.~XX and XXXI. Read
on the page images of the CMAA-hosted facsimile,
\texttt{artifact.catholic-church.\allowbreak missale-romanum.\allowbreak
vatican-typica-1962.\allowbreak cmaa-facsimile-pdf}, at the scan's native
resolution. Controls every Latin form, heading, reference, accent, point,
marginal number and rubric printed here, and both formulary boundaries.
\item \work{Missale Romanum}, editio iuxta typicam (New York: Benziger Brothers,
1962), printed pp.~390--392, read only in the uncorrected optical text layer of
the Internet Archive digitisation. Every difference recorded from it is evidence
about that layer.
\item \work{Missale Romanum}, Vatican \latin{typica} (1604), under the running
head \latin{Dominica xv. post Pentecost.} at lines 38,590--38,741 of the tracked
optical layer, its printed page number not legible there; \work{Missale Romanum}
(Ratisbon: Pustet, 1862), printed pp.~338--339, at lines 50,324--50,454 of its
layer; \work{Missale Romanum} (Venice, 1570), at lines 53,352--53,515 of its
layer. All three were read only in uncorrected public-domain optical layers,
and no page image of any of them was opened; the source records of the 1604
and the 1570 classify their layers degraded and forbid resting any negative
search on them, while the Pustet's classifies its layer prose Latin and bounds
that prohibition to searches over its accented text, all three admitting
quotation only through a checked passage. The 1604 and the 1570 carry all
ten elements in the same order and at the same references; the Pustet carries
nine, its Gospel standing there as a cross-reference to the Lenten Thursday
that shares the pericope. Where a reading, a spelling or a point of theirs is
reported in this guide, what is reported is what that layer shows: the strings
behind those reports are the lightly normalised renderings of the layers
recorded in \texttt{propers/verified.md}, not quotations of the books. No
negative result here rests on any of the three.
\item H.~A. Wilson (ed.), \work{The Gregorian Sacramentary under Charles the
Great} (Henry Bradshaw Society XLIX, 1915), section XXXIIII, heading
\latin{DOMINICA .XVI. POST PENTECOSTEN.}, printed p.~174, folio mark
\latin{[fo. 168v]}; and printed p.~173 for the Ottobonianus 313 chant margins.
Read on the page images of the Internet Archive scan and in its optical layer.
\item H.~A. Wilson (ed.), \work{The Gelasian Sacramentary} (Oxford: Clarendon,
1894), Book~III sect.~XI, printed p.~230, heading \latin{Item alia Missa}, with
the marginal apparatus \latin{R. S. / Gerb. 175. / Pam. 411. / Men. 179.} Read on
a page image and in two optical layers of the same printing that agree verbatim
on every word quoted.
\item The four non-Gregorian, non-Gelasian books over which the three orations'
negative was run, read as the uncorrected optical layers of the registered
corpus \texttt{corpus.catholic-church.\allowbreak
ancient-sacramentaries-2026-08-01}: the Veronense in Feltoe's edition, in two
independent scans; Férotin's \work{Liber mozarabicus sacramentorum};
Bannister's \work{Missale Gothicum}; and Lowe's \work{Bobbio Missal}. Searched
by literal string, after letter folding, for fourteen distinctive phrases of
the three orations and for five vocabulary controls, with the two Wilson layers
as the positive control; nothing is quoted from any of the four, no page image
of any was opened, and the negative carries the four bounds stated in the
appendix.
\item Honorius Augustodunensis, \work{Gemma animae} IV capp.\ LXVII, LXXII,
LXXIV--LXXX, PL~172 cols.\ 715--721, with this Sunday at cap.~LXXVIII. Read on
page images of a registered facsimile whose bytes are rights-restricted and are
not held here.
\item \work{Liber Comitis}, PL~30 cols.\ 521--522, the heading \latin{Dominica
mensis vi} and its Epistle and Gospel. Read on a page image of the Migne volume
and corroborated in that item's optical layer.
\item Ildefonso Schuster, \work{The Sacramentary}, vol.~III (London: Burns Oates
\& Washbourne, 1927), printed pp.~139--141, for the Lawrence reckoning, the
Lenten identities of the Gradual, the Offertory and the Communion, and the
absence of a station. Read on page images of the registered scan.
\item Prosper Guéranger, \work{The Liturgical Year: Time after Pentecost}
vol.~II, second edition, translated by Dom Laurence Shepherd (Stanbrook Abbey,
Worcester; London: Burns \& Oates, R.\ \& T.\ Washbourne, Art \& Book Company;
United States: Benziger Bros., 1909), printed pp.~344--356: the name ``the
Sunday of the widow of Naim,'' the three seasonal orations by cross-reference,
and the Magnificat antiphon at Vespers. Read in the digitisation's own embedded
text layer.
\item \work{Variationes in Breviario et Missali Romano}, cap.~IV n.~18,
\latin{Orationes pro diversitate Temporum abolentur}, \work{Acta Apostolicae
Sedis} 52 (1960), printed p.~709, with the \latin{Rubricae generales} n.~18 at
printed p.~599 distinguished from it. Read on a rendering of the Holy See's own
scan.
\item René-Jean Hesbert, \work{Antiphonale Missarum Sextuplex}, at AMS~187 and at
the eight loci of Ps.\ 94:3. \textbf{His pages are reached here only through the
gregorien.info database's index of them}, that edition being registered in this
repository at catalogue level with no artifact for want of an established
redistribution basis, and every antiphonary figure in this guide is attributed to
the database and not to him.
\item \texttt{src/sources/calendars/roman-1962/propers.yaml}, the tracked 1962
calendar registry, for the other appointments of this formulary's chants and
readings. A repository derivative and a finding aid, not the printed book.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Scripture, its versions, and the chronology corpus}

\begin{itemize}

\texttt{edition.catholic-church.\allowbreak vulgata-clementina.\allowbreak
ebible-latvuc}: Pss.\ 39, 46, 85, 91 and 94, Gal.\ 5--6, Lk.\ 7 and Jn.\ 6, the

magnus}, \latin{rex magnus super omnem terram} and \latin{misericordiam tuam et
veritatem tuam}, and the sweep of Luke for the stem \latin{visita-}.
\item \work{The Holy Bible, Douay--Rheims}, as revised by Bishop Challoner,
tracked as \texttt{edition.english-college-of-douay.\allowbreak
douay-rheims-bible.\allowbreak challoner-gutenberg-1581}: Ps.\ 39:2--4; 46:3; 85:1--4;
91:2--3; 94:3; Gal.\ 5:25--6:10; Lk.\ 7:11--16; Jn.\ 6:52. The English of every

tracked numbering concordance of the same edition.
\item \work{The Roman Missal translated into the English language for the use of
the laity}, first revised edition (Philadelphia: Eugene Cummiskey, 1861), printed
pp.~427--429, and the tracked transcription
\texttt{artifact.eugene-cummiskey.\allowbreak
roman-missal-english-laity.\allowbreak philadelphia-1861.\allowbreak
temporal-orations-en} at physical lines 155--157. The English of the Collect, the
Secret and the Postcommunion; and, at printed pp.~428--429, the book's own
renderings of the Alleluia's versicle and of the Offertory antiphon, reported at
those elements as identified nineteenth-century English of what the missal
sings and not as the Douay.
\item Eberhard Nestle, \work{Novum Testamentum Graece} (1904), in the
morphologically tagged digitisation of that text: Gal.\ 5--6, Lk.\ 7, Jn.\ 6, and
the exhaustive lemma sweeps behind the counts of \emph{bastazo},
\emph{monogenes}, \emph{splanchnizomai} and the visitation verb.
\item H.~B. Swete, \work{The Old Testament in Greek according to the Septuagint},
in a digitisation derived from the First1KGreek project: Psalmi at Pss.\ 39, 85
and 94, and Regnorum~III at 3~Kingdoms 17. The title of Ps.~94 is emitted at an

\item The Scripture chronology corpus, as resolved for this formulary into
\texttt{research/chronology.toml} and projected into the page-2 Date column. Its

the corpus names for the regnal era it sets beside the Davidic attribution of

Testament,'' vol.~XIV (1912), which the corpus names for the first of the Luke
and of the John composition labels; Aherne's ``Epistle to the Galatians'' in
vol.~VI, Prat's ``St.\ Paul'' in vol.~XI, Fonck's ``Gospel of St.\ John'' in
vol.~VIII and Maas's ``Chronology of the Life of Jesus Christ'' in vol.~VIII,
all in the same encyclopedia (New York: Robert Appleton, 1908--1912); the Pontifical Biblical Commission's 1912 responsa

\latin{Argumentum} to Galatians in his \work{Commentaria in omnes divi Pauli
epistolas} (Antwerp, 1614). No biblical date printed in this guide comes from
anywhere else.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Historical orientation for the page-2 dossiers}

\begin{itemize}
\item Pontifical Biblical Commission, \latin{De auctoribus et de tempore
compositionis Psalmorum}, \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 2 (1910), printed
pp.~354--355: the antiquity of the psalm titles and David as chief author of the
songs of the Psalter.
\item Pontifical Biblical Commission, \latin{De auctore, tempore et veritate
Evangeliorum Marci et Lucae}, \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 4 (1912), printed
pp.~463--465: composition before the end of the Roman imprisonment.
\item John Corbett, ``King David,'' \work{The Catholic Encyclopedia} vol.~IV
(1908), printed p.~642, for the reign in the usual chronology that the three
Davidic psalm dossiers carry as a reference point, reported there under the
article's own qualification; Walter Drum, ``Psalms,'' vol.~XII (1911);
Cornelius Aherne, ``Gospel of Saint Luke,'' vol.~IX (1910), and ``Epistle to the
Galatians,'' vol.~VI (1909); Leopold Fonck, ``Gospel of St.\ John,'' vol.~VIII
(1910); Ferdinand Prat, ``St.\ Paul,'' vol.~XI (1911); Anthony Maas, ``Chronology
of the Life of Jesus Christ,'' vol.~VIII (1910); and the article ``Naim,''
vol.~X (1911), for the \work{Onomasticon} placing and the modern village.
\end{itemize}
