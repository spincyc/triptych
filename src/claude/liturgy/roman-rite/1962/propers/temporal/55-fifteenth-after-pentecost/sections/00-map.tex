\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & A psalm asks God to bend down and hear one poor man crying all day long \cue{Int.}. Paul instructs the spiritual how to correct a brother, sets a burden to be shared against one that cannot be, and closes on sowing and reaping \cue{Ep.}. At the gate of Naim Christ meets a funeral, is moved with mercy, touches the bier and gives a dead son back alive to his widowed mother \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Allegorical & \textbf{Ambrose reads the widow as holy Church}, recalling the younger people from the pomp of the funeral by the tears she is forbidden to weep, \latin{quae flere prohibetur eum cui resurrectio debeatur}; and the bier is wood, so that the dead man \latin{spem resurgendi habebat, quia ferebatur in ligno} \cue{Gosp.}. \textbf{Bede makes the widowed mother the Church}, \latin{licet e multis collecta personis, una sit perfecta et immaculata virgo}, her motherhood that of Gal.\ 4:19, and each of the faithful her only son; the gate is one of the bodily senses.\\
Moral & \textbf{Augustine reads the three raisings as three grades of sin} \cue{Gosp.}: the girl within the house is sin consented to in the heart, the young man carried out of the gate is sin gone out into the deed, and Lazarus under the stone is habit, \latin{vis dura consuetudinis}. Jerome and Augustine make Gal. 6:1 a discipline of the corrector before a remedy for the corrected; Chrysostom makes it an economy of complementary weaknesses inside one body \cue{Ep.}.\\
Anagogical & \textbf{Cyril of Alexandria makes the raisings a pledge}, the three raised standing as ``a pledge of the hope prepared for us of a resurrection of the dead'' \cue{Gosp.}, and the flesh that touched the bier ``the body of life'' \cue{Comm.}. The lesson reaches as far: \latin{de spíritu metet vitam ætérnam}, at a harvest \latin{témpore \dots\ suo} \cue{Ep.}.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
