\section{The Appointed Texts}
\label{sec:appointed-texts}

The Latin below is that of the 1962 typical edition, \work{Missale Romanum}
(Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis), pp.~398--400, nos.~1602--1611, read on the page
images of the CMAA facsimile. Every element's wording already stands in the
Pustet \work{Missale Romanum} of Ratisbon, 1862; the two books differ only in
spelling, pointing, citation form and the length at which a conclusion is
printed. The English of the scriptural elements is the Douay--Rheims Bible in
Bishop Challoner's revision, which translates the Clementine Vulgate at the
Missal's own Vulgate numbering. It is given at the canonical verses, so that
wherever a chant or lesson adapts its source the English and the Latin part
company; each such place is named beneath the text. The English of the three
orations is the anonymous translation printed in \work{The Roman Missal,
Translated into the English Language for the Use of the Laity} (Philadelphia:
Eugene Cummiskey, 1861). Neither English is an approved liturgical
translation of the 1962 Missal. Psalms are cited in the Missal's numbering,
with the Hebrew numbering in parentheses.

\subsection{Introit}
\label{proper-introit}

\properlocus{Ps 118:137 and 124 (Heb.\ 119); verse Ps 118:1. Missal p.~398, no.~1602.}

\begin{latinproper}
Iustus es, Dómine, et rectum iudícium tuum: fac cum servo tuo secúndum
misericórdiam tuam. \textnormal{Ps.\ ibid., 1} Beáti immaculáti in via: qui
ámbulant in lege Dómini. ℣.~Glória Patri.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 118:137, 124, 1}
\textsuperscript{137}Thou art just, O Lord: and thy judgment is right.
\textsuperscript{124}Deal with thy servant according to thy mercy: and teach me
thy justifications. \textsuperscript{1}Blessed are the undefiled in the way,
who walk in the law of the Lord.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The antiphon joins verse 137 to the first half of verse 124; the words ``and
teach me thy justifications'' are not sung. After the \latin{Gloria Patri} the
antiphon is repeated, as the general rubrics direct (no.~427); the typical
edition prints no repetition cue.

\subsection{Collect}
\label{proper-collect}

\properlocus{\latin{Oratio}. Missal p.~399, no.~1603.}

\begin{latinproper}
Da, quǽsumus, Dómine, pópulo tuo diabólica vitáre contágia: et te solum Deum
pura mente sectári. Per Dóminum nostrum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVII Sunday after Pentecost, Collect}
Grant, we beseech thee, O Lord, that thy people may avoid all contagion of the
devil: and with a clean heart follow thee, the only true God. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Collect is the only oration of the Mass. The 1862 Missal adds a second and
a third collect, which belong to the discipline before 1955 and are no part of
the 1962 formulary.

\subsection{Epistle}
\label{proper-epistle}

\properlocus{\latin{Lectio Epistolae beati Pauli Apostoli ad Ephesios}. Eph 4:1--6. Missal p.~399, no.~1604.}

\begin{latinproper}
Fratres: Obsecro vos ego vinctus in Dómino, ut digne ambulétis vocatióne, qua
vocáti estis, cum omni humilitáte et mansuetúdine, cum patiéntia,
supportántes ínvicem in caritáte, sollíciti serváre unitátem spíritus in
vínculo pacis. Unum corpus, et unus spíritus, sicut vocáti estis in una spe
vocatiónis vestræ. Unus Dóminus, una fides, unum baptísma. Unus Deus, et Pater
ómnium, qui est super omnes, et per ómnia, et in ómnibus nobis. Qui est
benedíctus in sǽcula sæculórum. Amen.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Eph 4:1--6}
\textsuperscript{1}I therefore, a prisoner in the Lord, beseech you that you
walk worthy of the vocation in which you are called: \textsuperscript{2}With
all humility and mildness, with patience, supporting one another in charity.
\textsuperscript{3}Careful to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of
peace. \textsuperscript{4}One body and one Spirit: as you are called in one
hope of your calling. \textsuperscript{5}One Lord, one faith, one baptism.
\textsuperscript{6}One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through
all, and in us all.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal opens the lesson with \latin{Fratres: Obsecro vos} where the
Vulgate reads \latin{Obsecro itaque vos} (``I therefore \dots\ beseech you''),
and it closes the lesson with \latin{Qui est benedictus in saecula saeculorum.
Amen}, which is not part of Ephesians~4. No verse of the Douay answers either
addition.

\subsection{Gradual}
\label{proper-gradual}

\properlocus{Ps 32:12 and 6 (Heb.\ 33). Missal p.~399, no.~1605.}

\begin{latinproper}
Beáta gens, cuius est Dóminus Deus eórum: pópulus, quem elégit Dóminus in
hereditátem sibi. ℣.~Verbo Dómini cæli firmáti sunt: et spíritu oris eius
omnis virtus eórum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 32:12, 6}
\textsuperscript{12}Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord: the people
whom he hath chosen for his inheritance. \textsuperscript{6}By the word of the
Lord the heavens were established; and all the power of them by the spirit of
his mouth:
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The respond is verse 12 and the versicle verse 6, against the psalm's own
order. The Missal reads \latin{Dominus Deus eorum \dots\ quem elegit Dominus}
where the Clementine Vulgate, which the Douay translates, reads \latin{Dominus
Deus ejus \dots\ quem elegit}.

\subsection{Alleluia}
\label{proper-alleluia}

\properlocus{Ps 101:2 (Heb.\ 102:2; 102:1 where the title is not numbered). Missal p.~399, no.~1606.}

\begin{latinproper}
Allelúia, allelúia. ℣.~\textnormal{Ps.\ 101, 2} Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam,
et clamor meus ad te pervéniat. Allelúia.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 101:2}
Hear, O Lord, my prayer: and let my cry come to thee.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal's \latin{perveniat} stands where the Clementine reads
\latin{veniat}.

\subsection{Gospel}
\label{proper-gospel}

\properlocus{\latin{Sequentia sancti Evangelii secundum Matthaeum}. Mt 22:34--46. Missal p.~399, no.~1607.}

\begin{latinproper}
In illo témpore: Accessérunt ad Iesum pharisǽi: et interrogávit eum unus ex
eis legis doctor, tentans eum: Magíster, quod est mandátum magnum in lege? Ait
illi Iesus: Díliges Dóminum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo, et in tota ánima tua,
et in tota mente tua. Hoc est máximum et primum mandátum. Secúndum autem símile
est huic: Díliges próximum tuum sicut teípsum. In his duóbus mandátis univérsa
lex pendet, et prophétæ. Congregátis autem pharisǽis, interrogávit eos Iesus,
dicens: Quid vobis vidétur de Christo? cuius fílius est? Dicunt ei: David. Ait
illis: Quómodo ergo David in spíritu vocat eum Dóminum, dicens: Dixit Dóminus
Dómino meo, sede a dextris meis, donec ponam inimícos tuos scabéllum pedum
tuórum? Si ergo David vocat eum Dóminum, quómodo fílius eius est? Et nemo
póterat ei respondére verbum: neque ausus fuit quisquam ex illa die eum
ámplius interrogáre.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Mt 22:34--46}
\textsuperscript{34}But the Pharisees, hearing that he had silenced the
Sadducees, came together. \textsuperscript{35}And one of them, a doctor of the
law, asked him, tempting him: \textsuperscript{36}Master, which is the great
commandment in the law? \textsuperscript{37}Jesus said to him: Thou shalt love
the Lord thy God with thy whole heart and with thy whole soul and with thy
whole mind. \textsuperscript{38}This is the greatest and the first commandment.
\textsuperscript{39}And the second is like to this: Thou shalt love thy
neighbour as thyself. \textsuperscript{40}On these two commandments dependeth
the whole law and the prophets. \textsuperscript{41}And the Pharisees being
gathered together, Jesus asked them, \textsuperscript{42}Saying: What think you
of Christ? Whose son is he? They say to him: David's. \textsuperscript{43}He
saith to them: How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying:
\textsuperscript{44}The Lord said to my Lord: Sit on my right hand, until I
make thy enemies thy footstool? \textsuperscript{45}If David then call him
Lord, how is he his son? \textsuperscript{46}And no man was able to answer him
a word: neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more
questions.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal's opening, \latin{In illo tempore: Accesserunt ad Iesum pharisaei},
stands for the Vulgate's verse 34, which the Douay renders ``But the
Pharisees, hearing that he had silenced the Sadducees, came together.'' The
Gospel carries three citations of its own: Deut 6:5 in verse 37, Lev 19:18 in
verse 39 and Ps 109:1 (Heb.\ 110:1) in verse 44. The Creed follows.

\subsection{Offertory}
\label{proper-offertory}

\properlocus{\latin{Antiphona ad Offertorium}, cited Dan 9:17, 18 and 19. Missal p.~399, no.~1608.}

\begin{latinproper}
Orávi Deum meum ego Dániel, dicens: Exáudi, Dómine, preces servi tui:
illúmina fáciem tuam super sanctuárium tuum: et propítius inténde pópulum
istum, super quem invocátum est nomen tuum, Deus.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Dan 9:17--19, the source verses}
\textsuperscript{17}Now, therefore, O our God, hear the supplication of thy
servant, and his prayers: and shew thy face upon thy sanctuary, which is
desolate, for thy own sake. \textsuperscript{18}Incline, O my God, thy ear, and
hear: open thy eyes, and see our desolation, and the city upon which thy name
is called: for it is not for our justifications that we present our prayers
before thy face, but for the multitude of thy tender mercies.
\textsuperscript{19}O Lord, hear: O Lord, be appeased: hearken, and do: delay
not, for thy own sake, O my God: because thy name is invocated upon thy city,
and upon thy people.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The antiphon is a compilation and not a continuous quotation, and no Douay
verse renders it. Its narrative frame, \latin{Oravi Deum meum ego Daniel,
dicens}, stands outside verses 17--19 (compare 9:2 and 9:4), and its petitions
condense those verses. Where the Vulgate reads \latin{ostende faciem tuam},
the chant has \latin{illumina faciem tuam}, of a face made to shine.

\subsection{Secret}
\label{proper-secret}

\properlocus{\latin{Secreta}. Missal p.~399, no.~1609.}

\begin{latinproper}
Maiestátem tuam, Dómine, supplíciter deprecámur: ut hæc sancta, quæ gérimus,
et a prætéritis nos delíctis éxuant, et futúris. Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum
Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVII Sunday after Pentecost, Secret}
We humbly beseech thy Majesty, O Lord, that the sacred mysteries we celebrate
may cleanse us from all past offences, and from those we may hereafter be
guilty of. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The typical edition prints the conclusion only as far as \latin{in unitate};
the conclusion said is the full form of the general rubrics (no.~115~a),
continuing \latin{Spiritus Sancti, Deus, per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen}.
A direction to say the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity follows the Secret.

\subsection{Communion}
\label{proper-communion}

\properlocus{Ps 75:12--13 (Heb.\ 76:12--13; 76:11--12 where the title is not numbered). Missal p.~399, no.~1610.}

\begin{latinproper}
Vovéte et réddite Dómino Deo vestro, omnes, qui in circúitu eius affértis
múnera: terríbili, et ei qui aufert spíritum príncipum: terríbili apud omnes
reges terræ.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 75:12--13}
\textsuperscript{12}Vow ye, and pay to the Lord your God: all you that are
round about him bring presents. To him that is terrible,
\textsuperscript{13}Even to him who taketh away the spirit of princes: to the
terrible with the kings of the earth.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal reads \latin{apud omnes reges terrae}; the Clementine, which the
Douay translates, has no \latin{omnes}.

\subsection{Postcommunion}
\label{proper-postcommunion}

\properlocus{\latin{Postcommunio}. Missal p.~400, no.~1611.}

\begin{latinproper}
Sanctificatiónibus tuis, omnípotens Deus, et vítia nostra curéntur, et
remédia nobis ætérna provéniant. Per Dóminum nostrum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVII Sunday after Pentecost, Postcommunion}
May our vices be cured, O Almighty God! and an eternal remedy procured for us
by these sacred mysteries. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}
