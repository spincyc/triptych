\section{Scriptural Date and Location}

\zlabel{triptych:concise:chronology:start}
\begin{dossiertable}
Gradual & Ps 32:12, 6 (Heb.\ 33) & No place named & \chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled \latin{Psalmus David} (v.~1): an attribution by title,
with no traditional date or era. A hymn to God's creating word and to the
nation he chose. The date is the Psalter's modern critical boundary; no psalm
in it is securely dated.}
\midrule
Communion & Ps 75:12--13 (Heb.\ 76) & Judea and Sion named as God's dwelling
(vv.~2--3) & \chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled ``a psalm for Asaph: a canticle to the Assyrians''
(v.~1), with no date or era attached. God broke the weapons of war in Sion and
rose to judgment ``to save all the meek of the earth'' (vv.~4, 10). The date is
the Psalter's critical boundary only.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps 101:2 (Heb.\ 102) & Sion named in the psalm's turn to hope
(vv.~14, 17) & \chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled ``The prayer of the poor man, when he was anxious''
(v.~1); no author named, no traditional date held. The fifth penitential psalm,
moving from lament to the rebuilding of Sion. The date is the Psalter's
critical boundary only.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps 118:137, 124, 1 (Heb.\ 119) & No place named &
\chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{An alphabetical psalm in praise of God's law, with no title
beyond \latin{Alleluia}; no author named, no traditional date held. The date
is the Psalter's critical boundary only.}
\midrule
Offertory & Dan 9:17--19, compiled & Daniel at the court of Babylon; for the
Jews of his time and after. Event: first year of Darius the Mede (9:1); the
desolate sanctuary (9:17)
& \chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: Daniel the prophet, in the Exile,
telling his own prayer (``I, Daniel'', 9:2) as the seventy years ended. The
1908 \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} article holding that date sets against it a
more recent critical theory, widely accepted by contemporary scholars, that an
unknown later author composed the book; neither a date nor a place of writing
is given for that position. The place and hearers in the Location column are
that article's grounds for the traditional position.}
\midrule
Gospel & Mt 22:34--46 & Matthew in Judea, before he left Jerusalem; to the Jews
who had believed. Event: the Temple, Jerusalem (21:23) &
\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: the last of Christ's controversies in the Temple
(21:23--22:46), before the Passion is foretold ``after two days'' (26:1--2).}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St Matthew the Apostle (the Missal:
\latin{secundum Matthaeum}); the Date column keeps the
\work{Catholic Encyclopedia} ranges (1911, 1912) as disputed alternatives.
Critics named there date it variously, often later, with no date given. The
same article has the ecclesiastical writers agree that
Matthew wrote for the Jews, and Jerome that he published it in Judea and in
Hebrew, for believing Jews; the departure from Jerusalem is the article's own.
Zahn, quoted without adoption, thinks it meant for Jews not
yet Christians. The Temple above locates the event, not the writing.}
\midrule
Epistle & Eph 4:1--6 & Paul, a prisoner (4:1), at Rome or Caesarea; to the
saints at Ephesus (1:1) &
\chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St Paul, in a letter of his captivity;
the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} dates those letters within the range shown,
Rome or Caesarea open (1909), and its chronology of St Paul gives Ephesians the
year shown (1911). The first hearers are the church at Ephesus (1:1), gentiles
once afar off and now made nigh. Most liberal critics since Schleiermacher have
denied the letter to Paul; no date is given for that position. The same article
holds ``at Ephesus'' to be no part of the primitive text and reads the letter
as a circular, so the address above is the received text.}
\end{dossiertable}
