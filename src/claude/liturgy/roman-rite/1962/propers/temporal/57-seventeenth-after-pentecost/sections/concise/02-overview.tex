\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Jesus answers a doctor of the
law that the whole law hangs on loving God with the whole heart and the
neighbour as oneself, then asks whose son the Christ is and meets the answer
with David's own psalm. Paul begs the Ephesians to keep the unity of the
Spirit; Daniel prays for the desolate sanctuary; the psalms confess God just
and call for vows.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ is David's son
in the flesh and David's Lord from eternity, in the form of the servant and in
the form of God (Augustine, Jerome). The one God makes the heavens firm by his
Word and the Spirit of his mouth (Irenaeus, Basil, Cassiodorus). The poor man
who prays is Christ in his members (Augustine) or the poor of Christ
(Cassiodorus).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Love God leaving no part of life
free for another enjoyment (Augustine) and without measure (Gregory the
Great); love the neighbour for God's sake, and see God loved in him (Aquinas).
Bear the weak and wait with mercy on his defects (Jerome, Aquinas). Confess
God's justice and ask mercy, not reward (Augustine, Hilary).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The one hope of the
calling is the Father's one house with its many mansions (Jerome); the Son sits
at the Father's right hand until every enemy is his footstool (Aquinas); the
blessed nation will possess God and be possessed by him (Augustine). The mercy
the Introit asks is, for Rupert of Deutz, the host's ``Friend, go up
higher.''\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
