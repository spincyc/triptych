# Seventeenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical leaf
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/57-seventeenth-after-pentecost` and
for every output derived from it. It carries the Latin of the controlling
edition exactly as printed, the collation of every element against a second
1962 witness and against the public-domain 1862 antecedent, each element's
relation to the Clementine Vulgate, and the lawful English route for each
element. It contains no study prose.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1602

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Introitum` · `Ps. 118, 137 et 124`;
the psalm verse cited `Ps. ibid., 1`. Vulgate numbering; the common modern number
is **Ps. 119**.

> Iustus es, Dómine, et rectum iudícium tuum: fac cum servo tuo secúndum
> misericórdiam tuam. *Ps. ibid., 1* Beáti immaculáti in via: qui ámbulant in
> lege Dómini. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** printed p. 398, right column, foot, below the heading and rank;
artifact PDF p. 479. A five-line drop-capital `I`. **Checked:** 2026-09-18 at
200, 400 and native 500 ppi.

**Construction.** Verse 137 entire, then the first half of v. 124 only; the
second half (*et iustificatiónes tuas doce me*) is not sung. The join is not
marked. The psalm verse is v. 1. **No repetition cue** is printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation or
reference issue.

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1603

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Da, quǽsumus, Dómine, pópulo tuo diabólica vitáre contágia: et te solum Deum
> pura mente sectári. Per Dóminum nostrum.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, left column, head; artifact PDF p. 480. A
three-line drop-capital `D`. **Checked:** 2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Conclusion.** `Per Dóminum nostrum.` — two words of the long conclusion, not
written out.

**Verification result:** collated. The formulary appoints **one** oration. The
1862 Pustet at the same Sunday adds `Secunda Oratio. A cunctis nos. fol. 315.`
and `Tertia ad libitum.`; those belong to the pre-1955 discipline and are no part
of the 1962 formulary (RGMR 434 b, printed p. XXXI, admits no other oration on a
II-class Sunday beyond the commemoration of a II-class feast, and none is due on
20 September 2026; see `research/context.md`).

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1604

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Ephésios.` · `Ephes. 4, 1-6`.

> Fratres: Obsecro vos ego vinctus in Dómino, ut digne ambulétis vocatióne, qua
> vocáti estis, cum omni humilitáte et mansuetúdine, cum patiéntia, supportántes
> ínvicem in caritáte, sollíciti serváre unitátem spíritus in vínculo pacis.
> Unum corpus, et unus spíritus, sicut vocáti estis in una spe vocatiónis vestræ.
> Unus Dóminus, una fides, unum baptísma. Unus Deus, et Pater ómnium, qui est
> super omnes, et per ómnia, et in ómnibus nobis. Qui est benedíctus in sǽcula
> sæculórum. Amen.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, left column; artifact PDF p. 480. A three-line
drop-capital `F`. **Checked:** 2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Two adaptations, both printed and both to be kept.**

- The pericope opens `Fratres: Obsecro vos`, where the Vulgate reads
  `Obsecro itaque vos`: the liturgical vocative replaces a connective that points
  back to a chapter the Mass does not read.
- After v. 6 the Missal adds `Qui est benedíctus in sǽcula sæculórum. Amen.`,
  which is not part of Ephesians 4. Printed as continuous text with the pericope.

**Lower-case `spíritus`.** Both occurrences (v. 3 `unitátem spíritus`, v. 4
`unus spíritus`) are printed lower-case, as they are in the Benziger and the
1862. The Clementine as tracked prints `Spiritus` capitalized at both. This is
typography, not wording, and it does not decide the reference.

**Verification result:** collated.

## 4. Gradual — marginal no. 1605

**Printed heading and reference:** `Graduale` · `Ps. 32, 12 et 6`. Vulgate
numbering; modern **Ps. 33:12, 6**.

> Beáta gens, cuius est Dóminus Deus eórum: pópulus, quem elégit Dóminus in
> hereditátem sibi. ℣. Verbo Dómini cæli firmáti sunt: et spíritu oris eius
> omnis virtus eórum.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, left column; artifact PDF p. 480. No drop capital.
**Checked:** 2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Order.** Verse 12 is the respond and v. 6 the versicle; the Gradual runs
against the psalm's order.

**The same Gradual elsewhere in this Missal**, each read on the page images:
Wednesday after the Fourth Sunday of Lent, printed p. 110, no. 714, PDF p. 191
(after the lesson Isaiah 1:16-19, *Lavámini, mundi estóte*; the first lesson of
that day, Ezekiel 36, is followed by the other Gradual, *Veníte, fílii*);
Wednesday of the September Ember days, printed p. 401, no. 1618, PDF p. 482
(three days after this Sunday); the votive Mass of the Holy Spirit, printed
p. [57], no. 4613, PDF p. 865. **In the votive Mass of the Holy Spirit the
versicle is printed `et Spíritu oris eius`, with a capital S; at this Sunday
(no. 1605) it is printed `spíritu`, lower-case.** Both readings were taken from
the page images.

**Verification result:** collated. Two departures from the Clementine, tabulated
below.

## 5. Alleluia — marginal no. 1606

**Printed heading and reference:** no separate heading; the versicle is cited in
line, `℣. Ps. 101, 2`. Vulgate numbering. In the Missal's numbering the psalm's
title (*Orátio páuperis…*) is v. 1; Bibles that do not number the title call this
verse **Ps. 102:1**, and those that number it call it Ps. 102:2.

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 101, 2* Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam, et clamor
> meus ad te pervéniat. Allelúia.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, left column; artifact PDF p. 480. **Checked:**
2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Verification result:** collated. `pervéniat` for the Clementine's `veniat`.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1607

**Printed heading and reference:** a cross, then `Sequéntia sancti Evangélii
secúndum Matthǽum.` · `Mt. 22, 34-46`.

> In illo témpore: Accessérunt ad Iesum pharisǽi: et interrogávit eum unus ex eis
> legis doctor, tentans eum: Magíster, quod est mandátum magnum in lege? Ait illi
> Iesus: Díliges Dóminum Deum tuum ex toto corde tuo, et in tota ánima tua, et in
> tota mente tua. Hoc est máximum et primum mandátum. Secúndum autem símile est
> huic: Díliges próximum tuum sicut teípsum. In his duóbus mandátis univérsa lex
> pendet, et prophétæ. Congregátis autem pharisǽis, interrogávit eos Iesus,
> dicens: Quid vobis vidétur de Christo? cuius fílius est? Dicunt ei: David. Ait
> illis: Quómodo ergo David in spíritu vocat eum Dóminum, dicens: Dixit Dóminus
> Dómino meo, sede a dextris meis, donec ponam inimícos tuos scabéllum pedum
> tuórum? Si ergo David vocat eum Dóminum, quómodo fílius eius est? Et nemo
> póterat ei respondére verbum: neque ausus fuit quisquam ex illa die eum
> ámplius interrogáre.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, left column foot running to the right column;
artifact PDF p. 480. A two-line drop-capital `I`. **Checked:** 2026-09-18 at 200,
400 and 500 ppi.

**Opening.** `In illo témpore: Accessérunt ad Iesum pharisǽi: et` stands for the
Vulgate's v. 34, *Pharisæi autem audientes quod silentium imposuisset sadducæis,
convenerunt in unum*, and the connective of v. 35. From `interrogávit eum unus`
the text follows vv. 35–46 to the end.

**`Credo.`** is printed at the right margin of the last line, set apart from the
pericope. It is the rubric appointing the Creed and is not Gospel text.

**Verification result:** collated. The Gospel carries three embedded citations,
Deut 6:5 (v. 37), Lev 19:18 (v. 39) and Ps 109:1 (v. 44); they are not additional
appointed readings.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1608

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Offertorium`; the citation is
printed in line at the head of the text, `Dan. 9, 17, 18 et 19.`

> Orávi Deum meum ego Dániel, dicens: Exáudi, Dómine, preces servi tui: illúmina
> fáciem tuam super sanctuárium tuum: et propítius inténde pópulum istum, super
> quem invocátum est nomen tuum, Deus.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, right column; artifact PDF p. 480. **Checked:**
2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Construction.** Not a continuous quotation. The narrative frame (*Orávi Deum
meum ego Dániel, dicens*) lies outside vv. 17–19 (compare the Vulgate's *Et oravi
Dominum Deum meum … et dixi* at 9:4 and *ego Daniel* at 9:2), and the petitions
condense vv. 17–19. See *The Offertory's wording* below for the one textual
source this audit could test.

**Verse history.** The 1962 Missal prints the antiphon without verses. Two
verses once sung with it — *Adhuc me loquénte et oránte …* (from Dan 9:20) and
*Audívi vocem dicéntem mihi: Dániel, intéllige …* ending *nam et Míchael venit in
adiutórium meum* (from Dan 10) — are reported by Guéranger (*The Liturgical
Year*, vol. XI, pp. 384–385) and the first is cited by Rupert of Deutz (*De
divinis officiis* XII.17, PL 170); neither is part of this formulary.

**Verification result:** collated.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1609

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Maiestátem tuam, Dómine, supplíciter deprecámur: ut hæc sancta, quæ gérimus, et
> a prætéritis nos delíctis éxuant, et futúris. Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum
> Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.

Followed, on its own line, by the direction `Præfatio de Ssm̃a Trinitate.`
(abbreviation mark over the `m`, as printed).

**Witness:** printed p. 399, right column; artifact PDF p. 480. A two-line
drop-capital `M`. **Checked:** 2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Conclusion.** Printed at length only as far as `in unitáte.`, which is itself
an abbreviation: the full conclusion, in Rubricae generales n. 115 a (printed
p. XIX, PDF p. 17, read on the page image at 400 dpi), is `Per Dóminum nostrum
Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum, qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte Spíritus
Sancti, Deus, per ómnia sǽcula sæculórum. Amen`. How far a conclusion is printed
is a typographic variable of the edition, and RG 115 governs the conclusion in
use. The typical edition prints the same `… regnat in unitáte` form at many
orations (a literal count of the facsimile's text layer finds 288 occurrences of
`regnat in unit`, the rubric's own included), among them the Fifteenth Sunday's
Secret, no. 1589, printed p. 397 (PDF p. 478, read on the page image) and, in
the text layer, its Postcommunion, no. 1591, on the same page; and it prints
`Per Dóminum.` at others, among them the Sixteenth Sunday's Secret, no. 1599,
printed p. 398 (PDF p. 479, read on the page image). The Benziger conformed to
the typica prints this Secret's conclusion as `Per Dóminum.` (table above).
All three orations of this Mass are printed with a conclusion beginning `Per
Dóminum nostrum`, the form RG 115 a gives for a prayer addressed to the Father
with no mention of the Son at its beginning or end, so the Collect, the Secret
and the Postcommunion conclude identically when said. The printed length marks nothing
distinctive about this Secret.

**Verification result:** collated.

> reges terræ.

**Witness:** printed p. 399, right column, foot; artifact PDF p. 480. **Checked:**
2026-09-18 at 200, 400 and 500 ppi.

**Verification result:** collated. `apud omnes reges terræ` against the
Clementine's `apud reges terræ`.

column of printed p. 341 to the right column of printed p. 342, where `Feria
Quarta / Quatuor Temp. Septembris.` follows. **Every one of the ten elements
agrees with the 1962 in wording.** The differences are of orthography, pointing,

### Where the chant wording has a witness

This audit tested the chant readings that depart from the Clementine against the
psalm lemmata of two Latin commentators whose psalters are older than the
Clementine. It did not consult a critical edition of the Roman or any other Latin
psalter.

| Reading | Augustine, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* | Cassiodorus, *Expositio psalmorum* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ps 32:12 `Dominus Deus eorum` | `eorum` (En. in Ps. 32 I, 12; II s. 2, 15–18) | `eorum` (on v. 12) |
| Ps 32:12 `elegit Dominus` | `elegit Dominus` (En. I, 12) | `elegit` without `Dominus` |
| Ps 101:2 `perveniat` | `perveniat` (En. in Ps. 101 s. 1, 3) | `perveniat` (on v. 2 [his v. 1]) |
| Ps 75:13 `apud omnes reges` | `apud reges terrae` (En. in Ps. 75, 18) | `apud reges terrae` (on v. 13 [his v. 12]) |
| Ps 75:12 `Deo vestro`, `affertis` | `Deo nostro`, `offerent` (En. in Ps. 75, 16–17) | `Deo vestro`, `offertis` (on v. 12 [his v. 11]) |

So the Gradual's `eorum … elegit Dominus` and the Alleluia's `perveniat` stand in
the psalter Augustine expounded; the Communion's `omnes` before `reges` is found
in neither commentator and remains unexplained within this audit. The editions
read are listed in `research/scope.md`.

### The Offertory's wording

One of the Offertory's two departures from the Vulgate separates the versions.
At 9:17 the Missal's `illúmina fáciem tuam super sanctuárium tuum` agrees with
the Greek of Daniel in Theodotion's version as Swete prints it (First1KGreek
digitisation, `57.Daniel_Theodotionis_versio.txt`), `ἐπίφανον τὸ πρόσωπόν σου
ἐπὶ τὸ ἁγίασμά σου`, "cause thy face to shine upon thy sanctuary". The Vulgate
reads `ostende faciem tuam`, and the Old Greek (`56.Daniel_translatio_Graeca.txt`)
reads `ἐπιβλεψάτω τὸ πρόσωπόν σου ἐπὶ τὸ ὄρος τὸ ἅγιόν σου`. The other departure
does not separate them. At 9:19 the Missal's `propítius inténde` differs from the
Vulgate's `placare … attende` in form, but the Vulgate renders the same two
imperatives as Theodotion's `ἱλάσθητι … πρόσχες`, and the Old Greek has
`πρόσχες` and `ἱλάτευσον` at 9:18. Brenton's English of the Greek Daniel
(tracked) reads "cause thy face to shine" and "be propitious … attend". The
agreement at 9:17 is with Theodotion against the Vulgate and the Old Greek, but
it does not by itself point to Theodotion: the Revised Version, which translates
the Masoretic text, also reads "cause thy face to shine upon thy sanctuary that is
desolate" at 9:17 (tracked verse text), and the Hebrew itself was not consulted.
Nothing here identifies which Latin version the chant's compiler used; no Old
Latin witness of Daniel was consulted. Swete's two files were re-fetched on the
research stage's second reentry and matched the recorded digests.

## Discrepancies and open items carried forward

1. **Benziger misprints in the Collect** (`Deus … sectária`). A defect of that
   book, recorded above. Any finding aid built from the Benziger text layer
   inherits it.
2. **The Communion's `omnes` before `reges`.** Unexplained by the two psalm
   commentators checked; the Roman psalter and the chant manuscripts were not
   consulted.
3. **The Offertory's Latin source.** At 9:17 the chant agrees with Theodotion's
   Greek, and with the Hebrew's sense as the Revised Version renders it, against
   the Vulgate and the Old Greek; at 9:19 its wording separates no version. The
   Latin version behind the compilation is not identified.
4. **English for the adapted elements.** Only the canonical Douay verses and the
   registered Cummiskey orations and preface are available. The Cummiskey English
   of the chants, the Epistle's doxology and the Gospel's opening exists in the
   registered scan but has no passage record.
5. **No correction to `research/context.md` was required.** Its inventory,
   boundaries, loci and rubrical appointments were confirmed on the page images.
   One clarification: the Introit's repetition after `Glória Patri` is prescribed
   by RGMR 427 and is not printed in this formulary.
