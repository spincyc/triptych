\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Paul hands on what he was handed: Christ died, was buried, rose, was seen \cue{Ep.}. Mark narrates one healing in Gentile country --- fingers, spittle, a sigh, one word --- and a crowd that will not keep quiet \cue{Gosp.}. The Communion quotes Israel's wisdom on firstfruits \cue{Comm.}.\\
Allegorical & St.~Bede reads the fingers as the Holy Spirit and the spittle as divine wisdom on a catechumen's tongue \cue{Gosp.}; St.~Augustine glosses \latin{reflóruit caro mea} as \latin{resurréxit caro mea} \cue{Grad.}; St.~Jerome reads the Offertory psalm's title, the dedication of David's house, as the Resurrection \cue{Off.}.\\
Moral & Bede reads the firstfruits as those of \textit{every} good work, honoured to God by crediting it to grace \cue{Comm.} --- which is the Epistle's \latin{grátia Dei sum id quod sum} in the first person \cue{Ep.}. Theophylact draws from the command of silence an ethic of benefaction without applause \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Anagogical & St.~Hilary, Augustine and St.~Robert Bellarmine read the Introit's power and strength of the resurrection of the body \cue{Int.}; Bede reads the filled barns as heavenly satiety \cue{Comm.}; Schuster reads help to mind and body as the pledge of the final resurrection \cue{Postcomm.}.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
