# Notable-and-quotable audit — Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost

Hunted and verified under the cultural-afterlife rule on 2026-08-19. Straight
exegesis, devotional reuse, bare titles, mottoes without a register turn,
artworks and musical settings as such were excluded by rule. All chant wording
below is controlled by `propers/verified.md` (facsimile-collated), not by any
web transcription; the Divinum Officium text consulted during the hunt is a
community transcription and controls nothing.

A caution raised during the hunt is already settled by this leaf's collation:
at Ps 73:22 the Clementine reads `Exsurge, Deus, judica causam tuam` while the
chant sings `exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam` — divergence row 3 of
`propers/verified.md`, checked against the Clementine at drbo.org. The bull's
incipit matches the liturgical/Old-Latin form, not the Clementine.

## Material negative results

1. **Lk 17:17 "Were not ten made clean? and where are the nine?" — no
   verified non-devotional afterlife found.** Systematic hunt: Library of
   Congress Chronicling America ("but where are the nine": 2,814 pages;
   ~150 sampled contexts were sermons, church notices, Thanksgiving homilies —
   e.g. Wheeling Register 12 Sep 1898 p. 5; Edgefield Advertiser 1 Jul 1914,
   the closest miss, still homiletic); "the tenth leper" (10 hits, all
   church-service listings); UK Hansard API: zero exact matches ever; US bound
   Congressional Record via GovInfo: sole hit is the Senate chaplain's opening
   prayer, 2 May 1966 — devotional; Wikisource insource: scripture and
   patristics only; open-web searches surface only sermons (Spurgeon, "Where
   Are the Nine? or, Praise Neglected," etc.). The famous question's afterlife
   stayed stubbornly in the pulpit — itself a publishable observation, not an
   entry.
2. **Gal 3:16 "Non dicit: Et seminibus … sed quasi in uno" — nothing
   verified.** Searches for a lawyerly or critical register change returned
   only exegesis. Unverified lane, not a finding.
3. **Wis 16:20 "omne delectamentum" jocose secular use — open lead,
   unverified.** Google Books API quota-exhausted (HTTP 429), HathiTrust and
   archive.org full-text search blocked; a whisky-book lead (Barnard 1887)
   checked and disconfirmed. Joyce covers the pericope better anyway.
4. **"Tempora mea in manibus tuis" as sundial motto — negative.** Gatty, *The
   Book of Sun-Dials* (4th ed. 1900, archive.org full text): no "tempora
   mea," "manibus tuis," or "times are in thy hand" among ~1,600 recorded
   mottoes. A pretty hypothesis, killed by the data.
5. Blocked venues, recorded so the hunt is reproducible: loc.gov guides,
   chroniclingamerica ocr.txt endpoints, parliament.uk, IWM, web.archive.org,
   divinumofficium.com (all Cloudflare/403 from the research environment);
   workarounds used: LOC JSON API, GitHub source of Divinum Officium,
   archive.org djvu texts.
