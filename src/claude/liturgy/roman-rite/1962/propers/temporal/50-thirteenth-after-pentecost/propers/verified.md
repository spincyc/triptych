# Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical full research edition at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/50-thirteenth-after-pentecost` and
its mechanical `-synthesis` PDF companion. The canonical edition prints the
complete Latin below beside the identified English; the synthesis companion
omits the appointed-text component.

## Provenance

- **Controlling edition.** *Missale Romanum ex decreto Sacrosancti Concilii
  Tridentini restitutum, Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum*, editio typica
  (Vatican City: Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis, 1962). Source-library identity
  `edition.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-1962`.
- **Printed formulary heading.** `DOMINICA / DECIMA TERTIA / post Pentecosten`
  (two lines of large capitals, then upper-and-lower case).
- **Printed rank.** `II classis`.
- **Colour.** Green. The formulary itself prints no colour; green follows the
  temporal-season rule of the *Rubricae generales Missalis Romani* (1960) for
  Sundays after Pentecost, not a rubric on these pages. Recorded here as an
  inference from the general rubrics, not as a reading of pp. 393–394.
- **Printed location.** Printed pages 393–394. The formulary opens in the right
  column of p. 393, below the Postcommunion of the Twelfth Sunday, and ends in
  the right column of p. 394, above the heading of the Fourteenth Sunday.
  Marginal reference numbers 1562–1571 number its ten elements in order.
- **Controlling witness.** CMAA-hosted facsimile PDF,
  <https://media.churchmusicassociation.org/pdf/missale62.pdf>, artifact
  `artifact.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-1962.cmaa-facsimile-pdf`.
  SHA-256 `648fdb8fe830ed65a08aa4a95de6f94424c533ddf2398c8fc26b18735fd3518a`,
  82,815,941 bytes, 1,088 PDF pages. Printed pp. 393–394 are PDF pp. 474–475.
  The retained copy used for this collation was hashed and matched that
  registered digest on 2026-08-19 before any reading was taken from it.
- **Collation method.** The two PDF pages were rendered at 300 dpi for the
  whole-page reading and at 600 dpi for every disputed letter, accent, and
  punctuation mark. No transcription, OCR layer, or web text was allowed to
  supply a reading.
- **Second witness (independent edition, not merely a second image).**
  *Missale Romanum*, editio iuxta typicam (New York: Benziger Brothers, Inc.,
  1962), edition
  `edition.catholic-church.missale-romanum.benziger-iuxta-typicam-1962`,
  as digitised in Internet Archive item `MissaleRomanum1962RomanMissalColorLatin`
  (ARK `ark:/13960/t9n32mg8j`). Page images were read at leaves `n464`–`n465`,
  which carry printed pp. 388–389; the formulary occupies those two printed
  pages. The scan bears a `FATIMAMOVEMENT.COM` footer watermark on every leaf.
  The item's DjVuTXT OCR (SHA-256
  `2a2da44d4f2054ffd9fa5a0fa87ec427fe2a99410f438112282eed82254585ce`) was used

- **Status of the second witness.** Benziger 1962 is a *different edition* of
  the same 1962 revision, not an independent image of the Vatican typical
  edition. Where the two disagree, the difference below is an **edition
  difference**, not an uncertain reading in either. Nothing is blended, and no
  Benziger form is substituted for a Vatican typical form.
- **Three damaged readings resolved against the second witness.** In the CMAA
  scan, ink loss touches three words of the Gospel's left column on printed
  p. 394: the *m* of `magna`, the first *c* of `cécidit`, and the *ti* of
  `grátias`. All three were read at 600 dpi in the typical edition and
  confirmed letter for letter against the Benziger page images, and all three
  are the Vulgate's own wording at Lk. 17:15–16. They are printed below
  without brackets because the readings are secure; the damage is in the scan,
  not in the text.
- **Verification status and date.** All ten elements, their headings,
  scriptural references, wording, accentuation, punctuation, the printed rank,
  the `Credo` rubric, the Preface rubric, and both formulary boundaries were
  visually collated against the Vatican typical facsimile on **2026-08-19**,

- **Formulary boundary.** The formulary begins immediately after the
  Postcommunion of the Twelfth Sunday after Pentecost (marginal no. 1561,

  between those two boundaries is omitted here.
- **Scope of this record.** The reusable temporal formulary only. Occurrence
  for a particular civil year, commemorations, particular calendars, and

## Discrepancies between the two 1962 witnesses

Recorded, not harmonised. Every Vatican typical reading below is what this
guide prints.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1562

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Introitum` · `Ps. 73, 20, 19
et 23` (Vulgate numbering; the same verses are Ps. 74:20, 19, 23 in the
Hebrew numbering). The psalm verse is cited `Ps. ibid., 1`.

> Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum, et ánimas páuperum tuórum ne
> derelínquas in finem: exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam, et ne
> obliviscáris voces quæréntium te. *Ps. ibid., 1* Ut quid, Deus, repulísti in
> finem: irátus est furor tuus super oves páscuæ tuæ? ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, right column, below the
formulary heading; PDF p. 474. **Checked:** 2026-08-19, with the drop-cap
opening and the verse re-read at 600 dpi.

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** The antiphon is a centonisation. Its
first clause is v. 20a with `Dómine` added; its second is v. 19b with the
beasts clause of v. 19a omitted; `exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam`
corresponds to v. 22a although the printed reference names only vv. 20, 19
and 23; and the close reworks v. 23a. The psalm verse is v. 1 exactly as the
Clementine has it, question mark included. Verified as printed; the printed
reference is reproduced as printed and not corrected.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation,
or reference issue. The OCR finding aid's stray `*` before `in finem` is a
recognition artifact; no asterisk is printed.

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1563

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, da nobis fidei, spei et caritátis augméntum: et,
> ut mereámur ássequi quod promíttis, fac nos amáre quod prǽcipis. Per
> Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, right column; PDF
p. 474. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi for `fidei` (no printed accent),
`ássequi`, `prǽcipis`, and the punctuation.

**Note on punctuation.** The colon after `augméntum`, the comma after `et`,
and the comma after `promíttis` are as printed and are load-bearing: the
purpose clause `ut mereámur ássequi quod promíttis` is suspended between `et`
and the imperative `fac nos amáre quod prǽcipis`, so that the obtaining of
what is promised depends on the loving of what is commanded.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1564

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Gálatas.` · `Gal. 3, 16-22`.

> Fratres: Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes, et sémini eius. Non dicit: Et
> semínibus, quasi in multis; sed quasi in uno: Et sémini tuo, qui est
> Christus. Hoc autem dico: testaméntum confirmátum a Deo, quæ post
> quadringéntos et trigínta annos facta est lex, non írritum facit ad
> evacuándam promissiónem. Nam si ex lege heréditas, iam non ex promissióne.
> Abrahæ autem per repromissiónem donávit Deus. Quid ígitur lex? Propter
> transgressiónes pósita est, donec veníret semen, cui promíserat, ordináta
> per Angelos in manu mediatóris. Mediátor autem uníus non est: Deus autem
> unus est. Lex ergo advérsus promíssa Dei? Absit. Si enim data esset lex, quæ
> posset vivificáre, vere ex lege esset iustítia. Sed conclúsit Scriptúra
> ómnia sub peccáto, ut promíssio ex fide Iesu Christi darétur credéntibus.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 393, right column, continuing
at the head of p. 394, left column; PDF pp. 474–475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19,
the continuation re-read at 600 dpi.

**Notes.** The liturgical incipit `Fratres:` stands where the Clementine's
v. 16 begins directly `Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes`; Paul's own `Fratres`
belongs to v. 15, which is outside the lesson, so the incipit borrows the
address without borrowing the verse. The pericope ends at v. 22: the
law-as-pedagogue image of vv. 23–25 is *outside* the appointed lesson.
`heréditas` is the typical edition's spelling (Clementine `hæréditas`);
`Angelos` is printed with a capital.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 4. Gradual — marginal no. 1565

**Printed heading and reference:** `Graduale` · `Ps. 73, 20, 19 et 22`.

> Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum: et ánimas páuperum tuórum ne
> obliviscáris in finem. ℣. Exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam: memor esto
> oppróbrii servórum tuórum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, left column, below the
Epistle; PDF p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi.

**Note.** The Gradual sings the same two verses the Introit opens with, but
where the Introit's v. 19b has `ne derelínquas`, the Gradual has
`ne obliviscáris` — the Clementine's own verb. The verse's second clause,
`memor esto oppróbrii servórum tuórum`, is not the Clementine's v. 22b; see
the divergence table below.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1567

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Lucam.` · `Luc. 17, 11-19`.

> In illo témpore: Dum iret Iesus in Ierúsalem, transíbat per médiam Samaríam
> et Galilǽam. Et cum ingrederétur quoddam castéllum, occurrérunt ei decem
> viri leprósi, qui stetérunt a longe; et levavérunt vocem dicéntes: Iesu
> præcéptor, miserére nostri. Quos ut vidit, dixit: Ite, osténdite vos
> sacerdótibus. Et factum est, dum irent, mundáti sunt. Unus autem ex illis,
> ut vidit quia mundátus est, regréssus est, cum magna voce magníficans Deum,
> et cécidit in fáciem ante pedes eius, grátias agens: et hic erat
> Samaritánus. Respóndens autem Iesus, dixit: Nonne decem mundáti sunt? et
> novem ubi sunt? Non est invéntus qui redíret, et daret glóriam Deo, nisi hic
> alienígena. Et ait illi: Surge, vade; quia fides tua te salvum fecit.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Credo.` (printed at the end of the last
Gospel line, right-aligned).

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, left column; PDF p. 475.
**Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi for `miserére`, and for the three
scan-damaged words `magna`, `cécidit` and `grátias`, each confirmed against
the Benziger page images.

**Notes.** The pericope is the complete episode: v. 11 opens it and v. 19
closes it, and vv. 20–37 (the coming of the kingdom) are outside the appointed
Gospel. The incipit `In illo témpore: Dum iret Iesus in Ierúsalem` replaces
the Clementine's `Et factum est, dum iret in Ierúsalem`, supplying the subject
`Iesus` that the evangelist's sentence carries over from the preceding
narrative.

**Verification result:** collated; the three damaged readings are resolved as
recorded under Provenance; no unresolved issue remains.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1568

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Offertorium` · `Ps. 30, 15-16`
(Vulgate numbering; Hebrew Ps. 31:15–16). The reference is printed at the head
of the text line.

> In te sperávi, Dómine; dixi: Tu es Deus meus, in mánibus tuis témpora mea.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column, below the
`Credo` rubric; PDF p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi.

**Note.** The antiphon differs from the Clementine at three points recorded in
the divergence table below, of which `témpora mea` against the Clementine's
`sortes meæ` is the most consequential for the English.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1569

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Propitiáre, Dómine, pópulo tuo, propitiáre munéribus: ut, hac oblatióne
> placátus, et indulgéntiam nobis tríbuas, et postuláta concédas. Per Dóminum
> nostrum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column; PDF
p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi. A scan speck before `nobis` was
examined at 600 dpi and is not a printed mark.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.` — the Preface
of the Most Holy Trinity, printed with `Ssma` under a tilde abbreviation for
`Sanctissima`. Expanded here from the same book's own abbreviation practice.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1570

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Communionem` · `Sap. 16, 20`.

> Panem de cælo dedísti nobis, Dómine, habéntem omne delectaméntum, et omnem
> sapórem suavitátis.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column; PDF
p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi.

**Note.** The antiphon is an adaptation, not a quotation: the Clementine's
third-person `parátum panem de cælo præstitísti illis sine labóre` becomes a
first-person-plural address, `dedísti nobis, Dómine`, and the two closing
phrases are recast. The details are in the divergence table below and the
declared English gaps that follow it.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion — marginal no. 1571

**Printed heading:** `Postcommunio`.

> Sumptis, Dómine, cæléstibus sacraméntis: ad redemptiónis ætérnæ, quæsumus,
> proficiámus augméntum. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column; PDF
p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi.

**Note.** `redemptiónis ætérnæ … augméntum` brackets the petition: the genitive
phrase is split around `quæsumus`, and the prayer asks growth *toward* the
redemption already named — the same noun, `augméntum`, that the Collect asked
for the three virtues.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## Appointed text against the Bible text: the divergences

Checked against the Clementine Vulgate as presented at drbo.org (Latin beside
the Douay-Rheims) on 2026-08-19. The Clementine is the comparison text because
it is the Bible whose English the guide quotes; derivation of the chant
wording from any particular older psalter is **not** asserted here, no
*Psalterium Romanum* witness having been collated.

| # | Proper and locus | Appointed | Clementine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Int. & Grad., Ps. 73:20 | `Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum` | `Respice in testamentum tuum` (no `Domine`); the verse continues `quia repleti sunt…`, which neither chant uses |
| 2 | Int., Ps. 73:19 | `ne derelínquas in finem` | `ne obliviscaris in finem` — the Gradual keeps the Clementine's verb; the Introit sings `derelínquas` |
| 3 | Int. & Grad., Ps. 73:22 | `exsúrge, Dómine, et iúdica causam tuam` | `Exsurge, Deus, judica causam tuam` (`Deus`, and no `et`) |
| 4 | Int., Ps. 73:23 | `et ne obliviscáris voces quæréntium te` | `Ne obliviscaris voces inimicorum tuorum` — the seekers stand where the Clementine has the enemies |
| 5 | Grad., Ps. 73:22 | `memor esto oppróbrii servórum tuórum` | `memor esto improperiorum tuorum, eorum quae ab insipiente sunt tota die` — the chant clause is instead verbally identical, minus `Dómine`, with Ps. 88:51 `Memor esto, Domine, opprobrii servorum tuorum`; the printed reference nevertheless says Ps. 73, and this record reports the fact without asserting a source |
| 6 | All., Ps. 89:1 | `a generatióne et progénie` | `a generatione in generationem` |
| 7 | Off., Ps. 30:15 | `In te sperávi` opens the antiphon | `Ego autem in te speravi` (the antiphon drops `Ego autem`) |
| 8 | Off., Ps. 30:15 | `Tu es Deus meus` | `Deus meus es tu` (word order) |
| 9 | Off., Ps. 30:16 | `in mánibus tuis témpora mea` | `in manibus tuis sortes meae` — *my times* where the Clementine has *my lots*; the antiphon here agrees with the Hebrew (`‘ittōtāy`, "my times") against the Clementine |
| 10 | Comm., Sap. 16:20 | `Panem de cælo dedísti nobis, Dómine` | `paratum panem de caelo praestitisti illis sine labore` — person, verb and address all recast; `dedisti` is the verb of Ps. 77:24 `panem caeli dedit eis` rather than of Wis. 16:20 |
| 11 | Comm., Sap. 16:20 | `habéntem omne delectaméntum, et omnem sapórem suavitátis` | `omne delectamentum in se habentem, et omnis saporis suavitatem` (participle moved forward, `in se` dropped, the last phrase's genitive inverted) |
| 12 | Gosp., Lk. 17:11 | `In illo témpore: Dum iret Iesus in Ierúsalem` | `Et factum est, dum iret in Jerusalem` — the incipit drops Luke's connective and supplies the subject |

**Three negative results belong with the table.** The Epistle (Gal. 3:16–22)
and the Gospel (Lk. 17:12–19, after the incipit) match the Clementine word for
word, apart from the i/j and æ orthography and pointing; and the Introit's
psalm verse (Ps. 73:1) matches it exactly, question mark included.

## Rights

The 1962 typical edition's own copyright status is treated per
`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`,
recorded 2026-08-01: unresolved, and more likely than not restored in the
United States under 17 U.S.C. §104A to the end of 2057; the CC BY-NC-ND tag on
the Internet Archive copy of the facsimile confers nothing.

The basis for reproducing the Latin above is 17 U.S.C. §103(b): the copyright
in a revised edition extends only to the material that edition contributed and
"does not affect or enlarge the scope, duration, ownership, or subsistence of"
any copyright in the preexisting material. This formulary is preexisting
material of the 1962 edition. This is the project's own reading of the statute
and of the evidence, not a clearance anyone granted, and it is not legal
advice.

are printed, in the same order and at the same scriptural references, in a
Missal this repository tracks as public-domain text, and six of the ten in a
second one:

**Bounds of this check.** It was made at the level of element, printed
reference and incipit against uncorrected OCR text layers, and it is not a
word-level collation. Both witnesses print the older orthography and element
headings rather than the 1962 book's, and neither carries the 1962 rank or
marginal numbering: those are edition-specific matter and are no part of this
finding. Nothing here is quoted from either witness; they are read as finding
aids for the presence of the texts.

The project claims no rights in the received liturgical text and has not
composed, translated, modernised, conflated, or adapted any of it. Both
digitisations used above are third-party artifacts whose own rights status the
source library records as **unresolved**; neither is redistributed here, and
only focused extracts and citations are kept.

Every English rendering of an appointed text in this guide comes from one of
two registered public-domain witnesses, named at the point of use:

- **Scriptural elements** — the Douay-Rheims as revised by Bishop Challoner
  (1749–1752), edition
  `edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
  which is the English of the Clementine Vulgate this missal prints. Loci
  quoted: Ps. 30:15–16; 73:1, 19–20, 22–23; 89:1; Sap. 16:20; Lk. 17:11–19;
  Gal. 3:16–22; with Ps. 88:51 quoted once in the divergence apparatus.
- **Orations** — the anonymous English of the 1861 Philadelphia Cummiskey
  *Roman Missal … for the use of the laity*, edition
  `edition.eugene-cummiskey.roman-missal-english-laity.philadelphia-1861`,
  filed by 1962 formulary as `post-pentecosten-13` and confirmed on the
  printed Latin incipits and clause-for-clause correspondence.

**Declared places where the registered English does not answer the Latin
above.** They are declared in the guide and are not filled in by the project.

1. The Introit's `ne derelínquas` — the Douay renders the Clementine's
   `ne obliviscaris`, "forget not"; the missal's *forsake not* has no
   registered English.
2. The Introit's `voces quæréntium te` — the Douay reads "the voices of thy
   enemies," the Clementine's text; the chant's *seekers* have no registered
   English.
3. The Introit's and Gradual's `exsúrge, Dómine` — the Douay reads "Arise,
   O God."
4. The Gradual verse's `memor esto oppróbrii servórum tuórum` — the Douay at
   Ps. 73:22 renders the Clementine's different clause; the Douay wording that
   answers the chant is at Ps. 88:51, "the reproach of thy servants," and the
   guide quotes it only as apparatus, never as the verse the missal cites.
5. The Alleluia's `a generatióne et progénie` — the Douay's "from generation
   to generation" renders the Clementine's `in generationem`.
6. The Offertory's `témpora mea` — the Douay reads "My lots are in thy hands."
   The guide prints the two Douay verses whole with the antiphon's fragments
   marked, and supplies no rendering of *times*.
7. The Communion — an adapted centonisation, so the Douay verse is printed
   whole with the reused phrases marked rather than joined into an English
   antiphon the project would itself have composed.
8. The liturgical incipits `Fratres:` and `In illo témpore: Dum iret Iesus in
   Ierúsalem` are the missal's own and are likewise not translated.
