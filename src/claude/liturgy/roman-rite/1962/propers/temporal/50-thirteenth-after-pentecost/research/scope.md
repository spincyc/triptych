# Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

Audit record for the canonical guide at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/50-thirteenth-after-pentecost` and
its mechanical `-synthesis` companion. It records what was searched, what was
found, what was checked, what was not found, and what the published wording is
therefore bounded by. It is an audit record, not a diary, and it does not
repeat what the guide itself prints.

Companion records in this leaf: `propers/verified.md` (facsimile-collated
appointed text and the divergence tables), `research/source-bindings.toml`
(what the reusable source library controls and how), and the six element-level
records — `epistle-galatians-3.md`, `gospel-luke-17.md`, `psalmody.md`,
`orations-and-communion.md`, `notable-quotable-audit.md` and
`page-2-dossier-facts.md` — which carry the witness-by-witness detail this file
summarises.

## 0. Two outputs, one study

The leaf is componentized. `main.tex` with `proper-components.toml` produces
the canonical **Full PDF** and the sole web edition; `synthesis.tex` produces
the mechanical **Synthesis PDF** from the same source. The synthesis companion
owns no prose, no research record, and no source audit of its own: it omits the
complete appointed texts and the element-by-element sweep, and replaces the
latter with one integrated cross-proper commentary. Every finding below is
common to both outputs.

Per-element `proper-treatment` appears only in `research` mode and exactly one
`integrated-commentary` only in `synthesis` mode, as the profile requires for a
new guide. `appointed_text_completeness` is `complete`.

## 1. The deliverable, as promised

The requested deliverable was the complete componentized guide for this
formulary through installed Full and Synthesis PDFs and an installed web
edition. This record notes one thing about how it was reached, because the
history is visible in the tracked generation metadata: the collation, the
element-level reception research on the Epistle, Gospel, orations and
Communion antiphon, the cultural-afterlife hunt, and the first drafts of the
map, dossier sheet, appointed-text component, thematic movement, quotation
gallery and interpretive proposals were made under one model; the psalmody
reception research, the detailed commentary, both synthesis sections, the
terminal apparatus, this record, the source bindings, the build, the page
review and the web edition were made under another. Both are declared.

## 2. Formulary identity and text control

**Identity.** `DOMINICA DECIMA TERTIA post Pentecosten`, `II classis`, printed
pp. 393–394 of the 1962 Vatican `editio typica`, marginal nos. 1562–1571.
`Credo` and the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity are appointed; no Tract,
Sequence, second oration, blessing or ritual text is. Green is **inferred**
from the 1960 general rubrics and is not printed on those pages; the inference
is labelled as one in `verified.md` and in the guide's appendix.

**Boundaries.** After the Postcommunion of the Twelfth Sunday (no. 1561),
before the heading of the Fourteenth (no. 1572). Nothing between them is
omitted.

**Witnesses.** The CMAA facsimile of the Vatican typical edition controls every
published Latin form, read at 300 dpi whole-page and 600 dpi for every disputed
mark; the retained copy was hashed against the registered artifact digest
before any reading was taken. Page images of the 1962 Benziger `editio iuxta
typicam` are the second **edition** witness — not a second photograph — and
nine differences were found and are published as edition differences. Three
scan-damaged Gospel words (`magna`, `cécidit`, `grátias`) were resolved letter
by letter against Benziger. Internet Archive OCR located the formulary and
controls no wording.

**Twelve divergences from the Clementine** are tabulated in `verified.md` and
published in the commentary. Five fall in the two Psalm 73 chants; three in the
Offertory; two in the Communion; one each in the Alleluia and the Gospel
incipit. Three negative results belong with them: the Epistle matches the
Clementine word for word, the Gospel matches after the liturgical incipit, and
the Introit's psalm verse matches exactly.

**What is deliberately not asserted.** No `Psalterium Romanum` or other older
psalter witness was collated, so no derivation of any chant reading from a
particular psalter is claimed anywhere. The Gradual verse's second clause is
verbally identical with Ps. 88:51 rather than with the Ps. 73:22b the missal
cites; the guide reports the fact and does not correct the missal's printed
reference. The 1862 Pustet and 1570 Venice printings were read as finding aids
for the rights check at the level of element, reference and incipit only; they
are not word-level collations and nothing is quoted from them.

## 3. Corpora, languages, and method

Latin, Greek and English. The searches below were run between 19 and 20 August
2026. Every witness retained for publication was opened at its own work and
locus; nothing rests on a catena, aggregator or quotation site alone, and where
a text was verified in an electronic transmission without a column-bearing scan
that limitation is stated at the point of use.

**On the Epistle (Gal. 3:16–22).** Jerome, *Comm. in Gal.* lib. II (PL 26);
Augustine, *Expositio ep. ad Galatas* §§23–25 (PL 35); Chrysostom, *Comm. on
Galatians* c. 3 (PG 61, NPNF 1/13); Ambrosiaster (PL 17); Theodoret, *Interp.

**On the Gospel (Lk. 17:11–19).** Augustine, *Quaestiones Evangeliorum* II.40

Alexandria's surviving catena fragments (Payne Smith 1859); Ambrose, *Exp.

**On the appointed psalms (Ps. 30:15–16; 73:1, 19–20, 22–23; 89:1).** Augustine,

**On the Communion (Wis. 16:20).** Rabanus Maurus, *Comm. in librum
Sapientiae* III.9 (PL 109, 748–749); Ambrose, *De mysteriis* 8.44–49 (NPNF
2/10) as a tested and rejected witness to the wording; the *Rituale Romanum*

**On the three orations.** Feltoe's *Sacramentarium Leonianum* (1896);
Wilson's *Gelasian Sacramentary* (1894) with its apparatus; Wilson's
*Gregorian Sacramentary under Charles the Great* (HBS 49, 1915) with its

**Not claimed.** No exhaustive search of Syriac originals, chant manuscripts,
untranslated homiliaries, subscription databases, or current specialist
monographs. Deshusses and Mohlberg were not inspected — they are protected
editions — so no GeV, Ha or Sup numbering is claimed anywhere; Wilson's and
Feltoe's printed pages are the locators throughout. Pius Parsch treats this
Sunday and is in copyright; his existence is noted and nothing was consulted.

## 5. The psalmody sweep

Recorded separately because it was searched last and because it produced the
guide's most consequential textual findings. The detailed record, with every
locus, lemma, quotation and retrieval fact, is `research/psalmody.md`; what
follows is its audit summary.

**Witnesses opened.** Augustine, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* 73, 89 and 30 (four
expositions of Ps. 30, not three; Latin at augustinus.it, public-domain English
in NPNF 1/8 where it exists); Cassiodorus, *Expositio Psalmorum* at all three
psalms (Latin complete at monumenta.ch); Jerome, *Commentarioli in Psalmos* and
both series of the *Tractatus sive homiliae*, all three Morin volumes
downloaded whole; Theodoret, *Interpretatio in Psalmos* (PG 80) at all three
psalms, in two independent electronic transmissions of the Greek; the *Glossa
ordinaria* on the Psalter in the Gloss-e electronic edition; Bellarmine's
*Explanatio in Psalmos* in the Rome first edition of 1611, read on page images
because that folio's optical text is unusable, with O'Sullivan's 1866 English
where it exists; Guéranger and Schuster for liturgical reception.

**What it settled.** Four things the guide now publishes that it could not have
published before.

1. **`Respice in testamentum tuum` has four answers, not one, and the guide
   prints four.** (a) The **New Covenant**: Augustine and Cassiodorus
   independently make v. 20 the hinge of the psalm, both proving it from
   Jer. 31:31, and the *Glossa ordinaria* hardens it into the schoolroom gloss
   ("non vetus de terrenis sed tuum quod promisisti regnum celorum"). Augustine
   opens his exposition of the psalm by quoting Gal. 3:29, four verses past
   where this Sunday's lesson stops. Cassiodorus supplies the sentence that
   explains why this psalm opens this Mass: having no goods of the people's to
   recall, the psalmist "meminit promissionis Dominicae quae semper impletur" —
   which the Glossa compresses to "Meminit promissionis quia merita deficiunt."
   (b) The covenant **promised to Abraham and the patriarchs**: Rupert as
   Guéranger excerpts him, and Schuster, who says Abraham by name. (c) The
   **Old Covenant, and territorial**: Bellarmine, "pactum siue testamentum quod
   fecisti cum Patribus nostris … in terra, quam nobis dedisti," with no
   connection at this verse to Christ, the New Covenant or the kingdom of
   heaven — a flat contradiction of the Latin Fathers at the same clause.
   (d) **Silence**: Theodoret does not gloss the word at all.
2. **Theodoret's lemma at that verse is corrupt as well as silent.** His
   printed text reads "the covenant *of thy servants*," words Migne's own
   *Variae Lectiones* flag as absent from both codices, from the Hebrew and from
   the Septuagint; his note expounds only the verse's second half; Migne's
   facing Latin renders the word `foedus`, not `testamentum`.
3. **Two chant readings previously reported as bare divergences now have named
   commentators behind them.** Cassiodorus's lemmata agree with the appointed
   chants at four points where the Clementine does not — `Exsurge, Domine …
   causam tuam` (Ps. 73:22), `quaerentium te` (Ps. 73:23), `a generatione et
   progenie` (Ps. 89:1) and `dixi, tu es Deus meus … in manibus tuis tempora
   mea` (Ps. 30:15–16) — and his exposition of the Offertory verse turns on
   `tempora`, so it cannot be transposed to `sortes`. Jerome's *Psalterium iuxta
   Hebraeos* likewise reads `in manu tua tempora mea`, which makes the appointed
   antiphon a hybrid: the Septuagint's plural hands with the Hebrew's times.
   Theodoret names *times* as the reading of "the rest of the translators" —
   Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion — and refuses to choose, expounding both; the
   *Glossa ordinaria* keeps both on the page and credits the alternative to
   Cassiodorus ("SORTES. CASSIODORUS. Vel tempora vite"); and **Bellarmine
   reports the Hebrew as *tempora mea*, conjectures that the Greek κλῆροι should
   be read καιροί, and names as witnesses "the Office of the Mass, more than
   once, and the Psalter of the Church of St Peter at Rome."** That last is the
   strongest single datum in the file: a Counter-Reformation cardinal citing the
   Roman Mass propers themselves as the textual witness for the word this
   Offertory sings. Augustine reads `sortes` in all four expositions and builds
   on it his page on unmerited election, so his most famous reading of the verse
   is of a word this Sunday does not sing.
4. **Introit divergence no. 4 is not a chant liberty.** Migne's apparatus at
   Theodoret Ps. 73:23 records the whole situation in one line: the Hebrew has
   *thy enemies*, Aquila and Symmachus follow it, and the Septuagint has the
   suppliants. Augustine's lemma (`deprecantium te`), Cassiodorus's (`quaerentium
   te`, the chant's exactly) and Theodoret's Greek all carry the suppliants, and
   Gloss-e's psalter apparatus states the family assignment outright —
   `inimicorum` Gallican, `querentium` Roman, citing Jerome's letter to Sunnia
   and Fretela. That is reported as Gloss-e's apparatus, not as this guide's
   finding, because no Roman-Psalter witness was collated. The Clementine is the
   Hebrew; the chant is the Greek. The guide accordingly stops describing the
   reading as an alteration and rewrites the controlling limit of the
   interpretive proposal that had rested on it. **Bellarmine knew all of this
   and voted the other way**, attributing the Greek reading to Augustine and the
   Greek books and judging it a corruption of ἐχθρῶν into οἰκετῶν — so the Roman
   Rite sings, in this Introit, a reading its own greatest controversialist
   thought secondary.
5. **Two independent witnesses defend the Gradual's `causam tuam`.** Theodoret
   makes the pronoun the point of his whole note ("he did not say *my* case but
   *thy* case"); Bellarmine reports the Hebrew as `Litiga litem tuam`, judges
   the Septuagint and Vulgate right, and names Augustine's `causam meam` as
   unsupported: "nescio quid secutus sit." Augustine's lemma really is `meam`,
   so his exposition of that verse cannot be quoted for this Gradual without the
   flag, and the guide flags it.

**Where the witnesses disagree.** On which army sacked the temple: Augustine
reads Rome as past history (the `signa Romana`, Jn. 11:48, the Passover
timing); Cassiodorus reads Rome as prophecy and names Vespasian and Titus;
Theodoret reads Rome as prophecy and argues it by eliminating Antiochus (the
history does not record such a sack) and Babylon (the psalm says there is no
longer a prophet, and Jeremiah, Uriah, Ezekiel and Daniel were then alive); the
*Glossa ordinaria* keeps the patristic line (`vexilla romana`, `aquilas`, Titus
and Vespasian, spoken "nondum eversa"); **Jerome reads Babylon**; and
**Bellarmine reads Antiochus IV and the Maccabees**, naming Augustine,
Theodoret and Euthymius as holding the other view and rejecting it partly on a
liturgical ground — "neque Ecclesia frequentaret hunc Psalmum, si contineret
voces infidelium Iudaeorum." He concedes the hard verse himself: the burning of
the sanctuary at v. 7 "seems to be the only passage forbidding the application
of this Psalm to the persecution of Antiochus."

On the Ps. 89 title, three denials of Mosaic authorship reach the same result
by different routes: Augustine's (the psalm is not among his canticles in the
Pentateuch), the Glossa's (transmitting Augustine's), and Bellarmine's own
lifespan argument — a psalm that fixes a man's span at seventy or eighty is not
the arithmetic of an age in which Moses died at a hundred and twenty. On Ps. 73:22 Augustine's lemma is `causam meam`
against both the chant and the Clementine, so his exposition of that verse is
of a possessive the Sunday does not sing, while Theodoret makes the chant's
`causam tuam` the point of his whole note. On the Ps. 89 title, Augustine
denies Mosaic authorship outright, Jerome reports the Hebrew ascription of this
and ten following psalms to Moses, and Theodoret's lemma is a dative — a prayer
*for* Moses — which lets him report the Mosaic and Davidic views without
deciding.

**Two contacts with the day's Gospel that fall outside the appointed verses,
and are labelled as such wherever used.** At Ps. 73:11 both Augustine and
Cassiodorus find Moses' leprous hand (Ex. 4:6–7) and read it of a people made
unclean by going out from Christ and healed by returning — the same colour
theology Augustine applies to this Sunday's Gospel. And at Ps. 30:3 Augustine
derives `Samaritanus` as `custos` and applies it to Christ, attached to the
word `refugium`, which is the Alleluia's word. Neither verse is sung; both are
cited only as cross-references.

**One cross-chant contact that is inside the appointed verses.** Jerome, in his
first homily on Ps. 89, explains what a refuge is for and proves the "beast"
clause by quoting **Ps. 73:19**, the verse the Introit sings. The Alleluia's
psalm and the Introit's psalm are joined, in a fourth-century homily, at the
exact verse the Introit takes.

**Bounds.** Cassiodorus was read in an electronic transmission with no stated
underlying edition and no PL column marker, so no PL 70 column is asserted.
Augustine's PL columns were not read from a scan and are not asserted.
Theodoret's PG 80 columns are the markers embedded in the digitised Greek; a
parallel pass matched four of them against running heads on the page images,
and the record says plainly which check was made by whom. The Greek columns of
the archive.org scan of PG 80 are destroyed by a Latin OCR engine — the inverse
of the problem recorded at PG 82 for the Epistle — so the Greek was taken from
the digitised transmissions and the Latin columns and running heads from the
scan. Bracketed material in Migne's Theodoret marks readings absent from one or
both codices; the bracketed expansions at Ps. 73:19 are anti-Jewish and
Christological and are not attributed to Theodoret. Jerome's *Ep.* 140 ad
Cyprianum, an entire exposition of Ps. 89, was not opened.

## 6. Consequential negative results

These are published in the guide, not buried here.

1. **Ambrose has no exposition of Lk. 17:11–19.** All that exists is one
   transitional clause at *Exp. Luc.* VIII n. 32 (embedded marker PL 15, 1478).
   Method: a complete search of books VIII–IX of the raw text over the stems
   `lepr-`, `samari-`, `mundat-`, `alienigen-`, `decem`, `gratias`, `ingrat-`.
   The *Catena aurea*'s single one-clause Ambrose excerpt independently
   confirms that its thirteenth-century compilers found no more.
2. **Gregory the Great preached no homily on this pericope.** All forty
   *Homiliae in Evangelia* plus the preface were downloaded and searched; every
   hit is incidental (Mt 10:8 in I.4; Mt 11:5 in I.6; the Martyrius exemplum in
   II.39; Jn 8:48 in I.18). He is nevertheless one of the pericope's principal
   voices, through *Moralia* V.11.28, which Bede transcribed and the Catena
   transmits under Bede's name.
3. **No genuine or pseudonymous Chrysostom homily on the ten lepers was
   found**, in Latin, English or Greek-keyed searches. The *Catena aurea*'s
   total silence on Chrysostom at this lection corroborates the absence.
4. **Augustine's *Expositio* on Galatians never treats the four hundred and
   thirty years.** Verified by a string search of the full Latin text for
   `quadringent*`: zero hits. He replaces the chronological argument with a
   logical one.
5. **Marius Victorinus's comment on Gal. 3:11–19 is lost in a lacuna.**
   Verified by listing every `VERS.` lemma in the transmitted text; the tail of
   the v. 10 section already discusses the mediator, which shows the
   disturbance falling exactly there. The earliest Latin commentary on
   Galatians therefore has **no recoverable position** on the seed or the 430
   years. This is a finding and is published as one.
6. **Bede's sentence `electi in faciem et reprobi retrorsum cadunt` was not
   found verbatim in the *Moralia*.** Searched over all six parts of the
   wikisource text (~3.6 MB); only the doctrinally parallel Gen. 49:17
   exposition was found. The sentence is described as Gregorian in doctrine and
   unlocated in wording, and is not attributed.
7. **The Secret and the Postcommunion are not in Feltoe's Verona
   collection.** For the Secret, all thirteen occurrences of `propitiare` were
   inspected; the nearest verbal parallel (Muratori col. 432, `collatis in me
   per gratiam tuam propitiare muneribus`) is a different prayer in a bishop's
   own Mass. For the Postcommunion, variant searches were negative. Both
   results were reached by reading the printed edition, not by a replayable
   literal search: the artifact is not registered as indexable and the
   repository's raw-line search refuses it, so the results are bounded and
   correctable.
8. **Wis. 16:20's wording does not occur in Ambrose's *De mysteriis*.** See §4.
9. **`fac nos amare quod praecipis` was not found in the Thomistic corpus** by
   the method used: *ST* II-II qq. 23–24 and I-II qq. 109–114 were read, and an
   exact-phrase search of corpusthomisticum.org returned no page. A positive
   control was found in the same search — at I-II q. 113 a. 9 Aquinas quotes
   the **Tenth** Sunday's collect — so he demonstrably quotes this collect
   series and, where this guide looked, not this one.
10. **`da quod iubes` is nowhere in Trent Session VI**, in either language. The
    Augustine that chapter XI actually quotes is *De natura et gratia* 43,
    `Deus impossibilia non iubet`.
11. **No patristic witness links the two consecutive Samaritan Sundays.** The
    pericope of the ten lepers floated between Sundays in the medieval
    homiliaries — Bruno's homily on it is headed *Dominica XII post
    Pentecosten* in the Migne arrangement, with an editorial note that Haymo
    carries the exposition at *Dominica XIV*. The only commentator who binds
    them is Guéranger, through Rupert.
12. **No checked witness comments on the Introit/Gradual verb pair**
    (`derelínquas` / `obliviscáris`), or on Samaria being named before Galilee
    in Lk. 17:11. Both are recorded as textual observations, and the proposals
    that build on the first are labelled as proposals.
13. **Guéranger's chapter on this Sunday exists in volume 11 only.** Heading
    searches over the optical text of volumes 10 and 12–15 of the English set
    were all empty.
14. **Gihr, *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass*, has no treatment of this
    Sunday's propers** (zero hits for "Thirteenth Sunday").
15. **Jerome has no exposition of Ps. 30 anywhere, and none of Ps. 73:1, 19,
    20, 22 or 23.** Method: every psalm heading in all three Morin volumes
    listed by regular expression — the *Commentarioli* skip from Ps. XXVIIII to
    Ps. XXXI, the *Tractatus* first series from Ps. LXVII to Ps. LXXIIII, the
    *series altera* stops at Ps. XCVI — plus string searches of all three
    volumes for `Respice in testamentum` and `In manibus tuis sortes`, zero hits
    in each. His *Commentarioli* on Ps. 89 comment only on vv. 9 and 12.
16. **No witness read for this guide connects any of the three appointed psalms
    to the ten lepers or to the Gospel's thanksgiving.** Method: the Latin of
    Augustine's six relevant expositions and Cassiodorus's three counted for
    `lepr-`, `samarit-`, `gratias ag-` and `decem lepros-`, every hit inspected;
    the leprosy hits are the Moses'-hand typology at Ps. 73:11 in both authors,
    the Samaritan hits are the Good Samaritan at Ps. 30:3 and Jn. 8:48, and
    `decem lepros-` occurs nowhere. On the Greek side the stem for leprosy
    occurs **zero** times in the whole of Theodoret's commentary on all 150
    psalms. Bellarmine and the Glossa likewise carry nothing of the kind at
    these psalms.
17. **NPNF vol. 8 translates only the first of Augustine's four expositions of
    Ps. 30**, so his *sortes = gratia* passage has no public-domain English and
    is quoted in Latin.
18. **Guéranger prints the Alleluia and the Offertory without comment**; his
    psalm commentary on this Sunday is confined to the Introit and the Gradual.

## 7. Competing historical judgments, preserved

1. **The mediator of Gal. 3:19–20.** Christ (Jerome, as pre-incarnate Word;
   Augustine, precisely as man; Chrysostom, as the law's own Giver;
   Ambrosiaster, as arbiter of two peoples; Victorinus) against **Moses**
   (Theodoret, explicitly; Guéranger, in liturgical reception). Aquinas
   knowingly bridges them. The guide reports the split as a split.
2. **The seed of Gal. 3:16.** Christ singly (Jerome, Theodoret, Ambrosiaster,
   Aquinas) against Christ-with-his-members (Augustine). Both are
   Christological and they are not the same reading.
3. **The angels of Gal. 3:19.** Real angels (Jerome, Theodoret) against human
   messengers from Moses to John the Baptist (Ambrosiaster). Chrysostom allows
   both; the Glossa and Aquinas print both as alternatives.
4. **The date and destination of Galatians.** The 1910 *Catholic Encyclopedia*
   favours the South-Galatian theory and a date of AD 48–50 from Antioch; the
   New American Bible leans North-Galatian, around AD 54–55, most likely from
   Ephesus. The page-2 dossier prints both and settles neither.
5. **The date of Luke.** *Catholic Encyclopedia*: finished before Acts, by
   about AD 62–63, in Achaia on the authority of Julius Africanus. NAB: AD
   80–90, place unstated. Both printed.
6. **The horizon of Psalm 73.** The superscription names Asaph, David's chief
   musician; the psalm laments a sanctuary already destroyed. The tension is
   reported and not resolved; §5 records how the checked witnesses handled it.
   Six of them divide three ways — Rome (Augustine, Cassiodorus, Theodoret, the
   Glossa), Babylon (Jerome), Antiochus IV (Bellarmine) — and Bellarmine's
   ground for rejecting the Roman reading is liturgical rather than historical.
6a. **The `testamentum` of Ps. 73:20.** The New Covenant (Augustine,
   Cassiodorus, the Glossa) against the covenant promised to Abraham (Rupert
   *apud* Guéranger, Schuster) against the old, territorial covenant with the
   fathers (Bellarmine), with Theodoret silent. Four positions, printed as four.
6b. **The Offertory's word.** `sortes` = grace and unmerited election
   (Augustine, and the Glossa's Augustine gloss) against `tempora` = the times
   of life and death in God's will (Cassiodorus, Bellarmine, Jerome *iuxta
   Hebraeos*). Theodoret, the Glossa and Bellarmine each hold both in view; only
   Augustine is committed to `sortes` alone and only Cassiodorus to `tempora`
   alone. The missal sings `tempora`.
6c. **Ps. 73:23.** Gloss-e's apparatus and Migne's agree that the chant's
   `quaerentium te` is the Greek and `inimicorum tuorum` the Hebrew; Bellarmine,
   knowing both, judges the Greek a corruption. The guide reports the
   transmission and prints the chant.
7. **The year of the Passion.** AD 29 on the *Catholic Encyclopedia*'s
   computation, commonly AD 30–33 in later discussion. Not settled.
8. **The attribution of the Gregorian supplement.** Wilson-era scholarship
   credits Alcuin; the modern revision of that attribution rests on protected
   literature that was not consulted, so the guide names neither.
9. **Two printings' column numbering for Jerome on Galatians.** The
   transcription's inline markers read PL 26, 439–443 where Documenta Catholica
   Omnia bounds the work at 307–438D. Two printings, reported and not
   reconciled.
