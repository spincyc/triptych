\section*{The Propers: Map}

\begingroup
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.02}
\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit \cue{Int.}}{\latin{Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum} \properrefs{Ps. 73:20, 19, 23, with v.~1}}{Asaph's plea over a ruined sanctuary}{Holds God to his covenant, his poor and his own cause; the psalm verse asks why he cast them off.}
\properrow{Collect \cue{Coll.}}{\latin{Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, da nobis} \properrefs{composed oration}}{The three theological virtues, asked as one increase}{Faith, hope and charity asked for as \latin{augméntum}; attaining what is promised hangs on loving what is commanded.}
\properrow{Epistle \cue{Ep.}}{\latin{Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes} \properrefs{Gal. 3:16--22}}{Promise before law, by four hundred thirty years}{The confirmed \latin{testaméntum} stands; the law, added later for transgressions, can neither annul it nor give life.}
\properrow{Gradual \cue{Grad.}}{\latin{Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum} \properrefs{Ps. 73:20, 19, 22}}{The Introit's plea sung a second time}{Repeats the covenant plea with the psalter's own verb --- \latin{obliviscáris} where the Introit sang \latin{derelínquas}.}
\properrow{Alleluia \cue{All.}}{\latin{Dómine, refúgium factus es nobis} \properrefs{Ps. 89:1}}{The one psalm bearing Moses' name}{Answers the Introit's fear of being forsaken: the refuge has held for every generation there has been.}
\properrow{Gospel \cue{Gosp.}}{\latin{Dum iret Iesus in Ierúsalem} \properrefs{Lk. 17:11--19}}{Ten cleansed on the border, one returns}{All ten obey and are cleansed on the way; one returns, a Samaritan, and only he hears \latin{fides tua te salvum fecit}.}
\properrow{Offertory \cue{Off.}}{\latin{In te sperávi, Dómine} \properrefs{Ps. 30:15--16}}{Trust handing time itself over}{At the offering of the gifts the singer puts his times --- \latin{témpora mea} --- into the hands he trusts.}
\properrow{Secret \cue{Sec.}}{\latin{Propitiáre, Dómine, pópulo tuo} \properrefs{composed oration}}{One verb said twice}{\latin{Propitiáre} over the people, \latin{propitiáre} over the gifts: pardon and the things asked, through one oblation.}
\properrow{Communion \cue{Comm.}}{\latin{Panem de cælo dedísti nobis} \properrefs{Sap. 16:20}}{The manna verse turned into an address}{Wisdom's memory of the manna becomes ``thou hast given \emph{us}'': every delight, every taste's sweetness, at the rail.}
\properrow{Postcommunion \cue{Postcomm.}}{\latin{Sumptis, Dómine, cæléstibus sacraméntis} \properrefs{composed oration}}{The Collect's noun, asked again}{Having received, we ask to advance toward the \latin{augméntum} of eternal redemption --- the noun the day opened with.}

\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Asaph pleads a covenant over a devastated sanctuary and asks God to judge his own cause \cue{Int., Grad.}. Paul argues that the promise made to Abraham and his seed preceded the law by four hundred and thirty years \cue{Ep.}. Ten lepers are cleansed as they go; one returns \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Allegorical & Paul reads the promise's singular: the seed is Christ \cue{Ep.}, so the \latin{testaméntum} the Introit pleads is the covenant confirmed in him. The bread that fed the wilderness is sung as the faithful receive its fulfilment \cue{Comm.}; the priests' cleansing ends in the one who returns \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Moral & The day's petitions are verbs of growth and return: ask the increase of faith, hope and charity \cue{Coll.}; put your times into God's hands as you offer \cue{Off.}; come back and give thanks --- nine did not \cue{Gosp.}; ask that what was received not stop there \cue{Postcomm.}.\\
Anagogical & The refuge held from generation to generation \cue{All.} answers the plea not to be forsaken \latin{in finem} \cue{Int.}. The last increase asked is \latin{redemptiónis ætérnæ \dots\ augméntum} \cue{Postcomm.}; the promise is given \latin{credéntibus} \cue{Ep.}; the Gospel's last word is that faith has saved \cue{Gosp.}.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
