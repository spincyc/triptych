# Epistle reception record — Galatians 3:16–22

Verified reception research for the appointed Epistle. Every witness below was
opened and read at its own work and locus on 2026-08-19; nothing rests on a
catena or aggregator alone. The pericope ends at v. 22; every witness whose
argument runs on into the pedagogue of vv. 23–25 is flagged, because that image
is outside the appointed lesson.

## Boundary and use

This record feeds the detailed commentary's Epistle treatment, the
source-grounded synthesis (covenant/testamentum thread), and the reception
matrix in `scope.md`. Quotation discipline: NPNF (Chrysostom, the
Augustine–Jerome letters) and Shepherd's Guéranger (1900) are public domain and
may be quoted in English; Jerome, Augustine's *Expositio*, Ambrosiaster,
Theodoret, Victorinus, and Aquinas have no public-domain English at these
loci — the guide quotes their Latin or Greek and never a modern copyrighted
translation (Cain FOTC 121; Plumer 2003; Cooper 2005; Hill 2001/2007; Larcher).

## Direct exegesis, witness by witness

### Jerome, *Commentarii in Epistulam ad Galatas*, lib. II (PL 26)

Read in the la.wikisource/Corpus Corporum transcription of Migne; the
transcription's inline column markers at this passage read 439–443 while
Documenta Catholica Omnia bounds the work at PL 26, 307–438D — two printings'
numberings, reported, not reconciled.

- **Seed (v. 16).** Paul argues with the Galatians "non … his argumentis
  quibus ad Romanos, sed simplicioribus … et pene de trivio," which is why he
  prefaces *secundum hominem dico*; but the claim is materially true:
  "Omnes Scripturas sensu ac memoria peragrans, numquam plurali numero semina
  scripta reperi" — Jerome never found *semina* in the plural anywhere in
  Scripture. The nations were blessed not in Isaac or the patriarchs "sed in
  Christo Jesu."
- **Testamentum (vv. 15/17).** The philological jewel for this Sunday's
  Introit: "si quis diligenter Hebraea volumina … contulerit, inveniet ubi
  testamentum scriptum est, non testamentum sonare, sed pactum, quod Hebraeo
  sermone dicitur BERITH." The Hebrew means covenant/pact, not last will;
  Paul's will-image is another deliberate everyday argument.
- **430 years (v. 17).** An unsolved crux for Jerome: whether the count from
  promise to law is 430 against Genesis 15's 400 is "non … parva res … et a
  multis quaesita, nescio an ab aliquo sit inventa." (Aquinas later solves it;
  see below.) The "simplex sensus": later law cannot destroy prior promises.
- **Mediator (vv. 19–20): Christ, specifically the pre-incarnate Word.** The
  law was enacted "in manu mediatoris Christi Jesu, quia omnia per ipsum facta
  sunt"; after the Incarnation he is "mediator Dei et hominum homo Christus
  Jesus" (1 Tim 2:5), but "antequam vero humanum corpus assumeret … sine
  additamento hominis … mediator tantummodo nuncupatur." The angels of v. 19
  are real theophany-angels in whom the Mediator speaks; "manum mediatoris" =
  his power (*potentiam et virtutem*).
- **Conclusit (vv. 21–22).** The law "inter repromissionem et completionem
  ejus media subrepsit" as custody, not subversion — "in custodiam eam
  repromissionis, non in subversionem datam"; Israel held "legalibus vinculis
  … et, ut ita dicam, in carcerem" until the coming faith. Scripture is not
  the author of sin: like a judge who "concludit" criminals so that the
  sovereign may afterwards pardon.
- **Boundary flag.** Jerome's lemma "Vers. 21 seqq." already includes v. 23,
  and the prison imagery flows into the *paedagogus* section outside the
  lesson.
- **Supersessionist formulation, his own.** On v. 17 he allegorizes Thamar's
  twins (Gen 38): Israel's hand "pollutam cruore" of the prophets and the
  Savior, after which the gentile people broke forth. Attributed to Jerome
  where used; not generalized.

### Augustine, *Expositio epistulae ad Galatas* §§23–25 (PL 35, 2105–2148)

Read at augustinus.it (`esposizione_galati_libro.htm`; the frameset index
pages carry no text).

- **Seed (v. 16) — a real difference from Jerome.** "Et ideo semen Abraham …
  Christum dicit Apostolus, hoc est omnes Christianos fide imitantes Abraham;
  quod ad singularitatem redigit commendando, quod non dictum est: Et
  seminibus, sed: Semini tuo, quia et una est fides." The seed is
  Christ-with-his-members — an early *totus Christus* move — and at §24 "semen
  autem cui promissum est, populum significat." Jerome's seed is Christ singly
  (via Mt 1:1); Augustine's is Christ with all who imitate Abraham's faith.
- **430 years: absent.** Verified by string-search of the full Latin text
  (`quadringent*`: zero hits). Augustine replaces the chronological argument
  with the logical one: "Si enim lex iustificat, non est iustificatus
  Abraham."
- **Mediator: Christ precisely *secundum hominem*.** "Mediatorem Iesum
  Christum secundum hominem dici ex illa eiusdem Apostoli sententia fit
  planius … Mediator ergo inter Deum et Deum esse non posset, quia unus est
  Deus." Where Jerome stresses the pre-incarnate Word already bore the bare
  title, Augustine attaches the title to the Incarnation ("nec se exinaniret
  formam servi accipiens … non esset mediator") — a documentable divergence
  within the shared Christ-answer.
- **Old-Latin lemma variant.** His text reads "Lex transgressionis gratia
  proposita est … dispositum per angelos semen in manu mediatoris" — the
  *seed*, not the law, "disposed through angels." His gloss on the angelic
  economy is close to Jerome: "Per angelos autem ministrata est omnis
  dispensatio Veteris Testamenti."
- **Purpose of law: the anti-pride reading.** "Superbienti ergo populo lex
  posita est … ut transgressione humiliaretur, ut quaereret gratiam"; the law
  shows sin so that the humbled may know "non in sua manu esse salutem suam,
  sed in manu mediatoris" — a deliberate wordplay on *in manu*.
- Augustine's treatment of the pericope is essentially self-contained within
  the v. 22 boundary.

### Chrysostom, *Commentary on Galatians*, cap. 3 (PG 61, 611–682)

Public-domain English NPNF 1/13 (newadvent.org/fathers/23103.htm), quoted as
NPNF.

- The human-testament example is prefaced "I speak after the manner of men …
  that nothing might be deduced from it derogatory to the majesty of God."
- **430 years:** simply the law's posteriority — it "came four hundred and
  thirty years after" and cannot disannul.
- **Purpose of law:** "that the Law might be placed upon them as a bridle,
  guiding, regulating, and checking."
- **Mediator: Christ, verified verbatim:** "By Mediator here he means Christ,
  and shows that He was before it, and Himself the Giver of it." On v. 20,
  anti-Arian: "of whom then is Christ the Mediator? Plainly of God and of men
  … what therefore it was His to give, it is His to annul." For the angels he
  allows an alternative: "He either calls the priests Angels, or he declares
  that the Angels themselves ministered."
- **Conclusit:** "the Law was given to probe their wounds, that they might
  long for a physician. And the word shut up means convinced and conviction
  held them in fear."
- **Boundary flag:** his argument runs seamlessly into vv. 23–25.

### Ambrosiaster, *Commentarius in Epistulam ad Galatas* (PL 17, 337–372D)

Latin at la.wikisource; column range verified against Documenta Catholica
Omnia. The transcription carries inline markers "221/222" from another
printing — observed, not resolved.

- **Seed:** the promise firm "in Christo … non in multis, sed in uno";
  judaizers are "quasi reos falsati testamenti" — defendants charged with
  forging the testament.
- **Testament-as-will theology:** "Testimonium ergo promissionis Abrahae
  testamentum appellatur; ut post mortem ejus haeredes essent … ut post mortem
  Christi credentes, haeredes essent fidei Abrahae per testamentum sanguinis
  Christi."
- **Angels = human messengers — his most distinctive position:** "Angelos
  dicit Dei nuntios, id est, Moysen, et Jesum filium Nave, et caeteros
  prophetas usque ad Joannem Baptistam" (proof-text Mal 3:1); the law disposed
  "in manu, id est, potestate Salvatoris."
- **Mediator: Christ as arbiter of the two peoples:** "mediator, hoc est,
  arbiter non est unius, sed duorum; cum enim duo populi invicem adversus se
  dimicarent … Salvator horum arbiter venit, amputans utrisque populis per
  quod discordabant" — from the gentiles their many gods, from the Jews the
  works of the law. Judaizing destroys the arbitration: "unius utique populi
  arbiter nemo est."
- **Conclusit:** parallel to Rom 11:32; "Manifestatis enim peccatis suis,
  conclusi sunt, ut se excusare non possent, sed quaererent misericordiam,"
  sealed with Isa 59:20–21, where the promise is itself called *testamentum*.
- v. 23 begins a separate lemma; the boundary is respected in his sectioning.
- **Supersessionist formulations, his own,** reported where used: on v. 14 the
  blessing "transitum fecit ad gentes" citing Mt 21:43; on v. 17 the Jews
  "aemulatione quadam caecati." Attributed to Ambrosiaster only.

### Theodoret of Cyrus, *Interpretatio Epistulae ad Galatas* (PG 82)

Greek column of the Migne scan read via the archive.org OCR of PG 82 (the
parallel Latin column is OCR-destroyed); our verses around coll. 477–482, the
marker "479" legible beside vv. 19–21 — column bounds approximate. Hill's
translation not consulted; renderings are the researcher's working aid and are
not published.

- **Seed:** Christ alone is the seed κυρίως; Moses, Samuel, Elijah are seed
  κατὰ φύσιν, "οὐ μὴν ἐκεῖνό εἰσι τὸ τὴν πηγὴν τῆς εὐλογίας κομίσαν τοῖς
  ἔθνεσιν."
- **Diatheke (v. 17)** — directly relevant to the Introit: "Καὶ ἡ πάλαι Γραφὴ
  διαθήκην τοῦ Θεοῦ καλεῖ τὴν πρὸς τὸν Ἀβραὰμ γεγενημένην ἐπαγγελίαν" — the
  ancient Scripture itself calls the promise to Abraham God's *diatheke*.
- **Purpose of law:** "Τί οὖν ὁ νόμος;" is to be read in an objector's voice
  (καθ᾽ ὑπόκρισιν); the law "εἰς ἐπιμέλειαν ἐτέθη τοῦ γένους" — for the care
  of the race from which the seed was to sprout according to the flesh.
- **Mediator: MOSES — the clearest patristic witness for that answer:**
  "Ἐτέθη δὲ ἀγγέλων ὑπουργούντων, καὶ τῇ τούτου θέσει Μωϋσέως διακονοῦντος.
  Αὐτὸν γὰρ μεσίτην ἐκάλεσε"; on v. 20, "Ἐμεσίτευσε γὰρ τῷ λαῷ καὶ τῷ Θεῷ,"
  and "ὁ δὲ Θεὸς εἷς ἐστιν" is anti-Marcionite: the same God gave promise,
  law, and fulfillment.
- **v. 21:** the law could not vivify, glossed by Ezek 20:25, limited to the
  surplus ceremonial matter.
- **Conclusit:** "τὸ συνέκλεισεν ἀντὶ τοῦ ἤλεγξε τέθεικε" — "shut up" stands
  for "convicted"; faith's salvation offered as ἀλεξιφάρμακον, antidote.
- **Boundary flag:** his κγ´–κδ´ continue into the guard-wall and pedagogue —
  including a notably positive framing of Israel's guarding ("οἷόν τινι
  τειχίῳ τὸ Ἰσραηλιτικὸν φυλάττεσθαι γένος").

### Marius Victorinus, *In Epistulam Pauli ad Galatas* (PL 8) — mutilated here

Text at la.wikisource (Corpus Corporum/Migne 1844).

- **Bounded negative:** the transmitted book I jumps from the comment on 3:10
  directly to "VERS. 20" — **the comments on 3:11–19, including the seed
  argument and the 430 years, are lost in a lacuna** (verified by listing
  every `VERS.` lemma in the file; the v. 10 section's tail already discusses
  the mediator, showing disturbance exactly here). The earliest Latin
  commentary has no recoverable position on seed or 430 years. A finding, not
  an omission.
- **Mediator (v. 20): Christ, emphatically:** "Numquid lex mediator est? …
  ipse est enim solus mediator … solus enim Christus mediator, qui jungit illa
  inter quae mediator est. Ergo per Christum justificatio et liberatio, non
  per legem factorum." Distinctively Neoplatonic: the mediator "revocat ad
  superiorem Ecclesiam," joining the earthly members to the heavenly Church.
- **v. 22:** through Moses all is shut under sin "ut fides sola Jesu Christi
  sufficiat ad justificationem liberationemque nostram" — a strikingly crisp
  *fides sola* formula, four decades before Augustine's *Expositio*.

### Thomas Aquinas, *Super epistolam ad Galatas lectura*, cap. 3, lect. 6–8

Latin at corpusthomisticum.org/cgl.html (the whole lectura on one page; the
guessed cga1/cga3.html URLs are 404 — recorded so the search is not repeated).

- **Testament-as-will (lect. 6):** "nihil humanum tantam firmitatem habet
  sicut ultima voluntas hominis"; the promises are "quasi testamentum Dei,
  quia est quaedam ordinatio de haereditate danda," confirmed "iureiurando."
- **Seed:** Paul "veritatem autem testamenti aperit … eodem spiritu quo
  testamentum conditum est"; the singular grounded christologically: "quasi in
  uno, quod est Christus, quia ipse solus est per quem et in quo omnes
  poterunt benedici."
- **430 years — solved (against Jerome's *nescio*):** "si fiat computatio
  annorum a prima promissione facta Abrahae (Gen. XII) usque ad exitum … sic
  sunt anni quadringenti triginta … Si autem incipiat computatio a nativitate
  Isaac … sic sunt tantum quadringenti et quinque anni," with the patriarchal
  arithmetic laid out.
- **Purpose of law (lect. 7):** four ends — repress transgressions; tame
  concupiscence by wearying ceremonies (Acts 15:10); convict of impotence;
  instruct in figure of future grace (Heb 10:1).
- **Angels and mediator — the deliberate bridge:** "per Angelos, id est, per
  nuntios Dei, scilicet Moysen et Aaron … Vel per Angelos, id est, ministerio
  Angelorum" — Ambrosiaster's line and the literal line as alternatives. Then:
  "in manu mediatoris, id est, in potestate Christi … Iste mediator
  significatus est per Moysen, in cuius manu est lex data. Deut. V, 5: ego
  sequester et medius fui" — Christ the mediator, Moses his figure: Aquinas
  knowingly reconciles the Christ-answer and Theodoret's Moses-answer. Three
  expositions of "mediator unius non est," the third absorbing Ambrosiaster's
  two-peoples arbitration via Eph 2:14.
- **Conclusit (lect. 8):** the law manifests sin and manifests weakness;
  "sicut ista duo, scilicet cognitio morbi et impotentia infirmi, multum
  inducunt ad quaerendum medicum, ita cognitio peccati et propriae impotentiae
  inducunt ad quaerendum Christum."
- **Boundary flag:** lectio 8 continues without a break into vv. 23–29.

### Glossa ordinaria on Gal 3 — medieval reuse

Checked in the *Glossae Scripturae Sacrae electronicae* (IRHT-CNRS), Ad
Galatas cap. 3 (gloss-e.irht.cnrs.fr, liber 63), keyed to the Rusch facsimile
and Erfurt witness. Classification: **reuse**, a mosaic of Augustine and
Ambrosiaster with Chrysostom-matter — valuable as the medieval synthesis, not
fresh exegesis.

- v. 17 marginal: the law "post multum tempus a promissione facta Abrahe …
  data est"; interlinear: if blessing were by law, "frustra semini promisit et
  frustra semen ipsum venit … et Deus mendax."
- v. 19 marginal, ORDINATA PER ANGELOS: "id est Moysen et alios ministros Dei,
  vel ministerio angelorum" — exactly the pair Aquinas inherits. Interlinear
  on *in manu mediatoris*: "qui Deus et homo inter Deum et hominem."
- v. 20 marginal: the mediator "non est tantum unius populi, id est Iudeorum …
  sed et gentilium … sufficiens ad omnes reconciliandos Deo quia Deus."
- v. 22: "lex data est ut gratia quereretur" — Augustine §25 almost verbatim;
  and "Data est ut egrum de morbo convincet qui sibi sanus videbatur."

### The Jerome–Augustine Galatians controversy — context only

Augustine *Epp.* 28.3–5, 40.3–5, 82.3ff; Jerome's reply *Ep.* 75 (= Jerome
*Ep.* 112), NPNF 1/1. The dispute concerns **Gal 2:11–14**, not this pericope;
nothing in the correspondence engages 3:16–22 and the guide makes no such
claim. Usable only as background on how differently the two read the epistle.

### Guéranger, *The Liturgical Year* — liturgical reception

Time after Pentecost vol. II (= vol. 11 of the English set), tr. Laurence
Shepherd, 2nd ed. 1900, chapter "The Thirteenth Sunday after Pentecost,"
printed pp. 307–316 (Introit p. 309; Epistle pp. 310–311; commentary
pp. 311–316; Gradual p. 316); archive.org item V11TheLiturgicalYear. Material
negative: the chapter is in vol. 11 only — heading searches over the OCR of
vols. 10, 12, 13, 14, 15 were all empty.

- **Mediator: Moses** in liturgical reception: the law "made known to its
  subjects through the mediation of Moses and the angels," proved inferior to
  "the covenant made by Abraham directly with God."
- **The Introit/Gradual–Epistle link, explicit, via Rupert:** "It is on this
  account that the Gradual repeats the supplication of the Introit: *Respice
  Domine, in testamentum tuum*; for, as Rupert observes, it was the cry of the
  ancient people, who, sighing at the weakness of the powerless Law of Sinai,
  besought God to fulfil the covenant He had promised to Abraham's faith"
  (p. 316). Rupert of Deutz (*De divinis officiis*) is quoted only as
  excerpted by Guéranger; the original (PL 170) was not opened — unverified
  lead.
- Rupert (apud Guéranger) also bends the law/promise theology through the
  preceding Sunday's Gospel: the priest and levite figure the Law, "a simple
  safeguard, until such time as there should come the good Samaritan, the
  heavenly physician" (pp. 315–316).
- **Supersessionist framings are Guéranger's own,** harsher than any patristic
  witness above ("which has supplanted him"; "his now dead Law"; "the
  superannuated … pretensions of the Synagogue," p. 314). Where the guide
  cites him it attributes these to Guéranger the 19th-century author, never to
  the Fathers.

## Summary matrix

| Witness | Seed (v. 16) | 430 years (v. 17) | Mediator (vv. 19–20) | Purpose of law | Conclusit (v. 22) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jerome | *semina* never plural in all Scripture; Christ alone; knows *berith* = pactum | unsolved: "nescio an ab aliquo sit inventa" | Christ, pre-incarnate Word speaking in the angels | restrain transgressions; custody "in carcerem" | Scripture convicts, is not author of sin |
| Augustine | Christ with all Christians imitating Abraham's faith; singular = one faith | silent (verified absent) | Christ *secundum hominem*; no mediator between God and God | given to the proud, to humble into seeking grace | salvation "non in sua manu … sed in manu mediatoris" |
| Chrysostom | accepted without philological defense | posteriority cannot disannul | Christ, "Himself the Giver of it"; angels = angels or priests | a bridle | "shut up = convinced"; long for the physician |
| Ambrosiaster | firm "in uno … Christus"; judaizers "rei falsati testamenti" | law later, only signifies | Christ the arbiter of the two peoples; angels = human nuntii Moses→John Baptist | "lex factorum" segregates and schools in fear | none can self-excuse; seek mercy |
| Theodoret | Christ alone κυρίως; others κατὰ φύσιν | later law cannot alter the *diatheke* | **Moses** ("Αὐτὸν γὰρ μεσίτην ἐκάλεσε") | ἐπιμέλεια of the race until the seed | συνέκλεισεν = ἤλεγξε; antidote |
| Victorinus | lost in lacuna (3:11–19) | lost in lacuna | Christ alone, joining members to the heavenly Church | (context) law teaches about sins only | "ut fides sola Jesu Christi sufficiat" |
| Aquinas | Paul opens the testament by the same Spirit; Christ alone curse-free | **solved**: 430 from Gen 12; 405 from Isaac | Christ ("in potestate Christi"), **prefigured by Moses** | four ends | "cognitio morbi" drives to the physician Christ |
| Glossa ord. | (reuse) promise "per semen, non per legem," else "Deus mendax" | "post multum tempus" | Christ "Deus et homo"; angels = Moses and ministers, or angels | "lex data est ut gratia quereretur" | convicted so none can self-excuse |
| Guéranger | Christ "the only seed of salvation" | rhetorical: "four hundred and thirty years after" | "the mediation of Moses and the angels" | "a simple safeguard" until the good Samaritan | the powerless Law leaves man crying for the covenant |

**The mediator disagreement, preserved:** Christ — Jerome, Chrysostom,
Ambrosiaster (arbiter of two peoples), Victorinus, Augustine (as man), Aquinas
(with Moses as figure), Glossa. Moses — Theodoret explicitly; Guéranger in
liturgical reception. The Latin tradition solidified on Christ; the Antiochene
reading is Moses; Aquinas knowingly bridges them.

**Who grounds the Introit wordplay (testamentum):** Jerome states the double
sense philologically (*berith* = pactum); Ambrosiaster, Augustine, Aquinas,
and the Glossa develop testament-as-will; Theodoret notes Scripture itself
calls the promise God's *diatheke*; Guéranger, citing Rupert, makes the exact
Introit/Gradual–Epistle connection. The covenant thread is documented
liturgical reception, not a modern conceit.

## Material negative results

1. Augustine's *Expositio* nowhere treats the 430 years (`quadringent*` zero
   hits over the full Latin text).
2. Marius Victorinus's comments on 3:11–19 are lost in a lacuna; verified by
   listing every `VERS.` lemma in the transmitted text.
3. Guéranger's Thirteenth Sunday is in vol. 11 only; vols. 10, 12–15 negative.
4. PG 82's Latin column is OCR-destroyed in the archive.org text (Greek engine
   over both columns); the Greek column is sound and was quote-checked.
5. Dead/unusable URLs recorded so searches are not repeated:
   corpusthomisticum.org/cga1.html and /cga3.html → 404 (use cgl.html);
   aquinas.cc is a JavaScript shell; augustinus.it framesets carry no text
   (use `_libro.htm`); `ia801607.us.archive.org` direct-server URLs 404 (use
   the `archive.org/download/...` form).
6. PL 114 was not usable as a Glossa locus for Paul; the checked source is the
   Gloss-e electronic edition. Whether PL 114 carries the Pauline Glossa at
   all is an unverified lead, not an assertion.
