\section{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}

{\footnotesize
\textbf{Edition, formulary and occurrence.} The Mass treated is
\latin{In festo omnium Sanctorum}, \latin{Die 1 novembris}, I classis, of
the \work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica, Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis
1962, printed pp.~702--703, nos.~3974--3983, in the universal 1962
calendar, as said on Sunday 1~November 2026 with the commemoration of the
Twenty-third Sunday after Pentecost, printed pp.~416--417, nos.~1720, 1726
and 1728. The precedence of the feast over the Sunday, the commemoration
and its order, the colour, the Gloria, the Credo and the last Gospel rest
on the Missal's own general rubrics (RG~14, 16, 91, 108, 109~a, 111~a,
120~d; RGMR~431, 434~a, 436--437, 475, 509--510), as
\texttt{research/context.md} records. The Preface is not settled: the feast
has none of its own, and the general rubrics (RGMR~482, 483, 494~b, 498)
can be read for the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity (p.~293, no.~1082) or
for the common Preface (p.~297, no.~1086). Both candidates are named,
neither is asserted and neither text is given, and nothing in the study or
in this companion is built on either. The \latin{Asperges}, the Ordinary
and the Office of the feast are outside the formulary.

\textbf{Relation to the full study.} The three interpretations, their
witnesses and their four senses are developed in the expansive study of
this same feast, which this companion condenses; nothing evidence-dependent
is added here, and the fuller argument, its quotations and its apparatus
remain there. The loci, editions and bounds of the sweep are recorded in
\texttt{research/scope.md} and \texttt{research/interpretations.md}.

\textbf{Text and English.} The Latin was read on page images of the CMAA
facsimile of the 1962 typical edition and collated word for word with the
Tours printing of 1922 (Mame) for the feast's ten texts and with the
Ratisbon printing of 1862 (Pustet) for the Sunday's three orations; the
collation is in \texttt{propers/verified.md}. The scriptural English is the
Douay--Rheims in Challoner's revision. Psalms are cited in the Missal's
numbering, with the Hebrew numbering in parentheses. The English of the
Introit antiphon and of the orations is the Lasance \work{New Roman Missal}
of 1945, whose rubrics are those before 1960. None of this English is an
approved liturgical translation of the 1962 Missal, and no English is
composed here for a liturgical text. Patristic and later witnesses are
quoted in the nineteenth-century English of the editions named below, or in
their Latin or French.

\textbf{Reception and limits.} The carrying witnesses of the three
interpretations are Bede and Augustine of Hippo (the first); Augustine,
John Chrysostom, Thomas Aquinas and Hilary of Poitiers (the second); Basil
of Caesarea, Robert Bellarmine, Augustine and Thomas Aquinas (the third).
Victorinus of Pettau's commentary on the Lesson's chapter is quoted from
the Ante-Nicene Fathers text under its editors' recension caution ---
corrupted and interpolated manuscripts, a possible later corrector, an
uncertain author --- and Cyprian's martyrs-only reading of the crowd is
kept as the reading Bede rejects; neither carries an interpretation. No
commentary on the Lesson's chapter by St Augustine was used, and he is not
presented as interpreting it; his three loci on the number and the crowd
(\work{De doctrina christiana} III.35.51, \work{Letter XCIII} \S30,
\work{Sermon LXI} 1) are short applications inside other arguments.
Schuster's \work{The Sacramentary} V (English, 1930), Fromage's
continuation of Guéranger's \work{L'Année liturgique} (vol.~15, 1909) and
Durandus's \work{Rationale} are the commentaries read on this Mass as a
Mass; Fromage and Durandus are ecclesiastical writers and carry no
interpretation. Bellarmine is read in O'Sullivan's English, which numbers Psalm~33 one
verse lower than the Vulgate; Basil in the Latin version printed in PG~29;
Aquinas in the Venice and Parma printings. The Offertory's closing
\latin{alleluia} is not presented as ancient; when it entered is not
established. The sweep is bounded by the corpora the study's scope appendix
names, which also names the witnesses not read.
Every connection drawn between texts of the Mass belongs to the
interpretations unless a witness is named as making it, and the
commemorated Sunday's orations are read only for what they say where they
stand in this Mass.

\textbf{Chronology and records.} Every biblical date on page~2 is generated
from the repository's Scripture chronology corpus, whose sources are the
last three entries of the References below. The record holds no date for
the Sermon on the Mount or for the occasion of Psalm~32; each passage's
fuller evidence is given in the study's Scriptural Date and Location
appendix.

\textbf{Rights and use.} The Latin is published on 17 U.S.C. 103(b)
together with its public-domain witnesses, the Mame printing of 1922 and
the Pustet printing of 1862, on the pre-1931 line recorded in
\texttt{guidance/missals.md}, applied as of 26~October 2026. The
Douay--Rheims and the Lasance English are public domain in the United
States; the patristic editions are nineteenth-century or earlier
publications, Fromage's volume was published in Paris in 1909, and
Schuster's in London in 1930. This is a study aid to the 1962 Missal, not
an official liturgical book, translation or critical edition; this
companion's production record is \texttt{research/production-review.md}.
