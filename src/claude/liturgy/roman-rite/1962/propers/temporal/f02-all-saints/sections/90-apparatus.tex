\runninghead{Scope and References}
\section{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}
\label{sec:scope}

{\small
\textbf{Edition, Mass and occurrence.} The study treats the Mass
\latin{In festo omnium Sanctorum}, \latin{Die 1 novembris}, I classis, of the
\work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica, Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis 1962,
printed pp.~702--703, nos.~3974--3983, as said on Sunday 1~November 2026 in
the universal 1962 calendar and no particular calendar, with the commemoration
of the Twenty-third Sunday after Pentecost, pp.~416--417, nos.~1720, 1726 and
1728. The precedence of the feast over the Sunday, the commemoration and its
order, the colour, the Gloria, the Credo and the last Gospel rest on the
Missal's own general rubrics (RG 14, 16, 91, 108, 109~a, 111~a, 120~d; RGMR
431, 434~a, 436--437, 475, 480--481, 505, 509--510), as
\texttt{research/context.md} records. The feast has no Preface of its own. The
general rubrics (RGMR 482, 483, 494~b, 498) and the Missal's direction at the
Vigil of the Nativity (p.~16) admit both the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity
and the common Preface; no registered rubrical commentary read treats a feast
of the first class said on a Sunday of the second class, and the dated Ordo of
the Institute of Christ the King Sovereign Priest, which directs the Trinity
Preface, is one institute's direction. Neither Preface's text is printed. The
\latin{Asperges}, the Ordinary and the Office of the feast are not treated.

\textbf{Text control.} Every Latin form was read on page images of the CMAA
facsimile of the 1962 typical edition and collated word for word with the
Tours printing of 1922 (Mame) for the feast's ten texts and with the Ratisbon
printing of 1862 (Pustet) for the Sunday's three orations; the collation and
the comparison with the Clementine Vulgate are in
\texttt{propers/verified.md}. The rubrics of the 1962 book are described and
not reproduced. The history of the Offertory's wording rests on the chant
books printed in PL 78 and PL 151 and the Sarum Missal, on the text layers of
the Roman printings of 1570, 1604, 1862 and 1889 and of Fromage's volume of
1909, and on the page images of the Mame printing of 1922. No printing between
1909 and 1922 was read, so the entry of the Offertory's \latin{alleluia} is
bounded only by those two dates. The history of the Collect's
\latin{intercessoribus} and \latin{intercessionibus} rests on the sacramentaries
and missals named in the References; none of them shows which reading came
first.

\textbf{English.} The scriptural English is the Douay--Rheims in Challoner's
revision at the canonical verses, with the Missal's departures named beside
each text. The English of the Introit antiphon and of the six orations is the
Lasance \work{New Roman Missal} of 1945, whose rubrics are those before 1960.
The Lasance English of the Lesson's and the Gospel's liturgical openings and of
the Offertory is quoted on the Lasance's attribution only; it has no row in
the repository's register of translations. None of this English is an
approved liturgical translation of the 1962 Missal. Patristic and later
authors are quoted in the nineteenth-century English of the editions named in
the References, or in their Latin or French; where an edition named has no
English, the English beside the quotation is the study's own rendering, and
no English is composed for a liturgical text.

\textbf{Reception.} The interpretations rest on the loci named in the text
and recorded, with editions and bounds, in \texttt{research/scope.md} and
\texttt{research/interpretations.md}. The reading is bounded by the corpora
named there and is not complete. Among the commentaries not read are
Cassiodorus, Theodoret and St Athanasius on the psalms, held only in remote
volumes that were not fetched; St Jerome on Mt~5; St Thomas Aquinas on Mt~11;
St Gregory of Nyssa on the Beatitudes, which the library does not hold; and
the \work{Glossa ordinaria}, known here only as St Bonaventure reports it.
Each interpretation's principal authorities are Fathers and saints with a row
in the repository's register of authorial standing. St Bernard, St
Bonaventure, St Alphonsus Liguori, St Victorinus of Pettau, St Cyprian and St
Bruno of Würzburg have no row there and are cited in support only. Blessed
Ildefonso Schuster, Lucien Fromage, William Durandus and Sicard of Cremona are
read on this Mass as a Mass; Fromage, Durandus and Sicard are ecclesiastical
writers, and none of the four is a principal authority for an
interpretation; Durandus's Mass of the feast had another Alleluia,
\latin{O quam gloriosum}. St Rabanus Maurus is a compiler: only the words his commentary
marks as his own are credited to him, and his comment on Mt 11:28 is cited as
St Jerome's. Every connection drawn between texts of the Mass belongs to the
study's interpretations unless an author is named as making it, and the
orations of the commemorated Sunday are read only for what they say where they
stand in this Mass. St Augustine's \work{Enarrationes} is read in the Latin of
the Migne text, which keeps the Vulgate numbering; his sermons, \work{De
sermone Domini in monte} and \work{Enchiridion} in NPNF. St Robert Bellarmine
is read in O'Sullivan's abridged English, which numbers Psalm~33 one verse
lower than the Vulgate; St Basil in the Latin version printed in PG 29; St
Thomas on Matthew in the Venice printing of 1745, cited by page, with the
lecture number of the Corpus Thomisticum text, and on the Psalms in the Parma
printing of 1863; St Bonaventure in the Quaracchi edition; St Bernard in PL
183. The Parma, Quaracchi and several Migne volumes were read on uncorrected
optical text, with page images checked at the loci marked in
\texttt{research/scope.md}.

\textbf{Chronology.} Every biblical date in the Scriptural Date and Location
appendix is generated from the repository's Scripture chronology corpus, with
its sources named there. The record holds no date for the Sermon on the
Mount or for the occasion of Psalm~32.

\textbf{Rights.} The Latin is published on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) together with its
public-domain witnesses, the Mame printing of 1922 and the Pustet printing of
1862, on the pre-1931 line recorded in \texttt{guidance/missals.md}, applied
as of 26~October 2026. The Douay--Rheims and the Lasance English (public
domain in the United States by nonrenewal) are quoted from registered
editions. The other quotations are short and come from editions published
before 1931: Migne's \work{Patrologia Latina}, vols.~9, 26, 35, 78, 107, 142,
151, 183 and 213, and \work{Patrologia Graeca}, vol.~29; the Venice (1745) and
Parma (1863) Aquinas; the Quaracchi Bonaventure (1893); the Ante-Nicene and
Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers; the Library of the Fathers \work{Moralia};
Marshall's Bede (1878); Duffy's Alphonsus (1843); Feltoe's Leonine (1896) and
Wilson's Gelasian (1894) Sacramentaries; Legg's Sarum Missal (1916); Woolley's
Gilbertine rite (1922); the Durandus of 1568; Fromage's volume (1909); and
Schuster's (1930). The \work{Summa} and \work{Enchiridion} are quoted in older
public-domain translations; the Corpus Thomisticum and Wikisource texts are
used only for Latin that is itself medieval or ancient. Scripture, liturgical texts and quoted
translations are not relicensed by the repository's licence.

\textbf{Use.} This is a study aid to the 1962 Missal, not an official
liturgical book, translation or critical edition.
