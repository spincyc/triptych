\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & An angel seals God's
servants, twelve thousand from each tribe; John then sees a multitude no
man could number. The psalm bids the saints fear the Lord; those who seek
him lack no good. Jesus blesses the poor, the mourning and the persecuted;
the just are in God's hand, in peace.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The angel is the
Lord incarnate, his seal the cross; the number the whole Church, the crowd
the elect of all believers (Bede, Augustine; Victorinus: Elias and the last
times; Cyprian: the martyrs). The Beatitudes are the new law from the
mountain as from Sinai (Augustine), from the Father's majesty (Hilary); the
just are the martyrs (Schuster).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Rejoice in the Lord, not in
yourselves (Augustine); confess the faith openly, as the seal on the
forehead (Bede); begin in humility and be ready to suffer for justice
(Augustine, Chrysostom); keep the filial fear that lacks nothing (Basil,
Bellarmine), bearing want as a physician's diet (Aquinas).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The elect stand at
Christ's right hand, Benjamin's place at the list's end, praising God
unceasingly; the vision is true now of those saved in hope (Bede). The one reward
is begun on the way, completed in the homeland (Aquinas); the clean of
heart, perfected, see God (Hilary).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
