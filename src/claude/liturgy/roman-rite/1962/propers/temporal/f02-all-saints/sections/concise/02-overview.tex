\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & John hears the sealed
counted, twelve thousand from each tribe, then sees a multitude no one could
number, from every nation; Jesus calls blessed the poor in spirit, the
mourners and the persecuted; the psalmist promises no want to those who fear
the Lord; Wisdom's just seemed to die, and are in peace.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The angel from the
east is Christ sealing his own with the cross, and the sealed number is the
whole Church (Bede); Christ teaches the heavenly life from the height of the
Father's majesty (Hilary); the rich who lacked are Israel, and the seekers
who lack no good the nations (Basil).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & The beatitudes are steps rising
from humility and fear (Augustine), a golden chain of precepts (Chrysostom);
the fearer lacks nothing for any virtue (Basil); the gate stays narrow though
many pass it (Augustine); venerating the saints profits us, not them
(Bernard).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The city of angels and
men is complete in a number God knows (Augustine, Aquinas); the reward begun
on the way is perfect in the homeland (Aquinas), for those changed by
immortality (Hilary); God himself is the perfect good (Basil), and the souls
of the just dwell in his protection (Augustine).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
