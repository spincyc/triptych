# Orations and Communion record — Collect, Secret, Postcommunion, Wis 16:20

Verified compositional and reception history for the three orations and the
Communion antiphon. Method: public-domain critical editions and OCR full texts
downloaded and searched with exact and variant strings on 2026-08-19; every
quotation below was read at its printed locus, old orthography preserved
exactly (adsequi/assequi, precipis/praecipis, coelo/caelo). Deshusses and
Mohlberg were not inspected (protected editions); no GeV/Ha/Sup numbers are
claimed anywhere — Wilson's and Feltoe's printed pages serve as locators.

## Collect pedigree — *Omnipotens sempiterne Deus, da nobis fidei, spei et caritatis augmentum*

1. **Verona/Leonine sacramentary — the earliest surviving witness.** Feltoe
   p. 76, July series ("MENSE IULIO"), section "XXXIII. Item alia," marginal
   Muratori coll. 374–375: "Omp semp Ds da nobis fidei spei et caritatis
   augmentum et ut mereamur adsequi quod promittis fac nos amare quod
   praecipis per" (footnote: "MS. mereatur"). With `adsequi`. No Sunday
   assignment — a votive daily-Mass series.
2. **Vatican Gelasian** (Wilson 1894), twice:
   - Book III sect. IX "Item alia Missa," p. 229, in the sixteen unnumbered
     Sunday Masses: text as received ("assequi … quod praecipis"). The same
     missa carries the 1962 Postcommunion (below). Wilson's note: this missa
     (except the Secret) is assigned in the Rheinau and S. Gallen Gelasians to
     the **fifteenth** Sunday after Pentecost; Pamelius's Gregorian has the
     fourteenth, Menard's the fifteenth.
   - Book III sect. XXVI "Orationes pro Caritate," p. 247, a variant recension
     with a Deus-qui opening: "Deus, qui iustitiam tuae legis in cordibus
     credentium digito tuo scribis, da nobis fidei et spei caritatisque
     augmentum, et ut mereamur assequi quod promittis fac nos amare quod
     praecepisti" — apparatus: Pamelius, Gerbert, and the Leofric Missal read
     the received opening and phrasing; V as text.
   - Appendix p. 354: under "Hebd. xv post Pentecosten" the eighth-century
     Gelasians carry the whole 1962 set together — collect, "O. s. D. fac nos
     tibi semper," "Propitiare Dne. populo tuo," preface "VD. Qui nos de
     donis," "Sumptis Dne. caelestibus sacramentis."
3. **Gregorian supplement, Vat. Reg. 337** (Wilson 1915), p. 173
   (fo. 167v–168), section "CXXII. DOMINICA .XIIII. POST PENTECOSTEN.":
   "Omnipotens sempiterne deus. da nobis fidei spei et caritatis augmentum.
   et ut mereamur adsequi quod promittis. fac nos amare quod precipis. per
   dominum," followed by the Super oblata *Propitiare* and Ad complendum
   *Sumptis*. That these numbered Sunday sections belong to the supplement,
   not the Hadrianum proper, is explicit in Wilson's Introduction
   (pp. xxx–xxxi, xli); Wilson-era scholarship credits the supplement to
   Alcuin (pp. xli–xliii); the modern Benedict-of-Aniane attribution rests on
   protected literature and is not claimed.
   - **Chant cues, ninth century:** Wilson's note prints Reg. 337's marginal
     antiphonary cues at this Mass — "Ant. Respice domine in testamentum.
     Resp. Respice domine. Off. In te speraui domine. Com. Panem de caelo" —
     written per his Introduction (p. xxx) at the same time and place as the
     text by a different scribe, first half of the ninth century. **The whole
     1962 formulary, Communion included, is attached to this Sunday in a
     9th-century witness.**
4. **Sunday-number drift table** (all from sources opened): Verona — votive,
   no Sunday; Vatican Gelasian — 9th of sixteen unnumbered Sunday Masses;
   Rheinau/S. Gallen — Hebd. XV post Pentecosten; Gregorian supplement and
   Pamelius — Dom. XIIII; Menard — XV; Sarum (Pearson pp. 241–242) —
   **Fourteenth Sunday after Trinity**, with Gospel Luke 17:11–19;
   Tridentine/1962 — Dom. XIII post Pentecosten (so also the 1863 Trent
   edition's footnote).

## Collect theology and reception

- **Trent quotes the Collect verbatim.** Session VI cap. 10 ("De acceptae
  iustificationis incremento"), 1863 Latin: "Hoc vero iustitiae incrementum
  petit sancta ecclesia, quum orat: Da nobis, Domine, fidei, spei et caritatis
  augmentum" — the edition's own footnote: "Dominica XIII. post Pentecosten."
  Waterworth (1848): "And this increase of justification holy Church begs,
  when she prays, 'Give unto us, O Lord, increase of faith, hope, and
  charity.'" Precise claim: **Trent's only liturgical quotation in the
  justification decree's chapter on increase is this Sunday's Collect** (with
  "Domine" replacing the full address). Honest negative: cap. XI quotes
  Augustine *De natura et gratia* 43 ("Deus impossibilia non iubet …"), not
  the Confessions' "da quod iubes," which is nowhere in the decree.
- **Augustine's lex-orandi argument — context, not this collect.** *De dono
  perseverantiae* 23.63–65 (NPNF I/5): "consider more carefully their prayers,
  which the Church has always used and will use …"; "the Church would not pray
  that faith should be given to unbelievers, unless it believed that God
  converts to Himself both the averse and adverse wills of men." Augustine
  died 430; the collect's earliest witness is later — **he does not quote this
  collect**, but "fac nos amare quod praecipis" is a textbook instance of the
  petitionary logic he canonized against the Pelagians. Stated as context,
  never as citation.
- **Aquinas on the increase of charity** (Latin verified at Corpus
  Thomisticum): II-II q. 24 a. 4 co. ("caritas viae potest augeri … quanto Deo
  magis propinquamus"), a. 6 co. ("non quolibet actu caritatis caritas actu
  augetur, sed quilibet actus caritatis disponit ad caritatis augmentum"),
  a. 7 (no fixed terminus in via); I-II q. 114 a. 8 ("Terminus autem motus
  gratiae est vita aeterna, progressus autem … secundum augmentum caritatis
  vel gratiae … augmentum gratiae cadit sub merito condigni").
  **Honest negative:** "fac nos amare quod praecipis" occurs in neither file
  read, and an exact-phrase search over corpusthomisticum.org found no page —
  bounded to the loci and method stated. Positive control found en route:
  I-II q. 113 a. 9 s.c. quotes the *tenth* Sunday's collect ("Deus, qui
  omnipotentiam tuam parcendo maxime et miserando manifestas") — Aquinas
  demonstrably quotes this collect series, just not (where searched) this
  Sunday's.
- **Guéranger** (TAP II, pp. 309–310): "Let us, therefore, pray with the
  Church, in her Collect, for an increase of the three fundamental virtues of
  faith, hope, and charity; and, that we may deserve to reach the perfection
  of the good which is promised us in heaven, let us sue for the grace of
  devotedness to the commandments of God." Attribution nuance: the
  Time-after-Pentecost volumes were completed by Guéranger's continuators at
  Solesmes; the English is the Shepherd series.
- **The Anglican afterlife, verified at every step:** BCP 1549, "The xiiii
  Sonday [after Trinity]": "ALMIGHTYE and everlastyng God, geve unto us the
  increase of faythe, hope, and charitie; and that we may obteine that whiche
  thou doest promise; make us to love that whiche thou doest commaunde" — with
  the Sarum epistle and this very Sunday's Roman Gospel (Luke 17:11–19).
  Continuity 1552→1559→1604→1637 (adds "Amen")→1662 verified in the 1877
  Oxford parallel edition, pp. 174–175 ("Same as 1549"). Procter & Frere
  pp. 553–554 print the Latin original under "Fourteenth." Sarum antecedent
  verified in Pearson pp. 241–242. Correction established: the derived BCP
  collect is **Trinity 14** (Trinity 13's collect translates the Roman 12th
  Sunday's *Omnipotens et misericors Deus, de cuius munere*).

## Secret — *Propitiare, Domine, populo tuo, propitiare muneribus*

- Vatican Gelasian, Book III sect. XXX "Item alia Missa" (a penitential missa,
  "Parce, Domine, parce peccatis nostris"), p. 249: V reads "Propitiare,
  Domine, **populi tui propitiatus** muneribus; ut hac oblatione placatus et
  indulgentiam nobis tribuas et postulata concedas"; Wilson's apparatus:
  "populo tuo, propitiare" is the reading of the Rheinau and S. Gallen first
  hand and Pamelius — the received doubled-imperative form is the
  eighth-century Frankish reading.
- Gregorian supplement (Wilson 1915) p. 173, Super oblata: as received.
- Leonine: **not found** (all 13 "propitiare" hits inspected; the nearest
  verbal parallel at Muratori col. 432, "collatis in me per gratiam tuam
  propitiare muneribus," is a different prayer, a bishop's own Mass).

## Postcommunion — *Sumptis, Domine, caelestibus sacramentis*

- Vatican Gelasian, Book III.ix p. 229, in the **same missa as the Collect**:
  "Sumptis, Domine, caelestibus sacramentis, ad redemptionis aeternae,
  quaesumus, **proficiant** augmentum" — apparatus: V and S¹ read proficiat,
  R as printed, S², Gerbert, and Pamelius read **proficiamus**, "which gives a
  clearer sense" (Wilson); V itself has proficiamus in the two Book II
  reuses.
- Gelasian Book II reuses it with intercession clauses: II.xlv (St
  Hippolytus): "Sumptis, Domine, sacramentis … intercedente beato martyre tuo
  Ypolito ad redemptionis aeternae proficiamus augmentum"; II.liv (Nativity of
  the BVM), similarly with "intercedente beata et gloriosa semperque virgine
  Dei genitricis Maria."
- Gregorian supplement (Wilson 1915) p. 173, Ad complendum: as received, with
  proficiamus.
- Leonine: **not found** (variant searches negative).
- Schuster (vol. III p. 135) states the Missal cross-reference that this
  Postcommunion "is also that of Monday after the Fourth Sunday in Lent";
  reported as Schuster's statement, not independently collated in a missal
  scan.

## Communion — Wis 16:20 (*Panem de caelo*)

- **The verse and the adaptation.** The Vulgate as carried in Rabanus's lemma:
  "paratum panem de coelo praestitisti illis sine labore, omne delectamentum
  in se habentem, et omnis saporis suavitatem," continuing v. 21 "deserviens
  uniuscujusque voluntati, ad quod quisque volebat, convertebatur." The 1962
  antiphon's "dedisti nobis" is a first-person adaptation whose verb is that
  of Ps 77:24 ("panem caeli dedit eis") — the manna psalm the Wisdom verse
  itself reworks. (Wording control remains `propers/verified.md`.)
- **Ninth-century pairing with this Sunday:** the marginal cue "Com. Panem de
  caelo" against Dominica XIIII post Pentecosten in Vat. Reg. 337 (Wilson
  1915 p. 173 note; cues contemporary with the MS per Introduction p. xxx).
- **Rabanus Maurus, *Commentaria in librum Sapientiae* III.9 (PL 109,
  coll. 748–749** — la.wikisource carries the PL markers 109.0748A–0749A).
  Chapter heading "Praedicat Deum dedisse populo Israeli timenti se escam
  delectamenti." After the literal exposition ("cibum manna coelitus datum
  suavissimum ac saluberrimum"): "Mystice autem … in coelesti cibo consolatio
  superna … significatur. Aliter manna significat panem illum, qui in
  Evangelio ait: Ego sum panis vitae qui de coelo descendi … Quicunque ergo ad
  hujus panis participationem veraciter pervenit, procul dubio vitam aeternam
  sine fine possidebit" — the direct Eucharistic reading of the verse.
- **Liturgical reuse of the versicle** — *Rituale Romanum* (Pustet 1872):
  after-Communion devotion pp. 75–76 ("℣. Panem de coelo praestitisti eis.
  ℟. Omne delectamentum in se habentem," with *Deus qui nobis sub Sacramento
  mirabili*); Communion of the sick p. 81 (singular "praestitisti **ei**");
  Corpus Christi procession pp. 261–262 (after *Tantum ergo*, the same
  versicle with Alleluia, extended by rubric to all processions with the
  Sacrament — the classic Benediction formula). Corpus Christi office: Wis
  16:20 verbatim as the Tierce antiphon (Guéranger TAP I p. 272), and the
  procession versicle at p. 303.
- **Ambrose, *De mysteriis* 8.44–49** (NPNF II/10; the repository holds a
  verified English passage record for §§47–49): manna-vs-Eucharist typology
  on Ps 77:24–25 — "all those who ate that food died in the wilderness, but
  that food which you receive … is the Body of Christ." **Honest negative:**
  Wis 16:20's "omne delectamentum" wording does not occur in *De mysteriis*.
- **Guéranger on this Sunday's Communion** (TAP II p. 325): "sing all the more
  fervently in our Communion-anthem the divine sweetness of this true Bread of
  heaven" (his surrounding Juda/manna framing is his own and is attributed).
- **Schuster** (vol. III p. 135): the Eucharist is bread with "the sweetness
  of every taste" "because in it we receive not just one particular grace
  alone, but the author of all grace himself, and because, since it produces
  its effects according to the dispositions of those who receive it, it is
  wonderfully adapted to all their needs and desires — *Vere panis filiorum*."

## The formulary as a set

- Guéranger reads the whole formulary through promise and ingratitude (TAP II
  pp. 308–309: "the Epistle, and here our Introit, remind us of the time when
  man had nothing to cheer him but hope"); his Secret comment (p. 325) makes
  its two petitions continue the Collect's promise-theme.
- Schuster analyzes the Collect in two movements ("The second part of the
  Collect further elucidates the signification of the first … Here we see the
  effect of divine grace") and the Secret's order ("First comes the prayer of
  propitiation … Next we pray for the fruits of the Eucharist"); his chapter
  title "Tertia post Sancti Laurentii" documents the older naming of these
  Sundays from St Laurence (so too Guéranger p. 307).
- Pius Parsch treats this Sunday but is in copyright — existence noted,
  nothing consulted or quoted.

## Material negatives and failures (method-bounded)

- Collect absent from Wilson's Gelasian outside III.ix, III.xxvi, App. 354;
  collect, Secret, and Postcommunion absent from Feltoe's Leonine except the
  Collect at p. 76; the Secret's only Leonine "propitiare muneribus" is a
  different prayer.
- "fac nos amare quod praecipis" not in ST II-II q. 23–24 or I-II q. 109–114,
  nor findable on corpusthomisticum.org by exact-phrase search.
- "da quod iubes" not in Trent Session VI (either language).
- Wis 16:20 wording absent from Ambrose *De mysteriis*; no public Augustine or
  Cyprian locus on "omne delectamentum in se habentem" found (one bounded
  exact-phrase search; Corpus Corporum's JS search not drivable).
- Gihr, *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass*: no treatment of this Sunday's
  propers (zero hits for "Thirteenth Sunday").
- URL failures recorded: justus.anglican.org 1662 PDF (SSL failure); the
  archive.org item TheGelasianSacramentary's full text truncated at 38 KB
  (defective; gelasiansacrame00wilsgoog used instead).
