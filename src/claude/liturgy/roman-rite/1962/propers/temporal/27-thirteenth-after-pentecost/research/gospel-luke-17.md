# Gospel reception record — Luke 17:11–19 (the ten lepers)

Verified reception research for the appointed Gospel. Every witness below was
opened and read at its own work and locus on 2026-08-19 unless expressly
flagged as fragment-level or unverified. Renderings of public-domain Latin not
attributed to a named public-domain translation are researcher working aids
and are not published; the guide quotes the Latin or a public-domain English.

## Direct exegesis

### Augustine, *Quaestiones Evangeliorum* II.40 (PL 35)

Read complete in Latin at augustinus.it
(`questioni_vangeli_2_libro.htm`). The work occupies PL 35, 1321–1364; the
exact columns of q. 40 were not read from a scan and are not asserted.

- Leprosy is a defect of *color*, not of health or limb (the ten are
  *mundati*, not *sanati*); lepers therefore signify those who lack the
  *scientia verae fidei* and profess variegated doctrines of error: "Leprosi
  ergo non absurde intellegi possunt qui scientiam verae fidei non habentes
  varias doctrinas profitentur erroris … Nulla porro falsa doctrina est quae
  non aliqua vera intermisceat."
- Why priests: the Jewish priesthood prefigures the royal priesthood in the
  Church; doctrine and sacraments are the Church's province (Paul sent to
  Ananias, Cornelius to Peter — Cornelius's household "cleansed while going,"
  receiving the Spirit before baptism: the *dum irent* seed).
- The nine remain "imperfect in the number nine," outside the unity ten
  signifies; the one, the Samaritan — interpreted *custos*, "keeper" — keeps
  the unity of the kingdom by giving back to God what he received (Ps 58:10
  "Fortitudinem meam ad te custodiam").
- Supersessionist sentence, present and noted honestly: "Et quia illi erant
  Iudaei, amisisse per superbiam declarati sunt regnum caelorum, ubi maxime
  unitas custoditur." Attributed to Augustine where used.

### Augustine, *Sermo* 176 (PL 38) — preached on this pericope

Read at augustinus.it `discorso_227_testo.htm` and monumenta.ch (Maurist text;
the Maurist note records that Bede and Florus cite this sermon). Trap
recorded: augustinus.it's `discorso_176_testo.htm` serves Sermo 136/C, not
Sermo 176 — the site's file numbering does not match sermon numbers.

- Anti-Pelagian application of the three readings (1 Tim 1:15–16; Ps 94;
  Lk 17:12–19): none in Adam's *massa* is un-sick, none healed without
  Christ's grace; infants are carried to the physician on others' feet.
- "Doctrina inconstans, non habens unum colorem, mentis lepram significat";
  attributing one's amendment to oneself puts one among the nine: "Noli tibi
  tribuere, ne sis inter novem qui gratias non egerunt." The thankful one is
  the *alienigena* signifying the Gentiles; "numerus ille Christo decimas
  dedit"; "Nostrum nihil, nisi peccatum quod habemus."
- No public-domain English exists at this sermon (NPNF omits it under this
  number; Hill is copyrighted); the guide quotes the Latin.

### Bede, *In Lucae Evangelium Expositio* V, ad loc. (PL 92, 542B–544D)

Read in the repository's registered remote witness (edition
`latin-migne-corpus-corporum-web-2026-07-25`, artifact
`wikisource-in-lucam-116db0ca`), re-retrieved 2026-08-19; the retrieved byte
size (973,336) matches the registered artifact exactly. Embedded Migne column
markers bound the passage at 542B–544D, with the 17:20 section beginning at
545A.

- **Proven mosaic:** the leprosy-as-false-doctrine blocks, the
  Paul/Ananias–Cornelius material, the *custos* etymology, and the nine/ten
  number theology are verbatim Augustine QE II.40 (matched line for line);
  "Et bene, ut salventur, Iesum praeceptorem nominant …" is verbatim Gregory,
  *Moralia* V.11 (rec.) n. 28; the true-thanksgiving gloss reworks *Moralia*
  II.52 n. 82.
- **Bede's editorial mercy:** he **drops Augustine's harshest sentence** (the
  Jews-lost-the-kingdom line). Aquinas's Catena later restores it silently.
- **Bede's own contribution:** the closing paragraph ties this lection to the
  preceding parable (17:5–10) and distinguishes the cleansing of the ten from
  the *salvation* of the one: "Si autem fides salvum fecit eum … ergo perfidia
  perdidit eos qui de acceptis beneficiis Deo gloriam dare neglexerunt" —
  faith's *operatio*, not mere *agnitio*, saves.
- Negative within the witness: Bede abbreviates the lemma and has no comment
  on the route clause "per mediam Samariam et Galilaeam."

### Cyril of Alexandria, *Commentary on Luke* — fragments only

Payne Smith's 1859 Oxford translation (public domain), as carried by
tertullian.org (`cyril_on_luke_11_sermons_110_123.htm`; the retrieved byte
size 123,616 matches the repository's registered artifact
`tertullian-sermons-110-123-html`). **The Syriac of Sermons 113–116 is lost**;
what survives for this pericope is catena fragments printed at pp. 539–541
under the lemmata 17:12 and 17:14. One cannot cite an integral "Sermon 113 on
the ten lepers."

- The priests, "ever envious of His glory," are made witnesses of the cure:
  "He did not heal them first, but sent them to the priests, because the
  priests knew the marks of leprosy, and of its being healed. He sent them to
  the priests, and with them He sent also the healing."
- **The one ancient witness on the route clause:** the thankful one was "of
  foreign race, having been brought thither from Assyria: for the phrase is
  not without meaning, 'in the middle of Samaria and Galilee.'"
- Supersessionist edge, noted: "It shows therefore that the Samaritans were
  grateful, but that the Jews, even when benefited, were ungrateful."

### Bruno of Segni — twice: commentary and homily (PL 165)

Read in the archive.org scan of PL 165 (item
`bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_1854_165`, OCR text layer). Commentary:
*Commentaria in Lucam*, pars II, cap. XVII, sectt. XL–XLI, at approximately
coll. 425–428 (printed "426" visible in the OCR page furniture; asserted only
to that precision). Homily: Homilia C, headed "Dominica XII post Pentecosten"
in the Bruni/Migne arrangement (OCR could support XIII; flagged), with the
editor's note that Haymo carries the exposition at Dominica XIV — direct
evidence of the pericope's shifting pre-Tridentine Sunday assignment.

- The ten are **all sinners** ("Quid enim sunt decem viri leprosi, nisi omnes
  peccatores?"), ten the number of totality; excommunication is the Church's
  casting of lepers "extra castra."
- Penitential-juridical reading of the priests: "non ut sacerdotes eos
  mundarent, sed ut mundos eos esse judicarent" — the priest judges and
  declares clean.
- The *dum irent* theology at its sharpest: "Facile est Domino peccata
  dimittere. Prius enim multoties peccatori debita remittuntur, quam veniat ad
  sacerdotem … quia cor contritum et humiliatum Deus non spernit."
- Cleansing is not election: "Multi enim sunt baptizati, multi a peccatorum
  lepra mundati, qui … in electorum numero non computabuntur."
- The homily's exordium is the fullest medieval engagement with the route:
  "Sed quid per Galilaeam, nisi Judaeos? Et quid per Samariam, nisi gentiles
  intelligamus? … Per medium autem illorum transibat Jesus, quia hos et illos
  vocabat … et ad idem convertebat"; the *castellum* is the Church.
- Ends on faith: "Fides igitur salvat, fides justificat, fides et interius et
  exterius hominem sanat."

### Theophylact of Ohrid — partial access, via the Catena

His Luke commentary is PG 123, coll. 683–1126; no public scan of the specific
columns could be opened (archive.org's PG run lacks vol. 123; DCO's pages
404). Content verified in the Latin excerpts of Aquinas's Catena at
corpusthomisticum.org: the lepers stood afar "quasi verecundantes de
immunditia" yet "facti sunt proximi deprecando"; going to the priests implied
the promise of cure; and the most irenic Greek reading: "nihil impedit
quemquam placere Deo, etsi de genere profano processerit … Nullus etiam natus
ex sanctis superbiat." Stade's 1997 English is copyrighted and is not quoted;
public-domain English access is the 1843 Oxford Catena translation.

## Doctrinal and liturgical reception

### Gregory the Great, *Moralia in Iob* V.11 (rec.) n. 28 (PL 75)

Job's friends, who "mingle right things with perverse," typify heretics
reconciled by the Church's preaching: "Quos bene in Evangelio illa decem
leprosorum mundatio designat (Luc. XVII) … Leprosi itaque haereticos
exprimunt, quia dum rectis prava permiscent, colorem sanum maculis aspergunt.
Unde et bene ut salventur, clamant, Iesu praeceptor." Gregory never preached
on the pericope (see negatives) but is squarely in its reception stream, and
supplies Bede's "Iesu praeceptor" gloss verbatim.

### Pseudo-Augustine, *De vera et falsa poenitentia* c. 10 n. 25 (PL 40)

11th-century anonymous, transmitted among Augustine's works ("Auctor
incertus"); read at la.wikisource (Corpus Corporum TEI of Migne 1841). The
pericope's penance-theology artery:

- Oral, in-person confession grounded on v. 14: "Praecepit enim Dominus
  mundandis, ut ostenderent ora sacerdotibus (Luc. XVII, 14): docens corporali
  praesentia confitenda peccata, non per nuntium, non per scriptum
  manifestanda," with shame itself "partem habens remissionis."
- Contrition before the keys: "Mundati enim sunt leprosi, dum ibant ostendere
  ora sacerdotibus, antequam ad eos pervenirent … Unde patet Deum ad cor
  respicere."
- This treatise fed Gratian (De poen. D.1) and Lombard (Sent. IV) — the hinge
  by which the pericope entered scholastic penance theology. Certainly not
  Augustine; attribution stated wherever used.

### Aquinas, *Catena aurea in Lucam* c. 17 lect. 5 — reception map, audited

Read complete in Latin at corpusthomisticum.org/clc14.html. Voices, in order,
with audit results: **Ambrose** (one clause — checks out; see negatives),
**Titus of Bostra** (the route as peace-making: Christ crosses the two
estranged peoples "quasi pacificans … ut utrosque compingat in unum novum
hominem," Eph 2:15 applied to the route; fragment-level confidence, no
independent public edition), **Cyril** (checks out against the Payne
Smith/Mai fragments), **Theophylact** (consistent, unaudited against PG 123),
**Augustine QE** (checks out verbatim, **including** the supersessionist
sentence Bede had dropped — Aquinas transmits Augustine unsoftened),
**Bede** (checks out against PL 92, 543C–544D; under "Beda" the Catena
silently transmits Gregorian material Bede absorbed). What the map shows: by
the 13th century the pericope's canonical voices were five — Ambrose's clause,
the Greek catena tradition (Titus, Cyril), Augustine's QE, Bede — plus
Theophylact. No Chrysostom, no Gregory-in-name.

### Guéranger, *The Liturgical Year* (tr. Shepherd, Duffy 1900, vol. 11)

Thirteenth Sunday, pp. 307–326; Gospel commentary pp. 317–325. The archive.org
item V11TheLiturgicalYear is the repository's held edition
(`english-duffy-1900-volume-11`); the whole Sunday was read in its OCR.

- Literal explanation: "The lepers are made clean only while on their way to
  show themselves to the priests … That Law gave to the sons of Aaron the
  power, not of curing, but of discerning leprosy" (p. 322); the cure en route
  manifests the new priesthood "whose judgments … by pronouncing the sentence
  of absolution, are to effectually remove the leprosy of souls" — the
  sacrament-of-penance reading; Lev 14's sparrow rite read christologically.
- **The two-Samaritan link — found, via Rupert:** at pp. 315–316 Guéranger
  writes that the Good Samaritan Gospel "formerly was assigned to this present
  Sunday," and quotes Abbot Rupert: "'Every portion of the Office of the
  thirteenth Sunday,' says Abbot Rupert, 'bears on the history of that
  Samaritan, whose name signifies keeper; it is our Lord Jesus Christ who, by
  His Incarnation, comes to the rescue of man, whom the old Law was not able
  to keep from harm.'" Rupert reads the whole office — including the
  Introit/Gradual "Respice … in testamentum tuum" — through
  Samaritanus = *custos*. Rupert's own *De divinis officiis* (PL 170) was not
  opened; unverified lead.
- Frankly supersessionist in texture ("the nine are Jews …"), yet ends in a
  Romans-11 key: "How many ages … must pass over Juda too, before he will
  recognize and adore His God … Let us, by our fervent prayers, hasten the
  time which will be so glorious for the two peoples" (pp. 324–325). Framings
  attributed to Guéranger where cited.
- He also notes the Sunday "takes its name from the Gospel of the ten lepers"
  in the West, while the Greeks read the laborers in the vineyard (p. 307).

### Schuster, *The Sacramentary* vol. III (Burns Oates & Washbourne, 1927)

Thirteenth Sunday ("Tertia post Sancti Laurentii"), pp. 132–135, Gospel
paragraph p. 134; read in the OCR of the repository's held archive.org item
(LiberSacramentorum vol. 3). "This story prefigures the future mission of the
apostles, who, driven out by the faithless people of Judea, turned away and
preached with immense success to the Samaritans and the Gentiles." Blunt
supersessionist sentence quoted in full where used, attributed to Schuster.
US public domain (1927); may remain protected in life+70 jurisdictions —
quoted in short compass.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **The *dum irent* chain is the pericope's most consequential reception
   artery:** Augustine (Cornelius, Spirit before baptism) → Bede → ps-Augustine
   DVFP c. 10 (contrition cleanses before the priest is reached) → Bruno
   ("prius multoties peccatori debita remittuntur, quam veniat ad sacerdotem")
   → scholastic penance doctrine → Guéranger's absolution reading. The Sunday
   Gospel carries, in its reception, the theology of contrition and the keys.
2. **Bede's chapter is a two-source weave** (Augustine QE II.40 + Gregory
   Moralia V.11.28 and II.52.82) with one demonstrable editorial mercy — the
   omitted "quia illi erant Iudaei" sentence — which Aquinas's Catena
   silently reverses.
3. **Only Cyril and Bruno's homily engage the route clause**; Titus (via the
   Catena) turns it into the pericope's most generous theology. No checked
   witness comments on Samaria being named before Galilee.
4. **The two consecutive Samaritan Sundays:** no patristic witness links them;
   the pericope floated between Dom. XII and XIV in medieval homiliaries
   (Bruno; the Haymo cross-reference). The one commentator who binds them is
   Guéranger, through Rupert of Deutz, carrying Augustine's
   *Samaritanus = custos* onto the Good Samaritan as Christ.

## Material negative results

1. **Ambrose has no exposition of Luke 17:11–19** — only the transitional
   clause at *Exp. Luc.* VIII n. 32 (embedded marker PL 15, 1478): "Post haec
   reprehenduntur ingrati: atque ita demum ad tractatum futuri venitur
   judicii." Method: full grep of books VIII–IX raw wikitext over stems lepr-,
   samari-, mundat-, alienigen-, decem, gratias, ingrat-. The Catena's
   one-clause Ambrose excerpt independently confirms this is all he has.
2. **Gregory's XL Homiliae contain no homily on the pericope.** All forty
   homilies plus preface downloaded and grepped; all hits incidental (Mt 10:8
   in I.4; Mt 11:5 in I.6; the Martyrius exemplum in II.39; Jn 8:48 in I.18).
3. **No genuine or pseudo-Chrysostom homily on the ten lepers was found**
   (Latin, English, and Greek-keyed searches; corroborated by the Catena's
   total Chrysostom silence at this lectio).
4. **Bede's "electi in faciem et reprobi retrorsum cadunt" was not found
   verbatim in the Moralia** (grep over all six wikisource parts, ~3.6 MB);
   only the doctrinally parallel Gen 49:17 exposition was found. The sentence
   remains "Gregorian in doctrine, unlocated verbatim."
5. The archive.org 1791 Bruno Opera volume (`bub_gb_OycveJ0REe0C`) has zero
   "leprosi" hits — it evidently lacks the Luke commentary or its OCR fails;
   superseded by the PL 165 scan.
