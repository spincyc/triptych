\section*{The Propers: Map}

\begingroup
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.02}
\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit \cue{Int.}}{\latin{Ecce Deus ádiuvat me} \properrefs{Ps. 53:6--7, with v.~3}}{Psalm of a hunted man}{Antiphon takes the later verses, psalm verse the earlier: confidence is sung before the cry for rescue.}
\properrow{Collect \cue{Coll.}}{\latin{Páteant aures misericórdiæ tuæ} \properrefs{composed oration}}{God's ears opened, the petitioner's mouth formed}{Grants what is asked only after asking God to make us ask what pleases him. Desire is the thing prayed over.}
\properrow{Epistle \cue{Ep.}}{\latin{Non simus concupiscéntes malórum} \properrefs{1 Cor. 10:6--13}}{The wilderness generation read onto the baptized}{Craving, idolatry, fornication, testing, murmuring --- then: let him who thinks he stands watch that he not fall.}
\properrow{Gradual \cue{Grad.}}{\latin{Dómine Dóminus noster} \properrefs{Ps. 8:2}}{One psalm verse split at its caesura}{Answers the warning against self-estimate with a name filling the earth and majesty above the heavens.}
\properrow{Alleluia \cue{All.}}{\latin{Eripe me de inimícis meis} \properrefs{Ps. 58:2}}{Lament of a man watched by those who mean to kill him}{Puts a plea for rescue directly before the Gospel in which the Lord approaches a city that will not know him.}
\properrow{Gospel \cue{Gosp.}}{\latin{Cum appropinquáret Iesus Ierúsalem} \properrefs{Lk. 19:41--47}}{Approach, tears, oracle, Temple action, teaching}{Christ weeps, foretells the siege, clears the Temple with a text about prayer, then keeps teaching there daily.}
\properrow{Offertory \cue{Off.}}{\latin{Iustítiæ Dómini rectæ} \properrefs{Ps. 18:9, 10, 11, 12}}{Four verses centonised from a Law psalm}{Calls the Lord's judgments upright and sweeter than honey --- right after a Gospel in which judgment fell on a city.}
\properrow{Secret \cue{Sec.}}{\latin{Concéde nobis, quæsumus, Dómine} \properrefs{composed oration}}{A \latin{quia} clause supplying the reason}{As often as this victim's memorial is celebrated, the work of our redemption is carried on.}
\properrow{Communion \cue{Comm.}}{\latin{Qui mandúcat meam carnem} \properrefs{Jn. 6:57 Vulg.; 6:56 modern}}{Indwelling as a reciprocal pair}{Eating and drinking named as the way one abides in Christ and Christ in him: the only proper running both ways.}
\properrow{Postcommunion \cue{Postcomm.}}{\latin{Tui nobis, quæsumus, Dómine} \properrefs{composed oration}}{One subject, two objects}{One act is asked to confer purification and to bestow unity: a personal fruit and a corporate one.}
\end{properstable}
\endgroup

\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Christ weeps over Jerusalem, foretells its siege because it did not know the time of its visitation, clears the Temple with a word about the house of prayer, and goes on teaching there daily \cue{Gosp.}. Paul lists what happened to Israel in the desert and says it was written to correct readers like his own \cue{Ep.}.\\
Allegorical & The victim whose memorial is celebrated is the one who wept over the city, and in that memorial the work of our redemption is carried on \cue{Sec.}; the Communion says how that work reaches the communicant, by an eating that makes an abiding \cue{Comm.}.\\
Moral & The Epistle will not leave the warning in the past tense: \latin{qui se exístimat stare, vídeat ne cadat} \cue{Ep.}. The Collect asks what makes such a warning survivable --- that God form what we ask \cue{Coll.} --- and the Offertory calls judgments sweeter than honey to a servant who keeps them \cue{Off.}.\\
Anagogical & What the city missed was a \latin{tempus visitatiónis} \cue{Gosp.}; the Introit answers with a visitation already recognized \cue{Int.}, the Alleluia with the plea of one still under threat \cue{All.}. The Postcommunion names the end: purification and unity, neither yet complete \cue{Postcomm.}.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
