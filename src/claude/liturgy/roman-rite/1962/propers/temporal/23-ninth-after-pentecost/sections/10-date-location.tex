\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

Seven passages are directly appointed. Each has one dossier: a four-field
summary, the narrated event where there is one, then a short explanation.
Composed orations are not listed; their biblical echoes are not quotations.
Psalm citations give the missal's Vulgate number with the English-Bible
equivalent; the appendix adds the Masoretic series.

\begin{dossiertable}
Gradual & Ps. 8:2 Vulg. (Eng. 8:1) & Superscription \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus. Psalmus David.} No place named & Traditional: Davidic, \textit{c.}~1000 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title claims David and a musical direction, not an occasion; unlike Pss.\ 53 and 58 it anchors no episode, and nothing fixes a writing place or a moment in David's life. The 1910 \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} defends the titles as ancient and canonical while conceding many are obscure. Hebrews 2:6--8 later quotes vv.~5--7 verbatim; the Gradual quotes only v.~2.}
\midrule
Offertory & Ps. 18:9--12 Vulg. (Eng. 19:8--11) & Superscription \latin{In finem. Psalmus David.} No episode, place or life stage named & Traditional: Davidic, \textit{c.}~1000 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Torah half of a psalm whose first half praises the wordless witness of the heavens. Its title carries no historical notice at all, so even the traditional horizon is a bare attribution; Romans 10:18 quotes v.~5, Paul's warrant for hearing the psalm of apostolic preaching.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 53:3, 6--7 Vulg. (Eng. 54:1, 4--5) & Title names the Ziphites' report to Saul; narratives at 1 Sam.\ 23:19 and 26:1, in the Judaean wilderness & Traditional: David's pre-royal flight, \textit{c.}~1010 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title's \latin{absconditus est} matches 26:1 rather than 23:19's \latin{latitat}. A title associating a prayer with an episode is received tradition, not proof it was composed then; modern date and authorship remain open.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 58:2 Vulg. (Eng. 59:1) & Title names Saul's watch on David's house; narrative at 1 Sam.\ 19:11, at David's house & Traditional: David's pre-royal danger, \textit{c.}~1010 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title reads \latin{quando misit Saul, et custodivit domum ejus, ut eum interficeret}, and the appointed verse matches the Clementine psalter word for word. The horizon is a night of imminent assassination in a watched house: an inherited setting, not a proved date.}
\midrule
Gospel & Lk. 19:41--47 & Writing: place unknown; traditionally Achaia. Audience largely Gentile and non-Palestinian, through Theophilus & Traditional: before AD 70. Modern: AD 80--90\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: the descent of the Mount of Olives toward Jerusalem (Lk.\ 19:29, 37), then the Temple (19:45), both in present-day Jerusalem; the last week before the Passover of Jesus' death. AD 29 on the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia}'s computation, commonly AD 30--33 later; not settled here.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} identifies Luke the physician and Pauline companion and reports Julius Africanus for Achaia; the NABRE reports the same attribution but places composition after AD 70, many proposing AD 80--90, and leaves the place unstated.}
\midrule
Communion & Jn. 6:57 Clem. (mod. 6:56) & Writing: traditionally Ephesus; the NABRE reports Ephesus, Syria and others & Traditional: \textit{c.}~AD 96. Modern: final form AD 90--100\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: the bread-of-life discourse, located by Jn.\ 6:59 \latin{in synagoga \dots\ in Capharnaum} --- Capernaum on the Sea of Galilee, in present-day northern Israel; during the Galilean ministry.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} argues for John son of Zebedee at Ephesus about AD 96; the NABRE reports that most modern scholars find that evidence insufficient and describes a gospel edited in stages.}
\midrule
Epistle & 1 Cor. 10:6--13 & From Ephesus (1 Cor.\ 16:8), near present-day Selçuk, Türkiye, to Corinth in Greece & Third missionary journey, mid-AD 50s\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Pauline authorship is both traditional and the mainstream modern judgment, and \latin{permanebo autem Ephesi} supplies the writing place. Paul writes into a church he had founded, mixed Jewish and Gentile, divided over food associated with idols. At 10:1 he calls the wilderness generation \latin{patres nostri}: writer, recipients and narrated desert are three different places.}
\end{dossiertable}
