\section{The Appointed Texts}
\label{sec:appointed-texts}

The Latin below is that of the 1962 typical edition, \work{Missale Romanum}
(Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis), pp.~410--411, nos.~1669--1678, read on the page
images of the CMAA facsimile. Every element's wording already stands in the
Pustet \work{Missale Romanum} of Ratisbon, 1862 (pp.~350--351); the two books
differ only in spelling, pointing, capitalisation, citation form and the
rubrics on additional orations that the 1962 book no longer prints. The
English of the scriptural elements is the Douay--Rheims Bible in Bishop
Challoner's revision, which translates the Clementine Vulgate at the Missal's
own Vulgate numbering. It is given at the canonical verses, so that wherever a
chant adapts its source the English and the Latin part company; each such place
is named beneath the text, and where the Douay has no verse that carries the
chant's words, none is printed in its place. The English of the three orations
is the anonymous translation printed in \work{The Roman Missal, Translated into
the English Language for the Use of the Laity} (Philadelphia: Eugene Cummiskey,
1861). Neither English is an approved liturgical translation of the 1962
Missal. Psalms are cited in the Missal's numbering, with the Hebrew numbering
in parentheses.

\subsection{Introit}
\label{proper-introit}

\properlocus{Antiphon cited Ecclus 36:18; verse Ps 121:1 (Heb.\ 122). Missal p.~410, no.~1669.}

\begin{latinproper}
Da pacem, Dómine, sustinéntibus te, ut prophétæ tui fidéles inveniántur:
exáudi preces servi tui, et plebis tuæ Israël. \textnormal{Ps.\ 121, 1}
Lætátus sum in his, quæ dicta sunt mihi: in domum Dómini íbimus.
℣.~Glória Patri.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 121:1, the verse}
I rejoiced at the things that were said to me: We shall go into the house of
the Lord.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The antiphon is not the Vulgate's verse, and no Douay verse carries
it. It asks peace for those who wait on the Lord, that his prophets may be
found faithful, and a hearing for the prayers of his servant and of his people
Israel. At the cited place the Clementine Vulgate reads \latin{Da mercedem
sustinentibus te, ut prophetae tui fideles inveniantur: et exaudi orationes
servorum tuorum}, which the Douay renders ``Reward them that patiently wait
for thee, that thy prophets may be found faithful: and hear the prayers of thy
servants''. The chant keeps the middle clause word for word, asks
\latin{pacem} where the Vulgate asks \latin{mercedem}, adds the vocative
\latin{Domine}, and closes with one servant and the people Israel where the
Vulgate has many servants. The Greek of the same verse (Sir 36:15 in its
numbering) asks a reward, as the Vulgate does. After the \latin{Gloria Patri}
the antiphon is repeated, as the general rubrics direct (no.~427); the typical
edition prints no repetition cue.

\subsection{Collect}
\label{proper-collect}

\properlocus{\latin{Oratio}. Missal p.~410, no.~1670.}

\begin{latinproper}
Dírigat corda nostra, quǽsumus, Dómine, tuæ miseratiónis operátio: quia tibi
sine te placére non póssumus. Per Dóminum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVIII Sunday after Pentecost, Collect}
May the influence of thy mercy, O Lord, direct our souls: for without thy help
we can do nothing well pleasing to thee. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Collect is the only oration of the Mass. The 1862 Missal adds a
second and a third, which belong to the rubrics before 1960 and are no part of
the 1962 formulary. The conclusion is printed short; it is said in the full
form of the general rubrics (no.~115~a).

\subsection{Epistle}
\label{proper-epistle}

\properlocus{\latin{Lectio Epistolae beati Pauli Apostoli ad Corinthios}. 1 Cor 1:4--8. Missal p.~410, no.~1671.}

\begin{latinproper}
Fratres: Grátias ago Deo meo semper pro vobis in grátia Dei, quæ data est
vobis in Christo Iesu: quod in ómnibus dívites facti estis in illo, in omni
verbo, et in omni sciéntia: sicut testimónium Christi confirmátum est in vobis:
ita ut nihil vobis desit in ulla grátia, exspectántibus revelatiónem Dómini
nostri Iesu Christi, qui et confirmábit vos usque in finem sine crímine, in
die advéntus Dómini nostri Iesu Christi.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), 1 Cor 1:4--8}
\textsuperscript{4}I give thanks to my God always for you, for the grace of
God that is given you in Christ Jesus: \textsuperscript{5}That in all things
you are made rich in him, in all utterance and in all knowledge;
\textsuperscript{6}As the testimony of Christ was confirmed in you,
\textsuperscript{7}So that nothing is wanting to you in any grace, waiting for
the manifestation of our Lord Jesus Christ. \textsuperscript{8}Who also will
confirm you unto the end without crime, in the day of the coming of our Lord
Jesus Christ.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The address \latin{Fratres} is the liturgy's and has no Douay verse;
thereafter the lesson follows the Vulgate word for word. It ends at verse 8,
one verse before \latin{Fidelis Deus} and two before the appeal against schisms
(1:10).

\subsection{Gradual}
\label{proper-gradual}

\properlocus{Ps 121:1 and 7 (Heb.\ 122). Missal p.~410, no.~1672.}

\begin{latinproper}
Lætátus sum in his, quæ dicta sunt mihi: in domum Dómini íbimus. ℣.~Fiat pax
in virtúte tua: et abundántia in túrribus tuis.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 121:1, 7}
\textsuperscript{1}I rejoiced at the things that were said to me: We shall go
into the house of the Lord. \textsuperscript{7}Let peace be in thy strength:
and abundance in thy towers.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The respond is the Introit's verse, sung a second time; the versicle
is verse 7. The psalm's title, \latin{Canticum graduum}, which the Vulgate
counts within verse 1, is not sung.

\subsection{Alleluia}
\label{proper-alleluia}

\properlocus{Ps 101:16 (Heb.\ 102:16; 102:15 where the title is not numbered). Missal p.~410, no.~1673.}

\begin{latinproper}
Allelúia, allelúia. ℣.~\textnormal{Ps.\ 101, 16} Timébunt gentes nomen tuum,
Dómine, et omnes reges terræ glóriam tuam. Allelúia.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 101:16}
And the Gentiles shall fear thy name, O Lord, and all the kings of the earth
thy glory.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal drops the Vulgate's opening \latin{Et}, which the Douay's
``And'' translates, and ends the sentence where the psalm runs on into verse
17, \latin{quia aedificavit Dominus Sion}, ``For the Lord hath built up Sion''.

\subsection{Gospel}
\label{proper-gospel}

\properlocus{\latin{Sequentia sancti Evangelii secundum Matthaeum}. Mt 9:1--8. Missal pp.~410--411, no.~1674.}

\begin{latinproper}
In illo témpore: Ascéndens Iesus in navículam, transfretávit, et venit in
civitátem suam. Et ecce offerébant ei paralýticum iacéntem in lecto. Et videns
Iesus fidem illórum, dixit paralýtico: Confíde, fili, remittúntur tibi peccáta
tua. Et ecce quidam de scribis dixérunt intra se: Hic blasphémat. Et cum
vidísset Iesus cogitatiónes eórum, dixit: Ut quid cogitátis mala in córdibus
vestris? Quid est facílius dícere: Dimittúntur tibi peccáta tua; an dícere:
Surge, et ámbula? Ut autem sciátis, quia Fílius hóminis habet potestátem in
terra dimitténdi peccáta, tunc ait paralýtico: Surge, tolle lectum tuum, et
vade in domum tuam. Et surréxit, et ábiit in domum suam. Vidéntes autem turbæ
timuérunt, et glorificavérunt Deum, qui dedit potestátem talem homínibus.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Mt 9:1--8}
\textsuperscript{1}And entering into a boat, he passed over the water and came
into his own city. \textsuperscript{2}And behold they brought to him one sick
of the palsy lying in a bed. And Jesus, seeing their faith, said to the man
sick of the palsy: Be of good heart, son, thy sins are forgiven thee.
\textsuperscript{3}And behold some of the scribes said within themselves: He
blasphemeth. \textsuperscript{4}And Jesus seeing their thoughts, said: Why do
you think evil in your hearts? \textsuperscript{5}Whether is easier, to say,
Thy sins are forgiven thee: or to say, Arise, and walk?
\textsuperscript{6}But that you may know that the Son of man hath power on
earth to forgive sins, (then said he to the man sick of the palsy,) Arise,
take up thy bed, and go into thy house. \textsuperscript{7}And he arose, and
went into his house. \textsuperscript{8}And the multitude seeing it, feared,
and glorified God that gave such power to men.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The Missal's opening, \latin{In illo tempore: Ascendens Iesus in
naviculam}, supplies the subject where the Vulgate's verse 1 begins \latin{Et
ascendens in naviculam}; the Douay translates the Vulgate. The Creed follows.

\subsection{Offertory}
\label{proper-offertory}

\properlocus{\latin{Ant.\ ad Offertorium}, cited Ex 24:4 and 5. Missal p.~411, no.~1675.}

\begin{latinproper}
Sanctificávit Móyses altáre Dómino, ófferens super illud holocáusta, et
ímmolans víctimas: fecit sacrifícium vespertínum in odórem suavitátis Dómino
Deo, in conspéctu filiórum Israël.
\end{latinproper}

\noindent The antiphon is a compilation and not a quotation of the verses it
cites, and no Douay verse renders it; no English is printed for it here. Its
subject is Moses at the altar: he hallows it, offers holocausts and victims
upon it, and makes an evening sacrifice of sweet savour, while the children of
Israel look on. The cited
verses share only its substance: in the Douay, Moses ``built an altar at the
foot of the mount, and twelve titles according to the twelve tribes of
Israel'', and the young men ``offered holocausts, and sacrificed pacific
victims of calves to the Lord'' (Ex 24:4--5). The antiphon's
\latin{sanctificavit} stands where the Vulgate says \latin{aedificavit}; its
evening sacrifice and odour of sweetness are the language of the daily
sacrifice (Ex 29:41) and of the evening offering of Ps 140:2; and its last
words, \latin{in conspectu filiorum Israel}, occur in the same chapter at
verse 17, where the glory of the Lord is seen on the mountain ``in the eyes of
the children of Israel''.

\subsection{Secret}
\label{proper-secret}

\properlocus{\latin{Secreta}. Missal p.~411, no.~1676.}

\begin{latinproper}
Deus, qui nos, per huius sacrifícii veneránda commércia, uníus summæ
divinitátis partícipes éfficis: præsta, quǽsumus; ut, sicut tuam cognóscimus
veritátem, sic eam dignis móribus assequámur. Per Dóminum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVIII Sunday after Pentecost, Secret}
O God, who by the participation of this august sacrifice makest us partakers of
the one supreme divine nature: grant, we beseech thee, that as we know thy
truth, so we may shew it by a worthy conduct of life. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent A direction to say the Preface of the Most Holy Trinity follows the
Secret on the printed page.

\subsection{Communion}
\label{proper-communion}

\properlocus{Ps 95:8--9 (Heb.\ 96). Missal p.~411, no.~1677.}

\begin{latinproper}
Tóllite hóstias, et introíte in átria eius: adoráte Dóminum in aula sancta
eius.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps 95:8b--9a}
Bring up sacrifices, and come into his courts: Adore ye the Lord in his holy
court.
\end{englishwitness}

\noindent The antiphon begins inside verse 8, leaving aside ``Bring to the Lord
glory unto his name'', and stops before the second half of verse 9. Where the
Missal sings \latin{in aula sancta eius}, the Clementine Vulgate reads
\latin{in atrio sancto eius}, which is what the Douay's ``holy court''
translates; the Pustet Missal of 1862 already has \latin{aula}.

\subsection{Postcommunion}
\label{proper-postcommunion}

\properlocus{\latin{Postcommunio}. Missal p.~411, no.~1678.}

\begin{latinproper}
Grátias tibi reférimus, Dómine, sacro múnere vegetáti: tuam misericórdiam
deprecántes; ut dignos nos eius participatióne perfícias. Per Dóminum.
\end{latinproper}

\begin{englishwitness}{Cummiskey, The Roman Missal (1861), XVIII Sunday after Pentecost, Postcommunion}
Being fed, O Lord, with this holy sacrifice: we give thee thanks, humbly
beseeching thy mercy: that thou wouldst make us worthy of what we have
received. Thro'.
\end{englishwitness}
