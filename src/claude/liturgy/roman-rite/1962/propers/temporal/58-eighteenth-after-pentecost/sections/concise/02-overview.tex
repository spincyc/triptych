\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & A people waiting on the Lord
asks for peace and sings its way up to his house. Paul thanks God for the
gifts of a church he must next warn against schisms. In his own city Jesus
forgives a paralytic whom others carried, and bids him rise and go home, that
the scribes may know that the Son of man has power on earth to forgive. Moses
offers sacrifice in the sight of Israel; the nations are bidden into the Lord's
courts.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The house is the
Church built of living stones (Augustine, Hilary, Cassiodorus), whose two walls,
from Israel and the nations, meet in the Corner Stone (Augustine). The one who
forgives is God (Chrysostom, Augustine), and his power is exercised in the
Church by way of ministry, not authority (Aquinas). The covenant altar figures
the blood shed for the remission of sins (Chrysostom on Hebrews); in the
paralytic the nations are brought for healing (Hilary).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Peace is charity, and charity
``slayeth what we have been, that we may be what we were not'' (Augustine).
What the Corinthians most admired in themselves was given, and where grace is,
boasting has no ground (Chrysostom). The one present a creature can bring is a
contrite heart (Augustine). The man who was carried is told to carry his bed
(Aquinas).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The courts are entered
in this Mass, and the city is still ahead: the eternal Jerusalem (Augustine),
whence no one would ever wish to go out (Cassiodorus). The forgiven man's way home
is the way back to paradise (Hilary, Ambrose), or into the house of eternity
(Aquinas). The confirming to the end, in the day of the Lord's coming, and the
being made worthy of the gift are God's to give (1 Cor 1:8; the
Postcommunion).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
