# Eighteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical leaf
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/58-eighteenth-after-pentecost` and for
every output derived from it. It carries the Latin of the controlling edition
exactly as printed, the collation of every element against a second 1962 witness
and against the public-domain 1862 antecedent, each element's relation to the
Clementine Vulgate, and the lawful English route for each element. It contains
no study prose.

### Marginal numbers: what the scan does and does not show

The marginal numbers of the **right-hand** column of printed p. 410 are cut off at
the right-hand edge of the page as the CMAA digitisation reproduces it. Read at
native 500 ppi on a strip cropped at that margin, the four numbers of that column
print as `167` beside the Epistle, `167` plus a fragment of a fourth digit beside
the Gradual, `167` plus a fragment beside the Alleluia, and `167` beside the
Gospel. **Numbers 1671–1674 are therefore established by continuity** between the
fully legible 1670 in the left column of the same page and the fully legible
1675–1678 on p. 411, and not read whole. The left-hand column of p. 410 (1666,
1667, 1668, 1669, 1670) and both columns of p. 411 (1675–1679 at its left edge,
1680–1684 at its right) are legible.

**The loss is at the inner margin, not the fore-edge.** Printed p. 410 sets its
folio number at the far left of the running head and p. 411 sets its own at the
far right, so 410 is a verso and 411 a recto; the outer margin of p. 410 is
therefore its left-hand one, and that margin is intact. The right-hand margin of
a verso is the gutter. The same gutter margin is captured on p. 411, where
1675–1678 print legibly at the left edge, so the loss is a property of this one
page's reproduction and not a systematic feature of the scan.
`research/context.md` describes the same loss as running off the fore-edge; that
is a characterisation this stage corrects, and it does not touch that record's
substantive point, which is that the numbers cannot be read whole and are fixed
by continuity. The Internet Archive `sp07` digitisation of the same edition was
not opened in this stage; `research/context.md` records that it clips these
numbers entirely.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1669

**Printed rubric line.** `Ant. ad Introitum` … `Eccli. 36, 18`

**Latin as printed.**

> **D**a pacem, Dómine, sustinéntibus te, ut prophétæ tui fidéles inveniántur:
> exáudi preces servi tui, et plebis tuæ Israël. *Ps. 121, 1* Lætátus sum in his,
> quæ dicta sunt mihi: in domum Dómini íbimus. ℣. Glória Patri.

The `D` is a four-line drop capital. `Israël` carries a diaeresis. The psalm
citation `Ps. 121, 1` is set inline, in the same size as the antiphon, before the
verse. No repetition cue is printed.

**Against the Clementine.** Ecclus 36:18 reads *Da mercedem sustinentibus te, ut
prophetæ tui fideles inveniantur: et exaudi orationes servorum tuorum*. The
Missal keeps the middle clause word for word, reads `Da pacem, Dómine` for *Da
mercedem*, and closes `exáudi preces servi tui, et plebis tuæ Israël` for *et
exaudi orationes servorum tuorum*. Ps 121:1 reads *Canticum graduum. Lætatus sum
in his quæ dicta sunt mihi: In domum Domini ibimus*; the Missal omits the title,
which the Clementine counts inside v. 1, and points `in his, quæ`.

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1670

**Printed rubric line.** `Oratio`

**Latin as printed.**

> **D**írigat corda nostra, quǽsumus, Dómine, tuæ miseratiónis operátio: quia
> tibi sine te placére non póssumus. Per Dóminum.

The `D` is a two-line drop capital. The conclusion is printed short, as
`Per Dóminum.`; RG 115 a gives the full form. This is the one oration of the Mass
(RGMR 433, 434 b).

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1671 (clipped; fixed by continuity)

**Printed rubric line.** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli / ad Corínthios.`
… `1 Cor. 1, 4-8`

**Latin as printed.**

> **F**ratres: Grátias ago Deo meo semper pro vobis in grátia Dei, quæ data est
> vobis in Christo Iesu: quod in ómnibus dívites facti estis in illo, in omni
> verbo, et in omni sciéntia: sicut testimónium Christi confirmátum est in vobis:
> ita ut nihil vobis desit in ulla grátia, exspectántibus revelatiónem Dómini
> nostri Iesu Christi, qui et confirmábit vos usque in finem sine crímine, in die
> advéntus Dómini nostri Iesu Christi.

`Fratres:` is the liturgical address and is not part of 1 Cor 1:4. The lection
ends at v. 8 with no added doxology. There is no shorter form.

**Against the Clementine.** The Clementine reads *Gratias ago Deo meo semper pro
vobis in gratia Dei, quæ data est vobis in Christo Jesu* and continues to v. 8
in the same words, with `Jesu`/`Jesus` for the Missal's `Iesu`/`Iesus` and
`exspectantibus` as the Missal has it. No word differs.

## 4. Gradual — marginal no. 1672 (clipped; fixed by continuity)

**Printed rubric line.** `Graduale` … `Ps. 121, 1 et 7`

**Latin as printed.**

> Lætátus sum in his, quæ dicta sunt mihi: in domum Dómini íbimus. ℣. Fiat pax in
> virtúte tua: et abundántia in túrribus tuis.

Respond v. 1, verse v. 7, in that order. The respond omits the psalm's title
*Canticum graduum*. `Fiat` carries no accent: the edition accents only words of
three syllables or more.

**Against the Clementine.** Ps 121:1 as above; Ps 121:7 reads *Fiat pax in
virtute tua, et abundantia in turribus tuis* — a comma where the Missal sets a
colon. Pointing only.

## 5. Alleluia — marginal no. 1673 (clipped; fixed by continuity)

**Printed rubric line.** none; the citation `Ps. 101, 16` is set inline after the
second `allelúia`.

**Latin as printed.**

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 101, 16* Timébunt gentes nomen tuum, Dómine, et
> omnes reges terræ glóriam tuam. Allelúia.

**Against the Clementine.** Ps 101:16 reads *Et timebunt gentes nomen tuum,
Domine, et omnes reges terræ gloriam tuam:* — the Missal drops the opening
connective and closes the sentence where the Clementine runs on with a colon into
v. 17 (*quia ædificavit Dominus Sion*). The Clementine counts the psalm's
inscription as v. 1, so English Bibles that leave it unnumbered call this verse
102:15; the tracked concordance
(`…challoner-gutenberg-1581/artifacts/psalm-numbering-ee3c7757/`) records exactly
that, with `english_offset = 1` for Ps 101:2–29 and `0` for the inscription row.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1674 (clipped; fixed by continuity)

**Printed rubric line.** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthǽum.` …
`Mt. 9, 1-8`

**Latin as printed.**

> **I**n illo témpore: Ascéndens Iesus in navículam, transfretávit, et venit in
> civitátem suam. Et ecce offerébant ei paralýticum iacéntem in lecto. Et videns
> Iesus fidem illórum, dixit paralýtico: Confíde, fili, remittúntur tibi peccáta
> tua. Et ecce quidam de scribis dixérunt intra se: Hic blasphémat. Et cum vidísset
> Iesus cogitatiónes eórum, dixit: Ut quid cogitátis mala in córdibus vestris? Quid
> est facílius dícere: Dimittúntur tibi peccáta tua; an dícere: Surge, et ámbula?
> Ut autem sciátis, quia Fílius hóminis habet potestátem in terra dimitténdi
> peccáta, tunc ait paralýtico: Surge, tolle lectum tuum, et vade in domum tuam.
> Et surréxit, et ábiit in domum suam. Vidéntes autem turbæ timuérunt, et
> glorificavérunt Deum, qui dedit potestátem talem homínibus. Credo.

The cross precedes the rubric. `Credo.` closes the last line of the lection. The
text crosses the page break inside v. 6, at `tunc ait | paralýtico`.

**Against the Clementine.** Mt 9:1 reads *Et ascendens in naviculam,
transfretavit, et venit in civitatem suam*; the liturgical opening `In illo
témpore: Ascéndens Iesus in navículam` supplies the subject in place of the
connective. Thereafter vv. 2–8 agree word for word, with `Jesus` for `Iesus`
(vv. 2 and 4) and `jacentem` for `iacéntem` (v. 2), and **two** points of
pointing. Those two spellings are the only differences of letter: the list was
re-collated token by token on 2026-09-22 against row `Matt 9 2`–`Matt 9 8` of the
bound `47-matthew.tsv`, and at v. 6 the Clementine reads `Filius hominis`,
capitalised, so it differs from the Missal's `Fílius` by the accent alone. (An
earlier draft listed `filius` for `Fílius` here. Lower-case `filius` is the 1862
Pustet's reading — its table below prints `quia filius hóminis` — and not the
Clementine's, the same misattribution of an 1862 reading as the v. 5 comma
corrected below.)
At v. 2 the Clementine sets `Confide fili` without the Missal's comma. At v. 5 the
Clementine sets a **colon** where the Missal sets a semicolon: the Clementine reads
`Quid est facilius dicere: Dimittuntur tibi peccata tua: an dicere: Surge, et
ambula?`, the Missal `Quid est facílius dícere: Dimittúntur tibi peccáta tua; an
dícere: Surge, et ámbula?` — identical up to `dícere:`, and differing at `peccáta
tua;` against `peccata tua:`. `Surge, et ambula` is pointed the same in both.

**A correction to this record's own earlier draft.** It reported three points and
gave the second as the Clementine's setting `Quid est facilius, dicere` with a
comma after *facilius*. **There is no comma there.** The tracked chapter file
`src/sources/bibles/clementine-vulgate/chapters/Matt/9.json` and the bound
`artifact.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc.verse-text-47-matthew-2d02758d`
(row `Matt 9 5` of `47-matthew.tsv`) both read `Quid est facilius dicere:`, which
is the Missal's own pointing at that place; the asserted difference did not exist,
and the real one — the semicolon against the colon — was unrecorded. Both witnesses
were re-read on 2026-09-22. **Where the stray comma came from is visible in this
record's own 1862 table below**: the 1862 Pustet prints `Quid est facílius,
dícere`, with exactly that comma, against the 1962's `Quid est facílius dícere`.
It is the antecedent Missal's pointing, not the Clementine's, and the earlier
draft attributed it to the wrong witness. The same table shows the 1862 agreeing
with the Clementine at v. 2 as well — `Confide fili`, where the 1962 points
`Confíde, fili`.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1675

**Printed rubric line.** `Ant. ad Offertorium` … `Exodi 24, 4 et 5`

**Latin as printed.**

> Sanctificávit Móyses altáre Dómino, ófferens super illud holocáusta, et
> ímmolans víctimas: fecit sacrifícium vespertínum in odórem suavitátis Dómino
> Deo, in conspéctu filiórum Israël.

**Against the Clementine.** This is not a quotation of Ex 24:4–5 and not a
rendering of them. See *The Offertory's wording* below.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1676

**Printed rubric line.** `Secreta`

**Latin as printed.**

> **D**eus, qui nos, per huius sacrifícii veneránda commércia, uníus summæ
> divinitátis partícipes éfficis: præsta, quǽsumus; ut, sicut tuam cognóscimus
> veritátem, sic eam dignis móribus assequámur. Per Dóminum.
>
> *Præfatio de Ss͞ma Trinitate.*

The `D` is a two-line drop capital. The conclusion is printed short. The rubric
line naming the Preface is set in the smaller rubric type immediately under the
prayer; `Ss͞ma` carries a macron. Said secretly and in the same number as the
opening orations (RGMR 480–481), hence one.

The printed length of a conclusion is a typographic variable of the edition, not
a property of the prayer: on the same page p. 410 the preceding formulary's
Secret at no. 1666 prints `Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui
tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.`, itself short of the full form RG 115 a gives.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1677

**Printed rubric line.** `Ant. ad Communionem` … `Ps. 95, 8-9`

**Latin as printed.**

> Tóllite hóstias, et introíte in átria eius: adoráte Dóminum in aula sancta eius.

**Against the Clementine.** Ps 95:8 reads *afferte Domino gloriam nomini ejus.
Tollite hostias, et introite in atria ejus;* and v. 9 *adorate Dominum in atrio
sancto ejus. Commoveatur a facie ejus universa terra;*. The antiphon begins
inside v. 8, omitting its first clause, ends before the second clause of v. 9,
and reads `in aula sancta eius` where the Clementine reads *in atrio sancto ejus*.
That last is a real difference of wording and not of pointing.

## 10. Postcommunion — marginal no. 1678

**Printed rubric line.** `Postcommunio`

**Latin as printed.**

> **G**rátias tibi reférimus, Dómine, sacro múnere vegetáti: tuam misericórdiam
> deprecántes; ut dignos nos eius participatióne perfícias. Per Dóminum.

The `G` is a two-line drop capital. The conclusion is printed short. As many as
the opening orations (RGMR 505), hence one.

## Appointed text against the Bible text

### Where the chant wording follows the Clementine

The Gradual (both members), the Alleluia (apart from the dropped connective and
the closing point) and the Introit's psalm verse follow the Clementine word for
word. The Epistle and the Gospel follow it word for word apart from the
liturgical address, the liturgical opening, orthography and pointing.

### The Introit's wording

Three separate things happen at Ecclus 36:18, and none of them is a scribal
variant of the Clementine:

1. `Da pacem, Dómine` stands where the Clementine reads *Da mercedem*. The Greek
   at the corresponding verse — Sir 36:15 in the Greek numbering — reads
   *δὸς μισθὸν τοῖς ὑπομένουσίν σε*, "give **reward** to them that wait for thee",
   so the Latin *mercedem* renders the Greek and *pacem* renders neither. Checked
   in the CATSS morphological Septuagint
   (`artifact.catss.lxxm-morphology.unicode-fddec9b.github-archive-fddec9b-74c72484`,
   SHA-256 matched on fetch), `34.Sirach.txt`, Sir 36:15.
2. `Dómine` is added; neither the Clementine nor the Greek has a vocative there.
3. `exáudi preces servi tui, et plebis tuæ Israël` stands where the Clementine
   reads *et exaudi orationes servorum tuorum* (plural servants, no Israel). The
   Greek's next verse, Sir 36:16, reads *εἰσάκουσον κύριε δεήσεως τῶν ἱκετῶν σου*
   — again plural, and again without Israel.

A literal search of the whole tracked Clementine finds **no occurrence of
`Da pacem` anywhere in that edition**, and exactly one occurrence of
`plebis tuae Israel`, at Luke 2:32 (*lumen ad revelationem gentium, et gloriam
plebis tuæ Israël*). The nearest scriptural shape for the antiphon's closing
petition is Solomon's prayer at the dedication of the Temple, 3 Kings 8:30
(*ut exaudias deprecationem servi tui et populi tui Israël*) and its parallel
2 Par 6:21 (*exaudias preces famuli tui, et populi tui Israël*); neither is
verbatim, and `preces servi tui` occurs nowhere in the Clementine. **These are
textual observations. This record asserts no source and no compiler's intention
for the antiphon's wording**; `research/scope.md` §2 carries what the search
found and what it did not.

### The Offertory's wording

The antiphon is cited to `Exodi 24, 4 et 5` and is a compilation, not a
quotation. Ex 24:4–5 in the Clementine reads:

> *Scripsit autem Moyses universos sermones Domini: et mane consurgens, **ædificavit
> altare** ad radices montis, et duodecim titulos per duodecim tribus Israël.
> Misitque juvenes de filiis Israël, et **obtulerunt holocausta, immolaveruntque
> victimas** pacificas Domino, vitulos.*

Only the emphasised substance is shared. Four features of the antiphon stand
outside those two verses, and each was located by literal search of the whole
tracked Clementine:

- **`Sanctificávit … altáre`.** The Clementine has no `sanctificavit altare`
  anywhere. Its two places where Moses sanctifies the altar are Lev 8:11
  (*Cumque sanctificans aspersisset altare septem vicibus … sanctificavit oleo*)
  and Num 7:1 (*unxitque et sanctificavit cum omnibus vasis suis, altare
  similiter*). Ex 24:4 says *ædificavit*.
- **`fecit sacrifícium vespertínum`.** `sacrificium vespertinum` occurs three
  times in the whole Clementine: Ps 140:2, 1 Esd 9:4 and 4 Kings 16:15. It does
  not occur in Exodus. The evening member of the daily sacrifice is described at
  Ex 29:41 (*Alterum vero agnum offeres ad vesperam … in odorem suavitatis*).
- **`in odórem suavitátis`.** Ten occurrences, all in the sacrificial formulae of
  Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Ezechiel, and once in Eph 5:2. Ex 29:41 is the
  one that also carries the evening.
- **`in conspéctu filiórum Israël`.** Five occurrences, one of which is
  **Ex 24:17** — the same chapter, three verses past the cited range: *Erat autem
  species gloriæ Domini quasi ignis ardens super verticem montis in conspectu
  filiorum Israël.*

The Greek does not account for the difference either. LXX Ex 24:4 reads
*ᾠκοδόμησεν θυσιαστήριον* ("he built an altar"), and Ex 24:5
*ἀνήνεγκαν ὁλοκαυτώματα καὶ ἔθυσαν θυσίαν σωτηρίου τῷ θεῷ μοσχάρια* — a
peace-offering, with no sanctifying, no evening and no odour of sweetness; LXX
Ex 24:17 ends *ἐναντίον τῶν υἱῶν Ισραηλ*. Checked in the same CATSS artifact,
`02.Exod.txt`.

**So the Offertory's distinctive clauses are in neither the Hebrew-based Latin nor
the Greek of Ex 24:4–5.** What Latin exemplar or chant tradition supplied them was
not established here; `research/scope.md` §5 records the search that was run and
the witnesses that were not reached.

### The Communion's wording

`in aula sancta eius` for the Clementine's *in atrio sancto ejus* is the one word
of the ten elements where the chant and the Bible text of the same edition
disagree outright. The 1862 Pustet prints `in aula sancta ejus` too, so the
reading is at least a century older than the 1962 book and is not a 1962 error.
Whether it descends from the Roman Psalter or from another Latin psalter was not
settled: **no Roman Psalter witness was reached in this stage.**

## English: the lawful route for each element

- **The six scriptural elements** take the Douay–Rheims (Challoner),
  `edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
  at the loci resolved in the Missal's Vulgate psalm numbering. Its canonical
  verses are **not** the Missal's wording in five places, and English must never
  be composed to close the gap:
  - Introit antiphon: the Douay at Ecclus 36:18 reads *Reward them that patiently
    wait for thee, that thy prophets may be found faithful: and hear the prayers
    of thy servants* — it cannot carry `Da pacem, Dómine` or `plebis tuæ Israël`.
  - Epistle: the address *Fratres* has no Douay verse.
  - Gospel: the Douay at Mt 9:1 reads *And entering into a boat, he passed over
    the water and came into his own city* — it cannot carry `Ascéndens Iesus`.
  - Offertory: the Douay at Ex 24:4–5 renders the Vulgate, not the antiphon.
  - Communion: the Douay at Ps 95:9 reads *Adore ye the Lord in his holy court* —
    it cannot carry `in aula sancta eius`.
  Where the Douay cannot carry the Missal's form, the profile's rule applies: say
  so, and give the Latin with a description of what the text says.
- **The three orations** take the 1861 Cummiskey hand missal,
  `passage.eugene-cummiskey.roman-missal-english-laity.philadelphia-1861.post-pentecosten-18`
  over the tracked public-domain payload `…philadelphia-1861.temporal-orations-en`,
  under the heading *XVIII. SUNDAY AFTER PENTECOST*, the three rows being Collect,
  Secret and Postcommunion in liturgical order. This stage read that passage in
  the tracked payload; **it did not open the 1861 scan**, so the payload has not
  been checked against its own page images here.
- **The Trinity Preface** is appointed by rubric and located at printed p. 293,
  no. 1082. Any reproduction needs its own public-domain Latin antecedent and a
  registered historical English; neither was established here.

The Latin of all ten elements is publishable on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) with the tracked
1862 Pustet as the public-domain antecedent: every element's wording stands there,
as the element-by-element table above shows. The repository's settled holding is
`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`. Four of

elements carry no such row; this record supplies the collation a row would rest

## Discrepancies and open items carried forward

1. **Marginal numbers 1671–1674** are clipped at the inner margin of printed
   p. 410 in the CMAA digitisation and are fixed by continuity, not read. A run
   that needs them read whole must open a digitisation that captures more of that
   page's inner margin; the Internet Archive `sp07` scan, which
   `research/context.md` reports clips them entirely, is not that.
2. **`expectántibus` (1862) against `exspectántibus` (1962)** in the Epistle is a
   real spelling difference between antecedent and target, read on the 1862 page
   image. It does not disturb the publication basis, which rests on wording.
3. **The Offertory's textual source is not identified.** What is established is
   negative and exact: its distinctive clauses are in neither the Clementine nor
   the Greek of Ex 24:4–5, and their Latin phrases live elsewhere in the
   Pentateuch's sacrificial vocabulary and at Ex 24:17.
4. **`in aula sancta eius`** is attested in 1862 and 1962 alike; which Latin
   psalter it comes from is unresolved, no Roman Psalter witness having been
   reached.
5. **The Cummiskey English was read only in the tracked payload**, not against
   the 1861 page images.
6. **The Benziger leaf images are served at 800 × 1158 only**, which is enough
   for pointing and accents but not for a disputed letterform; none was disputed.
