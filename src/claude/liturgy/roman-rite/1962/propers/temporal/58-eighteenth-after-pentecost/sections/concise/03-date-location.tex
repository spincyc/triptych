\section{Scriptural Date and Location}

\zlabel{triptych:concise:chronology:start}
\begin{concisedossiertable}
Offertory & Ex 24:4--5, compiled & Sinai: an altar at the foot of the mount,
with twelve titles for the twelve tribes (24:4) &
\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: the ratifying of the covenant at Sinai, after the
people have answered with one voice (24:3); the altar is built ``rising in the
morning'' (24:4), a relative date within the narrative.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The chronology record dates the narrated event only, and only
relatively; it holds no composition date for Exodus at these verses. The
antiphon is the liturgy's compilation: beside the altar, holocausts and victims
of 24:4--5 it sets the verb of Moses' consecration of the altar (Lev 8:11; Num
7:1), the evening sacrifice of the daily offering (Ex 29:41; Ps 140:2), and the
closing \latin{in conspectu filiorum Israel} of 24:17.}
\midrule
Communion & Ps 95:8--9 (Heb.\ 96) & The house of God and its courts; by the
title, the house built after the captivity, at Jerusalem &
\chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled in the Vulgate \latin{Canticum ipsi David, quando domus
aedificabatur post captivitatem} (v.~1). The record reads that setting against
the raising of the Temple under Zorobabel after the return (\work{Catholic
Encyclopedia}, ``Temple of Jerusalem'') and holds no Davidic era for the psalm;
for its composition it gives only the Psalter's critical boundary. The third
answer is not a date of the psalm: the record reads Ps~95 as looking
prophetically to Christ's coming, and its two years are two \work{Catholic
Encyclopedia} articles' dates for the Nativity.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps 101:16 (Heb.\ 102) & Sion, to be rebuilt and seen in glory
(vv.~14, 17) & \chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled ``The prayer of the poor man, when he was anxious'' (v.~1);
no author named, and no traditional date held. The lament turns at verse 14 to
Sion, and verse 17, unsung, gives the nations' reason: ``the Lord hath built up
Sion''. The date is the Psalter's critical boundary only.}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps 121:1, 7 (Heb.\ 122); verse 1 is also the Introit's verse &
Jerusalem: the house of the Lord, and the city's walls and towers (vv.~1--7) &
\chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Titled \latin{Canticum graduum}, one of the Songs of Ascents
(from Ps~119 to Ps~133 in the Missal's numbering), sung by pilgrims going up to the house
of the Lord; no author named, and no traditional date held. St John Chrysostom
and Theodoret set it at the return from the Babylonian exile, of a city whose
walls and towers lay in ruins. The date is the Psalter's critical boundary
only.}
\midrule
Introit & Ecclus 36:18, adapted; its verse, Ps 121:1, stands with the Gradual &
``Jesus the son of Sirach, of Jerusalem'' (50:29), praying for Jerusalem and
Sion (36:14--16) & \chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: Jesus the son of Sirach, named in the
Greek prologue; his grandson rendered the book into Greek in Egypt
(\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, ``Ecclesiasticus''). The record answers the two
loci separately: at Ecclus 36:18 a preferred interval and an alternate
approximate year for the book, from that article; at Ps 121:1 the Psalter's
boundary given with the Gradual. Neither holds at both loci, so the element has
no single date. The antiphon's opening and close are neither the Vulgate's nor
the Greek's.}
\midrule
Gospel & Mt 9:1--8 & Matthew in Judea, for the Jews, before his departure from
Jerusalem. Event: ``his own city'' across the lake (9:1), Capernaum for
Chrysostom, Nazareth for Jerome &
\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Narrated event: the healing of the paralytic, after the crossing
from the country of the Gerasens (8:28--9:1) and before the call of Matthew
(9:9); the record gives the episode no date.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St Matthew the Apostle. The Date column
keeps the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia}'s ranges as disputed alternatives: early
writers counting years from the Ascension; a dispersal of the Apostles by a
tradition it calls ``admittedly not too reliable''; the Catholic critics of its
day; a later apostolic departure; and St Irenaeus, whom it finds too uncertain
for a positive conclusion. The last figure, from ``The New Testament'' (1912),
dates the Aramaic original alone. The same Matthew article reports the
Protestant and liberal critics of its day as widely divided. The town locates
the healing, not the writing.}
\midrule
Epistle & 1 Cor 1:4--8 & Paul at Ephesus (16:8), to ``the church of God that
is at Corinth'' (1:2) & \chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Traditional attribution: St Paul, with Sosthenes (1:1). The
\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}'s ``Epistles to the Corinthians'' (1908) has Paul,
at Ephesus, learn of party strife at Corinth and write at once; the table in its
``St.\ Paul'' (1911) gives the year of the letters to Corinth and Galatia. Two
Catholic authorities disagree, and both are shown. The first calls the evidence
for Paul's authorship so strong that the most advanced critical schools of its
day admitted it.}
\end{concisedossiertable}
