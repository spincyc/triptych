\section{Scriptural Date and Location}

\zlabel{triptych:concise:chronology:start}
\begin{passagedossiertable}
\textbf{Introit (verse) --- Ps 77:1 (Heb.\ 78)}. Asaph, by the title, to Israel as ``my
people''; a history from Egypt to Sion and David (vv.~68--70)\\*
\chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}\\*
The Vulgate's unsung \latin{Intellectus Asaph} names no occasion or date. The NABRE Psalms introduction gives the whole-Psalter bound and finds no individual psalm securely datable. The antiphon has no Vulgate verse and no separate dossier.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Alleluia --- Ps 104:1 (Heb.\ 105)}. Israel, bidden to declare God's deeds among
the Gentiles; sung before the ark at Jerusalem (1 Par 15:3; 16:7--8)\\*
\chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}\\*
The Vulgate title is \latin{Alleluia}, omitted by the Missal. Asaph sings these words when the ark enters David's tent (1~Par 16:1, 7); the displayed bound dates the Psalter, not that day.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Communion --- Ps 118:4--5 (Heb.\ 119)}. The psalmist, ``a sojourner on the
earth'' (v.~19), singing the law ``in the place of my pilgrimage'' (v.~54)\\*
\chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}\\*
The title \latin{Alleluia} names no author; these verses belong to \latin{Aleph}. Haydock reports general Davidic attribution with competing occasions. The reign identifies David; the composition bound applies to the Psalter.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Offertory --- Ps 137:7 (Heb.\ 138)}. David, by the title; praise ``in the sight
of the angels'' and worship ``towards thy holy temple'' (vv.~1--2)\\*
\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\*
The title \latin{Ipsi David}, ``For David himself'', names no occasion. Corbett's ``usual chronology'' (\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, IV, ``David, King'') gives a regnal reference, with a later reckoning beside it. The composition bound is the Psalter's.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Gradual --- Ps 140:2 (Heb.\ 141)}. David, by the title; an evening prayer beside
Israel's incense and evening sacrifice (v.~2)\\*
\chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}\\*
The title \latin{Psalmus David} names no occasion. The reign is a reference to David, not a date for this prayer; composition has the same Psalter bound.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Gospel --- Mt 22:1--14}. Writing: as Matthew left Palestine (Eusebius). Event:
Jerusalem, in the temple (21:23)\\*
\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\*
\textit{Narrated event: the third parable Jesus speaks in the temple, on
the day after his entry into Jerusalem (21:1--18), when the chief priests and
ancients have questioned his authority (21:23), to the chief priests and
Pharisees (21:45); St Matthew gives it no date beyond that day.}\\*
Traditional attribution: Matthew the Apostle. Maas (\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, VIII, ``Jesus Christ'') supplies the journey chronology from which the event year is derived. Jacquier (X, ``Gospel of St Matthew'') reports Matthew's Hebrew writing on leaving Palestine (Eusebius) and five unsettled datings from the Ascension, Apostles' dispersal, Irenaeus and Catholic critics. Durand (XIV, ``The New Testament'') dates the lost Aramaic original and leaves its Greek translation undated. The separate NABRE critical comparison gives a lower bound for the Greek Gospel; it dates neither the Aramaic original nor this parable.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Epistle --- Eph 4:23--28}. Paul, ``a prisoner in the Lord'' (4:1), in Caesarea or
Rome; to ``the saints who are at Ephesus'' (1:1)\\*
\chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\*
Paul names himself and his chains (1:1; 3:1; 6:20). Ladeuze (\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, V, ``Ephesians'') leaves Caesarea or Rome disputed; Prat (XI, ``St Paul'') dates the Roman captivity differently and groups the letter with Philemon, Colossians and Philippians. NABRE's later-disciple range is a conditional alternative to a secretary under Paul's direction or traditional captivity, not consensus; it identifies no later writing place.\\
\end{passagedossiertable}
