# Epistle record — 1 Corinthians 15:1–10

Element key: `epistle`. Companion to `scope.md`.

## The pericope-boundary finding (load-bearing)

The 1962 lection ends at **"et gratia eius in me vacua non fuit"** and omits
the rest of v. 10 (*sed abundantius illis omnibus laboravi; non ego autem,
sed gratia Dei mecum*). The omission is exegetically load-bearing: the single
most influential patristic argument built on this passage — the
grace-and-free-will argument — turns precisely on the omitted words.

| Witness | What depends on the omitted clause |
| --- | --- |
| Augustine, *De gratia et libero arbitrio* 5.12 | the proof of free will is *sed plus omnibus illis laboravi*, and the anti-Pelagian correction *Non ego autem, sed gratia Dei mecum: id est, non solus…* |
| Gregory the Great, *Moralia in Iob* 16.25 | *Non enim diceret **mecum** si cum praeveniente gratia subsequens liberum arbitrium non haberet* — the prevenient/subsequent schema hangs on *mecum* |
| Chrysostom, Hom. 38 §7 | the "laboured more abundantly… yet not I" seesaw is the centrepiece of Paul's humility |
| Aquinas, *Super I Cor.* c. 15 lect. 1 n. 909 | *gratia cooperans* is derived from *non autem ego, sed gratia Dei mecum* |
| Ambrosiaster ad 15:10 | separate lemmata and comments on the two omitted clauses |
| A Lapide ad 15:10 | the anti-Reformation free-will argument is built on "*with* me, not *in* me" and "I laboured more abundantly" |

The guide may use these witnesses for vv. 1–10a, but any claim that "the
Fathers expound this Epistle's teaching on grace" must reckon with the fact
that their classic expositions quote past the lection's last word. The same
mid-verse ending stands in the 1570 and 1862 Missals (`propers/verified.md`),
so it is the received lectionary boundary, not a 1962 novelty; the guide says
so and treats the boundary honestly in the commentary.

## Direct exegesis

- **Chrysostom, Homiliae in ep. I ad Corinthios, Hom. 38** (on 15:1–11; the
  resurrection-denial argument begins in Hom. 39). English: NPNF 1/12,
  tr. Chambers (1889, PD). Moves: (a) Paul says *delivered* and *received*,
  not *taught*: the gospel is transmission, credible from deeds ("neither
  here does he say, I said to you, nor, I taught you, but uses the same
  expression again, saying, *I delivered unto you that which also I
  received*," §2). (b) Anti-Manichaean centre: since Christ was sinless, His
  death "for our sins" can only be the body's death — "if being without sin
  He died, He died — not the death of sin… but the death of the body" (§3);
  "according to the Scriptures" pins the sense (Ps 21/22, Isa 53); burial
  clinches it. (c) The appearance list is a chain of witnesses ordered by
  credibility (Cephas "the most credible of all"; "some are fallen asleep,"
  not "are dead," itself preaches resurrection; James the Lord's brother,
  first bishop of Jerusalem; "all the apostles" includes the Seventy). (d)
  The *ektroma* is calculated modesty — "This is rather an expression of
  modesty than anything else" (§5) — Paul appeared last but "more
  illustrious than many which were before him." (e) On v. 10 the grace–labour
  dialectic is humility "with reserve": "he said not, I have displayed a
  diligence worthy of His grace, but, *it was not found vain*" (§7); "having
  in so many ways depressed himself and uttered but one lofty word, not even
  this does he call his own." His instinct is **moral** where Augustine's on
  the same words is **dogmatic**.
- **Ambrosiaster, Commentaria in ep. ad Corinthios primam, ad 15:1–11**
  (PL 17; read in the wikisource transcription of the Migne text; the inline
  column marker 161 falls at v. 4, consistent with the 1845 printing's
  coll. c. 160–162; CSEL 81/2 not consulted). Moves: deserters of the
  tradition *perdiderunt quod crediderunt; omnis enim spes credentium in hoc
  sensu est, quia mortui resurgent* (vv. 1–2); *secundum Scripturas* = the
  Old Testament (Isa 53:7–9), with the excursus on prophetic past tenses
  (*Agnus qui occisus est a constitutione mundi*, Apoc 13:8; the Old Latin
  *Dominus regnavit a ligno*, Ps 95:10; Deut 28:66); third day from Hos 6:3
  *sub persona populi*: *In Christo enim omnes resurrexisse, sicut in Adam
  omnes mortuos esse, nemo fidelium denegat*; Peter's appearance from
  Lk 24:34; the 500 on Paul's own credit; James singly *ut multifaria
  apparentia fidem resurrectionis firmaret*; *abortivus = extra tempus natus*
  — *quia extra tempus natus in Christo, apostolatum accepit jam Christo in
  coelos recepto cum carne*, adding the appearances of Acts 9 and 22:17;
  v. 9 *tempore enim minor est, non dignitate*; v. 10 *Ne se extulisse
  jactanter putaretur, statim se humiliat*. **Text-critical find:** his v. 10b
  lemma is a Vetus Latina variant — *Et gratia ejus **pauper** in me non
  fuit* ("his grace was not poor in me"); a Lapide still knows the reading as
  "S. Ambrose's." The Vulgate/1962 *vacua* is not his.
- **Aquinas, Super I ad Corinthios, cap. 15, lectio 1 (nn. 888–911) —
  authenticity verified.** The lacuna filled from Peter of Tarantaise covers
  1 Cor 7:15–10:33 (nn. 347–581); the authentic *reportatio vulgata* resumes
  at 11:1 (Corpus Thomisticum's c1v file is headed "a capite XI ad caput XVI,
  Reportatio vulgata"). **Chapter 15 is authentic Thomas** — the safe side of
  the seam this project already mapped at the Ninth Sunday. Lectio 1 covers
  15:1–11 entire; lectio 2 begins at v. 12, so the 1962 pericope sits wholly
  in lectio 1. Content: commendation of the gospel (nn. 888–890); the four
  *tradita* (mors, sepultura, resurrectio, apparitio); three reasons burial
  is mentioned; why the women's appearances are omitted (*noluit ponere
  testimonia nisi authentica*); the Augustine "duodecim" note — *Augustinus
  dicit quod debet dicere duodecim, sed corruptum est vitio scriptorum*;
  **abortivus threefold** (n. c. 904): *vel quia nascitur extra tempus
  debitum, vel cum violentia educitur, vel quia non perducitur ad debitam
  quantitatem* — Paul reborn after Pentecost, converted **by coercion** (*Et
  hoc multum valet contra haereticos, qui dicunt quod nullus debet cogi ad
  fidem, quia Paulus coactus fuit*), and self-reputed beneath the apostles;
  v. 10a (n. 908): *ex me nihil sum, sed id quod sum, gratia Dei sum… Et
  dicit "id quod sum," quia homo sine gratia nihil est*; v. 10b (n. 909, past
  the lection's cut): *Deus enim non solum infundit gratiam… sed etiam movet
  ad bene utendum gratia infusa, et haec vocatur gratia cooperans*. English:
  Larcher's translation is modern and not PD — the Latin is quoted instead.
- **Theodoret, Interpretatio in xiv epistulas S. Pauli, in 1 Cor 15**
  (PG 82, cols. 347–352; read in the PG scan's Latin column, Greek
  spot-checked, e.g. ὥσπερεί τῷ ἐκτρώματι; Hill's English not used). Moves:
  Paul begins from the scope of the Incarnation because impostors denied the
  bodily resurrection — cross, passion and resurrection happened *propter
  resurrectionem communem*; *si enim non creditur resurrectio corporum,
  supervacanea est praedicatio Evangelii* (v. 2); v. 3 *a Christo Domino
  accepi doctrinam*, with Isa 53, Isa 57:2 (*erit in pace sepultura ejus*),
  Ps 15:10, and **Jonah as the type carrying the number of the three days**;
  vv. 5–7 an ascending argument from testimony (the 500 *a suspicione
  alienum*, checkable because *multi manent usque adhuc*; James *frater
  Domini… vocabatur enim Justus*); v. 8 **abortivus as extreme
  self-abasement** — *abortivo fetui seipsum comparat, qui in hominum
  catalogo non recensetur* (the aborted foetus is not entered in the register
  of men); v. 10 *Divitias occultare conatur, et aperire cogitur… Occultat
  enim miracula, et solum laborem ostendit, atque hunc quoque dicit esse
  divinae gratiae*.

## Earliest reception of the credal formula

- **Irenaeus, Adversus Haereses 3.18.3** (ANF 1, Roberts–Rambaut, 1885, PD):
  quotes the formula verbatim inside the anti-docetic argument that Christ
  truly suffered.
- **Tertullian, De resurrectione carnis 48** (ANF 3, Holmes, 1885, PD):
  "since you are told that He died according to the Scriptures… no otherwise
  than in the flesh, you will also allow that it was in the flesh that He was
  raised from the dead. For the very same body which fell in death, and which
  lay in the sepulchre, did also rise again; (and it was) not so much Christ
  in the flesh, as the flesh in Christ." Bounded note: *Adversus Marcionem* 5
  engages 1 Cor 15 only from v. 12 onward — do not cite Adv. Marc. for the
  appearance list.
- The anti-docetic/anti-Manichaean use is the **oldest layer** of this
  passage's reception; the grace-reading of vv. 9–10 is a later, Latin
  development.

## The grace trajectory (doctrinal genealogy of v. 10)

Chrysostom (moral: humility with reserve) → Augustine, *De gratia et libero
arbitrio* 5.12 (dogmatic: Paul's merit was real but **evil** — *Meritum enim
fuit quidem in apostolo Paulo, sed malum*; then *nec gratia Dei sola, nec
ipse solus, sed gratia Dei cum illo. Ut autem de caelo vocaretur… gratia Dei
erat sola*) → Gregory, *Moralia* 16.25 (prevenient/subsequent grace: *Superna
ergo pietas prius agit in nobis aliquid sine nobis, ut, subsequente quoque
nostro libero arbitrio, bonum quod iam appetimus agat nobiscum*) → Aquinas
(gratia operans/cooperans, lectura n. 909) → a Lapide (anti-Reformation
free-will proof: "the Apostle says *with* me, not *in* me," citing Bernard,
Jerome, Gregory, Augustine — Cobb's PD English, 1908). **Every stage past
Chrysostom's §7 depends on the clause the 1962 pericope omits.** Augustine's
homiletic reuses: Enarr. in Ps 70, sermo 1, 1–2 (*praelectum nobis et
Apostolum audivimus* — an Apostle-reading containing 15:9 read in the liturgy
of his day, paired with 1 Tim 1:15) and Sermo 279, 5–6 (Paulus = *modicus*;
*Et minimus, et novissimus, tamquam fimbria de vestimento Domini* — least and
last as the hem of the Lord's garment that healed the woman with the issue
of blood).

## Aquinas in the Summa

- **ST III q. 55 a. 3 ad 3** cites 1 Cor 15:6–7 as appearances "of which no
  mention is made in the Gospels"; ad 4 quotes Chrysostom on the list.
- **ST III q. 57 a. 6 ad 3** uses v. 8 dogmatically: the Damascus vision
  *veritatem resurrectionis non probaret nisi ipsum verum corpus visum
  fuisset ab eo* — against reducing it to a vision (a Lapide repeats the
  argument and applies the two-places corollary to the Eucharist).
- **Material negative:** the grace treatise ST I-II qq. 106–114 nowhere cites
  1 Cor 15:10 verbatim (checked across the Corpus Thomisticum files for
  *gratia Dei sum id quod sum*, *gratia Dei mecum*, *vacua*, *abundantius*);
  q. 111 a. 2's sed contra rests on Augustine's *De gratia et libero
  arbitrio* instead. The verse enters Thomas's grace-doctrine through the
  lectura, not the Summa.

## Distinct readings of *abortivus* (real disagreement)

1. *Untimely-born* — Ambrosiaster, Chrysostom (reborn after the
   ascension/Pentecost);
2. *unworthy/unregistered* — Theodoret (not counted in the catalogue of men);
3. *violently extracted* — the coercion motif, systematized by Aquinas into
   all three notes (extra tempus, cum violentia, non ad debitam quantitatem).
A Lapide catalogues six readings (adding Anselm's half-dead/blind birth, the
expulsion from the Synagogue's womb, and Baronius's supernumerary-senator
theory, which he rejects).

## "Twelve" vs. "undecim" (v. 5)

The Greek and Augustine read "twelve" (corporate name; Augustine calls
"undecim" a scribal corruption, per Aquinas n. c. 892 and a Lapide); the
Vulgate and the 1962 lection print *undecim*. The guide quotes the missal's
text and reports that the tradition mostly defends the harder reading.
