# Orations and Communion record — Collect, Secret, Postcommunion, Prov 3:9–10

Element keys: `collect`, `secret`, `postcommunion`, `communion`. Companion to
`scope.md`; loci here are the checked loci behind the published claims.

## Sacramentary transmission of the three orations

All three orations of this formulary travel together as one mass-set from the
Old Gelasian onward; the Secret and Postcommunion are older still. Every
witness below was read in the named public-domain edition.

**Chant-cue observation (Ottob. 313 margins, from Wilson 1915's footnotes,
pp. 171–172).** The antiphon set of 1962 Pentecost XI — *Deus in loco sancto
suo… Exaltabo te Domine… Honora Dominum* — is cued in Ottob. 313's margin
against **Dominica X post Pentecosten** (the *Pateant aures* mass), two masses
before the orations it accompanies in 1962; Greg. Dom. XII itself carries the
cues *Respice… in testamentum* and *Panem de caelo* (= 1962 Pent. XIII
chants). The Tridentine pairing of the *Honora Dominum* Communion with the
*abundantia pietatis* Collect is therefore a product of the later realignment
of the chant and oration cycles, not the Carolingian pairing. Published as
documented historical orientation, with the drift stated and no compiler's
intention inferred.

## Later liturgical reception of the Collect

- **Postconciliar reuse.** The Collect is reused verbatim (with 1962's
  *adicias*) as the Collect of Dominica XXVII per annum in the postconciliar
  Missale Romanum. Verified in two independent unofficial Latin transcriptions
  (a Latin liturgical-texts blog and breviarium.info's Liturgia Horarum, which
  shares the Sunday collect); the official locus is "MR 2002, Dominica XXVII
  per annum, Collecta," and no free official scan was located, so the official
  edition remains a lead. The library's
  `edition.catholic-church.missale-romanum.vatican-typica-tertia-2002` is the
  identity to cite; the guide reports the reuse without quoting the
  postconciliar book.
- **Guéranger**, *The Liturgical Year*, tr. Shepherd, Time after Pentecost
  vol. II, 2nd ed. (1909), Eleventh Sunday, pp. 272–274: keys the Collect to
  the publican Gospel (Luke 18) that an older arrangement read on this Sunday,
  citing Rupert of Deutz, *De divinis officiis* XII, 11 for that arrangement.
  PD quotation held: "she, with motherly tenderness, comes and takes up his
  faltering prayer, and gives it her own eloquence… granting them what they,
  poor sinners, are too afraid to presume to ask for."
- **Schuster**, *The Sacramentary*, tr. Levelis-Marke, vol. III (1927),
  "Eleventh Sunday after Pentecost — Prima post Sancti Laurentii,"
  pp. 124–128: on the Collect, "a perfect liturgical gem… This short petition
  from the Sunday Liturgy contains a complete treatise on prayer"; on the
  Secret (p. 127), "the *servitus* of which the Missal speaks is precisely
  that which the Greeks term λειτουργία — that is, the sacerdotal ministry";
  on the Communion (p. 127), it "departs from the usual rule of the classical
  antiphons" and suits "these summer Masses, during the time of harvest," the
  firstfruits consecrating the whole harvest, which he connects to the old
  blessing of firstfruits at *Per quem haec omnia*; on the Postcommunion
  (pp. 127–128), the body's contact with the Body of Jesus confers "the grace
  of the final resurrection." The 1927 English volume is US public domain.

## Doctrinal illumination of the Collect (not sources of the prayer)

- **Eph 3:20 (Vulgate):** *Ei autem, qui potens est omnia facere
  superabundanter quam petimus aut intelligimus…* — a scriptural echo, not a
  quotation: the prayer's two clauses map onto Paul's doxology (giving that
  exceeds *quam petimus* and *quam intelligimus*). Published only as an echo.
- **Aquinas, ST II-II q. 83 a. 2 ad 3:** *Deus multa nobis praestat ex sua
  liberalitate etiam non petita* — God gives much unasked, out of liberality;
  illuminates *et adicias quod oratio non praesumit*.
- **Aquinas, ST II-II q. 83 a. 16 co. and sed contra:** the sinner's prayer is
  heard *non quasi ex iustitia… sed ex pura misericordia*, the sed contra
  citing Augustine on the publican (*si peccatores non exaudiret Deus, frustra
  publicanus dixisset: Domine, propitius esto mihi peccatori*) — illuminating
  *merita supplicum excedis* and meshing with Guéranger's publican key.
  Both articles read at Corpus Thomisticum (sth3082).

## Proverbs 3:9–10 reception (Communion)

Text note: the 1962 antiphon trims the Clementine (drops *omnium*, *da ei*,
the second *tua* — see `propers/verified.md`), while much Greek patristic
citation follows the LXX instead (*τίμα τὸν κύριον ἀπὸ σῶν δικαίων πόνων*,
"honour the Lord from thy just labours"), which is why verbatim Vulgate-form
patristic quotations are scarce.

| Witness | Exact locus | The interpretive move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bede, *In Proverbia Salomonis* I, on Prov 3:9–10 | PL 91, cols. 950–952 (running heads; the commentary spans PL 91, 937–1040) | Three levels. Literal: alms must come from one's *own*, honestly gotten substance, for God's honour and not human applause (*Multi multa pauperibus, sed de aliena substantia… tribuunt*). Spiritual: the firstfruits are of *every* good work and every grace received — one honours the Lord *de sua substantia* who credits all his good to grace and not to his own powers and merits, *memor illius verbi: sine me nihil potestis facere* (Jn 15:5). Eschatological: the filled barns are heavenly satiety (*satiabor… dum manifestabitur gloria tua*, Ps 16), the overflowing presses the *interna suavitas aeternorum*; the giver of earthly aid is repaid *caelesti remuneratione, ditior dando* |
| Rabanus Maurus, *Expositio in Proverbia Salomonis* I | PL 111, cols. c. 688–690 (the Expositio spans PL 111, 679–792) | Full Vulgate lemma, then **Bede's exposition reproduced verbatim** (minus one clause): the Carolingian canonization of Bede's reading, transmitting it to the medieval schools |
| Salvian of Marseilles, *Ad Ecclesiam (Adversus avaritiam)* I | PL 53, cols. 214–216 (volume index: "Deus honorandus de nostra substantia, 214") | Genuine 5th-c. patristic use, wholly literal (almsgiving/restitution): since all we have is God's gift, God calls His own property "ours" precisely so that our giving can merit reward — *Cum totum suum sit quod ab eo accepimus, nostrum esse dicit ut demus… Si devotus es, da quasi tuum; si indevotus, redde quasi non tuum* |
| Salonius of Geneva, *Expositio mystica in Parabolas Salomonis* | PL 53, 967–994 | **Bounded negative:** the dialogue is highly selective; the whole Expositio was searched for *Honora*, *substantia*, *primiti-*, *horrea*, *torcular-*; no treatment of Prov 3:9–10 |

Cyprian negative: *De opere et eleemosynis* (ANF 5) quotes Prov 20:9, 21:13,
10:3, 20:7, Sir 29:12, Tob 4 — not Prov 3:9; *Ad Quirinum* III.1 cites Isa 58,
Job, Tobit ("give alms of thy substance" is Tob 4:7, the near-twin), Mic 6 —
not Prov 3:9. Greek use of Prov 3:9 was not verified within the bounded
effort (the LXX wording defeats Vulgate-phrase search); recorded as a lead
only, and not cited.

## Wording-level commentary on the orations

Expected negative confirmed: no patristic or early-medieval commentary on the
*wording* of these three composed orations was found. The nearest medieval
item is Rupert of Deutz, *De divinis officiis* XII, 11–12 (as cited by
Guéranger), which treats this Sunday's Introit–Gospel correspondence, not the
orations' text. The earliest wording-level commentary located is modern
liturgical commentary (Guéranger's continuation, tr. 1909; Schuster, 1927).
Close verbal exegesis of the orations in the guide is therefore labelled
source-grounded synthesis, resting on the sacramentary witnesses, the
scriptural echo and scholastic loci above, and the modern commentators.

## Cummiskey divergences carried to the point of use

Three divergences between the 1861 English and its Latin are published at the
element (see `research/source-bindings.toml`, Cummiskey binding): the
Collect's "we dare not presume to ask" shifts the subject from the prayer
(*quod oratio non praesumit*) to the petitioners; the Postcommunion inverts
*mentis et corporis*; its "enjoy the full effect" loses *gloriemur*.
