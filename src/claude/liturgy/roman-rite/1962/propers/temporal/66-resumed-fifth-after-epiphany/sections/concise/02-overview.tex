\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & God promises the exiles
peace, a hearing and return; the psalms give thanks and cry from the depths; a
householder leaves the cockle sown while men slept to grow with his wheat
until the harvest; Paul asks the Colossians to bear with and forgive one
another and to let Christ's word dwell in them; Jesus promises that prayer made
in faith receives.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ will make the
separation through angels ``who cannot make mistakes'' (Augustine); the
cockle is the teaching of heretics (Jerome) or evil Christians among the good
(Augustine); Christ turns away the captivity of sin, and the propitiation with
God is the sacrifice offered for us (Augustine); the peace in the heart is the
peace Christ made between God and man (Aquinas).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Judge slowly where the case is
doubtful (Jerome), since we may be deceived (Augustine); bear injury without
revenge (Rabanus); pray, because God urges prayer so that his people may
receive what he promised (Jerome), and faith asks to be heard (Hilary); stay
awake in the moral faculty (Chrysostom); let Christ's word dwell richly in the
heart, and sing with the heart, not the lips only (Chrysostom, Aquinas).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & At the harvest the
angels separate the cockle from the wheat: ``In the field they may be with
you, but they will not be so in the barn'' (Augustine); from the confusion of
this age we are brought back ``to our own place, the Church'' (Jerome);
complete deliverance ``will be thoroughly accomplished in the resurrection
only'' (Bellarmine); he who endures to the end shall be saved (the Lord's
words, quoted by Chrysostom).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
