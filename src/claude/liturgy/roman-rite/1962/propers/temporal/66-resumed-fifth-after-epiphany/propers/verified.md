## Which printing the study's Latin follows

The 1962 Missal prints this formulary's three orations twice: in the
Epiphany season (nos. 327, 333, 335) and in this resumed formulary (nos. 1750,
1756, 1758). The bodies are word for word the same. They differ in two
setting variables only:

- the **Postcommunion's conclusion**: the Epiphany-season printing (no. 335)
  writes it out, *Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum
  vivit et regnat in unitáte.*; the resumed printing (no. 1758) prints the cue
  *Per Dóminum.* Both are the one fixed conclusion of the Missal;
- the **Epistle's pointing** at v. 17: no. 328 (p. 48) sets *Omne,
  quodcúmque*, no. 1751 sets *Omne quodcúmque*.

**The study follows the resumed printing, nos. 1749-1758**, because that is the
formulary appointed on 8 November 2026: its Postcommunion cue is *Per
Dóminum.* and its Collect alone carries the written-out conclusion. A reader
who says the Postcommunion aloud says the full conclusion either way.

## The publication basis of the Latin

The repository's holding
(`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`) is
that the 1962 printing is a collation witness and that 17 U.S.C. 103(b) frees
only preexisting material: an element's Latin may be published where a
public-domain printing carries the same words. The rule applied is the one in
`guidance/propers-for-agents.md`: the words must agree; orthography, pointing,
capitalisation, accents and the length of a conclusion cue are transformations
and do not change a verdict.

- **The three orations and the two lessons.** Pustet 1862, *Dominica V. post
  Epiphaniam*, printed pp. 42-44, read on its page images, prints every word of
  the Collect (p. 42, right column), the Epistle (p. 43, left column), the
  Gospel (p. 43, left column to the head of the right column), the Secret
  (p. 43, right column) and the Postcommunion (p. 44, left column). The
  differences are orthography (*Jesus*, *Jesu*, *Jesum*, *ejus*, *cujus* for
  the 1962 *i*; *coeléstis* and *coelórum* for *cæléstis*, *cælórum*;
  *charitátem* for *caritátem*; *exúltet* for *exsúltet*), pointing (*quǽsumus
  Dómine* without a comma in the Collect; *Fratres.* with a full stop and
  *querélam. Sicut* in the Epistle; *Deo et Patri, per Iesum* with a comma;
  *Quǽsumus omnípotens Deus:* without a comma in the Postcommunion), and the
  Collect's conclusion cue (*Per Dóminum.* where the 1962 writes the conclusion
  out). No word of any body differs. The 1862 Collect sets a **comma** after
  *innítitur*, as the 1962 does; the 1862 text layer reads a colon there, and
  the page image does not.
- **The five chants.** Pustet 1862, *Dominica XXIII. post Pentecosten*, printed
  pp. 357-358, read on its page images, prints every word of the Introit
  antiphon and its psalm verse (p. 357, left column; heading *Introitus.
  Jeremiæ 29.*, verse *Ps. 84.*), the Gradual and its verse (p. 357, right
  column; *Ps. 43.*), the Alleluia verse (p. 358, left column; *Ps. 129.*), the
  Offertory (p. 358, left column; *Ps. 129.*) and the Communion (p. 358, right
  column; *Marci 11.*). The differences are orthography (*Jacob*, *Allelúja*),
  pointing (*Benedixísti Dómine* without a comma; *Liberásti nos Dómine*;
  *clamávi ad te Dómine, Dómine exaúdi* in the Alleluia verse; *crédite, quia
  accipiétis* in the Communion) and citation form. No word differs, including
  every place where the chant departs from the Clementine Vulgate (see below).
- **The assignment of the chants.** The same 1862 page, p. 358 right column to
  p. 359, prints the rubric for the Sundays left over after the Epiphany: they
  are resumed *quoad Orationes, Epistolas et Evangelia*, with the order for
  25-28 Sundays (*Si fuerint xxvi. in Dominica xxiv. dicitur Missa Dominicæ v.
  post Epiphaniam*), and *Introitus autem, Graduale, Offertorium, et Communio in
  prædictis Dominicis semper dicuntur ut in Dominica vigesimatertia*. The 1862
  prints no resumed formulary of its own. The 1962 prints it whole (RGMR 298;
  research/context.md).

**Result: every word of all ten elements stands in the public-domain Pustet
1862 printing, read on its page images.** The Latin of the whole formulary may
be published on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) with that witness, in the 1962 page's
orthography. The Latin provenance ledger

## Appointed text, as printed in 1962

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — no. 1749, printed p. 420, left column

*Antiphona ad Introitum*, printed citation *Ierem. 29, 11, 12 et 14*; verse
headed *Ps. 84, 2*.

> Dicit Dóminus: Ego cógito cogitatiónes pacis, et non afflictiónis:
> invocábitis me, et ego exáudiam vos: et redúcam captivitátem vestram de
> cunctis locis. *Ps. 84, 2* Benedixísti, Dómine, terram tuam: avertísti
> captivitátem Iacob. ℣. Glória Patri.

The page carries a speck after *ad* in the heading (*ad˙*); it is not a mark of
the text.

### 2. Collect (`collect`) — no. 1750, printed p. 420, right column

*Oratio.*

> Famíliam tuam, quǽsumus, Dómine, contínua pietáte custódi: ut, quæ in sola
> spe grátiæ cæléstis innítitur, tua semper protectióne muniátur. Per
> Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in
> unitáte.

### 3. Epistle (`epistle`) — no. 1751, printed p. 420, right column

*Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Colossénses.* — *Col. 3, 12-17*

> Fratres: Indúite vos sicut elécti Dei, sancti et dilécti, víscera
> misericórdiæ, benignitátem, humilitátem, modéstiam, patiéntiam: supportántes
> ínvicem, et donántes vobismetípsis, si quis advérsus áliquem habet querélam:
> sicut et Dóminus donávit vobis, ita et vos. Super ómnia autem hæc, caritátem
> habéte, quod est vínculum perfectiónis: et pax Christi exsúltet in córdibus
> vestris, in qua et vocáti estis in uno córpore: et grati estóte. Verbum
> Christi hábitet in vobis abundánter, in omni sapiéntia, docéntes, et
> commonéntes vosmetípsos, psalmis, hymnis, et cánticis spirituálibus, in
> grátia cantántes in córdibus vestris Deo. Omne quodcúmque fácitis in verbo
> aut in ópere, ómnia in nómine Dómini Iesu Christi, grátias agéntes Deo et
> Patri per Iesum Christum Dóminum nostrum.

### 4. Gradual (`gradual`) — no. 1752, printed p. 420, right column

*Graduale* — *Ps. 43, 8-9* (Heb. 44:8-9)

> Liberásti nos, Dómine, ex affligéntibus nos: et eos, qui nos odérunt,
> confudísti. ℣. In Deo laudábimur tota die, et in nómine tuo confitébimur in
> sǽcula.

### 5. Alleluia (`alleluia`) — no. 1753, printed p. 420, right column

*Ps. 129, 1-2* (Heb. 130:1-2)

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. De profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine: Dómine, exáudi
> oratiónem meam. Allelúia.

No Tract and no Sequence.

### 6. Gospel (`gospel`) — no. 1754, printed p. 421, left column

✠ *Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthǽum.* — *Mt. 13, 24-30*

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus turbis parábolam hanc: Símile factum est regnum
> cælórum hómini, qui seminávit bonum semen in agro suo. Cum autem dormírent
> hómines, venit inimícus eius, et superseminávit zizánia in médio trítici, et
> ábiit. Cum autem crevísset herba, et fructum fecísset, tunc apparuérunt et
> zizánia. Accedéntes autem servi patrisfamílias, dixérunt ei: Dómine, nonne
> bonum semen seminásti in agro tuo? Unde ergo habet zizánia? Et ait illis:
> Inimícus homo hoc fecit. Servi autem dixérunt ei: Vis, imus, et collígimus
> ea? Et ait: Non: ne forte colligéntes zizánia, eradicétis simul cum eis et
> tríticum. Sínite útraque créscere usque ad messem, et in témpore messis dicam
> messóribus: Collígite primum zizánia, et alligáte ea in fascículos ad
> comburéndum, tríticum autem congregáte in hórreum meum.

Printed cue after the Gospel: *Credo.*

### 7. Offertory (`offertory`) — no. 1755, printed p. 421, left column

*Ant. ad Offertorium* — *Ps. 129, 1-2* (Heb. 130:1-2)

> De profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam: de
> profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine.

### 8. Secret (`secret`) — no. 1756, printed p. 421, left column

*Secreta.*

> Hóstias tibi, Dómine, placatiónis offérimus: ut et delícta nostra miserátus
> absólvas, et nutántia corda tu dírigas. Per Dóminum.

Printed direction after the Secret: *Præfatio de Ssm̃a Trinitate.*

### 9. Communion (`communion`) — no. 1757, printed p. 421, left column

*Ant. ad Communionem* — *Marc. 11, 24*

> Amen dico vobis, quidquid orántes pétitis, crédite quia accipiétis, et fiet
> vobis.

### 10. Postcommunion (`postcommunion`) — no. 1758, printed p. 421, right column

*Postcommunio.*

> Quǽsumus, omnípotens Deus: ut illíus salutáris capiámus efféctum, cuius per
> hæc mystéria pignus accépimus. Per Dóminum.

## The chants and lessons against the Clementine Vulgate

Checked on 2026-11-05 against the tracked Clementine verse texts. These are
textual observations about wording and boundary; no origin is asserted for
any form (research/scope.md, section 2.3, records the search for one).

- **Introit.** Not a continuous Vulgate text. Clementine Jer 29:11 *Ego enim
  scio cogitationes quas ego cogito super vos, ait Dominus, cogitationes pacis
  et non afflictionis, ut dem vobis finem et patientiam*; v. 12 *Et invocabitis
  me, et ibitis: et orabitis me, et ego exaudiam vos*; v. 13 *Quaeretis me, et
  invenietis, cum quaesieritis me in toto corde vestro*; v. 14 *Et inveniar a
  vobis, ait Dominus: et reducam captivitatem vestram, et congregabo vos de
  universis gentibus et de cunctis locis ad quae expuli vos …*. The Missal
  opens *Dicit Dóminus: Ego cógito cogitatiónes pacis*, has nothing of *ut dem
  vobis finem et patientiam*, of *et ibitis: et orabitis me*, of v. 13, or of
  *et congregabo vos de universis gentibus*, and joins *redúcam captivitátem
  vestram* directly to *de cunctis locis*. The psalm verse agrees with Ps 84:2.
- **Epistle.** *Fratres: Indúite vos sicut* where the Clementine opens v. 12
  *Induite vos ergo, sicut*; *per Iesum Christum Dóminum nostrum* at the end of
  v. 17 where the Clementine reads *per ipsum*. Every other word of vv. 12-17
  agrees.
- **Gradual.** *Liberásti nos, Dómine, ex affligéntibus nos: et eos, qui nos
  odérunt, confudísti* where the Clementine Ps 43:8 reads *salvasti enim nos de
  affligentibus nos, et odientes nos confudisti*; *in sǽcula* where v. 9 reads
  *in saeculum*.
- **Alleluia and Offertory.** *Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam* where the
  Clementine Ps 129:2 reads *Domine, exaudi vocem meam*; both stop before v. 2b
  (*Fiant aures tuae intendentes …*); the Offertory repeats its first line.
- **Gospel.** The liturgical opening *In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus turbis
  parábolam hanc:* stands for the Clementine's *Aliam parabolam proposuit
  illis, dicens:*. Every other word of vv. 24-30 agrees.
- **Communion.** *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orántes pétitis … et fiet vobis*
  where the Clementine Mk 11:24 reads *Propterea dico vobis, omnia quaecumque
  orantes petitis, credite quia accipietis, et evenient vobis*. The two words
  that differ at the edges are the words of the preceding verse: Mk 11:23
  opens *Amen dico vobis* and ends *fiet ei*. Whether the antiphon was drawn
  from another Latin text or shaped from vv. 23-24 is not established.

## English

English is quoted from registered public-domain witnesses and never composed.

- **Scripture.** Douay-Rheims (Challoner), at the Vulgate loci. It carries
  the Epistle's body (vv. 12-17, but *Put ye on therefore* and *by him* at the
  edges), the Gospel's body (vv. 24-30, but *Another parable he proposed to
  them, saying* at the head) and Ps 84:2. It does not carry the Missal's form
  of the Introit antiphon, the Gradual (*But thou hast saved us …: and in thy
  name we will give praise for ever*), the Alleluia and Offertory (*Lord, hear
  my voice*) or the Communion (*Therefore I say unto you, all things,
  whatsoever you ask … and they shall come unto you*).
- **A hand missal that prints this exact formulary.** Lasance and Walsh, *The
  New Roman Missal*, revised edition (Benziger, 1945), registered
  `edition.francis-xavier-lasance.the-new-roman-missal.benziger-revised-1945`,
  public domain in the United States by non-renewal (edition record and
  `src/sources/inventories/lasance-new-roman-missal-rights-v1.toml`). Its
  *Supplementary Mass No. 2. Mass of the Fifth Sunday after Epiphany (Green)*,
  printed pp. 744-747 (= PDF pp. 742-745 of the registered whole PDF
  `…benziger-revised-1945.internet-archive-facsimile-pdf-6cf3c3d0`, fetched
  whole on 2026-11-05, 75,268,976 bytes, SHA-256
  `6cf3c3d01b853bc4a35c1cc9b0a4e54a0489a5ccf5d9a742bb681ac10608ce70`, matched),
  prints the whole resumed formulary in Latin and English under the rubric
  *If there are twenty-six Sundays after Pentecost, this is said on the
  twenty-fourth; if twenty-seven, on the twenty-fifth; if twenty-eight, on the
  twenty-sixth.* Its Latin carries the *Dicit Dominus* chants, the Collect,
  Epistle, Gospel, Secret and Postcommunion of this formulary. Read on page
  images 2026-11-05. Its English of the chants renders the Missal's forms, not
  the Douay's:
  - Introit: *The Lord saith, I think thoughts of peace, and not of
    affliction: you shall call upon Me, and I will hear you; and I will bring
    back your captivity from all places. Ps. 84, 2. Lord, Thou hast blessed Thy
    land: Thou hast turned away the captivity of Jacob. ℣. Glory.* (p. 744);
  - Gradual: *Thou hast delivered us, O Lord, from them that afflict us: and
    hast put them to shame that hate us. ℣. In God we will glory all the day:
    and in Thy name we will give praise for ever.* (p. 745);
  - Alleluia: *From the depths I have cried to Thee, O Lord: Lord, hear my
    prayer.* (p. 745);
  - Offertory: *From the depths I have cried out to Thee, O Lord; Lord, hear my
    prayer: from the depths I have cried out to Thee, O Lord.* (p. 746);
  - Communion: *Amen, I say to you, whatsoever you ask when you pray, believe
    that you shall receive, and it shall be done unto you.* (p. 746).
  It renders *in sǽcula* as *for ever*, as the Douay does for *in saeculum*.
- **Orations.** The repository's English overlay
  (`src/sources/inventories/roman-1962-proper-translations-v1.toml`,
  `mass = "epiphany-5"`) gives Lasance 1945 for all three, image-checked by
  its owner: Collect p. 196 *Keep Thy family, we beseech Thee, O Lord, with
  Thy continual mercy that, leaning only upon the hope of Thy heavenly grace,
  it may ever be defended by Thy protection. Through our Lord.*; Secret p. 746
  *We offer Thee, O Lord, the sacrifice of reconciliation, that Thou mayest
  mercifully forgive our sins and direct our wavering hearts. Through our
  Lord.*; Postcommunion p. 198 *We pray Thee, O almighty God, that we may
  receive the effect of that salvation of which we have received the pledge in
  these mysteries. Through our Lord.* This stage read pp. 196-198 and 744-747
  on their page images. Two facts the overlay does not record: the Secret's
  p. 746 lies in *Supplementary Mass No. 2* (its running head; the overlay's
  witness note calls it *Supplementary Mass No. 3*, which begins on p. 747
  with the Fourth Sunday after the Epiphany), and the Epiphany-season Secret on
  p. 198 misprints the Latin *mutántia* for *nutántia* while its English is
  the same. The resumed printing's Postcommunion (pp. 746-747) reads *… of
  which we have received the pledge. Through our Lord.*, without *in these
  mysteries*, which its Latin has (*per hæc mystéria*); the p. 198 English
  carries the phrase and is the fuller rendering of the Latin.

## Unresolved discrepancies

None in the 1962 text. Outside it: the overlay's *Supplementary Mass No. 3*
label for the Secret's page, the ledger's statement that the 1862 Collect
sets a colon after *innititur*, and the finding aid's expanded Postcommunion
conclusion for this formulary are reported to their owners in the research
result. None changes a word published here.
