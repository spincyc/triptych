\section*{Note on Sources and Delivery}
\runninghead{Sources and Delivery}

{\footnotesize

\textbf{Audience and occasion.} An adult parish assembly, at the Mass
\latin{D.~N. Iesu Christi Regis}, \latin{Dominica ultima octobris}, I classis,
of the 1962 \work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica, pp.~697--698,
nos.~3938--3949, with the proper Preface, p.~291, no.~1080, for the occurrence
of Sunday 25~October 2026, when the feast takes the place of the
Twenty-second Sunday after Pentecost without commemoration. The speech
supposes that it is preached after the Gospel: it speaks of the Introit,
Collect, Epistle, Gradual and Alleluia as heard, and of the Offertory, Secret,
Preface, Communion and Postcommunion as still to come. The votive Tract and
the paschal Alleluia printed with the formulary are not said on the feast and
are not used.

\textbf{Length and pace.} The spoken body runs to about 1,440 words, some
11.1 to 12.0 minutes at an unhurried 120 to 130 words a minute by arithmetic
alone; no one has delivered or timed it. It was read through in full,
silently, for sense and ease of speech.

\textbf{Relation to the study's interpretations.} The homily follows the
second of the full study's three interpretations, \textit{A Kingdom from
Above, Gathered from Every Nation and Served by Kings}: Augustine's distinction of ``of'' from ``in'' and of ``from
hence'' from ``here'' (his own reading of the Gospel's words, with the
kingdom here until the harvest and sojourning as a stranger); Aquinas's
``not from here, yet here, because everywhere''; Chrysostom's earthly
kingship whose strength lies in servants; the hearers of the truth;
Augustine's kingdom of those who believe, made no longer of the world by
rebirth in Christ, with the Epistle's translation into the kingdom as his
account of that change; the Collect's wound and its most
loving dominion; Augustine's two places for the Gradual's kings, among the
poor who confess their sins and in the authority that leads men into the
Church; Bellarmine's literal sense of the Introit's verse; the Postcommunion's
warfare as a share in the victory over sin, on Hilary's kingdom where sin is
overcome, under Schuster's standard of the Cross; the homage given willingly
now and by all at the judgment, which Theodoret and Bellarmine read in the
Gradual's verse; and Augustine's peace in himself at the Communion. From the
first interpretation, \textit{The Slain Lamb Receives the Kingdom That Was Always
His}, it takes Chrysostom's prisoner made captive by consenting, the
Offertory's command to ask as Aquinas and Augustine read it, the Secret's
victim and its gifts of unity and peace, and the Lamb worthy because slain.
The difference that matters to the argument is kept: Chrysostom's King, born
a king and receiving nothing in addition, stands beside the dominion received
as man through the passion, and the kingship is not made to begin in the
trial. From the third interpretation it takes only Schuster's note on the Communion,
the King enthroned in the faithful heart; the handing over of the kingdom and
its difficulty are left to the studies. Following the Homiletic Directory,
the speech proclaims the kingdom won in Christ's passion and offered at the
altar, prepares the assembly for the Eucharist as a share in that sacrifice,
and closes its application with one response, said once to the hearers in
the second person and drawn from Augustine's two places for the Gradual's
kings: to pray the Introit's verse before they use the authority they hold.
It ends by answering its opening question, that the King of the Gradual and
the prisoner of the Gospel are one King, before the Introit's acclamation.
These connections are the homily's own and credited to no Father: the
Epistle's translation applied to the hearers' own entry into the kingdom;
the Secret's unity as the healing of the Collect's wound; the kings' two
places extended to whatever authority the hearers hold, and the Introit's
verse taken as a prayer for its use; the truth and a life given up
as the weapons of the Postcommunion's warfare, and the closing statement that
Christ rules his kingdom by the truth and by the life he gave up; and the
Gospel's counter-question put to the hearers. Augustine's \work{Letter}~185
and \work{Quas primas} are not used.

\textbf{Texts quoted, and their route.} Scripture is the Douay--Rheims,
Challoner's revision: Jn 18:34, 36, 37; Apoc 5:12; 1:6; Ps 71:2, 8, 11, 12;
Col 1:13; Dan 7:14; Ps 2:8; Ps 28:10. The scene is drawn from Jn 18:1, 12
and 28. The Collect's ``separated by the wound of sin'' and ``His most loving
dominion'', the Secret's ``this host for the reconciliation of humanity'' and
``the gifts of unity and peace'', the Preface's goods of the kingdom and the
Postcommunion's ``who glory to fight under the standard of Christ the King''
are in the English of the Lasance \work{New Roman Missal} of 1945, a
historical study aid and not an approved liturgical translation of the 1962
Missal; the rest of those prayers is described, not quoted. Augustine and
Chrysostom are quoted in the English of the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers,
Bellarmine in O'Sullivan's English and Schuster in the English of 1930.
Aquinas and Hilary are paraphrased from their Latin; Augustine on those who
belong to the kingdom, on the authority of kings and on peace, and Chrysostom on the King born a king, are
paraphrased from the same English.

\textbf{Exact loci.} Introit, Apoc 5:12; 1:6; verse Ps 71:1 in the Missal's
numbering (Douay 71:2), no.~3938; Collect, no.~3939; Epistle, Col 1:13,
no.~3940; Gradual, Ps 71:8, 11, no.~3941, with Ps 71:12; Alleluia, Dan 7:14,
no.~3942; Gospel, Jn 18:33--37, no.~3945; Offertory, Ps 2:8, no.~3946;
Secret, no.~3947; Preface, no.~1080; Communion, Ps 28:10--11 in the Missal's
numbering (one Douay verse, 28:10), no.~3948; Postcommunion, no.~3949.
Augustine, \work{Tractates on the Gospel of John} 115.2; \work{Enarrationes in
Psalmos} 2.7, 28.11, 71.13--14. Chrysostom, \work{Homilies on John} 83.4.
Aquinas, \work{Super Io.}\ 18 lect.~6 (the kingdom not from here and yet
here); \work{Postilla super Psalmos} 2, nn.~5--6 (the asking by prayer and
passion). Hilary, \work{Tractatus super Psalmos} 2.42. Bellarmine,
\work{Explanatio}, Ps~71 (on vv.~1 and 11). Theodoret, \work{Interpretatio in
Psalmos} 71 (on v.~11). Schuster, \work{The Sacramentary} V, p.~204 (the
Communion and the Postcommunion).
