## The publication basis of the Latin

The feast was instituted by Pius XI's *Quas primas* of 11 December 1925, so no
typical edition before the 1962 book that this repository tracks (1570, 1604,
1862, the 1922 Mame) can carry it, and
`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml` names
the sanctoral matter first published after the 1920 typical edition among what
103(b) does not free *from the facsimile*. Its holding is the route: the Latin
travels on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) plus a public-domain witness to the preexisting
words, never on a property of the 1962 book.

That witness is the formulary's first publication. The Sacred Congregation of
Rites' decree *Urbis et Orbis* of 12 December 1925 approved the Office with the
Mass and the Martyrology elogium of the feast and ordered them for the
universal Church; it is printed, with the Mass under the heading `AD MISSAM`,
in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* 17 (1925), fascicle n. 15 dated 28 December 1925,
printed pp. 666–668, the decree itself closing p. 668 over the signatures of
A. Card. Vico, Prefect, and Alexander Verde, Secretary. A work first published
outside the United States before 1931 is in the United States public domain on
the line `guidance/missals.md` §5 records (Cornell's chart as of 1 January
2026); that line goes stale every January and is applied here as of
2026-10-19. The 1962 edition's copyright, whatever it is, reaches only its own
contribution and does not enlarge the duration of protection in this 1925
material.

- **Witness read.**
  `artifact.holy-see.acta-apostolicae-sedis.volume-17-1925.ia-microfilm-issue-15-pdf-9cb9e63b`,
  registered by this stage: the Internet Archive's 400-ppi microfilm scan of
  fascicle n. 15, SHA-256
  `9cb9e63b61fdf9ef317354d736d73f49bc5fa0727fc9715ca79e7b4b7b7ed56a`,
  30,625,498 bytes, fetched whole on 2026-10-19 and hashed before use; SHA-1
  and MD5 equal the values the item's metadata lists. PDF pp. 78, 79 and 80 =
  printed pp. 666, 667 and 668. Each page was read whole at 200 dpi
  (1392 × 2002, 1392 × 2002 and 1392 × 2018 px); the Collect with the Epistle's
  opening (p. 666) and the stretch from the Epistle's close through the
  paschal Alleluia (p. 667) were read again in crops of the 400-dpi renderings
  (2784 × 3940 px).
- **Witness rejected.** The Holy See's own volume PDF
  (`https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-17-1925-ocr.pdf`,
  SHA-256 `c101831ef1fc721d22a890da35ab53b28eb669d553de951a1adcca6f74ef329e`)
  carries no page image at these pages, only an OCR reconstruction set in
  substitute fonts, and misreads them (`auferatur`, `corrumpatur`,
  `disgregata`, `Fini`, `Paufi`). It supplied no reading.
- **Result.** Every word of every proper element and of the Preface's body, as
  the 1962 page prints them, stands in the 1925 first publication, with the
  three bounded exceptions below. The differences are orthography (the 1925
  *j*: `ejus`, `Judǽis`, `Jesu`, `justítiam`, `judícium`, `subjéctis`,
  `Majestáti`), capitalisation (`Géntium`, `Gentes`, `Quem`, `Ipse`, `Ipso`),
  pointing (listed under each element), the headings (`Introitus`,
  `Offertorium`, `Communio` for the 1962 `Ant. ad Introitum`, `Ant. ad
  Offertorium`, `Antiphona ad Communionem`; `Coloss.` and `Cap.` for `Col.` and
  `Io.`), and the rubrics, which are discussed separately. None changes which
  word is said.
- **Exceptions.**
  1. *Conclusions.* The 1925 page abbreviates every conclusion to `Qui tecum.`
     The 1962 page prints `Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.` The working
     rule recorded in `guidance/propers-for-agents.md` holds: conclusion
     length changes no verdict; reproduce the conclusion the target prints.
     The full formula stands in the public-domain typical editions as the
     common ending of a prayer addressed to the Father that names the Son at
     its end.
  2. *The Preface's closing.* The 1925 page prints the proper body and ends
     `Et ideo...`. The 1962 page completes it with the common closing `Et ídeo
     cum Angelis et Archángelis, cum Thronis et Dominatiónibus, cumque omni
     milítia cæléstis exércitus, hymnum glóriæ tuæ cánimus, sine fine
     dicéntes:` That closing stands in the tracked public-domain Pustet 1862
     (`artifact.catholic-church.missale-romanum.pustet-ratisbon-1862.missale-romanum-1862-text-f34bc7cf`,
     physical lines 40238–40243, the Nativity preface), read on the text layer
     only; the dialogue before the Preface and the *Sanctus* after it are the
     Ordinary.
  3. *The paschal Alleluia's last word.* The 1925 page ends the second verse
     at `Dóminus dominántium.`; the 1962 page adds `Allelúia.` This element is
     not said on 25 October 2026 (below).
- **The rubrics are not covered.** The 1962 `Et non fit commemoratio
  dominicæ.` (after each oration) and `Praefatio propria.` are 1960 rubrical
  matter; the 1925 page directs the opposite (`Et fit Commemoratio Dominicae
  occurrentis.`, and after the Postcommunion `cujus Evangelium legitur in
  fine`) and prints the Preface in place. The votive and paschal rubrics at
  nos. 3943–3944 do stand in the 1925 text word for word (with `Paschali` and
  `Graduale` capitalised and `Versu` for `versu`). The studies describe the
  1962 rubrics; they do not reproduce them as text.
- **The witness's own boundary.** The fascicle is the 1925 state of the Mass.
  It is not the 1962 order and is not cited as such: it witnesses the words,
  not their 1962 arrangement, rank, rubrics or Preface placement.

## Appointed text, as printed in 1962

Accents, ligatures and pointing are those of the page images. `℣.` renders the
versicle sign. Elements 1–11 are said on 25 October 2026; the two printed
options marked *not said* belong to votive use of the formulary on other days
and are recorded because they are text-bearing parts of the printed formulary.

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — no. 3938, printed p. 697, left column

Heading: `Ant. ad Introitum` · citation `Apoc. 5, 12; 1, 6`

> Dignus est Agnus, qui occísus est, accípere virtútem, et divinitátem, et
> sapiéntiam, et fortitúdinem, et honórem. Ipsi glória et impérium in sǽcula
> sæculórum. *Ps. 71, 1* Deus, iudícium tuum Regi da: et iustítiam tuam Fílio
> Regis. ℣. Glória Patri.

The repetition of the antiphon after the *Gloria Patri* is the rubrical rule
(RGMR 427), not printed. 1925 (p. 666): same words; `judícium`, `justítiam`;
heading `Introitus`, citation `Apoc. 5, 12; 1, 6`, verse citation
`Ps. 71, 1.` Clementine: a cento of Apoc 5:12 (without `dicentium voce magna`
and the closing `et gloriam, et benedictionem`) and the last clause of Apoc
1:6 (without `et fecit nos regnum, et sacerdotes Deo et Patri suo` and
`Amen`); the verse is the first clause of the Clementine's Ps 71:2, which
numbers the title (`Psalmus, in Salomonem`) as v. 1. The Missal's *Ps. 71, 1*
counts the title unnumbered, as the NPNF English of Augustine's exposition
also does (`research/scope.md` §3.3).

### 2. Collect (`collect`) — no. 3939, printed p. 697, left column

Heading: `Oratio`

> Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, qui in dilécto Fílio tuo, universórum Rege, ómnia
> instauráre voluísti: concéde propítius; ut cunctæ famíliæ géntium, peccáti
> vúlnere disgregátæ, eius suavíssimo subdántur império: Qui tecum vivit et
> regnat in unitáte.

Rubric: `Et non fit commemoratio dominicæ.` 1925 (p. 666): same words;
`Géntium`, `ejus`, conclusion `Qui tecum.` The conclusion follows RG 115 c
(the prayer names the Son at its end).

### 3. Epistle (`epistle`) — no. 3940, printed pp. 697–698

Heading: `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Colossénses.` · `Col. 1,
12-20`

> Fratres: Grátias ágimus Deo Patri, qui dignos nos fecit in partem sortis
> sanctórum in lúmine, qui erípuit nos de potestáte tenebrárum, et tránstulit
> in regnum Fílii dilectiónis suæ, in quo habémus redemptiónem per sánguinem
> eius remissiónem peccatórum: qui est imágo Dei invisíbilis, primogénitus
> omnis creatúræ: quóniam in ipso cóndita sunt univérsa in cælis et in terra,
> visibília et invisibília, sive throni, sive dominatiónes, sive principátus,
> sive potestátes: ómnia per ipsum et in ipso creáta sunt: et ipse est ante
> omnes, et ómnia in ipso constant. Et ipse est caput córporis Ecclésiæ, qui
> est princípium, primogénitus ex mórtuis: ut sit in ómnibus ipse primátum
> tenens: quia in ipso complácuit omnem plenitúdinem inhabitáre: et per eum
> reconciliáre ómnia in ipsum, pacíficans per sánguinem crucis eius, sive quæ
> in terris, sive quæ in cælis sunt, in Christo Iesu Dómino nostro.

The Epistle crosses the page at `ple-` / `nitúdinem`. 1925 (pp. 666–667): same
words; pointing `sánguinem ejus, remissiónem`, `Qui est imágo`,
`creatúræ;`, `ómnia per ipsum, et in ipso creáta sunt, et ipse`, `tenens;`,
`inhabitáre, et per eum`, `crucis ejus sive quæ`; `Jesu`. Clementine:
`gratias agentes` at v. 12 for the Missal's `Fratres: Grátias ágimus`, and no
`in Christo Iesu Domino nostro` at v. 20; every other word agrees (token
comparison recorded in `research/context.md`). The headpiece caption above the
formulary quotes v. 13.

### 4. Gradual (`gradual`) — no. 3941, printed p. 698, left column

Heading: `Graduale` · `Ps. 71, 8 et 11`

> Dominábitur a mari usque ad mare: et a flúmine usque ad términos orbis
> terrárum. ℣. Et adorábunt eum omnes reges terræ: omnes gentes sérvient ei.

1925 (p. 667): same words; `ad mare, et a flúmine`, `Gentes`. Clementine:
without the initial `Et` of v. 8; the verse agrees with v. 11.

### 5. Alleluia (`alleluia`) — no. 3942, printed p. 698, left column

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Dan. 7, 14* Potéstas eius, potéstas ætérna, quæ non
> auferétur: et regnum eius, quod non corrumpétur. Allelúia.

1925 (p. 667): same words, read at 400 dpi as `auferétur` and `corrumpétur`;
`ejus`. Clementine: the second half of Dan 7:14 only, without `Et dedit ei
potestatem, et honorem, et regnum: et omnes populi, tribus, et linguae ipsi
servient`. No Sequence.

### — Tract (`tract`), *not said on this date* — no. 3943, printed p. 698

Rubric: `¶ In Missis votivis post Septuagesimam, omissis Allelúia et versu
sequenti, dicitur` · heading `Tractus` · `Ps. 88, 27-28 et 30`

> Ipse invocábit me: Pater meus es tu: Deus meus, et suscéptor salútis meæ.
> ℣. Et ego primogénitum ponam illum, excélsum præ régibus terræ. ℣. Et ponam
> in sǽculum sǽculi semen eius: et thronum eius sicut dies cæli.

1925 (p. 667): same words and the same rubric; `illum:`, `ejus`. Clementine:
vv. 27, 28 and 30, omitting v. 29. The condition (votive Masses after
Septuagesima) can never apply to the feast on the last Sunday of October.

### — Paschal Alleluia (`paschal-alleluia`), *not said on this date* — no. 3944, printed p. 698

Rubric: `Tempore autem paschali omittitur graduale, et eius loco dicitur:`

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Dan. 7, 14* Potéstas eius, potéstas ætérna, quæ non
> auferétur: et regnum eius, quod non corrumpétur. Allelúia. ℣. *Apoc. 19, 16*
> Habet in vestiménto et in fémore suo scriptum: Rex regum et Dóminus
> dominántium. Allelúia.

1925 (p. 667): same words except the final `Allelúia`, which the 1925 page
does not print (exception 3 above); `Rex regum, et Dóminus`. Clementine: Apoc
19:16 without its initial `Et`. The feast never falls in paschal time.

### 6. Gospel (`gospel`) — no. 3945, printed p. 698

Heading: `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Ioánnem.` · `Io. 18, 33-37`

> In illo témpore: Dixit Pilátus ad Iesum: Tu es Rex Iudæórum? Respóndit
> Iesus: A temetípso hoc dicis, an álii dixérunt tibi de me? Respóndit Pilátus:
> Numquid ego Iudǽus sum? Gens tua et pontífices tradidérunt te mihi: quid
> fecísti? Respóndit Iesus: Regnum meum non est de hoc mundo. Si ex hoc mundo
> esset regnum meum, minístri mei útique decertárent, ut non tráderer Iudǽis:
> nunc autem regnum meum non est hinc. Dixit ítaque ei Pilátus: Ergo Rex es
> tu? Respóndit Iesus: Tu dicis, quia Rex sum ego. Ego in hoc natus sum, et ad
> hoc veni in mundum, ut testimónium perhíbeam veritáti: omnis qui est ex
> veritáte, audit vocem meam.

Cue: `Credo.` 1925 (p. 667): same words; `Judæórum`, `Judǽus`, `Judǽis`,
`mihi; quid`, `Judǽis; nunc`, `Tu dicis quia`; heading `Cap. 18, 33-37`.
Clementine: the liturgical opening `In illo tempore: Dixit Pilatus ad Iesum:`
stands for `Introivit ergo iterum in praetorium Pilatus: et vocavit Jesum, et
dixit ei:`; every other word of vv. 33–37 agrees.

### 7. Offertory (`offertory`) — no. 3946, printed p. 698, right column

Heading: `Ant. ad Offertorium` · `Ps. 2, 8`

> Póstula a me, et dabo tibi gentes hereditátem tuam, et possessiónem tuam
> términos terræ.

1925 (p. 667): same words; `Gentes`. Clementine: agrees (`haereditatem`).

### 8. Secret (`secret`) — no. 3947, printed p. 698, right column

Heading: `Secreta`

> Hóstiam tibi, Dómine, humánæ reconciliatiónis offérimus: præsta, quǽsumus;
> ut, quem sacrifíciis præséntibus immolámus, ipse cunctis géntibus unitátis
> et pacis dona concédat, Iesus Christus, Fílius tuus, Dóminus noster: Qui
> tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.

Rubrics: `Et non fit commemoratio dominicæ.` `Præfatio propria.` 1925
(p. 667): same words; `ut Quem … Ipse cunctis Géntibus`, `Jesus Christus
Fílius tuus Dóminus noster: Qui tecum.`

### 9. Preface (`preface`) — no. 1080, printed p. 291

Heading: `PRÆFATIO DE D.N. IESU CHRISTO REGE` · rubric as in Provenance.
Dialogue `Per ómnia sǽcula sæculórum` … `Dignum et iustum est.` (Ordinary).

> Vere dignum et iustum est, æquum et salutáre, nos tibi semper et ubíque
> grátias ágere: Dómine, sancte Pater, omnípotens ætérne Deus: Qui unigénitum
> Fílium tuum, Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Sacerdótem ætérnum et
> universórum Regem, óleo exsultatiónis unxísti: ut, seípsum in ara Crucis
> hóstiam immaculátam et pacíficam ófferens, redemptiónis humánæ sacraménta
> perágeret: et, suo subiéctis império ómnibus creatúris, ætérnum et universále
> regnum imménsæ tuæ tráderet maiestáti: regnum veritátis et vitæ; regnum
> sanctitátis et grátiæ; regnum iustítiæ, amóris et pacis. Et ídeo cum Angelis
> et Archángelis, cum Thronis et Dominatiónibus, cumque omni milítia cæléstis
> exércitus, hymnum glóriæ tuæ cánimus, sine fine dicéntes:

Then the *Sanctus* (Ordinary). 1925 (p. 668, headed `Præfatio`, printed in
place between the Secret and the Communion): the body in the same words to
`amóris et pacis. Et ídeo...`; pointing `Dómine sancte, Pater omnípotens,
ætérne Deus`, `Fílium tuum Dóminum nostrum Jesum Christum`, `ut seípsum in ara
crucis, hóstiam immaculátam et pacíficam ófferens`, `et suo subjéctis império
ómnibus creatúris, ætérnum et universále regnum, imménsæ tuæ tráderet
Majestáti`, `regnum justítiæ`. The 1925 and 1962 pointings of the opening
group the vocatives differently (`Dómine sancte, Pater omnípotens` against
`Dómine, sancte Pater, omnípotens`) without changing a word. Scriptural echoes,
not appointed Scripture: `óleo exsultatiónis` (Ps 44:8; Heb 1:9) and the
handing over of the kingdom (1 Cor 15:24).

### 10. Communion (`communion`) — no. 3948, printed p. 698, right column

Heading: `Antiphona ad Communionem` · `Ps. 28, 10 et 11`

> Sedébit Dóminus Rex in ætérnum: Dóminus benedícet pópulo suo in pace.

1925 (p. 668): same words; heading `Communio`, citation `Ps. 28, 10 et 11`.
Clementine: the second half of v. 10 (without `Dominus diluvium inhabitare
facit, et`) and the second half of v. 11 (without `Dominus virtutem populo suo
dabit;`).

### 11. Postcommunion (`postcommunion`) — no. 3949, printed p. 698, right column

Heading: `Postcommunio`

> Immortalitátis alimóniam consecúti, quǽsumus, Dómine: ut, qui sub Christi
> Regis vexíllis militáre gloriámur, cum ipso, in cælésti sede, iúgiter
> regnáre possímus: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.

Rubric: `Et non fit commemoratio dominicæ.` 1925 (p. 668): same words; `cum
Ipso`, `Qui tecum.`

### Nothing else is proper

The formulary prints no Sequence, prophecy, proper *Communicantes* or *Hanc
igitur*, *Oratio super populum*, second oration, blessing or alternative
reading (RGMR 501). The Ordinary is not reproduced.

## English routes

English is never composed, translated, adapted or paraphrased by the studies.
Every English text below is quoted from a registered public-domain witness and
attributed at first use and in the references. None is an approved liturgical
translation.

- **Scriptural elements: the Douay-Rheims (Challoner),**
  `edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
  read through `tools/tpt mass-propers show --bible douay-rheims` and the
  verse-text artifacts directly. Where the Missal selects part of a verse, the
  Douay words for that part are quoted, with an ellipsis for what the Missal
  omits; nothing is reworded to fit. The Douay cannot carry these Missal forms,
  which the studies give in Latin with a plain statement of what they are:
  - the Epistle's liturgical address `Fratres:` and its closing `in Christo
    Iesu Dómino nostro` (the Douay's v. 12 begins `Giving thanks to God the
    Father`, the participle of the Clementine, not the Missal's `Grátias
    ágimus`);
  - the Gospel's liturgical opening `In illo témpore: Dixit Pilátus ad Iesum`
    (the Douay's v. 33 reads `Pilate therefore went into the hall again and
    called Jesus and said to him`).
  The Lasance hand missal (next item) prints its own English of these frames
  (`Brethren, we give thanks to God the Father`; `in Christ Jesus Our Lord`;
  `At that time: Pilate said to Jesus`), public domain on the same record; an
  author who prefers to quote it there does so under the Lasance's attribution
  and says so.
  - **Psalm numbering in English.** The Introit's verse is Douay Ps 71:2a
    (`Give to the king thy judgment, O God, and to the king's son thy
    justice`), not Douay 71:1, which is the title (`A psalm on Solomon`). The
    Communion's two half-verses stand, in the tracked Challoner text, inside
    one row numbered 28:10 (`The Lord maketh the flood to dwell: and the Lord
    shall sit king for ever. The Lord will give strength to his people: the
    Lord will bless his people with peace.`); that text has no 28:11 row. The
    studies quote `and the Lord shall sit king for ever … the Lord will bless
    his people with peace` and cite it as Ps 28:10-11 in the Missal's
    numbering, saying where the Douay's verse division differs.
