\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Angels acclaim the slain Lamb
as worthy of power; the Father promises the nations to his Son, who is to ask
for them; Paul gives thanks for the transfer into the Son's kingdom; bound
before the governor, Jesus says that his kingdom is not of this world.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & King by nature, Christ
receives as man the nations he asked for by his passion, by two rights
(Aquinas). The kingdom is the faithful, not of the world but in it
(Augustine), its empire from above (Chrysostom), handed over as the redeemed
brought to the vision of God (Augustine, Hilary).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Ask, as the Son was bidden
(Aquinas), and share the priesthood of the King who offered himself (Bede);
serve him as a man by living faithfully, and a ruler also as ruler
(Augustine); fight sin under the standard, which is the Cross (Schuster); be pure in
heart to see God (Augustine, Hilary).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & All kings bend at the
judgment (Bellarmine). The Son hands over the kingdom without ceasing to
reign, for the Apostle's ``until'' sets no term (Cyril of Jerusalem); the King
who sits now in his saints sits in them for ever (Augustine).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
