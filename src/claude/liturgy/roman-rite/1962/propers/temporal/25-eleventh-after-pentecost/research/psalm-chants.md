# Psalm-chants record — Introit (Ps 67), Gradual (Ps 27), Alleluia (Ps 80), Offertory (Ps 29)

Element keys: `introit`, `gradual`, `alleluia`, `offertory`. Vulgate
numbering throughout (Heb./modern 68, 28, 81, 30). Companion to `scope.md`.

Editions read: Augustine, *Enarrationes in Psalmos* — Latin at augustinus.it
(files numbered by exposition sequence: Ps 27 = 031, Ps 29 = 033/034, Ps 67 =
084, Ps 80 = 099), English NPNF 1/8 (1888, PD) at New Advent (NPNF psalm
numbers are the English numbers: 28, 30, 68, 81); Cassiodorus, *Expositio
Psalmorum* (PL 70 text) at monumenta.ch; Jerome, *Commentarioli in Psalmos*
and *Tractatus (LIX) in Psalmos*, Morin's editions (Anecdota Maredsolana
III.1, 1895; III.2, 1897), read in the archive.org OCR of the bound item;
Hilary, *Tractatus super Psalmos*, CSEL 22 (Zingerle, 1891); Theodoret,
*Interpretatio in Psalmos*, Greek PG 80 text in the TLG-sourced transcription
hosted by Documenta Catholica Omnia (Hill's copyrighted English not used);
Aquinas, *In psalmos Davidis expositio* at Corpus Thomisticum; Bellarmine in
the PD English of John O'Sullivan (Dublin, 1866); Peter Lombard,
*Commentarium in Psalmos* (PL 191), archive.org OCR of the 1854 Migne volume
(column numbers illegible in that OCR; cited by psalm and verse).

## The load-bearing textual finding: lemma vs. chant

| Chant reading | Clementine | Witnesses' lemmata |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introit **unanimes in domo** | *unius moris* | **Cassiodorus alone lemmatizes *unanimes*** — the Old Latin/Roman-psalter type: the chant sings Cassiodorus's Bible. Augustine's lemma is *unius modi*, his gloss "**unanimes, unum sentientes**"; Hilary reads *unimores*; Jerome *unius moris* (Hebrew note: *monachos*); Theodoret LXX μονοτρόπους; Lombard and Bellarmine carry *unius moris* glossed via Augustine as "unanimes"/"of one mind." The chant's word is the patristic gloss become text |
| Gradual **In Deo speravit** / **confitebor illi** / **ne discedas a me** | *in ipso… ei*; *ne sileas… nequando taceas* | *In ipso* in every witness (Augustine, Cassiodorus, Aquinas, Lombard, Bellarmine); *confitebor **illi*** is **Cassiodorus's** reading (Aquinas's lemma: *tibi*); **"ne discedas a me" appears in NO checked witness** — all expound *ne sileas / nequando taceas* (Aquinas records Jerome's Hebrew: *ne te tacente assimiler*). | Alleluia's condensed v. 3 (drops *et date tympanum; psalterium*) | full verse | No witness has the short form; Augustine's Old Latin even reads *Accipite* for *Sumite*. Every commentator's exegesis of the verse turns partly on the tympanum the chant omits |

## Introit — Ps 67:6–7, 36; verse 67:2

- **Hilary, Tractatus super Ps 67 §§2, 8, 37 (CSEL 22).** Earliest Latin
  witness. Lemma at v. 7 *unimores* (hyper-literal for μονοτρόπους). The whole
  psalm is prophecy of Christ: on v. 2, *resurrectionis enim nunc in deo
  tempus optatur* — "Exsurgat Deus" prays the Resurrection, at which the
  demons who hate him were routed. On vv. 6–7 he is the earliest to weld the
  verse to Acts 4:32: *ubi unius moris ac uoluntatis pacificus unanimisque
  conuentus — erat enim secundum Actus apostolorum credentium anima et cor
  unum* (§8). On v. 36 he closes with the resurrection of the body: God
  wonderful in his saints *quos in uitae resurrectionem excitabit aeternos*;
  *dat itaque uirtutem plebi suae et fortitudinem… quae inmortalis effecta
  intra se uitam uirtutemque contineat* (§37).
- **Augustine, Enarr. in Ps 67 §7 (vv. 6–7), §§2–3 (v. 2), §44 (v. 36; NPNF
  sec. 40).** Lemma *unius modi*, glossed at once *unanimes, unum sentientes:
  iste est locus sanctus Domini*; philological note that Greek μονότροποι can
  be rendered by either *modi* or *mores*, and that the Greek lacks the *in-*
  of *inhabitare*. God, contained by no place, makes his own holy place: *ne
  quaeramus eum absque nobis; sed potius unius modi habitantes in domo,
  mereamur ut habitare etiam ipse dignetur in nobis*; the place is built by
  grace, not merits (§8). **He does not cite Acts 4:32 here** (checked in the
  full Latin — the Acts link is Hilary's and Cassiodorus's). On v. 2 the verse
  is already fulfilled in the Resurrection (NPNF sec. 2: "Already this has
  come to pass: Christ has risen up… and His enemies have been dispersed
  through all nations"). On v. 36 the Introit's last limb becomes resurrection
  doctrine: *Seminatur enim hoc corpus in infirmitate; surget in virtute*
  (1 Cor 15:43 — the day's own Epistle chapter), *virtutem resurrectionis
  eius* (Phil 3:10), *fortitudinem autem, qua inimica destruetur mors*;
  "Israel" = *videns Deum*, fulfilled in the beatific vision.
- **Jerome, Tractatus de Ps 67 (Morin).** Lemma *unius moris*, with the
  Hebrew note: *In hebraeo autem habet "Dominus inhabitare facit monachos in
  domo": id est, in quibus non cohabitat peccatum* — Heb. *yeḥidim*, the monk
  hearing "monks." Reading: moral constancy — *Qui unius moris sunt, et non
  mutantur… Peccator autem mutatur*; *ubicumque sanctitas est, ibi
  habitaculum Dei est*. On v. 2 a stated double key: *specialiter* of the
  Lord — *ut resurgat a mortuis, et dispergat inimicos suos, hoc est diabolum
  et exercitum eius* — and *generaliter* whenever we are in tribulation. **The
  homily breaks off before v. 36** (last lemma v. 31); the *Commentarioli* on
  Ps 67 begin only at v. 10 — nothing on 67:6–7 or 67:36.
- **Cassiodorus, Exp. in Ps 67 ad vv. 6, 7, 36.** The only checked lemma with
  the chant's *unanimes*: *Deus, qui habitare facit unanimes in domo*, with
  Acts 4:32 restored (*Multitudinis autem credentium erat cor unum et anima
  una*) and Ps 132:1; *In domo vero Ecclesiam significat*. Rhetorical frame:
  definition *per laudem* (κατ᾽ ἔπαινον). On v. 36 he **departs from Hilary
  and Augustine**: no resurrection — *Dominus dabit fidelibus suis virtutem
  patientiae, et fidei fortitudinem, ut possint ad aeterna praemia pervenire*;
  the gift moralized into patience and faith, eschatology retained only as
  *praemia*.
- **Theodoret, In Ps 67 ad v. 7 (PG 80:1380) and v. 36 (PG 80:1400).** LXX
  μονοτρόπους: *τοὺς ἕνα σκοπὸν ἔχοντας, καὶ τῆς ἀρετῆς μόνης ἐπιμελεῖσθαι
  προαιρουμένους* — the single-mannered are those with one aim, choosing
  virtue only. No Acts, no monasticism, no resurrection at these verses; on
  v. 36 the saints are lovers of virtue who make God be praised (Mt 5:16),
  the strengthening providential.
- **Peter Lombard, In Ps 67 ad v. 6 (PL 191).** Pure Augustine with the gloss
  embedded: *unius moris… id est unanimes, unum sentientes… Hunc autem locum
  gratia aedificat, non meritis* (tagged [Aug.]) — the conveyor belt to the
  schools.
- **Bellarmine, In Ps 67 (O'Sullivan pp. 196–201).** Acts 4:32 now standard
  ("of one mind, one will… 'One soul, one heart'"); on v. 35–36 Augustine's
  resurrection reading, not Cassiodorus's moralization: God "will endow his
  elect with true and real immortality."
- **Aquinas: negative.** His psalms commentary ends at Ps 54 (terminal file
  closes "Super Psalmo 54 n. 14… Deo gratias"); no Ps 67 or Ps 80.

## Gradual — Ps 27:7; verse 27:1

- **Augustine, Enarr. in Ps 27 §1, §7.** The psalm is the Mediator's:
  *Ipsius mediatoris vox est, manu fortis in conflictu passionis* (§1); "be
  not silent from me" = let not the Word be separated from the assumed
  manhood. At v. 7 the flattest possible gloss of the Gradual's central word:
  *Et refloruit caro mea: id est, et resurrexit caro mea*; freed from the fear
  of death, believers confess him *ex voluntate*, by free will.
- **Cassiodorus, Exp. in Ps 27 ad vv. 1, 7.** Lemma *confitebor illi* (the
  chant's word). Follows Augustine on the voice (*Christus Dominus clamat ad
  Patrem tempore passionis*) and develops the flower-figure: *Refloruit…
  quia et primo floruit: quippe quae ex Virgine sine peccato tanquam
  pulcherrimi floris singulare decus emicuit* (Isa 11:1) — the flesh flowered
  in the virginal conception and re-flowered in the Resurrection; *Refloruit
  ergo significat resurrexit*.
- **Jerome, Commentarioli in Ps 27.** On v. 1: *Lacus significat infernum*
  (lemma Gallican — again no *ne discedas*). On v. 7 a text-critical and
  mystical doublet: *Pro carne, Theodotion "cor" interpretatus est. Aliter:
  quippe consurreximus Christo* — Theodotion's variant registered, and the
  flourishing read of OUR co-resurrection (Isa 66:14). No Tractatus homily on
  Ps 27 exists (the series jumps 14 → 66).
- **Aquinas, Super Ps 27 nn. 1, 7.** On v. 1, scholastic division; mystically
  (from the Glossa) the man Christ prays not to be deserted by the Godhead:
  *ne sileas subtrahendo mihi in beneficium resurrectionis, et ne taceas,
  idest ne deseras in passione*. He records Jerome's Hebrew *ne te tacente
  assimiler*. On v. 7, Adam's flower and Christ's re-flower: *In primo homine
  floruit caro eius per innocentiae puritatem, sed peccando inquinata est.
  Sed in Christo refloruit in resurrectione* (Cant 1:15); soul-side, *ex
  voluntate mea confitebor tibi* (his lemma *tibi*) — the converted will
  praising freely; *vel si dicatur hoc de Christo, refertur ad membra sua*.
- **Theodoret, In Ps 27 ad v. 7 (PG 80:1060) — the dissent.** Historical-
  Davidic (Saul, Doeg, the Ziphites): released from the hardships of flight,
  *ἀνέθηλά τε καὶ ἤνθησα* — "I sprouted and bloomed again," bodily vigour
  recovered from good spirits. **No resurrection**: the one major witness who
  declines the Christological reading of *refloruit caro mea*. *Ἐκ θελήματος*
  = the hymn offered freely.
- **Peter Lombard, In Ps 27 ad v. 7 (PL 191).** Transmits the double tradition
  with tags: the flesh *quae in Adam defloruit per peccatum* [Gl. int.];
  *Vel, caro mea refloruit, id est iterum in resurrectione floruit* [Cassiod.]
  — Cassiodorus's synthesis, correctly attributed.
- **Bellarmine, In Ps 27 (O'Sullivan pp. 68–69).** "He now passes to foretell
  the glory of the Lord's resurrection…"; on v. 7, "My flesh, that had
  withered up in death, is not only restored to life, but to the bloom of
  youth, health, joy, and beauty."
- **Hilary: negative** (CSEL 22 contents: Instructio; Pss 1, 2, 9, 13, 14,
  51–69, 118–150, fragments — no Ps 27, 29, or 80).

## Alleluia — Ps 80:2–3

- **Augustine, Enarr. in Ps 80 §§3–5.** Asaph = *congregatio*; the title's
  winepresses = the Church's press separating oil from dregs. V. 2
  anti-spectacle: *Exsultate alii circo, vos Deo* — let others shout in the
  circus; you, to God our helper. V. 3 the exchange-allegory: *Psalmus
  spiritalis est, tympanum carnale… accipite spiritalia, et date carnalia* —
  receive the spiritual word, give back carnal things (material support,
  mercy); psaltery (sounding from above) = heavenly preaching joined to the
  cithara of earthly deeds — word and work must sound together.
- **Cassiodorus, Exp. in Ps 80 ad vv. 2–3.** On *adiutori*: *fictitius deus
  non est adiutor, sed elisor; non sanans, sed vulnerans* — the true God, not
  idols, *heals* (the same *sanare* the day's Offertory sings); *Iubilate
  dictum est a iuvando, id est delectando*. Augustine's frame re-tooled
  ascetically: the drum's stretched hide becomes the mortified body — *sic
  hominum corpus, dum pro Domino tribulatione quatitur, ad superna mandata
  dulcius temperatur; tunc Deo bene damus tympanum dum eleemosynas facimus,
  cum ieiuniis corpus affligimus*; psaltery = God's words, cithara = human
  acts, *in unam societatem debeant convenire*; plus his rhetorical tag
  (*enthymematicus, id est rhetoricus syllogismus*).
- **Jerome (Morin): treats Ps 80 but NOT these verses.** The Tractatus is a
  riff on the title — the three *pro torcularibus* psalms (8, 80, 83):
  *Octavus dies resurrectionis est… in octavo ergo vincimus, et in octogesimo
  cum Domino congregamur*; the winepress = the Passion (*Torcular calcavi
  solus*) — then leaps from the title to v. 5 (*linguam quam non noverat
  audivit* = the Gospel-tongue heard on leaving Egypt). Vv. 2–3 receive no
  exposition; the Commentarioli likewise (title scholion — Asaph =
  *congregatio* — then v. 8). N.B. Jerome's own tympanum-allegory (*nos sumus
  tympanum… quod non habet carnes, nisi quod tenditur*) occurs in the homily
  on Ps 149 in the same volume, NOT at 80:3 — noted to prevent a mis-citation.
- **Theodoret, In Ps 80 ad vv. 2–3 (PG 80:1520–1521).** Literal-festal with
  hexaplaric notes (Symmachus εὐφημεῖτε, Aquila αἰνοποιεῖτε; ψαλτήριον =
  Symmachus λύραν); the jubilus a victory-shout; the instruments stay
  instruments — *τῶν Παραλειπομένων ἡ ἱστορία διδάσκει* (Chronicles teaches
  the antiphonal orchestra). The sharpest contrast with Augustine/Cassiodorus.
- **Bellarmine, In Ps 80 (O'Sullivan pp. 246–247).** With Theodoret rather
  than Augustine: the new-moon festival calendar, "but it matters little
  which"; and the devotional point — "he who sings moodily… gives to
  understand that he has very little affection for him whose praises he
  chants."
- **Aquinas: negative** (ends at Ps 54). **Hilary: negative.** **Peter
  Lombard on Ps 80: not checked** (bounded check spent on Pss 27 and 67).

## Offertory — Ps 29:2–3, with the title *in dedicatione domus David*

- **Augustine, Enarr. in Ps 29, two expositions** (NPNF carries only the
  first; Enarr. II read in Latin). Enarr. I: a psalm "of the joy of the
  Resurrection… not only of the Lord, but also of the whole Church";
  *Exaltabo… suscepisti me: sublimitatem tuam laudabo* (§2); the healing is
  from mortality itself (§3). Enarr. II: the title solves the singer's
  paradox — *Modo enim fabricatur domus, id est Ecclesia, postea dedicabitur.
  In dedicatione apparebit gloria populi christiani, quae modo latet* (§6),
  Christ's risen body the *exemplum*; on v. 3 (§12): *Quem sanavit, qui
  nunquam aegrotavit…? Non, sed mortem carnis portabat, vulnus tuum portabat,
  sanaturus te de vulnere tuo. Sanata est autem caro. Quando? Cum resurrexit*
  (1 Cor 15:54 quoted — again the day's Epistle chapter).
- **Cassiodorus, Exp. in Ps 29, title and vv. 2–3.** The compression: *Domus
  significat dominici corporis templum; dedicatio vero resurrectionem*. On
  v. 2 a figure on the chant's very verb: *Delectasti vero posuit, pro
  delectare fecisti. Quae figura dicitur hypallage*. On v. 3, Augustine's
  paradox nearly verbatim: *Quemadmodum sanatus est, qui non legitur
  aegrotasse? Sed occulte ille aegrotabat, quando universum hominem sua
  incarnatione suscepit… quia in illo sanati sumus, sanatum se ipse
  profitetur* — a textbook case of his dependence on Augustine.
- **Jerome, Commentarioli in Ps 29, ad titulum.** One decisive sentence:
  *Dedicatio domus David, resurrectio Salvatoris intellegitur, in qua omnia
  vitae corpora dedicantur*. Vv. 2–3 receive no scholion; no Tractatus homily
  on Ps 29 exists.
- **Aquinas, Super Ps 29 nn. 1–2.** Weighs the letter (David never dedicated
  the LORD's house; perhaps his own, 2 Sam 5) — *Tamen melius intelligitur
  quod referatur ad mysterium domus David, idest Christi… dedicationem, quae
  fuit in resurrectione, quando corpus Christi indutum est gloria
  immortalitatis. Super corpus Christi continue construitur; et per
  conversionem fidelium dedicabitur* — fusing Cassiodorus's body-dedication
  with Augustine's Church-dedication in one two-stage scheme. On v. 3: God
  heals the whole man; *Christum vero sanavit a corporali solum
  passibilitate*; *eduxisti ab inferno… ad litteram intelligitur de Christo*.
- **Theodoret, In Ps 29, title and vv. 2–3 (PG 80:1069–1072).** David neither
  built the temple nor fits the words; therefore *ἐγκαινισμὸν οἴκου* is *τὴν
  τῆς ἀνθρωπίνης φύσεως νεουργίαν* — the dedication of the house is the
  re-making of human nature, effected by Christ's accepting and destroying
  death. On v. 2 the Master takes "our firstfruits" (τὴν ἡμετέραν ἀπαρχήν)
  and does not let nature become the demons' gloating-prize. On v. 3 Antiochene
  precision returns: *ἐκέκραξα… καὶ ἰάσω με* fits **Hezekiah** "in the obvious
  sense" (κατὰ τὸν πρόχειρον νοῦν), while human nature received resurrection
  unasked, by pure φιλανθρωπία. Even the sober Antiochene reads the psalm
  through death-destroyed-and-nature-renewed; only the mechanics differ.
- **Bellarmine, In Ps 29 (O'Sullivan pp. 73–74).** "In a prophetic sense, it
  applies literally to Christ. 'Thou hast healed me' of the wounds I suffered
  on the cross… 'saved me' by my resurrection"; all the saints will say it
  "most completely, in soul and body" on the last day.
- **Hilary: negative** (no tractatus on Ps 29).

## Development map (who follows whom)

- **Introit:** Hilary (Acts 4:32 + resurrection at v. 36) → Augustine
  (unanimity-gloss + philology; v. 36 sharpened by 1 Cor 15:43/Phil 3:10;
  Acts dropped) → Cassiodorus (restores Acts, lemma *unanimes*, but moralizes
  v. 36 into patience/faith) → Lombard (transmits Augustine) → Bellarmine
  (Acts + Augustinian immortality). Jerome runs a parallel monastic track
  (*monachos in domo*). Theodoret stands apart (μονότροποι = single-aimed).
- **Gradual:** Augustine's *refloruit = resurrexit* → Cassiodorus adds
  conception-flower/resurrection-reflower (Isa 11:1) → Lombard transmits both
  tagged → Aquinas scholasticizes (Adam/Christ, flesh/soul) → Bellarmine
  popularizes. Jerome supports (Theodotion note; *consurreximus Christo*).
  Theodoret dissents (David's recovered vigour).
- **Alleluia:** Augustine's spiritalia/carnalia exchange → Cassiodorus
  re-tools drum = mortified flesh; Jerome never touches the verses (title
  mysticism: 80 = resurrection-number); Theodoret and (here) Bellarmine keep
  the feast literal.
- **Offertory:** Augustine Enarr. II's *Quem sanavit, qui nunquam aegrotavit?*
  → Cassiodorus's near-verbatim rerun → Aquinas's two-stage
  constructio/dedicatio → Bellarmine. Jerome's one-liner is the tersest form;
  Theodoret converges from the Antiochene side (ἐγκαινισμός = νεουργία).
- Liturgical aptness (source-grounded observation, no compiler claim): the
  Offertory's *sanasti me* tradition sits beside the day's Gospel of a healing;
  Cassiodorus's *non sanans, sed vulnerans* (of idols) falls in his exposition
  of the day's own Alleluia psalm; and every Latin witness reads Introit
  v. 36, Gradual v. 7, and Offertory vv. 2–3 as one resurrection-arc.

## Material negative results

1. **Aquinas absent on Pss 67 and 80** — the Corpus Thomisticum series ends
   "Super Psalmo 54 n. 14… Deo gratias."
2. **Hilary absent on Pss 27, 29, 80** — CSEL 22 heading scan; extant
   tractatus: Instructio; Pss 1, 2, 9, 13, 14, 51–69, 118–150, fragments.
   (monumenta.ch's Hilary "p67" leaf is broken — returns Ps 149's text; the
   Ps 67 tractate was read in CSEL 22.)
3. **Jerome, Tractatus:** none on Pss 27 or 29 (series jumps 14 → 66); the
   Ps 67 homily ends at v. 31; the Ps 80 homily skips vv. 2–4.
4. **Jerome, Commentarioli:** Ps 67 scholia begin at v. 10; Ps 80
   title-scholion then v. 8; Ps 29 title and v. 6 only.
5. **Augustine does NOT cite Acts 4:32 at Ps 67:7** (checked in the full
   Latin) — the Acts link reaches Bellarmine through Hilary/Cassiodorus.
6. **NPNF carries only Enarratio I on Ps 30 (Vulg. 29)**; Enarr. II (the
   eschatological dedication and *Quem sanavit*) exists in Latin only.
7. **Peter Lombard:** checked on Pss 27 and 67 only; Pss 29/80 not checked.
8. Failed routes recorded: Documenta Catholica Omnia's Hilary PDF is
   image-only; two archive.org items labeled "Patrologiae… 80" are not PG 80
   (Theodoret reached via the TLG-authorized DCO transcription instead).
