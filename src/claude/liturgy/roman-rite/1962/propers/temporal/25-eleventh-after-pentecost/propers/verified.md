# Eleventh Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical full research edition at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/25-eleventh-after-pentecost` and its
mechanical `-synthesis` PDF companion. The canonical edition prints the
complete Latin below beside the identified English; the synthesis companion
omits the appointed-text component.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1542

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 67, 6-7 et 36`
(Vulgate numbering; Hebrew/modern Ps. 68). The psalm verse is cited
`Ps. ibid., 2`.

> Deus in loco sancto suo: Deus qui inhabitáre facit unánimes in domo: ipse
> dabit virtútem et fortitúdinem plebi suæ. *Ps. ibid., 2* Exsúrgat Deus, et
> dissipéntur inimíci eius: et fúgiant, qui odérunt eum, a fácie eius.
> ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 390, foot of the right
column, below the formulary heading; PDF p. 471. **Checked:** 2026-08-19,
with the 600 dpi rendering read for `unánimes`, the absence of a comma after
the second `Deus`, and `a fácie` (which the OCR runs together).

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** The antiphon is a centonisation
declared by the missal's own reference `6-7 et 36`: it takes the closing
clause of v. 6 (`Deus in loco sancto suo`), the opening clause of v. 7
(`Deus qui inhabitáre facit … in domo`), and the central clause of v. 36
(`ipse dabit virtútem et fortitúdinem plebi suæ`), omitting what stands
between and around them. At v. 7 it sings `unánimes` where the Clementine
prints `unius moris` — see the divergence table below.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent,
punctuation, or reference issue.

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1543

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Omnípotens sempitérne Deus, qui, abundántia pietátis tuæ, et mérita
> súpplicum excédis et vota: effúnde super nos misericórdiam tuam; ut
> dimíttas quæ consciéntia métuit, et adícias quod orátio non præsúmit. Per
> Dóminum nostrum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, top of the left column;
PDF p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19 at 600 dpi for `adícias` and the comma
pair around `abundántia pietátis tuæ`.

**Notes.** The typical edition prints `adícias`, the contracted orthography;
Benziger prints `adiícias` and the 1862 Pustet hand missal `adjicias` — three
spellings of one verb. The punctuation is load-bearing: the colon after
`vota` closes the relative clause of praise, the semicolon after `tuam`
separates the petition from its double purpose clause, and the two commas
around `abundántia pietátis tuæ, et mérita` are as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1544

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Corínthios.` · `1 Cor. 15, 1-10`.

> Fratres: Notum vobis fácio Evangélium, quod prædicávi vobis, quod et
> accepístis, in quo et statis, per quod et salvámini: qua ratióne
> prædicáverim vobis, si tenétis, nisi frustra credidístis. Trádidi enim
> vobis in primis, quod et accépi: quóniam Christus mórtuus est pro peccátis
> nostris secúndum Scriptúras: et quia sepúltus est, et quia resurréxit
> tértia die secúndum Scriptúras: et quia visus est Cephæ, et post hoc
> úndecim. Deínde visus est plus quam quingéntis frátribus simul, ex quibus
> multi manent usque adhuc, quidam autem dormiérunt. Deínde visus est Iacóbo,
> deínde Apóstolis ómnibus: novíssime autem ómnium tamquam abortívo, visus
> est et mihi. Ego enim sum mínimus Apostolórum, qui non sum dignus vocári
> Apóstolus, quóniam persecútus sum Ecclésiam Dei. Grátia autem Dei sum id
> quod sum, et grátia eius in me vácua non fuit.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, left column; PDF
p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Notes.** The liturgical address `Fratres:` stands where the Clementine
reads `Notum autem vobis fácio, fratres` — the lesson keeps Paul's sentence
but moves his address to the front and drops `autem`. The lesson **ends
mid-verse**: the Clementine's v. 10 continues `sed abundántius illis ómnibus
laborávi: non ego autem, sed grátia Dei mecum`, and the missal cuts before
it, so the last thing read is grace not made void, not the Apostle's labour.
The same mid-verse ending stands in the Venice Missal of 1570 and the Pustet
Missal of 1862 (see the rights section), so it is the received lectionary
boundary, not a 1962 novelty.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue in the Vatican
typical witness.

## 4. Gradual — marginal no. 1545

**Printed heading and reference:** `Graduale` · `Ps. 27, 7 et 1` (Vulgate
numbering; Hebrew/modern Ps. 28).

> In Deo sperávit cor meum, et adiútus sum: et reflóruit caro mea, et ex
> voluntáte mea confitébor illi. ℣. Ad te, Dómine, clamávi: Deus meus, ne
> síleas, ne discédas a me.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, lower left column; PDF
p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** The verse order is inverted against the psalm: the chant's body is
v. 7 and its versicle v. 1, as the reference `7 et 1` declares. Both halves
diverge from the Clementine — `In Deo` for `in ipso`, `confitébor illi` for
`confitébor ei`, `clamávi` for `clamábo`, and `ne discédas a me` where the
Clementine has no such clause — see the divergence table.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 5. Alleluia — marginal no. 1546

**Printed form and reference:** no separate heading; the reference
`Ps. 80, 2-3` (Vulgate numbering; Hebrew/modern Ps. 81) is printed inline
after the versicle sign.

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 80, 2-3* Exsultáte Deo adiutóri nostro,
> iubiláte Deo Iacob: súmite psalmum iucúndum cum cíthara. Allelúia.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, foot of the left
column, continuing at the head of the right column; PDF p. 472.
**Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** The verse is a condensation of Ps. 80:3: the Clementine reads
`Súmite psalmum, et date tympanum; psaltérium iucúndum, cum cíthara`, and
the chant drops the timbrel and the psaltery, attaching `iucúndum` to
`psalmum`. Verified as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1547

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Marcum.` · `Marc. 7, 31-37`.

> In illo témpore: Exiens Iesus de fínibus Tyri, venit per Sidónem ad mare
> Galilǽæ, inter médios fines Decapóleos. Et addúcunt ei surdum et mutum, et
> deprecabántur eum, ut impónat illi manum. Et apprehéndens eum de turba
> seórsum, misit dígitos suos in aurículas eius: et éxspuens, tétigit linguam
> eius: et suspíciens in cælum, ingémuit, et ait illi: Ephphetha, quod est
> adaperíre. Et statim apértæ sunt aures eius, et solútum est vínculum
> linguæ eius, et loquebátur recte. Et præcépit illis, ne cui dícerent.
> Quanto autem eis præcipiébat, tanto magis plus prædicábant: et eo ámplius
> admirabántur, dicéntes: Bene ómnia fecit: et surdos fecit audíre, et mutos
> loqui.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Credo.` (printed run-on, right-aligned at
the end of the Gospel's last line).

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, right column; PDF
p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19, at 600 dpi for `Ephphetha`, `Galilǽæ`, and
`suspíciens`, and at 1200 dpi for the opening line.

**Notes.** The typical edition prints `Exiens` and `finibus` **without**
accent marks, against its own practice for words of three or more syllables
elsewhere on the page (`fúgiant`, `fínes` is two); read twice at 1200 dpi and
recorded as printed. `Ephphetha` — with the second aspirate — is the spelling
of the typical edition, of Benziger, and of the Clementine; the Douay–Rheims
prints `Ephpheta`. The liturgical incipit `In illo témpore:` supplies `Iesus`
and drops the Clementine's opening `Et íterum` — Mark's backward link to the
earlier Tyre episode — so the lesson opens without the evangelist's `again`.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue in the Vatican
typical witness.

## 7. Offertory — marginal no. 1548

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Offertorium` · `Ps. 29, 2-3`
(Vulgate numbering; Hebrew/modern Ps. 30).

> Exaltábo te, Dómine, quóniam suscepísti me, nec delectásti inimícos meos
> super me: Dómine, clamávi ad te, et sanásti me.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, right column, below the
Gospel; PDF p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** The antiphon joins v. 2 whole to v. 3 shorn of its opening
vocative's second half: the Clementine's `Dómine Deus meus, clamávi ad te`
loses `Deus meus`. Verified as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1549

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Réspice, Dómine, quǽsumus, nostram propítius servitútem: ut, quod
> offérimus, sit tibi munus accéptum, et sit nostræ fragilitátis subsídium.
> Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, right column; PDF
p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** Both 1962 witnesses print the short conclusion cue `Per Dóminum.`
here. The Venice Missal of 1570 reads `ad nostram … servitútem` with a
preposition the 1962 text does not carry; that is that edition's own reading,
recorded in the rights section below and not blended into this text.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.` — the Preface
of the Most Holy Trinity, printed with `Ssma` under a tilde abbreviation for
`Sanctissima`. Expanded here from the same book's own abbreviation practice.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1550

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Communionem` · `Prov. 3, 9-10`.

> Honóra Dóminum de tua substántia, et de primítiis frugum tuárum: et
> implebúntur hórrea tua saturitáte, et vino torculária redundábunt.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 391, foot of the right
column; PDF p. 472. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** The antiphon compresses the Clementine: v. 9 loses `ómnium` and the
closing imperative `da ei`, and v. 10 loses the second `tua` — see the
divergence table. Verified as printed.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 10. Postcommunion — marginal no. 1551

**Printed heading:** `Postcommunio`.

> Sentiámus, quǽsumus, Dómine, tui perceptióne sacraménti, subsídium mentis
> et córporis: ut, in utróque salváti, cæléstis remédii plenitúdine
> gloriémur. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 392, head of the left
column; PDF p. 473. **Checked:** 2026-08-19.

**Note.** `Tui … sacraménti` is a split genitive phrase governed by
`perceptióne`; the subject of `sentiámus` is the praying assembly, and
`utróque` resumes `mentis et córporis`. The comma pair `ut, in utróque
salváti,` is the typical edition's; Benziger drops the first comma.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## Appointed text against the Clementine Vulgate

Eleven places where the appointed text is not the Clementine's text were
verified directly against the Clementine chapters read at drbo.org/lvb on
2026-08-19 and are textual observations, not inferences. They are published
in the guide's detailed commentary.

| # | Proper and locus | Appointed | Clementine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Int., Ps. 67:6–7 et 36 | one continuous antiphon | a centonisation of v. 6b, v. 7a and v. 36b, declared by the missal's own triple reference; the verse's `Mirábilis Deus in sanctis suis; Deus Israël` and `benedíctus Deus` are outside it |
| 2 | Int., Ps. 67:7 | `unánimes in domo` | `unius moris in domo` — a different reading, not a variant spelling; the chant preserves an older psalter's text, and every English of the Clementine renders `unius moris` |
| 3 | Ep., 1 Cor. 15:1 | `Fratres: Notum vobis fácio` | `Notum autem vobis fácio, fratres` — the liturgical address moves `fratres` forward and drops `autem` |
| 4 | Ep., 1 Cor. 15:10 | ends `in me vácua non fuit.` | the verse continues `sed abundántius illis ómnibus laborávi: non ego autem, sed grátia Dei mecum` — the lesson ends mid-verse |
| 5 | Grad., Ps. 27:7 | `In Deo sperávit cor meum` | `in ipso sperávit cor meum`, after an opening `Dóminus adjútor meus et protéctor meus` the chant omits |
| 6 | Grad., Ps. 27:7 | `confitébor illi` | `confitébor ei` |
| 7 | Grad., Ps. 27:1 | `clamávi … ne síleas, ne discédas a me` | `clamábo … ne síleas a me: nequándo táceas a me, et assimilábor descendéntibus in lacum` — a different tense, and `ne discédas a me` has no Clementine counterpart |
| 8 | All., Ps. 80:3 | `súmite psalmum iucúndum cum cíthara` | `Súmite psalmum, et date tympanum; psaltérium iucúndum, cum cíthara` — the chant drops timbrel and psaltery and moves `iucúndum` from the psaltery to the psalm |
| 9 | Gosp., Mk. 7:31 | `In illo témpore: Exiens Iesus` | `Et íterum éxiens` — the incipit supplies the subject and drops Mark's `íterum` |
| 10 | Off., Ps. 29:3 | `Dómine, clamávi ad te` | `Dómine Deus meus, clamávi ad te` — the chant drops `Deus meus` |
| 11 | Comm., Prov. 3:9–10 | `de primítiis frugum tuárum … torculária redundábunt` | `de primítiis ómnium frugum tuárum da ei … torculária tua redundábunt` — the antiphon drops `ómnium`, the imperative `da ei`, and the second `tua` |

**Negative results belonging with the table.** The Alleluia's first verse
(Ps. 80:2) and the Offertory's first verse (Ps. 29:2) match the Clementine
word for word, and the Gospel from v. 32 and the Epistle from v. 2 to the cut
in v. 10 likewise match, orthography aside (`eius`/`ejus`, `iubiláte`/
`jubilate`, `adícias`-class contractions).

**One unresolved transcription-level question, not published as a
divergence.** At Mk. 7:34 the missal prints `suspíciens in cælum` (looking
up), as does the Pustet Missal of 1862; the two Clementine transcriptions
consulted (drbo.org/lvb and the sacredbible.org 1914-based text) both print
`suscípiens`. Those two transcriptions may not be independent, and no printed
Clementine facsimile was collated for this verse, so the guide does not claim
a missal–Clementine divergence here; the question is recorded in
`research/scope.md` as an unresolved lead.

## Rights

No public-domain conclusion is claimed for the 1962 typical edition.
`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`,
recorded 2026-08-01, finds the 1962 typical edition's own copyright status
unresolved and more likely than not restored in the United States under
17 U.S.C. §104A to the end of 2057, and finds that the CC BY-NC-ND tag on the
Internet Archive copy of the facsimile confers nothing.

The basis for reproducing the Latin above is 17 U.S.C. §103(b): the copyright
in a revised edition extends only to the material that edition contributed
and "does not affect or enlarge the scope, duration, ownership, or
subsistence of" any copyright in the preexisting material. This formulary is
preexisting material of the 1962 edition, so the 1962 edition's copyright
does not reach it. This is the project's own reading of the statute and of
the evidence, not a clearance anyone granted, and it is not legal advice.

word-level collation. Both witnesses print the older orthography and element
headings (`Introitus`, `Oratio`, `Offertorium`, `Communio`), and neither
carries the 1962 rank or marginal numbering: those are edition-specific
matter and are no part of this finding. Nothing here is quoted from either
witness; they are read as finding aids for the presence of the texts.

The project claims no rights in the received liturgical text and has not
composed, translated, modernised, conflated, or adapted any of it. Both
digitisations used above are third-party artifacts whose own rights status
the source library records as **unresolved**; neither is redistributed here,
and only focused extracts and citations are kept.

Every English rendering of an appointed text comes from one of two registered
public-domain witnesses, named at the point of use:

- **Scriptural elements** — the Douay-Rheims as revised by Bishop Challoner
  (1749–1752), edition
  `edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
  which is the English of the Clementine Vulgate this missal prints. Loci
  quoted: Ps. 67:2, 6–7, 36; 27:1, 7; 80:2–3; 29:2–3; Prov. 3:9–10; Mk.
  7:31–37; 1 Cor. 15:1–10.
- **Orations** — the anonymous English of the 1861 Philadelphia Cummiskey
  *Roman Missal … for the use of the laity*, edition
  `edition.eugene-cummiskey.roman-missal-english-laity.philadelphia-1861`,
  filed by 1962 formulary as `post-pentecosten-11` (printed pp. 418–420)
  and confirmed on the printed incipits `Omnipotens` and `Sentiamus`, the
  Secret being confirmed clause for clause against the appointed Latin.

**Where the registered English does not answer the Latin above.** Declared in
the guide and not filled in by the project.

1. The Introit sings `unánimes`; the Clementine, and therefore the Douay,
   reads `unius moris`, "of one manner." The Douay cannot be quoted for the
   chant's word, and the guide declares the gap instead of filling it.
2. The Introit and both chants of praise are centonisations (Ps. 67:6b + 7a +
   36b; Ps. 80:3 condensed), so the Douay verses are printed whole with the
   sung fragments marked rather than stitched into English sentences the
   project would itself have composed.
3. The Gradual sings `clamávi … ne discédas a me`; the Douay renders the
   Clementine's future `clamábo` ("will I cry") and has no counterpart for
   `ne discédas a me`.
4. The Offertory drops `Deus meus` from Ps. 29:3; the Douay's "O Lord my God"
   renders the words the chant does not sing.
5. The missal prints `Ephphetha`; the Douay prints `Ephpheta`. The difference
   is declared, and neither spelling is altered.
6. The liturgical incipits `Fratres:` and `In illo témpore: Exiens Iesus` are
   the missal's own and are not translated; the Douay's "Now I make known
   unto you, brethren" and "And again going out" carry the words the missal
   moves or drops.
7. The Epistle's English stops where the lesson stops, at "And his grace in
   me hath not been void"; the Douay's v. 10 continues into the labour clause
   the missal cuts, which is described and not printed as appointed text.
