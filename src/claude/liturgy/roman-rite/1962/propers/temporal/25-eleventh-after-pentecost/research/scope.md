# Eleventh Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

## Identity, question, and evidence boundary

This audit governs stable temporal ID `25`, *Dominica Undecima post
Pentecosten*, II classis, in the 1962 *Missale Romanum*, printed pp. 390–392,
marginal nos. 1542–1551. It asks how all ten appointed elements work in their
scriptural contexts and checked reception, without claiming an official
interpretation, a recovered compiler's intent, or exhaustive coverage of all
witnesses.

The controlling Latin is the checksum-matched CMAA facsimile of the Vatican
editio typica; the 1962 Benziger editio iuxta typicam was read independently
as a second edition; the Internet Archive OCR located material and controls
no reading. The complete provenance, the two-edition discrepancy table, the
eleven verified divergences from the Clementine Vulgate, and the rights basis
are in `propers/verified.md`. English: registered Douay–Rheims (Challoner)
verse rows and the 1861 Cummiskey orations, both bound with fingerprints in
`research/source-bindings.toml`.

Evidence classes remain separate: appointed wording and canonical context are
textual observations; dates, transmission, and geography are documented
historical orientation; named exegesis and later liturgical or cultural uses
are documented reception; cross-proper conclusions supported by those classes
are source-grounded synthesis; the five connections in the interpretive
section are expressly exploratory.

Detailed per-element records beside this file:
`orations-and-communion.md`, `psalm-chants.md`, `epistle.md`, `gospel.md`,
`notable.md`.

## Formulary and transmission audit

The page images establish marginal items 1542–1551 in the order Introit,
Collect, Epistle, Gradual, Alleluia, Gospel with Credo, Offertory, Secret
with Trinity Preface, Communion, and Postcommunion. No Tract, Sequence,
second oration, blessing, or ritual text is printed.

All three orations stand together as one mass-set in the Old Gelasian
(Wilson 1894, p. 228, Book III § VII *Item alia Missa*, among unassigned
Sunday masses) and in the Gregorian Hadrianum (Wilson 1915, p. 172) under
**Dominica XII post Pentecosten** — the Gregorian series runs one Sunday
ahead of 1962 through this stretch, and the Frankish Gelasians place the set
at the thirteenth Sunday. The Secret and Postcommunion are older: Veronese
sacramentary, Feltoe p. 142 (in a drought mass, *De siccitate temporis*) and
p. 80 (a July mass), with the variants recorded in
`orations-and-communion.md`. The Collect is **absent from the Veronese book**
(bounded negative, method recorded there); its oldest verified witness is the
Gelasian. The Ottobonianus margins of the Hadrianum cue this Sunday's chant
set against the *previous* Gregorian mass, so the 1962 chant–oration pairing
is a product of later cycle realignment; no compiler's intention is inferred
from it anywhere in the guide. These witnesses establish transmission and
variants; they do not establish personal authorship, composition of the ten
elements as a set, or the reason for the 1962 placement.

The Collect is reused verbatim as the Collect of Dominica XXVII per annum in
the postconciliar Missale Romanum; verified only in secondary Latin
transcriptions (the official 2002 locus remains a lead), and reported in the
guide at that strength.

## Competing historical judgments preserved

- Introit v. 36 reception: resurrection of the body (Hilary, Augustine,
  Bellarmine) against moralization into patience and faith (Cassiodorus) and
  providential strengthening (Theodoret).
- Gradual v. 7: *refloruit* as Resurrection (Augustine through Bellarmine)
  against Theodoret's recovered bodily vigour.
- Alleluia instruments: allegory (Augustine, Cassiodorus) against literal
  festival orchestra (Theodoret, Bellarmine's abridgment).
- The deaf-mute's identity, the sigh, and the silence command: four-way
  patristic disagreement recorded in `gospel.md`.
- *Abortivus*: three genuinely different readings, catalogued to six by a
  Lapide.
- Psalm attributions and dates: the page-2 dossiers distinguish traditional
  attribution from modern judgment; nothing is settled here.
- Sacramentary drift: Gelasian (unassigned) → Frankish Gelasian (13th
  Sunday) → Gregorian (12th) → Tridentine (11th) — reported as transmission,
  not as anyone's design.

## Unresolved leads and displaced qualifications

- **Mk 7:34 *suspiciens*:** the missal and the 1862 Pustet print *suspiciens
  in caelum*; the two Clementine transcriptions consulted (drbo.org/lvb and
  the sacredbible.org 1914-based text) print *suscipiens*. The two
  transcriptions may not be independent, and no printed Clementine facsimile
  was collated for this verse, so the guide does not publish a
  missal–Clementine divergence here. Unresolved lead; a Clementine facsimile
  collation would settle it.
- **MR 2002 official Latin:** the Dominica XXVII reuse of the Collect rests
  on two secondary transcriptions; the editio typica tertia locus remains a
  lead.
- **Hadrianum digitization identity:** the library's registered artifact is
  the Internet Archive DjVuTXT of item `gregoriansacrame00cath`; the research
  pass also read item `gregoriansacrame0049cath`. Both digitize Wilson's 1915
  HBS 49 edition; page references are to Wilson's printed pagination, which
  both carry.
- **Guéranger edition split:** reception quotations were verified in the
  1909 Stanbrook second edition (archive.org item V11TheLiturgicalYear); the
  library's registered edition is the Duffy 1900 printing of volume XI. The
  guide cites the edition actually read.
- **Benziger acquisition boundary** (carried from the previous production
  pass): individual JPEG page images were read from the Internet Archive
  item; acquisition of the exact registered DjVu bytes is deliberately not
  claimed.
- Operational retrieval detail (URLs read, OCR failure modes, tool-access
  failures and workarounds) is kept in the per-element records, not in the
  PDF.

## Production review

### 2026-08-20 — completion, build, page review, installation

The guide was completed from the verified per-element records above:
`00-map`, `10-date-location`, `20-themes`, `30-commentary`,
`32-integrated-commentary`, `35-source-grounded-synthesis`, `40-notable`,
`50-interpretive`, `90-scope`, and `99-references` were written; the two

notable-and-quotable element keys, the Introit psalm verse being one of the

Historical orientation for the page-2 dossiers was taken from two named
witnesses read for this pass: the 1907–1913 *Catholic Encyclopedia* articles
"Psalms," "Gospel of Saint Mark," "Book of Proverbs," "Epistles to the
Corinthians," "Decapolis," "Tyre" and "Sidon"; and the *NABRE* introductions
to the Psalms, Proverbs, Mark and First Corinthians (summarised, never
reproduced). Two claims were **not** made because the sources do not support
them: the *Catholic Encyclopedia*'s article on the Corinthian letters does not
cite 1 Cor. 16:8 (its Ephesus dating rests on Paul's itinerary, and the verse
is cited in the guide as Scripture, not as that article's evidence); and it
gives no date for Proverbs 1–9, ascribing those chapters to Solomon, so the
"chapters 1–9 added in the early postexilic period" framing is reported as the
*NABRE*'s alone and the disagreement is printed.

Both editions were built and reviewed page by page from 150 dpi rasters: the
Full PDF at 34 pages and the Synthesis PDF at 17 pages, both free of overfull
boxes (the one underfull box is the shared rights-notice minipage in
`common/preamble.tex`). `tools/check-proper-components --aux` proves the brief
synthesis at pages 3, 4 and 5 in each edition. Four defects found in review
were corrected and rebuilt: page-1 and page-2 overset (both trimmed to one
page each), three compounds that broke across a line at their own hyphen, a
Greek term quoted inside a Schuster quotation (the term is now named outside
the quotation, since the guide sets no Greek and may not silently alter quoted

Both PDFs were installed byte-identical to the build, and this leaf's web
edition alone was installed from the generated Markdown after reading it
against the installed PDF.
