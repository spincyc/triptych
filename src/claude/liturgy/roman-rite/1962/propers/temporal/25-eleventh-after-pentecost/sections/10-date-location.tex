\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

Seven passages are directly appointed, in four books; composed orations are not
listed, their resemblances to Scripture being echoes rather than quotations.
Each dossier is a summary row, an event row where a passage narrates one, then
a short explanation. Psalm citations give the missal's Vulgate number with the
English-Bible equivalent; the appendix supplies the Masoretic series and the
two witnesses' general judgments on the psalm titles.

\begin{dossiertable}
Gradual & Ps. 27:7, 1 Vulg. (Eng. 28:7, 1) & Superscription \latin{Psalmus ipsi David}. No episode, occasion or place & Traditional: Davidic, \textit{c.}~1000 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{A bare attribution: no occasion and no musical direction, so nothing fixes a place or a stage of David's life. Theodoret's reading of the psalm as David in flight from Saul is exegesis, not a dated setting.}
\midrule
Offertory & Ps. 29:2--3 Vulg. (Eng. 30:1--2) & Superscription \latin{Psalmus cantici in dedicatione domus David}: an occasion named, no place & Traditional: Davidic, \textit{c.}~1000 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The only title of the four naming a dedication, and it drives the psalm's whole reception. Aquinas notes the historical difficulty --- David did not dedicate the Lord's house --- and Theodoret argues that the letter cannot be David's own building.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 67:6--7, 36, with v.~2 Vulg. (Eng. 68:5--6, 35, with v.~1) & Superscription \latin{In finem, psalmus cantici ipsi David}. No occasion or place & Traditional: Davidic, \textit{c.}~1000 BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\latin{In finem} renders a term the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} says is ``now generally interpreted `of the Director','' marking a collection rather than a date or an event; Jerome rendered it \latin{victori}. The psalm is a processional but anchors no episode, and the missal takes three clauses from it.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 80:2--3 Vulg. (Eng. 81:1--2) & Superscription \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus, psalmus ipsi Asaph}. Levitical temple choir & Traditional: Asaph or his family, under the monarchy\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The one appointed psalm not ascribed to David. The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} makes Asaph a Levite, one of the three chiefs of the Levitical choir, and judges it probable that his family composed the psalms later gathered into an Asaph psalter; \latin{pro torcularibus} renders a term that ``may mean that these psalms were to be sung to some vintage-melody.''}
\midrule
Communion & Prov. 3:9--10 & A father's wisdom instruction; no place named. Traditionally Solomon at Jerusalem & Traditional: Solomonic, 10th c.\ BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The two witnesses disagree. The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} (Gigot, 1911) reports Catholic writers ``wellnigh unanimous'' in ascribing chapters i--ix to Solomon and dates the arrangement only to ``either under Ezechias, or as late as Esdras''; the \work{NABRE} makes chapters 1--9 an early postexilic introduction. Neither is preferred here.}
\midrule
Gospel & Mk. 7:31--37 & Writing: Rome, for Roman Christians (\work{Cath.\ Enc.}); the \work{NABRE} adds a possible Syrian setting & AD 50--67 (\work{Cath.\ Enc.}); often after AD 70\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: out of the district of Tyre (Arabic Sour) north through Sidon (Saida), both in present-day Lebanon, then to the Sea of Galilee through the Decapolis --- for the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, which names Mk.\ 7:31 among its three New Testament loci, a region east and south of that lake whose cities lie east of the Jordan but for Scythopolis. Largely Gentile territory, in the Galilean ministry; no year is settled here.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} takes the evangelist to be Peter's interpreter, holds that ``it is certain that the Gospel was written at Rome,'' says ``the date of the Gospel is uncertain,'' and settles for AD 50--67; the \work{NABRE} reports the attribution to John Mark, notes that the book is anonymous, and prefers a date shortly after AD 70.}
\midrule
Epistle & 1 Cor. 15:1--10 & From Ephesus, near present-day Selçuk, Türkiye, to Corinth in Greece & About Easter AD 57 (\work{Cath.\ Enc.}); about AD 56 (\work{NABRE})\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{Pauline authorship is both traditional and the mainstream modern judgment, and the letter names its own writing place at 16:8, ``But I will tarry at Ephesus, until Pentecost.'' The \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} reaches Ephesus by Paul's itinerary instead, and has Titus carry the letter to a church founded about the end of AD 51; chapter 15 answers people there saying there is no resurrection of the dead.}
\end{dossiertable}
