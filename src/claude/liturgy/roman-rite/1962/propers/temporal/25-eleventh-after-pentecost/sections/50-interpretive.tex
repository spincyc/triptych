\section*{The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities}

\begin{studybox}{What this section is}
Everything below is exploratory editorial and AI proposal. None of it is
sourced historical intent, attributed teaching, or documented reception, and
none of it should be quoted as any of those. Each proposal was tested against
the corpus checked for this guide and against a targeted search for its own
distinctive conjunction; the anchors, mechanism, search result and controlling
limit of each are recorded in \texttt{research/scope.md}. ``Not located in the
checked corpus'' is bounded to that corpus and never means that a connection is
unprecedented. Evidence, doctrine, and the literal sense of the appointed texts
govern; where a proposal and a checked witness conflict, the witness wins.
\end{studybox}

\begin{proposal}{The day has two openings, and the second is a harvest}
\pfield{Classification}{Not located in the checked corpus.}
\pfield{Anchors}{Gospel \cue{Gosp.}, \latin{Ephphetha, quod est adaperíre};
Communion \cue{Comm.}, \latin{implebúntur hórrea tua saturitáte, et vino
torculária redundábunt}; Collect \cue{Coll.}, \latin{effúnde super nos
misericórdiam tuam}.}
\pfield{Mechanism}{Three of the day's texts describe a container being opened
or filled past its own capacity, and they run in one direction. The Collect
asks God to \textit{pour out} --- a verb of overflow --- and then to add what
prayer does not presume; the Gospel's one spoken word is an imperative to open;
the Communion ends on presses that run over. Read in sequence, what God opens
does not merely function again but produces more than it can hold. The
formulary would then have a shape rather than a subject: unstopping, then
overflow.}
\pfield{Fruit}{Healing stops being restoration to a norm. The measure of an
opened ear, on this reading, is not that it hears but that what comes out of
the man afterwards exceeds him --- which is exactly what the crowd's disobedient
publishing does at the end of the pericope.}
\pfield{What the ordinary reading misses}{Element by element the Communion is
an antiphon about almsgiving and the Gospel is a healing narrative, and the
Collect's \latin{effúnde} is read as ordinary petitionary idiom. The three
verbs are never set side by side.}
\pfield{Strongest limit}{The chant-cue evidence at the Collect is decisive
against any design claim: the Ottobonianus margins cue this Communion two
masses away from these orations, so the pairing is a late accident of two
independently drifting cycles. St.~Bede's firstfruits-of-grace reading and
Schuster's harvest reading each control the Communion severally, and neither
joins it to the Gospel. And the antiphon's overflow is conditional on a giving
away that the missal's trimming of \latin{da ei} has removed from view.}
\end{proposal}

\begin{proposal}{The loosed tongue is given a psalm to say, four elements later}
\pfield{Classification}{Not located in the checked corpus.}
\pfield{Anchors}{Gospel \cue{Gosp.}, \latin{solútum est vínculum linguæ eius,
et loquebátur recte}; Offertory \cue{Off.}, \latin{clamávi ad te, et sanásti
me}; Epistle \cue{Ep.}, \latin{grátia eius in me vácua non fuit}.}
\pfield{Mechanism}{Mark records that the healed man spoke rightly and never
records a word of it. The Offertory, sung a few minutes later, is the only
proper of the day in the voice of somebody already healed, and it says in the
first person precisely what the Gospel has just narrated in the third: I cried
to thee and thou hast healed me. The Epistle supplies the grammar for such
speech --- a man whose account of himself begins from grace and not from
himself. The proposal is that the liturgy supplies the missing sentence, and
that the supplied sentence is a confession rather than a report.}
\pfield{Fruit}{Right speech is defined by content, not by fluency. The
formulary's answer to ``what does a loosed tongue say?'' would be: it says
where it came from.}
\pfield{What the ordinary reading misses}{Taken alone the Offertory is a
generic thanksgiving assigned to a Sunday, and \latin{loquebátur recte} is a
clinical detail about articulation.}
\pfield{Strongest limit}{This is the proposal that most directly contradicts
the checked tradition, and the contradiction is not marginal. Jerome,
Augustine, Cassiodorus, Aquinas, Theodoret and Bellarmine all read Ps. 29's
healing of the Resurrection, on the strength of the psalm's title; Augustine
asks in so many words who healed a man who was never sick. Assigning the verse
to the deaf-mute reads it against every witness checked. The nearest material
found is Cassiodorus's remark that idols do not heal but wound --- and that
falls in his exposition of this day's Alleluia psalm, not of the Offertory.}
\end{proposal}

\begin{proposal}{Silence asked of God, and silence ended by God}
\pfield{Classification}{Near analogue located.}
\pfield{Anchors}{Gradual \cue{Grad.}, \latin{Deus meus, ne síleas, ne discédas
a me}; Gospel \cue{Gosp.}, the groan and then \latin{Ephphetha}; Alleluia
\cue{All.}, \latin{Exsultáte \dots\ iubiláte \dots\ súmite psalmum}.}
\pfield{Mechanism}{Three consecutive sung and read elements make an
anthropology of sound. The Gradual's versicle asks God not to be silent; the
Alleluia commands a shout; between them the Gospel puts a groan before a word,
and ends with a crowd that cannot be silenced. The day would then move through
four registers of voice in order --- petition that God speak, jubilation,
inarticulate groan, and finally right speech --- and locate the human voice as
something that only exists downstream of a divine one.}
\pfield{Fruit}{Liturgical song acquires an anthropology: the assembly's
jubilus is not self-expression but the audible form of having been opened, and
the groan is given a place inside worship rather than outside it.}
\pfield{What the ordinary reading misses}{Element by element these are a
Gradual, an Alleluia and a miracle story; the sequence of vocal states across
them is invisible unless the three are read as one movement.}
\pfield{Strongest limit}{Two hard checks. The Gradual's \latin{ne discédas a
me} has no patristic lemma at all --- every checked expositor has
\latin{ne síleas} and \latin{nequándo táceas} --- so the closing plea cannot be
given a documented reception. And Augustine assigns the whole of Psalm 27 to
Christ in the Passion, not to a man asking to be spoken to. The near analogue
is real but partial: the seventh-century commentary under Jerome's name says
the opened ears open \latin{ad hymnos, cantica, et psalmos}, joining the
healing to psalmody, and Bede supplies the groan as \latin{exemplum gemendi};
neither joins either to this Gradual.}
\end{proposal}

\begin{proposal}{The shape of the lesson preaches what the lesson does not say}
\pfield{Classification}{Not located in the checked corpus.}
\pfield{Anchors}{Epistle \cue{Ep.}, cut at \latin{et grátia eius in me vácua
non fuit}; Collect \cue{Coll.}, \latin{adícias quod orátio non præsúmit};
Communion \cue{Comm.}, the antiphon's dropped imperative \latin{da ei}.}
\pfield{Mechanism}{Three independent editorial boundaries in one formulary all
fall at the same place --- immediately before the point at which a human
contribution would be named. The lection stops before Paul's labour; the
Collect makes the prayer, not the petitioner, the thing that does not presume;
the antiphon removes the verb of donation and leaves the filling. None of the
three is a feature of its source text: each is something the liturgy did to it.
The proposal is that the formulary preaches prevenient grace by its cuts rather
than by its assertions.}
\pfield{Fruit}{An account of how a lectionary teaches. What is not read is part
of what is heard, and a reader who has noticed the pattern will read the day's
silences as deliberate rather than arbitrary.}
\pfield{What the ordinary reading misses}{Each boundary looks like a separate
accident of chant construction or lectionary convenience, and the Epistle's
mid-verse ending in particular is usually noted, if at all, as a curiosity.}
\pfield{Strongest limit}{The boundaries are received, not designed here: the
same Epistle ending and the same trimmed antiphon stand in the Missals of 1570
and 1862, and no source states a motive for either. More seriously, the
tradition whose key clause is cut --- Augustine, Gregory, Aquinas --- teaches
human cooperation with grace on the strength of exactly those unread words. The
pattern cannot be pressed into a denial of what they teach, and this guide does
not press it.}
\end{proposal}

\begin{proposal}{A green Sunday that sings a small Easter, with nobody's intention behind it}
\pfield{Classification}{Near analogue located.}
\pfield{Anchors}{Introit \cue{Int.}, \latin{ipse dabit virtútem et
fortitúdinem plebi suæ}; Gradual \cue{Grad.}, \latin{reflóruit caro mea};
Offertory \cue{Off.}, the title \latin{in dedicatione domus David} and
\latin{sanásti me}; Epistle \cue{Ep.}, the kerygma of 1 Cor. 15.}
\pfield{Mechanism}{Four elements of this Mass have, independently, a
resurrection reading in the checked tradition --- and the four readings were
made by different men in different centuries, none of them thinking about this
Sunday. Set the received expositions side by side and an arc appears across the
formulary: the risen body promised (Introit), the flesh flowering again
(Gradual), the house dedicated in the Resurrection (Offertory), and the event
itself confessed in the lesson. The proposal is that a green Sunday in late
summer carries a compact paschal formulary, and that it does so as a
\textit{reception effect} rather than as a composition.}
\pfield{Fruit}{A way of describing liturgical coherence that does not require a
compiler. Texts assembled for other reasons can converge because the tradition
that reads them converges, and this Mass is a clean specimen of the phenomenon.}
\pfield{What the ordinary reading misses}{Element by element these are a
processional psalm, a thanksgiving, an offertory chant and an epistle. Only the
reception makes them one thing, and the reception is not on the missal page.}
\pfield{Strongest limit}{The arc is not a property of the texts, and two
checked witnesses break it: Cassiodorus moralises the Introit's last clause
into patience and faith with no resurrection at all, and Theodoret reads the
Gradual's \latin{reflóruit} of a hunted man's recovered bodily vigour. Against
design, the chant-cue drift recorded at the Collect shows these chants and
these orations were not paired when either was composed. And the convergence is
partly an artefact of which witnesses survive: it is strongest in the Latin
line that runs from Augustine through Cassiodorus to Aquinas.}
\end{proposal}
