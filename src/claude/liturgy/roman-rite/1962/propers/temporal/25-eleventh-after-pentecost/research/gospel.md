# Gospel record — Mark 7:31–37 (Ephphetha; *Bene omnia fecit*)

Element key: `gospel`. Companion to `scope.md`. All witnesses read in public
sources; Latin from pre-1929 editions, quotable English from named
public-domain translations only.

## Canonical context controls

The pericope follows the Syrophoenician woman (7:24–30) and precedes the
feeding of the four thousand (8:1–9); the itinerary Tyre → Sidon → Sea of
Galilee → Decapolis keeps Jesus in largely Gentile territory throughout. Mark
7:31–37 has no true synoptic parallel — Matt 15:29–31 is a summary notice of
many healings on the mountain with no deaf-mute individual, no
fingers/spittle/sigh, and no Ephphatha — which is why the scene surfaces in
Augustine's *De consensu evangelistarum* Book IV (his sweep of Mark-only
material), not in Books II–III. The 1962 incipit drops Mark's opening *Et
iterum* (see `propers/verified.md`, divergence 9), the evangelist's backward
link to the earlier Tyre episode.

## Direct exegesis

| Bede, *In Marci evangelium expositio* II, on 7:31–37 | PL 92, cols. 203–206 (the work spans PL 92, 131–302; modern ed. ## Liturgical reception — the baptismal Ephphetha

- **Ambrose, De mysteriis 1.3–4** (NPNF 2/10, de Romestin, 1896, PD):
  "celebrating the mystery of the opening, we said, Epphatha, which is, Be
  opened… Christ made use of this mystery in the Gospel, as we read, when He
  healed him who was deaf and dumb." Earliest witness (c. 390, Milan) that
  the pericope generated a baptismal ceremony; independent of and earlier
  than the whole Latin exegetical chain except Victor's sources.
- **De sacramentis I.1.2–3** (tr. T. Thompson, ed. Srawley, SPCK 1919, PD;
  the 1919 title reflects then-current doubt about authenticity, now generally
  resolved in Ambrose's favour): "What did we do on the Saturday? What but
  'the opening'? … the priest touched thine ears and nostrils… It is a Hebrew
  word, which rendered into Latin is adaperire, that is, Be opened," with the
  transfer from mouth to nostrils explained on grounds of propriety toward
  women. The Latin was not independently read (image-only scan); section
  numbers are Thompson/Srawley's.
- **Rituale Romanum** (Pustet, Ratisbon/New York, 1872), Ordo baptismi
  parvulorum, printed pp. 11–12: *Postea Sacerdos digito accipiat de saliva
  oris sui, et tangat aures et nares infantis: tangendo vero aurem dexteram,
  et sinistram, dicat: **Ephpheta, quod est, Adaperire**: deinde tangit nares,
  dicens: In odorem suavitatis. Tu autem effugare, diabole; appropinquabit
  enim judicium Dei.* Repeated in the Ordo baptismi adultorum, p. 33
  (*pollice accipit de saliva*). Saliva and ears directly imitate the Gospel
  gestures. Spelling note: the Rituale prints **Ephpheta**; the Clementine at
  Mark 7:34, the 1962 Missal, the Catena, and PG 123's Latin print
  **Ephphetha**; Bede's lemma has **Effeta** — one word, three orthographies
  across the tradition.

## Disagreements and developments

- **Who is the deaf-mute?** Ps-Jerome: the human race in Adam, healed as the
  Gospel passes from Judaea to the Gentiles. Bede: any individual soul that
  neither hears nor confesses, brought by the already-catechized. Theophylact:
  a demoniac's residue, historically and morally read. Victor/"Chrysostomus":
  Adamic human nature restored in Christ's own perfect humanity.
- **The sigh:** Bede — pure *exemplum gemendi* (an anti-subordinationist
  guard: Christ needs nothing). Victor — Christ "taking our cause upon
  himself." Theophylact — both prayer and pity, openly hedged. Ps-Jerome —
  *gemere nos docuit*. A Lapide — sympathy plus genuinely impetratory prayer.
  Augustine never touches the sigh.
- **The silence command:** Augustine poses and answers the prescience problem
  (a spur to lazy preachers); Bede transcribes Augustine; Victor adds a
  prudential motive (not to inflame envy *ante tempus*); Theophylact converts
  it into an ethics of benefaction; a Lapide alone denies it was a
  *praeceptum* at all.
- **Lines of dependence:** ps-Jerome (7th c.) → Bede (c. 720s, who also
  embeds Augustine verbatim) → Catena aurea (Theophylact + Victor + Bede +
  ps-Jerome + Augustine + an untraced Glossa) → a Lapide (re-cites Bede,
  Theophylact, Augustine; adds philology). **Gregory the Great is absent from
  every link of this chain.** Ambrose's liturgical line runs independently
  into the 1962 Rituale.

## Material negative results

1. **Gregory the Great — corpus-wide bounded negative.** The complete OCR of
   PL 75 and PL 76 (Moralia I–XXXV, Homiliae in Ezechielem, XL Homiliae in
   Evangelia with indices) was searched case-insensitively and
   OCR-tolerantly for *ephphetha/ephpheta/effetha*, *surdum et mutum*,
   *surdus et mutus*, *surdo et muto*, plus review of every "Marc." citation
   in both volumes (66 in PL 76): no exposition of Mark 7:31–37 anywhere; no
   Gospel homily on the pericope (the XL Homiliae index lists all forty; the
   near hit "ingemuit (Marc. vi…)" is Herod and the Baptist). Corroboration:
   the Catena aurea gathers no Gregory for this pericope, and Bede here
   borrows from Augustine. Bounds: OCR-based; PL 77 (Regula pastoralis,
   Dialogi, Registrum) not searched.
2. **Authentic Jerome:** the *Tractatus in Marci evangelium* (Morin, Anecd.
   Mareds. III/2) covers Mark 1:1–12; 1:13–31; 5:30–43; 8:1–9; 8:22–26;
   9:1–7; 11:1–10; 11:11–14; 11:15–17; 13:32–33 with 14:3–6 — the series
   jumps from 5:30–43 to 8:1–9; **no tractate on 7:31–37**.
3. **Augustine, Sermones:** the NPNF selection's table of contents shows
   sermons on Mark 8:5, 8:34, 13:32 only; none on 7:31–37. Full PL 38–39 not
   searched.
4. **Chrysostom, Hom. in Matt. 52** (on Matt 15:21–31; NPNF 1/10, PD):
   reaches the mountain summary but has no deaf-mute individual, no
   spittle/Ephphatha, no reference to Mark.
5. **Theophylact in English:** no public-domain English of the Mark
   commentary exists (Stade 1993 is copyrighted); Migne's Latin quoted.
6. **Catena aurea "Glossa"** on v. 37: flagged "non occ." by the 1842 Oxford
   editors — not found in the extant Glossa ordinaria.
7. **A Lapide Latin:** archive.org Latin scans lack usable OCR; content
   verified through Mossman's PD English.
