\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & Mardochai, under sentence,
confesses that no one can resist God's will. Satan asks to tempt Job and is given
power over his goods and his flesh, and no further. St Paul arms the Ephesians
against spirits of wickedness, not flesh and blood. A forgiven servant who will
not forgive is handed to the torturers; the psalmist asks when God will judge his
persecutors.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The armour of God is
Christ (Jerome); Job bears the type of the Redeemer with his Body (Gregory). The
king is God (Thomas), and the ten thousand talents are the sins forgiven in
baptism (Augustine). The Exodus is the Church's going out of the world, led by
Jesus, with Christ for its Passover (Chrysostom); the Communion's plea is the
martyrs' cry under the altar (Augustine).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Do not mistake the brother for the
enemy; pour the wrath on the devil alone (Chrysostom). Take each trial as
measured by the hand that bridles the tempter (Gregory). Forgive every debt from
the heart and not with the lips (Chrysostom; Augustine), correcting without
rancour (Augustine). Live as on a march (Chrysostom), and forgive while God's
patience waits for the conversion of enemies (Augustine).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The king's reckoning,
begun in tribulation, is completed at the judgment (Thomas), where the debtor who
would not forgive remains a debtor for ever (Chrysostom). The adversary, bridled
now, is judged at the resurrection (Hilary). The last exodus hastens to the true
land of promise (Fromage), fed on the food of immortality toward the life
everlasting that Bellarmine hears the singer desire.\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
