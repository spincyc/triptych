## The appointed texts

Latin as printed in the 1962 typical edition, with its accents, ligatures and
pointing. `℣.` renders the printed versicle sign, `℟.` the printed response
sign and `✠` the printed cross. The drop capital of each element is joined to
its word; a drop capital prints no accent. The *fi* ligature likewise prints
no acute, and the words set with it are transcribed without one: Epistle
*fidei*, Gradual *fierent*, Gospel and Offertory *filios*, no. 1806 *fieri*,
no. 1808 *proficiat* (read at 600 dpi on PDF pp. 495, 496 and 508 in research
iteration 1).

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — no. 1699, printed p. 413

*Antiphona ad Introitum* — *Esth. 13, 9 et 10-11*

> In voluntáte tua, Dómine, univérsa sunt pósita, et non est qui possit
> resístere voluntáti tuæ: tu enim fecísti ómnia, cælum et terram, et
> univérsa quæ cæli ámbitu continéntur: Dóminus universórum tu es.
> *Ps. 118, 1* Beáti immaculáti in via: qui ámbulant in lege Dómini.
> ℣. Glória Patri.

The antiphon is not a continuous Vulgate text; see *Relation to the
Clementine Vulgate* below. The psalm citation stands inside the text line,
after *tu es.*

*Oratio*

### 6. Gospel (`gospel`) — no. 1704, printed p. 414

✠ *Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthǽum.* — *Mt. 18, 23-35*

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis parábolam hanc: Assimilátum
> est regnum cælórum hómini regi, qui vóluit ratiónem pónere cum servis suis.
> Et cum cœpísset ratiónem pónere, oblátus est ei unus, qui debébat ei decem

> venúmdari, et uxórem eius, et filios, et ómnia quæ habébat, et reddi.
> Prócidens autem servus ille, orábat eum, dicens: Patiéntiam habe in me, et
> ómnia reddam tibi. Misértus autem dóminus servi illíus, dimísit eum, et

These are not part of the formulary. `research/context.md` resolves when they

445–446, 453); the commemoration of St Luke follows under a separate conclusion
in a low or conventual Mass only (RG 108, 111; RGMR 434, 437 a).

## Relation to the Clementine Vulgate

Textual observations about wording and boundary; no origin is asserted for any
chant's Latin.

- **Introit.** Mardochai's prayer in the Clementine, Esth 13:9–11, reads *et
  dixit: Domine, Domine rex omnipotens, in ditione enim tua cuncta sunt
  posita, et non est qui possit tuæ resistere voluntati, si decreveris salvare
  Israël. Tu fecisti cælum et terram, et quidquid cæli ambitu continetur.
  Dominus omnium es, nec est qui resistat majestati tuæ.* The Missal reads *In
  voluntate tua* for *in ditione enim tua*, *universa* for *cuncta*,
  *resistere voluntati tuæ* in another order, adds *enim … omnia* (*tu enim
  fecisti omnia*), reads *universa quæ … continentur* for *quidquid …
  continetur* and *Dominus universorum tu es* for *Dominus omnium es*, omits
  the vocative *Domine, Domine rex omnipotens*, the condition *si decreveris
  salvare Israël* and the closing *nec est qui resistat majestati tuæ*. The
  verse agrees with Ps 118:1 apart from the Clementine's title *Alleluja* and
  letter heading *Aleph*, which it counts inside v. 1. In the Clementine the
  prayer belongs to the chapters that follow the Hebrew book's end (Esth
  10:4–16:24), introduced by the colophon of Esth 11:1 (*Anno quarto
  regnantibus Ptolemæo et Cleopatra …*); its own close, Esth 13:16–17, asks
  God not to despise the portion *quam redemisti tibi de Ægypto* and to let
  the people live *ut viventes laudemus nomen tuum*.
- **Epistle.** The Missal reads *Fratres: Confortamini* where the Clementine
  opens v. 10 *De cetero, fratres, confortamini*, and spells *exstinguere* for
  *extinguere*. Every other word of vv. 10–17 agrees. The Clementine continues
  at vv. 18–20 with prayer *in spiritu* and Paul's request for himself, *pro
  quo legatione fungor in catena*.
- **Gradual.** The Missal reads *a generatione et progenie* where the
  Clementine at Ps 89:1 reads *a generatione in generationem*, and omits the
  title *Oratio Moysi, hominis Dei*. The verse agrees. Augustine's lemma in
  the tracked Latin of *Enarr. in Ps.* 89.2 reads *refugium factus es nobis in
  generatione et generatione* (a third form); it is reported, not adopted as a
  source.
- **Alleluia.** The verse agrees with Ps 113:1, without the Clementine's title
  *Alleluja*.
- **Gospel.** The liturgical opening *In illo tempore: Dixit Iesus discipulis
  suis parabolam hanc:* stands for the Clementine's *Ideo* at the head of
  v. 23, so the appointed text omits Peter's question and the answer *usque
  septuagies septies* (vv. 21–22) to which *Ideo* refers. The Missal reads
  *suffocabat* where the Clementine at v. 28 reads *suffocavit*, and spells
  *milia* and *venumdari* for *millia* and *venundari*. Every other word of
  vv. 23–35 agrees.
- **Offertory.** The Missal cites only *Iob 1*. Its first clause agrees with
  Job 1:1 in shortened form (the Clementine reads *Vir erat in terra Hus,
  nomine Job: et erat vir ille simplex, et rectus, ac timens Deum, et recedens
  a malo*); the rest narrates the testing in words that are not the
  Clementine's: *quem Satan petiit, ut tentaret* has no verbal counterpart;
  *data est ei potestas … in facultates et in carnem eius* summarises Job 1:12
  and 2:6; *perdiditque omnem substantiam ipsius et filios* summarises Job
  1:13–19; *carnem quoque eius gravi ulcere vulneravit* stands for Job 2:7,
  *percussit Job ulcere pessimo*. The finding aid, and therefore the chronology
  record, resolve the element to Job 1:1, 1:12 and 2:6–7.
- **Communion.** The Missal reads *In salutari tuo* for the Clementine's
  *Defecit in salutare tuum* (v. 81), *speravi* for *supersperavi*, *iniqui*
  for *inique* (v. 86), omits *Quot sunt dies servi tui?* (v. 84) and *Omnia
  mandata tua veritas* (v. 86), and adds *Domine Deus meus* after *adiuva me*.
  It omits the letter heading *Caph*. PL 9 prints, in the Caph text set at
  the head of Hilary's Littera XI (col. 571D), *iniqui* with the editor's note
  *Hil. inique*; Hilary's commentary gives at v. 81 the lemma *Defecit in
  salutare tuum anima mea, et in verbum tuum spero* (col. 572A) and at v. 84
  *quando facies mihi de persequentibus me judicium?*, the *mihi* supplied by
  the editors (col. 573C, note 3). These are textual observations about other
  witnesses, not a source for the chant.
