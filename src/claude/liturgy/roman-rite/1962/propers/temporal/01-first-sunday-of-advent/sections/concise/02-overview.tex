\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & A man mocked by enemies
lifts his soul to God and trusts that none who wait for God are put to shame;
Paul tells the Romans it is the hour to rise, because salvation is nearer;
Psalm 84 asks God for mercy and salvation and promises the land its fruit;
Christ foretells signs and his coming, and tells the disciples to lift their
heads.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & Christ, who came in
secret (Cyril of Alexandria) and in a humility that was not heard (Gregory),
comes in power and majesty. The failing lights figure the Church in
persecution, when many fall away (Ambrose; Augustine); for Ambrose the sun is
Christ, for Augustine the Church is sun, moon and stars. The salvation is
Christ (Augustine; Bellarmine), given as man, to be given as God (Augustine);
the fruitful earth is the Virgin (Rupert; Bellarmine).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Do not love what cannot stand,
and fear the judge now (Gregory); keep faith (Ambrose) and watch daily
(Augustine); rise from sin by penance and from negligence by good works
(Aquinas), the more as the goal nears (Chrysostom).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The dead rise
incorrupt and creation is renewed (Cyril of Alexandria); the clouds of
mourning pass and the eternal sun shines (Gregory); the generation of believers
outlasts every trial (Chrysostom); we shall see Christ as he is (Augustine,
with 1~Jn 3:2).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
