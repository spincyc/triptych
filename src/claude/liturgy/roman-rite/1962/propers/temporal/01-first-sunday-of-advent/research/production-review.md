**What the text changes.** The governing question is the Gospel's own: the
same signs make men wither "for fear and expectation" and are the moment for

mundus, cui amici non estis, prope fit redemptio quam quaesistis* (I.1.3),
which puts the difference in what each loves, with the Gradual's *exspéctant*
beside the Gospel's *exspectatióne*, one verb for two waitings that differ in
their object; and then the Epistle's command beside Augustine's "presently",
answered by the Collect's request that Christ come and set us free
(*te liberánte salvári*). Fear of the end is understood as a question of
love, and the hearer who knows he loves the world is directed to a petition
the Mass has already made. The speech states this as what the texts say, in
the preacher's voice. With unrelated readings the argument does not survive:
each step rests on Lk 21:25-31, Rom 13:11-14a, Ps 24:1-3, Ps 84:8, the
Collect or the Secret.
