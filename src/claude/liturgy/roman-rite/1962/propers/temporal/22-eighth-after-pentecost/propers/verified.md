## 1. Introit (marginal no. 1512)

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` — `Ps. 47, 10-11`
(modern 48:10–11), with the psalm verse marked `Ps. ibid., 2` (modern
48:2).

> Suscépimus, Deus, misericórdiam tuam in médio templi tui: secúndum
> nomen tuum, Deus, ita et laus tua in fines terræ: iustítia plena est
> déxtera tua. *Ps. ibid., 2* Magnus Dóminus, et laudábilis nimis: in
> civitáte Dei nostri, in monte sancto eius. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387, left column (PDF p. 468).
**Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Collation against the Clementine Vulgate.** Clementine Ps 47:11 reads
`Secundum nomen tuum, Deus, **sic** et laus tua in fines terrae`; the
Introit reads `**ita**`. Clementine Ps 47:2 reads `laudabilis **nimis**`,
which the Introit's psalm verse follows. Both differences are recorded,
not harmonized.

**Internal cross-reference verified in the same book.** The identical
Introit, with the identical psalm verse and reference, is printed at
marginal no. 2107 for `IN PURIFICATIONE B. MARIÆ VIRG.` (2 February),
printed p. 467 (PDF p. 548) — collated as an image 2026-07-25.

**Unresolved:** none.

## 2. Collect (marginal no. 1513)

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`. Composed oration; no scriptural reference
is printed.

> Largíre nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine, semper spíritum cogitándi quæ recta
> sunt, propítius et agéndi: ut, qui sine te esse non póssumus, secúndum
> te vívere valeámus. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387, left column (PDF p. 468).
**Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Note on the printed word order.** The adverb `propítius` stands between
the two gerunds (`cogitándi quæ recta sunt, propítius et agéndi`), not
before them. The 1861 English witness relocates it silently; the Latin as
printed is preserved here without emendation.

**Unresolved:** none.

## 3. Epistle (marginal no. 1514)

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Romános.` — `Rom. 8, 12-17`.

> Fratres: Debitóres sumus non carni, ut secúndum carnem vivámus. Si enim
> secúndum carnem vixéritis, moriémini: si autem spíritu facta carnis
> mortificavéritis, vivétis. Quicúmque enim spíritu Dei agúntur, ii sunt
> fílii Dei. Non enim accepístis spíritum servitútis íterum in timóre, sed
> accepístis spíritum adoptiónis filiórum, in quo clamámus: Abba (Pater).
> Ipse enim Spíritus testimónium reddit spirítui nostro, quod sumus fílii
> Dei. Si autem fílii, et herédes: herédes quidem Dei, coherédes autem
> Christi.

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387, left and right columns
(PDF p. 468). **Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Pericope boundary.** The printed lection **stops inside verse 17**,
after `coherédes autem Christi`. The remainder of the Clementine verse,
`si tamen compatimur, ut et conglorificemur`, is **not** printed here.
This is a boundary observation about the appointed text, not a claim about
the biblical verse.

**Collation against the Clementine Vulgate.** Clementine Rom 8:12 opens
`Ergo fratres, debitores sumus…`; the missal replaces the connective with
the liturgical incipit `Fratres:`. Clementine Rom 8:14 reads
`Quicumque enim Spiritu Dei aguntur, **hi filii Dei sunt**`; the missal
reads `**ii sunt fílii Dei**`. Clementine spells `haeredes` /
`cohaeredes`; the missal spells `herédes` / `coherédes`. The missal
capitalizes `Spíritus` at verse 16 and leaves `spíritu` lower case at
verses 13 and 14.

**Unresolved:** none.

## 4. Gradual (marginal no. 1515)

**Printed heading and references:** `Graduale` — `Ps. 30, 3` (modern 31:3)
for the responsory; the verse carries its own printed reference
`℣. Ps. 70, 1` (modern 71:1).

> Esto mihi in Deum protectórem, et in locum refúgii, ut salvum me fácias.
> ℣. *Ps. 70, 1* Deus, in te sperávi: Dómine, non confúndar in ætérnum.

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387, right column (PDF p. 468).
**Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Collation against the Clementine Vulgate.** Clementine Ps 30:3 reads
`Esto mihi in Deum protectorem, et in **domum** refugii, ut salvum me
facias`. Clementine Ps 70:3 reads `Esto mihi in Deum protectorem, et in
**locum munitum**, ut salvum me facias`. The Gradual's responsory, printed
under the reference `Ps. 30, 3`, reads `in **locum refúgii**` — the noun
of Ps 70:3 with the genitive of Ps 30:3. Recorded as a discrepancy between
the appointed chant text and the Clementine psalter, not harmonized.

Clementine Ps 70:1 reads `In te, Domine, speravi; non confundar in
aeternum`; the Gradual verse reads `Deus, in te sperávi: Dómine, non
confúndar in ætérnum`, with `Deus` supplied and `Dómine` displaced.

**Adjacent-formulary observation (verified, not part of this formulary).**
The Communion of the Seventh Sunday, marginal no. 1510 on the same printed
page 387, is also referenced `Ps. 30, 3` and reads `Inclína aurem tuam,
accélera, ut erípias me` — where Clementine Ps 30:3 reads `accelera ut
**eruas** me`.

**Unresolved:** none.

## 5. Alleluia (marginal no. 1516)

**Printed reference:** `℣. Ps. 47, 2` (modern 48:2). No Tract is printed.

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 47, 2* Magnus Dóminus, et laudábilis valde:
> in civitáte Dei nostri, in monte sancto eius. Allelúia.

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387, right column (PDF p. 468).
**Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Discrepancy recorded, not harmonized.** Psalm 47:2 is printed **twice in
this one formulary in two different Latin forms**: the Introit's psalm
verse (no. 1512) reads `laudábilis **nimis**` and the Alleluia verse (no.
1516) reads `laudábilis **valde**`. The Clementine reading is `nimis`. The
1962 book prints both without note. Both are transcribed exactly as
printed. The 1861 hand missal (p. 412) likewise prints `nimis` in the
Introit and `valde` in the Alleluia, so the divergence is transmitted, not
a 1962 setting error; no claim is made here about which Latin psalter each
form descends from, because that was not verified at a psalter witness.

**Unresolved:** the version-history of the `valde` reading. Recorded as an
open question in `../research/scope.md`.

## 6. Gospel (marginal no. 1517)

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Lucam.` — `Luc. 16, 1-9`.

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis parábolam hanc: Homo
> quidam erat dives, qui habébat víllicum: et hic diffamátus est apud
> illum, quasi dissipásset bona ipsíus. Et vocávit illum, et ait illi:
> Quid hoc áudio de te? redde ratiónem villicatiónis tuæ: iam enim non
> póteris villicáre. Ait autem víllicus intra se: Quid fáciam, quia
> dóminus meus aufert a me villicatiónem? fódere non váleo, mendicáre
> erubésco. Scio quid fáciam, ut, cum amótus fúero a villicatióne,
> recípiant me in domos suas. Convocátis ítaque síngulis debitóribus
> dómini sui, dicébat primo: Quantum debes dómino meo? At ille dixit:
> Centum cados ólei. Dixítque illi: Accipe cautiónem tuam: et sede cito,
> scribe quinquagínta. Deínde álii dixit: Tu vero quantum debes? Qui ait:
> Centum coros trítici. Ait illi: Accipe lítteras tuas, et scribe
> octogínta. Et laudávit dóminus víllicum iniquitátis, quia prudénter
> fecísset: quia fílii huius sǽculi prudentióres fíliis lucis in
> generatióne sua sunt. Et ego vobis dico: fácite vobis amícos de mammóna
> iniquitátis: ut, cum defecéritis, recípiant vos in ætérna tabernácula.

**Printed after the Gospel:** `Credo.`

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 387 right column and p. 388 left
column (PDF pp. 468–469). **Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Pericope boundary.** The lection stops at verse 9. Luke 16:10–13 —
including `Non potestis Deo servire et mammonae` — is **not** appointed
here.

**Collation against the Clementine Vulgate.** Clementine Luke 16:1 opens
`Dicebat autem et ad discipulos suos`; the missal replaces this with the
liturgical incipit `In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis parábolam
hanc`. Clementine 16:3 punctuates `Quid faciam? quia dominus meus aufert a
me villicationem. Fodere non valeo, mendicare erubesco.`; the missal
punctuates `Quid fáciam, quia dóminus meus aufert a me villicatiónem?
fódere non váleo, mendicáre erubésco.` — the same words, a different
sense-division. Clementine spells `hujus saeculi`, `mammona`; the missal
spells `huius sǽculi`, `mammóna`.

**Unresolved:** none.

## 7. Offertory (marginal no. 1518)

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Offertorium` — `Ps. 17, 28 et
32` (modern 18:28, 32).

> Pópulum húmilem salvum fácies, Dómine, et óculos superbórum humiliábis:
> quóniam quis Deus præter te, Dómine?

**Witness:** CMAA facsimile, printed p. 388, left column (PDF p. 469).
**Checked:** 2026-07-25.

**Collation against the Clementine Vulgate.** Clementine Ps 17:28 reads
`Quoniam **tu** populum humilem salvum facies, et oculos superborum
humiliabis`; the Offertory drops `Quoniam tu` and inserts a vocative
`Dómine`. Clementine Ps 17:32 reads `Quoniam quis Deus praeter
**Dominum**? aut quis Deus praeter Deum nostrum?`; the Offertory reads
`quóniam quis Deus **præter te, Dómine**?` — third person turned into
direct address, and the second half of the verse dropped. Recorded as
printed.

**Unresolved:** none.

**Editorial note on this witness.** The 1861 English is a translation of the
appointed liturgical wording, not of the Clementine psalter, and it shows
that fact: at the Gradual it renders `in locum refúgii` as "a place of

Gospel and Epistle follow the Douay–Rheims tradition closely but not
identically (`A hundred barrels of oil` for Douay `An hundred barrels of
oil`). Its Collect silently moves `propítius` ("mercifully") out of the
sentence. These are the witness's own decisions and are reported, not
corrected.
