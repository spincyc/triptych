# Psalm-chant element record — Vulgate Pss 69, 33, 87, 103

Per-element record for the four psalm chants: Introit (Ps 69:2–3 with v. 4),
Gradual (Ps 33:2–3), Alleluia (Ps 87:2), Communion (Ps 103:13–15). Only the
appointed verses are in scope. Copyrighted translations — Walsh's ACW
Cassiodorus, Way's FC 46 Basil, Hill's Theodoret — were **not used**; Latin and
Greek were taken from the Migne scans directly, so nothing needed summarising
from them.

## I. Introit — Ps 69:2–4

**Superscription (Clementine):** `In finem. Psalmus David in rememorationem,
quod salvum fecerit eum Dominus.`

**Textual observation.** The Missal reads `confundantur et revereantur
**inimici mei**, qui quaerunt animam meam` and `qui **cogitant** mihi mala`;
Clementine Ps 69:3–4 has neither `inimici mei` nor `cogitant` (`qui volunt mihi
mala`). Cassiodorus's psalter lemma carries **both** chant readings; Augustine's
lemma at v. 4 reads `qui cogitant mihi mala`. The chant preserves the old Latin
psalter the Fathers expounded. Derivation from a named *Psalterium Romanum*
witness is **not** asserted: none was collated.

**The Ps 39/69 doublet.** Ps 69 ≈ Ps 39:14–18 nearly word for word. Who notes
it: **Bellarmine** explicitly ("The remainder of the Psalm is almost word for
word with Ps. 39, which see"); **Cassiodorus** at verse level, with a division
of labour — in Ps 39 the words are Christ's in his own person, here his
members'. **Bounded negative: Augustine nowhere notes it**, verified against
the full Latin of both *Enarrationes* and both New Advent pages; Theodoret's
Ps 69 columns contain no such remark either.

| Witness | Locus | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Augustine | *En. in Ps.* 69, §§1–4, 7 (one sermon; NPNF 1/8 "Psalm 70") | the whole Christ, Head and members; `opus enim habemus sempiterno adiutorio`; the historical line drawn through `cogitant`; `help I seek, always mine infirmity I confess` |
| Cassiodorus | *Expositio* 69 (PL 70, coll. 493–494 — the running head numerals actually seen) | `ex persona martyrum`; the gloss on `intende` / `festina`; and the citation of Cassian by name and chapter for the `trina iteratio` |
| Cassian | *Conf.* X.10 (NPNF 2/11) | the verse as the formula of unceasing prayer, "fitly and satisfactorily adapted to every condition, and all assaults" |
| Rule of St Benedict | cc. 17, 18, 35 | the verse legislated at the head of every Hour, and said three times by the entering kitchen server |
| Theodoret | *Interpretatio* 69 (PG 80, coll. 1415–1418, seen) | Antiochene setting: David fleeing Absalom, stung more by the memory of his sin than by his enemies; Migne's apparatus notes the second clause absent from the LXX's two great codices but present in the Hebrew, Apollinaris and Augustine |
| Bellarmine | O'Sullivan 1866, artifact p. 220 | "all the divine offices commence with it"; the doublet |

## II. Gradual — Ps 33:2–3

**Superscription (Clementine, drbo):** `David, cum immutavit vultum suum coram
Achimelech, et dimisit eum, et abiit.` (Edition variance flagged: many
Clementine printings read `Davidi`; not collated against a second edition.)
Verses 2–3 match the Gradual exactly.

**The Achimelech-vs-Achis puzzle — four documented solutions:**

1. **Basil** (PG 29, *Hom. in Ps. 33* §1): "Abimelech" is the common dynastic
   title of Philistine kings, as "Pharaoh" in Egypt; "Anchus" the private
   birth-name. He also allows the title to fit both episodes.
2. **Theodoret** (PG 80, 1101–1104): Abimelech = Abiathar, `ὡς διώνυμον`, as
   Mark 2:26 also names him; he reports and rejects the Geth setting as not
   fitting the psalm's words.
3. **Augustine** (*En. in Ps.* 33, s. 1, §§1–4): the histories nowhere record
   it under this name — `sed non invenimus hoc, et tamen aliquid invenimus unde
   appareat hoc esse tractum`; `Mutatio nominis sacramentum est`; Abimelech =
   `regnum patris mei`.
4. **Cassiodorus**: follows Augustine.

**Bounded negative:** the Catholic Encyclopedia's "Psalms" (1911) does not
treat this title or the name problem. The NABRE note was unreachable in the
lane's bounds; no modern judgment on the title is printed as verified.

**The Vetus Latina title-plus.** Augustine's psalter title continued `et
ferebatur in manibus suis`, which is in neither the Clementine title nor the
Douay (verified absent). The eucharistic exegesis of the psalm hangs on that
Old-Latin clause: `Ferebatur enim Christus in manibus suis, quando commendans
ipsum corpus suum, ait: Hoc est corpus meum.`

**New Advent's "Psalm 34" carries only Sermon 2** of Augustine's two sermons on
Ps 33, verified by incipit-matching against the Latin; the Sermon 1 material is
quoted from the Latin only.

Retained readings of `semper` and `mansueti`, all at the loci above: Basil (the
spiritual mouth of the inner man; the meek as victors "without battle," and
"perhaps, prophetically, the disciples of Christ"); Cassiodorus (blameless
thought and speech are themselves praise; `non dixit, lege docti, non
ieiunantes, non psallentes, sed mansueti`; and the *conclusio psalmi*
— `ut eum conscriptum putes temporibus Christianis`); Augustine (bless in
giving and in taking away; be the Lord's `iumentum`); Theodoret ("fleeing or
pursuing… I hymn my Master"; the meek as co-singers); Bellarmine (`always`
means in due time and place, and through the Church's continuing psalmody —
an unwitting description of the Gradual itself).

**Rights:** no public-domain English of Basil's homily exists (NPNF 2/8 lacks
the psalm homilies; only FC 46, Way 1963, in copyright). Migne's parallel Latin
is quoted instead.

## III. Alleluia — Ps 87:2

**Superscription:** `Canticum Psalmi, filiis Core, in finem, pro Maheleth ad
respondendum. Intellectus Eman Ezrahitae.` Verse 2 matches the chant exactly.

The verse splits the tradition cleanly and the guide keeps both poles:

- **Augustine** (*En. in Ps.* 87 §§1–2): "The Passion of our Lord is here
  prophesied"; `pro Maheleth` = "for the chorus"; sons of Core = sons of
  Calvary; `Aeman` = "his brother"; and on the appointed verse, prayer offered
  "both in prosperous times, that is, by day, and in calamity… night."
- **Cassiodorus** (PL 70; col. 623 seen in the running matter): near-verbatim
  dependence on Augustine — `Per totum psalmum Dominus Salvator eloquitur a
  dispensatione qua passus est`; day = joy, night = adversity, "and since these
  two times embrace the whole of human life"; the *conclusio* turns the dark
  psalm into the martyrs' chorus.
- **Bellarmine** (O'Sullivan 1866, artifact pp. 275–278): the pair concretised
  into Good Friday and Gethsemane.
- **Theodoret** (PG 80, 1567–1573, the last figure approximate): the Antiochene
  counter-reading — the Jews' calamities and the Babylonian servitude, "and at
  the same time… the pains of all human nature after sin"; Eman a choirmaster
  of 1 Par. 6, not a figure.

**Bounded negative:** no checked Father remarks that this is formally the
darkest psalm of the psalter. That observation is modern and the guide says so.

## IV. Communion — Ps 103:13–15

**Title:** `Ipsi David` (no title in the Hebrew). Creation hymn following the
order of the works of Genesis 1 — so already Cassiodorus, who says one finds
here "things left unsaid in Genesis."

The reception runs a full spectrum and the spectrum is the commentary:

- **Theodoret** (PG 80, 1693–1707; vv. 13–16 under cols 1699–1700, seen): pure
  natural providence — bread to strengthen and nourish, wine to gladden, oil to
  nourish within and brighten without. No sacramental turn, from an expositor
  who allegorises freely on the preceding psalm.
- **Bellarmine** (artifact pp. 333–339): literal, then three morals —
  gratitude, the Father's generosity "even the luxuries," and frugality.
- **Augustine** (*En. in Ps.* 103, Sermo 3 §§12–14 = NPNF "Psalm 104"
  §§19–21): "What bread? Christ. Out of what earth? From Peter, from Paul";
  the sober inebriation of the Lord's cup; the chrism that anoints "over His
  whole Body"; and `panis cordis`.
- **Cassiodorus** (PL 70; column numerals not legibly readable for this psalm,
  therefore not cited): the most explicitly sacramental witness — `vinum
  laetificat cor hominis, cum sacratum fuerit in sanguinem Domini Christi`;
  `Exhilaratur quoque facies in oleo, cum regale chrisma conficitur`; and his
  proof-text for the illumined face is **Ps 33:6**, the Gradual's own psalm
  quoted inside the Communion's.

That the 1962 books appoint these verses as a **Communion** chant sits on the
Augustine–Cassiodorus line. Whether any compiler chose them for that reason is
undocumented and is not claimed.

## V. Bounded negatives and flags, complete

1. **Basil wrote no homilies on Pss 69, 87 or 103.** Verified two ways: the
   transmitted corpus in PG 29 covers LXX Pss 1, 7, 14, 28, 29, 32, 33, 44, 45,
   48, 59, 61, 114 only; and greps of the full PG 29 text for the Latin
   incipits of Pss 69, 87 and 103 returned nothing anywhere in the volume.
2. No public-domain English of Basil's *Hom. in Ps. 33* exists.
3. Augustine nowhere notes the Ps 39/69 doublet (full Latin of both
   *Enarrationes* checked).
4. New Advent's "Psalm 34" carries only Sermon 2; "Psalm 104" is a continuously
   renumbered abridgment of four Latin sermons.
5. **PL 70 column numbers**: only Ps 69 (coll. 493–494) and Ps 87 (col. 623)
   were legible; Ps 33 and Ps 103 ranges are therefore not cited.
6. **PG 80 column ranges** are given only where the printed numerals were read;
   the Ps 87 end-boundary (≈1573) is inferred from the next psalm's header page
   and marked approximate.
7. **Bellarmine 1866 page numbers** are heading-adjacent artifact pages, not
   quote-level pages.
8. **NABRE notes**: only Ps 70's was retrievable; the USCCB site returned 403
   for Pss 34, 88 and 104, so no modern-judgment claim rests on them.
9. The Catholic Encyclopedia's "Psalms" (1911) does not discuss the Ps 33 title
   problem, and its duplicate-psalms sentence was not located verbatim; CE is
   therefore not cited for the doublet.
10. `drbo.org` prints `David` at Ps 33:1 where most Clementine printings read
    `Davidi`; single-source superscription, edition variance flagged.
