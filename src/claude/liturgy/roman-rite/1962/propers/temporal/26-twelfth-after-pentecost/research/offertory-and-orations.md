# Offertory and orations element record

Per-element record for the Offertory `Precatus est Moyses` (Ex 32:11, 13, 14)
and for the three composed orations. Loci, rights, and bounded negatives.
Guide-wide bounds are in `scope.md`; the collated text is in
`propers/verified.md`.

## A. The Offertory

### A.1 Chant against the Clementine Vulgate

Chant text as printed in the 1962 typical edition and, with the doubled
opening phrase of the Gradual, in Ott, *Offertoriale sive versus
offertoriorum* (Desclée, 1935), no. 59, pp. 97–98.

| Chant | Clementine Vulgate | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Precatus est Moyses in conspectu Domini Dei sui, et dixit` | `Moyses autem orabat Dominum Deum suum, dicens` (v. 11) | prayer made *before* the Lord; the Vulgate takes a direct object |
| `Quare, Domine, irasceris in populo tuo?` | `Cur, Domine, irascitur furor tuus contra populum tuum, quem eduxisti de terra Aegypti…` (v. 11) | second person addressed to God, Grecizing `in` + ablative; the Egypt clause dropped |
| `Parce irae animae tuae` | `quiescat ira tua, et esto placabilis super nequitia populi tui` (v. 12b) | matter of a verse the printed citation does not name; anger doubled upon anger |
| `memento Abraham, Isaac et Iacob` | `Recordare Abraham, Isaac, et Israel servorum tuorum` (v. 13) | different verb; `Iacob` for `Israel`; `servorum tuorum` dropped |
| `quibus iurasti dare terram fluentem lac et mel` | `quibus jurasti per temetipsum… Multiplicabo semen vestrum sicut stellas caeli…` (v. 13) | the star-promise replaced by the milk-and-honey formula of Ex 3:8 / 33:1–3, and in the accusative where Jerome has ablative `lacte et melle` |
| `Et placatus factus est Dominus de malignitate, quam dixit facere populo suo` | `Placatusque est Dominus ne faceret malum quod locutus fuerat adversus populum suum` (v. 14) | a different construction: "concerning the evil which he said he would do" |

**Old-Latin character — the checkable proof.** Augustine's lemma in
*Quaestiones in Heptateuchum* II, q. 143 reads **`Et propitiatus est Dominus de
malitia quam dixit facere populo suo`** — the chant's construction, not
Jerome's. Latin verified at augustinus.it (PL 34 / CSEL 28.2).

**Greek: a deliberate omission.** Each divergence above has the shape the
Septuagint gives Ex 32:11–14, and that is the standard explanation of the
chant's text-form. No Greek witness was collated in this lane and the LXX could
not be quoted verbatim from a reachable source, so **the guide names the Greek
as the explanation and quotes none of it.** An earlier draft of the commentary
carried a Greek clause; it was removed on 2026-08-20 because it rested on no
verified witness. No printed Vetus Latina edition of Ex 32 (Sabatier or
successor) was consulted.

### A.2 The dropped historic verses (history, not appointed text)

Verified visually in Ott 1935, no. 59, pp. 99–100:

- V. 1 (p. 99): `Dixit Dominus ad Moysen: Invenisti gratiam in conspectu meo et
  scio te prae omnibus…` (cf. Ex 33:17 with 34:7–9), refrain `Et placatus.`
- V. 2 (p. 100): `Dixit Moyses et Aaron… ad omnem synagogam filiorum Israel:
  Accedite ante Deum…` (cf. Ex 16:6–12), refrain `Et placatus.`

Public-domain framing for the disappearance of offertory verses generally:
Catholic Encyclopedia, "Offertory" (vol. 11, 1911) — the offertorium was once a
whole psalm with antiphon; Durandus already complains the verses were being
omitted. **Bounded negative:** no verse containing `poenitet me` exists in
Ott's edition of this offertory and none was located in any public witness.

### A.3 Musical afterlife

- Palestrina, *Precatus est Moyses*, 5vv, in *Offertoria totius anni*
  (Rome: Francesco Coattino, 1593), secunda pars no. 2, under the rubric
  `Dominica XII post Pentecosten`.
- Lassus, *Precatus est Moyses*, LV 858, SATB; first publication RISM 1585a;
  *Sämtliche Werke* vol. 3 (Breitkopf & Härtel, 1895), pp. 23–25.
- **Bounded negative:** no parody mass "Missa Precatus est Moyses" by
  Palestrina or anyone could be verified. Treated as nonexistent on present
  evidence and not mentioned in the guide.

Both settings are cited for existence and publication facts only; the guide
makes no register-turn claim for them and they are therefore not eligible for
*Notable and Quotable*.

### A.4 Patristic reception of Ex 32:11–14

| Witness | Locus | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Augustine | *Quaest. in Hept.* II, qq. 141–149; esp. 143 (`malitiam hic poenam intellegi voluit`; `malus enim Deus non est; sed malis ingerit mala, quia iustus est`), 147 (`securus quidem hoc dixit, ut a consequentibus ratiocinatio concludatur`), 149 (`dilectio tua in illos intercedit mihi`; God "was loving them the more secretly while terrifying them aloud") | the mechanics of intercession for the undeserving |
| Augustine | *De civitate Dei* XV.25 (`ira Dei non perturbatio animi eius est, sed iudicium quo inrogatur poena peccato`) | the doctrinal guard-rail on divine anger and "repentance" |
| Gregory the Great | *Moralia* IX.xvi.23 (Moses resists God's wrath "by the oblation of his own death"; "What is it to say to His servant, Let Me alone; but to give him boldness to supplicate?"); XX.v.14 (Moses the pattern ruler, "mercy united with severity") | intercessory boldness as a doctrine of government |
| Tertullian | *Adv. Marcionem* II.26 — chapter heading names it: "Moses, When Deprecating God's Wrath Against Israel, a Type of Christ" | the **only** checked witness that states the Moses–Christ typology outright |
| John Chrysostom | *Hom. 16 on Romans* (on Rom 9:3) | Moses as the model intercessor whose daring serves God's glory |
| Johner | *The Chants of the Vatican Gradual* (1940), pp. 296–297 | mode 8 analysis, "violent agitation" over `Quare`; "Moses is but a weak type of Christ and His redemptive work" |

**Bounded negatives.** Jerome on Ex 32:32: an aggregator attributes a comment
to *Ep.* 121 (ad Algasiam); the New Advent text of that letter contains no such
passage. UNVERIFIED, not cited. Johner does not raise the Old-Latin question
for this piece (his Itala awareness appears at p. 16 on another chant).
Gregory's *Moralia* paragraph numbers are taken from the Library of the Fathers
section markers; PL columns unchecked and therefore not printed.

## B. The three orations

### B.1 Located sacramentary instances

- **Verona ("Leonine"), ed. Feltoe 1896.** Collect at p. 74 (Mense Iulio, Mass
  XXVIIII), in the shorter form `tribue ut`, lacking `quaesumus nobis`.
  Postcommunion at p. 60, reading `participatio **tui** sancta mysterii`.
  **Negative:** the Secret was not found in Feltoe.
- **Gelasian, ed. Wilson 1894, Book III no. VIII, pp. 228–229.** All three
  orations stand together in one Mass-set. Secret reads `largiaris` where the
  later books read `largiendo` (apparatus records `largiendo` in the
  eighth-century family). Wilson's note assigns the Missa in some witnesses to
  the fourteenth Sunday after Pentecost; the incipit index and the appendix
  collation at p. 354 confirm the set.
- **Gregorian (Hadrianum), ed. Wilson 1915, no. LXXXI, p. 173**, under
  `DOMINICA .XIII. POST PENTECOSTEN`: all three in the 1962 wording, including
  `largiendo`. The old Sunday count runs one ahead of the later missal's.
  A footnote to the preceding Mass (no. LXXX, pp. 172–173) records the chant
  cues `Ant. Deus in adiutorium. Resp. Benedicam dominum. Off. Precatus est
  moyses. Com. De fructu operum` — this Sunday's exact four chants. The
  postcommunion recurs at p. 199 in the Leonine `tui` wording.

Scope note: these are **located instances**. No wider transmission claim is
made beyond Verona → Gelasian → Gregorian(Hadrianum family) → 1962, with the
`largiaris`/`largiendo` and `tui`/`huius` variants as tabulated.

### B.2 Postconciliar survival

All three survive verbatim in the *Missale Romanum* editio typica tertia: the
Collect at Dominica XXXI per annum; the Secret as the super oblata of Feria
quinta post Cineres; the Postcommunion of Feria tertia hebdomadae III
Quadragesimae. Caution recorded: the current thirteenth Sunday per annum has a
**different** `Vivificet nos…` prayer; the two are not conflated.

### B.3 Commentators

- **Schuster**, *The Sacramentary*, vol. III, pp. 128–132: the offertory "a
  little liturgical gem"; 2 Cor 3:5 quoted against any suspicion the Collect
  overstates; the Secret's pardon "to which every other favour is
  subordinate"; the Postcommunion "an epitome of Eucharistic doctrine."
  Rights: the 1924–30 English translation may still be in copyright in some
  jurisdictions; quoted only in short compass.
- **Guéranger**, *The Liturgical Year* vol. 11, pp. 288–307, was read for this
  Sunday (Collect and translation p. 290, Moses typology on the Epistle
  pp. 294–295, Offertory p. 305, Secret pp. 305–306). **It supplies no claim
  the guide publishes and is therefore not in the references.** Its printed
  chant text reads `irasceris populo tuo`, omitting the `in` that the missal
  and Ott print; its Honorius of Autun reference (*Gemma animae* iv, 69) was
  not independently verified.
- **Gihr**, *The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass* (1902) — **bounded negative**:
  full-text searches show he cites none of these three orations anywhere.
  OCR-based search, so a residual chance of a missed hit remains. Not cited.
