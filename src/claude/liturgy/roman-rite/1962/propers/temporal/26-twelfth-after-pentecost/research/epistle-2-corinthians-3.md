# Epistle element record — 2 Cor 3:4–9

Per-element reception record for the Epistle of the Twelfth Sunday after
Pentecost. Loci, rights status, and bounded negatives for every witness the
guide retains or deliberately declines. Claim roles and the guide-wide bounds
are in `scope.md`; the published text is in `propers/verified.md`.

## Text state

The missal follows the Clementine exactly, including the shift from
`ministrátio` to `ministérium` in the last clause. The only divergence is the
liturgical incipit: `Fratres:` displaces the Clementine's `autem` at v. 4.
The Douay's "And such confidence we have" renders that displaced `autem`, and
the guide declares the gap rather than repairing it.

## The internal Augustinian tension, documented at both loci

*De doctrina christiana* III.5.9 (c. 396) makes 2 Cor 3:6 a rule of exegesis:
letter = the literal sense of a figure, spirit = its signification. *De spiritu
et littera* 4.6 (412) quotes that very interpretation back and corrects its
sufficiency **without retracting it** — "not in that way only … but also, and
indeed especially" (`eoque vel maxime`) of the law without grace, so that the
Decalogue itself is the killing letter when grace is absent. The later text
adjudicates the rank of the earlier; both remain in the corpus. The guide
prints both and says which one Augustine subordinated to which.

## Bounded negatives (corpora named; not claims about literature outside them)

1. **Gregory the Great on 2 Cor 3:6** — no verifiable locus located
   (full-text search of the *Moralia* and domain-restricted searches of the
   standard public collections). Nothing is cited for Gregory on this verse.
2. **Origen, *De principiis* IV** — the great hermeneutical book grounds the
   spiritual sense on 1 Cor 2 and Prov 22:20–21 and does **not** quote 2 Cor
   3:6 there; only an allusion to 2 Cor 3:14–16 (the veil) at IV §6. The
   letter/spirit citation is at *Contra Celsum* VII.20, not VI.70.
3. **Aquinas, *Super II Cor.* c. 3** — *De spiritu et littera* is **not** cited
   by name in that chapter (checked); Augustine is named there only on a
   Trinitarian point. The Augustinian doctrine arrives in the *Summa*.
4. **Theodoret, *Interpretatio* in 2 Cor (PG 82, from col. 376)** — verified as
   publicly reachable; his sentences were **not** verified at the Greek within
   this lane's bounds, and Robert Hill's English is in copyright. Nothing is
   printed as Theodoret's words on this passage. The guide says so in the text.
5. **Ambrose, *Expositio in Ps. CXVIII*** — a full-text hit for `littera
   occidit` exists in that work but the sermo/section could not be pinned;
   lead only, not cited.
6. **Canon-law reuse (Decretum/Decretals)** — no locus verified; nothing
   asserted.

## Historical orientation used on the page-2 dossier

Catholic Encyclopedia, "Epistles to the Corinthians" (vol. 4, 1908): written
from Macedonia, carried by Titus. NABRE introduction (summarised, never
quoted): generally dated autumn A.D. 57, composed in Macedonia, with the modern
partition discussion reported as a modern critical judgment and left open. The
Exodus 34:29–35 background (Moses' shining face, the tables of stone) is the
passage's own narrated allusion and is treated as such.
