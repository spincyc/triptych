\properrow{Gradual \propercue{Grad.}}{\latin{Esto mihi in Deum protectórem}
\properrefs{Ps. 30, 3; verse Ps. 70, 1}}{Two psalms of refuge; twin

\properrow{Alleluia \propercue{All.}}{\latin{Magnus Dóminus, et laudábilis

\properrow{Gospel \propercue{Gosp.}}{\latin{Homo quidam erat dives, qui

\properrow{Communion \propercue{Comm.}}{\latin{Gustáte et vidéte}

\vspace{0.35em}
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & A dismissed estate manager, ordered to produce his accounts,
discounts his master's debtors' bills so that they will house him; the master
commends the shrewdness, and Christ draws a lesson about using money to make
friends against the day it fails. Around that reading the Mass sets Paul's
claim that those led by the Spirit are sons and heirs, and psalm verses of
received mercy, refuge, humility, and tasting.\\
Allegorical & The audited steward is the creature holding what is not his
own; the reckoning is judgment. Augustine reads the Offertory's ``who is God
but the Lord?'' as the sons possessing God himself as ``the hoped-for
inheritance'' (\work{Enarr. in Ps.} 17.32) --- the Epistle's \latin{herédes
quidem Dei}.\\
Moral & Everything in hand is administered, not owned, and the audit is
already announced. The Collect therefore asks not for one right act but for a
standing \latin{spíritus cogitándi et agéndi}; the Offertory supplies the
posture, humility, that the steward never reaches.\\
Anagogical & The Gospel's \latin{a\'et\'erna tabern\'acula} and the Secret's
\latin{gáudia sempitérna} name the same end; the Postcommunion asks that its
effect be felt already, and the Communion's \latin{gustáte} gives the mode ---
the end is tasted before it is seen.\\
\end{sensestable}
