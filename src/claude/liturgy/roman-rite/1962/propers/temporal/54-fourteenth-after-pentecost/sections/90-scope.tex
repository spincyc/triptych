\section*{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}

This appendix carries the work-wide bounds. Claim-local qualifications stand
beside their claims above; retrieval mechanics, checksums, query detail,

\subsection*{Edition, formulary identity and text-verification state}

The controlling text is the \work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica (Vatican
City: Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis, 1962), printed pp.~394--396, the ten
appointed elements carrying marginal reference numbers 1572--1581. The
formulary is bounded by no.~1571, the Thirteenth Sunday's Postcommunion, and
no.~1582, the Fifteenth Sunday's Introit; nothing between those boundaries is
omitted from this guide. Rank \latin{II classis}; \latin{Credo}; Preface of the
Most Holy Trinity. \textbf{Green follows the general rubrics and is not printed
at the formulary}, and is recorded as an inference from them rather than as a
reading of pp.~394--396.

\textbf{Text-verification state.} Every published Latin form was read as page
images off the identified facsimile of the typical edition, the whole page at
300~dpi and every disputed letter, accent and point re-read at 500~dpi, which is
the scan's native resolution. No transcription, no optical layer and no web text
was allowed to supply a reading. \textbf{One mark on these pages is damaged and
it is not text}: the Gospel's marginal reference number has lost a digit to ink
loss and is resolved by the unbroken series, not by a second witness.

\textbf{The second 1962 witness is weaker than the first, and the difference is
declared.} The Benziger \latin{editio iuxta typicam} was read only in an
uncorrected optical text derivative; the sibling page-image artifact was not
opened. \textbf{Every Benziger reading in this guide is therefore evidence about
a text layer and not a collated reading of the Benziger book.} Where the two
books differ, the difference is an edition difference or an optical artifact and
never an uncertain reading in the typical edition; nothing is blended and no
Benziger form is substituted for a Vatican typical form.

\textbf{English.} Scriptural elements take the Douay--Rheims as revised by
Challoner; the three orations take the anonymous English of a registered 1861
hand missal. \textbf{This guide has composed, translated, adapted and
paraphrased nothing of the appointed text.} All three orations were read on page
images of the registered 1861 Internet Archive scan, its bytes hash-matched to
the registered digest before a page was opened, at printed pp.~425 and 427, and
all three stand there word for word and point for point as this guide prints
them. \textbf{That is one digitisation of the 1861 book and not a collation of
two copies}, and no lane has established who made the translation, so the
edition is named and no translator is. Both English witnesses are public domain
in the United States. \textbf{Neither is an official liturgical translation and
neither is the text of the 1962 books}: this is a study companion, not an altar
book or a hand missal.

\textbf{Translations of the witnesses, as against the appointed text.} Augustine
--- in the \work{Enarrationes}, in \work{De sermone Domini in monte} and in
\work{De opere monachorum} --- and Chrysostom on Galatians and on Matthew are
quoted in the English of the \work{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers}, first
series, and Cyril of Jerusalem and Ambrose in the second series, vols.~7 and 10;
that English is named at each use and is not the Father's own words, and for
those volumes no registered record names a translator, so the edition is named
and no translator is. Guéranger is quoted in \textbf{Dom Laurence Shepherd's}
English, from the Duffy 1900 edition of \work{The Liturgical Year} vol.~XI, and
Schuster in \textbf{Arthur Levelis-Marke's}, from the Burns Oates \&
Washbourne 1927 \work{The Sacramentary} vol.~III. Theodoret on Pss.~83, 94 and
117 and Chrysostom on Galatians are read in Greek, the medieval Latin witnesses
in an optical transcription of Migne.

\textbf{Every biblical date on page~2 is the Scripture chronology corpus's
answer at the verses that element appoints, printed in its source's own words;
where the corpus dates a passage nowhere the cell says so and carries no
figure.} One modern critical horizon is held, for the Epistle alone.

\textbf{Psalms are cited by the Vulgate numbering the missal prints, and the
parentheses on page~2 give the English number and name the series.} The
conversions stand in the tracked concordance
\texttt{psalm-numbering-ee3c7757}, registered as an artifact of the
Douay--Rheims Challoner edition quoted here, whose rows give an English offset
of one verse for Vulgate Psalms 33 and 83 and of none for 94 and 117, each
marked uniform through the psalm.

\textbf{The rights argument under which this formulary's Latin is published
reaches the three orations at their opening clauses only.} No public-domain
conclusion is claimed for the 1962 typical edition itself, and this project
claims no rights in the text and has composed, translated, modernised,
conflated and adapted none of it; what permits the Latin here is
\textbf{17 U.S.C.\ \S103(b)}, under which a revised edition's copyright reaches
only that edition's own contribution and not the preexisting material it
reprints. All ten appointed elements of this formulary are printed in the Pustet
Missal of Ratisbon, 1862, which this repository tracks as public-domain text ---
including the Communion's recasting, the one substantive departure this
formulary makes from its Bible text. Seven of the ten
elements are Scripture and reach a public-domain antecedent through the tracked
Clementine and Douay artifacts independently of any missal. \textbf{The other
three do not, so the preexisting-material argument carries them alone} --- and
the record behind it quotes each of the three at its incipit and first clause
and no further, with both older witness layers classified degraded and the
Venice 1570 record expressly forbidding a negative search to rest on it.
\textbf{So the record attests those three prayers in 1862 and 1570 by heading,
order, reference and opening clause: strong evidence that the formulary is
preexisting material, and weaker evidence that the exact printed wording is.}
This project's reading of the statute is stated plainly in the leaf's own
records and \textbf{is not a clearance anyone granted}; a word-level comparison
of the three orations across both witness layers is a local read, not a
retrieval, and remains to be made.

\subsection*{Evidence-state discipline}

\textbf{No patristic or medieval witness in this guide is \emph{verified} in the
source-library sense}, because verification under this profile means collating
the locus against the controlling critical or Migne edition, and \textbf{Migne
was opened for none of them}. The tiering used throughout is: \emph{acquired}
(exact bytes obtained and hashed), \emph{searched} (a query run over the
retained artifact), \emph{inspected} (the identified passage read with its
surrounding context), and, where a witness in the original language
independently agrees, \emph{corroborated} --- which is short of collation and is
never called more. \textbf{Possession is not reading, a search hit is not
inspection, and inspection of a transcription is not collation against a page
image.}

\textbf{The whole medieval Latin layer this revision added stands at
\emph{inspected} on one optical transcription of \work{Patrologia Latina}, and
four recognition errors were met in it} --- a duplicated line in Godfrey,
transposed lines in Bruno the Carthusian, \latin{wercede} for \latin{mercede} in
Bruno of Segni, and a wrong psalm number in the Glossa. \textbf{Two passages
carry a standing instruction not to quote them until a page image is read},
PL~152:1250 and PL~174:583, \textbf{and neither is quoted anywhere in this
guide}: Bruno the Carthusian's twofold answer at the Gradual and Godfrey's
\work{Homilia} LXXXIII are reported in this guide's own words instead. \textbf{Alcuin's \work{De usu Psalmorum} VII and Wibald of
Corvey's \work{Epistola} CCCLXXV were reached only by an exact-phrase index over
the \work{Patrologia Latina}, PL~101 and PL~189 standing unopened at both
places}, and they are reported at the Collect as unverified leads carrying no
claim. \textbf{None of the witnesses added this revision has a
source-library record or a binding at the loci used}, so none of those citations
can acquire a fingerprint, a passage record or a rights disposition from the
library as it stands.

\textbf{Two page-image readings behind the historical sections are of
unregistered derivatives of the same Internet Archive items as the registered
optical layers} --- Wilson 1915 and the two Migne volumes. \textbf{They are
those digitisations read better and not independent second witnesses to the
printed books}, and their digests are recorded for replay rather than as
repository fingerprints.

\textbf{The notable-and-quotable gallery is no longer uniform in state.}
\textbf{Two of its five entries are collated against the page images of the
printed newspapers} and are verified for the crop regions quoted, and no
further --- the surrounding matter on those pages is optical text. The other
three were read in digital surrogates and are \emph{inspected}.

\textbf{Three appointed elements were swept for a qualifying afterlife and
produced none.} At the Introit, six distinctive strings return nothing from
the American case-law corpus, and the one newspaper string tested returns a
count dominated by standing church notices, a representative hit on the page
setting the phrase as a devotional tag at the foot of a service listing. At
the Alleluia the obstacle is identity rather than scarcity: the King James
wording of its verse stands at five psalms --- Pss.\ 66, 81, 95, 98 and 100
--- so no later use of it can be tied to the appointed psalm on wording at
all. At the Offertory the negative is conditional: one candidate qualifies on
the rule, and its evidence state --- secondary sources only, no edition
collated, no page settled --- and its copyright keep it unpublishable, so the
negative holds until that is closed. The three orations lie outside the
gallery, which is confined to wording from the scriptural propers.

\subsection*{Source scope, and what it does not reach}

\textbf{The Greek side of the reception sweep is the sweep's largest gap, and
this revision made the imbalance worse rather than better.} It added one Greek
work and one spurious Greek homily against eleven Latin witnesses. \textbf{Five
Greek readings stand} --- Theodoret at Pss.~83, 94 and 117, Chrysostom on
Galatians, and a spurious Ps.~94 homily of the Chrysostomic corpus, all read in
Greek --- against Chrysostom on Matthew and the \work{Mystagogical Catecheses},
which remain English only. \textbf{Theodoret's exposition of Ps.~33 stands in a
volume of his psalm commentary this research did not fetch, and no other Greek
witness expounds the Offertory's psalm at the appointed verses.} That element's
reception stands in Latin --- Augustine, Cassiodorus, Arnobius, Prosper and
Godfrey --- and in English translation at Ambrose and the \work{Mystagogical
Catecheses}: \textbf{nothing at the Offertory was read in Greek, so that element
admits no Greek--Latin comparison at all.} \textbf{Elsewhere no Greek--Latin
comparison in this guide is presented as evenly grounded}, and nothing here
supports a claim about what the Fathers \emph{as a body} held. \textbf{Three
Greek works this guide previously called one retrieval away are not:} the collection that appeared to hold
Chrysostom on the Psalms carries a fragment covering other psalms entirely; it
holds no Greek \work{Homiliae in Matthaeum} at all; and neither it nor the
mirror consulted holds any Pauline commentary of Theodoret. \textbf{Nine Greek
authors were not swept for any appointed passage} --- Origen, Basil, Gregory of
Nyssa, Gregory of Nazianzus, Cyril of Alexandria, Theophylact, Euthymius
Zigabenus, Didymus and Athanasius --- and the \work{Opus imperfectum in
Matthaeum} is a \work{Patrologia Graeca} text and was not reached in the Latin
collection read here.

\textbf{Named as not swept on the Latin side, so that an unreachable work can be
told from one merely not looked for:} Pelagius's \work{Expositio}, Nicholas of
Lyra, Denis the Carthusian and Cornelius a Lapide's commentary on Galatians;
Albert the Great, Nicholas of Lyra, Maldonado and Remigius of Auxerre in his own
person on Matthew; Bellarmine's Latin \work{Explanatio} and pseudo-Jerome's
\work{Breviarium} on the Psalms; the Glossa on the Psalms and Remigius's
\work{Enarrationes in Psalmos}, both reachable and never opened; and
\textbf{Bruno of Segni's own \work{Expositio in Psalmos}, which reaches all four
appointed psalms and is the cheapest remaining repair.}

\textbf{Papias has no record of any kind here, and Eusebius's \work{Historia
ecclesiastica} Book~III is out of reach:} the only registered Greek artifact of
that work is Books~VI--X and the registered English edition is Book~VI, so
III.24.6 and III.39.16 are unreachable and nothing in this guide rests on
either witness. The page-2 Gospel dossier accordingly stands on the encyclopedia
volumes the chronology corpus names and on no earlier witness.

\textbf{Four of the six scriptural loci have no registered witness of any kind.}
Mt.\ 6:24--33, Ps.~83, Ps.~94 and Ps.~117 have zero registered passages anywhere
in the source library --- tracked, remote or restricted. Gal.\ 5:16--24 has
exactly one, Anthony's sermon. Ps.~33 has two, and \textbf{both are registered
for a different Sunday's Gradual and not for this Mass's Offertory.}
\textbf{The gap is not closable from the library}, and every witness the
commentary carries was reached by direct retrieval rather than through a
registered holding. The repository's passage-to-work index returns candidate
commentators for every appointed locus, but \textbf{no entry carries a
verse-level locus and no entry carries an artifact binding}, so every one is a
lead in the strict sense and none is cited from it.

\textbf{Editions absent from the scripture side, and the three consequences that
matter.} \textbf{No Hebrew text of any appointed psalm is held here}; no Greek
psalter is held; and \textbf{no Vetus Latina, Psalterium Romanum, Psalterium
iuxta Hebraeos, Weber--Stuttgart, Nova Vulgata, Nestle-Aland or UBS edition is
registered.} No chant book of any kind is registered beyond one catalog-level
record. So: \textbf{(a)} it could not be checked whether the appointed psalm
wording is Gallican as against Roman or \latin{iuxta Hebraeos} psalter wording,
which is the standard question for a chant text --- the finding that the chants
match the tracked Clementine answers a \emph{narrower} question than which
psalter the chant descends from; \textbf{(b)} the alphabetic acrostic of Ps.~33
is carried only as Cassiodorus's report about the Hebrew and is nowhere asserted
as a fact about the Hebrew text; and \textbf{(c)} every critical-text statement
about the Epistle's lists is one step removed from a critical edition, having
reached this research only through the inline apparatus of the tracked Greek
files.

\textbf{Four of the five sacramentary books behind the displacement are
unopened.} Rheinau, S.~Gallen, Pamelius and Menard have had no independent check
at the locus, and for them \textbf{the disagreement rests entirely on Wilson's
apparatus.} Gerbert is open at his printed p.~173 in two independently produced
Internet Archive optical layers that agree verbatim, \textbf{neither of them a
registered artifact of this repository} --- the registered e-rara excerpt covers
the first fifty leaves only and stops 123 pages short --- \textbf{with no page
image collated} and eighteenth-century long-s type under the recognition, so
that whole lane is an optical-text lane. A repository sweep for all five books and their editors returns no
record of any kind; the only sacramentary editions registered here are Wilson
1894, Wilson 1915, Feltoe 1896 and one modern edition. \textbf{Two liturgical
instruments remain out of reach for two different reasons.} Hesbert's
\work{Antiphonale Missarum Sextuplex} could not be opened: no book artifact is
inspected or registered and \textbf{no affirmative United States redistribution
basis for exact edition bytes was established}, so a modern database of its
formularies was used instead --- \textbf{a declared and material downgrade in
evidence state.} Pamelius's \work{Liturgica Latinorum} tomus~II was searched in
one digital library only, which returns its companion volume alone; no other
library's digitisation was tried, \textbf{so that negative repeats the earlier
sweep rather than extending it.}

\textbf{The Veronense negative is replayable and it is not a reading of printed
pages.} Feltoe's 1896 edition is registered here as work, edition and two
optical artifacts, so the seventeen-string search that found none of the three
orations in the Verona book can be repeated exactly --- \textbf{but it is a
search of two uncorrected optical layers}, the edition prints the codex's
contractions, and \textbf{the Veronense has no ordered Sunday series at all}, so
absence there is much weaker evidence about a Sunday formulary than absence from
an ordered sacramentary would be.

\textbf{Every cross-formulary measurement in this guide rests on the same
denominator, and it is small.} Twenty-one collated text records covering
\textbf{fifteen distinct 1962 identities} and forty-five orations --- what this
repository happens to have collated, not a sample of the Missal, and audit
records rather than page images. \textbf{Every count drawn from it is evidence
about those records.}

\textbf{Provenance legibility, recorded because it limits checking rather than
claiming.} Five of the seven tracked witnesses behind the page-2 collation
negatives are held public-domain text but \textbf{unbound}, so a reader cannot
reconstruct from the bindings file alone which seven were counted; and the
leaf's bindings record carries no entry for any witness added in the last two
revisions.

\subsection*{Search limits}

\textbf{A search that finds nothing has not established that nothing is there;
it has established what the search did.} Five traps met in this research resolve
successfully and \emph{wrongly}, and every negative in this guide is bounded
against them.

\textbf{Optical hyphenation.} A whole-word search over the tracked Missal misses
an occurrence of the Offertory broken across a line end, and a search of
forty-seven newspaper issues for a two-word phrase returned zero while the
phrase was present, broken the same way. \textbf{The bound on the missal
payloads is worse than that:} four appointed phrases tested return zero from all
nine payloads while the same books demonstrably print them, so \textbf{single
words and fragments carry weight there and multi-word phrases carry almost
none}, and no whole-phrase negative over those artifacts is reported here as
absence.

\textbf{A phrase index that knows one orthography.} The exact-phrase index of
\work{Patrologia Latina} makes a new class of negative statable --- that a phrase
does not occur in that whole collection --- \textbf{but only in Migne's
orthography and only where the phrase is unbroken in the unit indexed.}
\textbf{No patristic or medieval exegesis of the Secret \latin{Concéde nobis,
Dómine} or of the Postcommunion \latin{Puríficent semper et múniant} stands
anywhere that index reaches}, every hit on their distinctive phrases being a
sacramentary printing the prayer --- \textbf{two of the ten appointed elements
with no documented exegetical reception in any period.} \textbf{The Collect
stands outside that negative}, Honorius citing it by incipit, Godfrey of Admont
glossing it word by word and Schuster commenting on it continuously. The
negative on those two prayers carries exactly the bound above.

\textbf{Phrase-search degradation.} The newspaper service's quoted-string search
\textbf{does not do exact-phrase matching for stopword-heavy strings; it
degrades to an AND of terms.} This was established by calibration, one test
query returning tens of thousands of items whose first-ranked result did not
contain the clause at all. \textbf{Every count from that corpus in this guide is
an AND count and never a phrase count, and every negative is bounded to the
ranked results actually checked.} \textbf{That corpus is thin after 1922 and
effectively ends about 1963, and only English-language American newspapers were
searched}: no British, Irish, Continental or Latin-American press, and no
non-English, book, periodical or musical-repertory corpus, was searched for any
element. The case-law corpus is \textbf{overwhelmingly American} and no
Commonwealth sweep was run.

\textbf{A partial file that looks complete.} Two collections have now been met
in which a work's title promises the whole and the file carries a fragment ---
Cassiodorus in one web library, and Chrysostom on the Psalms in a Greek one.
\textbf{A title is not a table of contents.}

\textbf{The shared incipit, the running head, and the fused reference letter.}
\latin{Protector noster aspice, Deus} opens this Mass's Introit, another
Sunday's Gradual and a composed collect that quotes no psalm at all, so
\textbf{a search hit on an incipit proves nothing on its own.} A running head in
either of Wilson's editions names the section the page \emph{reaches}, not the
one it opens in, and two loci in this guide were misread that way before being
corrected against the section headings themselves. \textbf{And an optical layer
fuses a superscript footnote reference letter with the section numeral that
follows it}, which is how a section CXXXIII was manufactured out of
\latin{cXXXIII} and how the Ottobonianus chant cues were briefly attached to the
wrong Mass. \textbf{Both were corrected against page images.}

\textbf{The precedent corpus.} Every ``not located in the checked corpus''
verdict behind the interpretive section is bounded to exactly this: nine
full-text missal payloads in eight editions, whose Sunday numbering and some
assignments differ from 1962; 188 published documents of this collection and of
the sibling provider, of which 92 carry interpretive proposals; and the tracked
1962 registry. \textbf{The census of proper guides in it is 35, none of them
sanctoral or votive, one of them ritual.}

\subsection*{Rights boundary}

\textbf{Three summary states are distinguished on page~2 and they are not the
same thing}, which is the distinction this appendix owes that sheet.
\emph{Summarised by choice} is a public-domain source this guide could quote and
does not, generally because its bytes are not retained and the wording would be
this guide's own. \emph{Summarise-only} is a source whose rights are recorded
\emph{unresolved} --- a recorded non-determination and not a restriction, treated
conservatively here as though it were one. \emph{Summary compelled} is a source
whose rights are recorded \emph{restricted}.

Public domain and quotable: the volume facsimiles of the encyclopedia used on
page~2, the 1910 responsa, the 1614 commentary, the 1866 Bellarmine
translation, the 1900 Guéranger, Wilson 1894 and 1915, Feltoe 1896, the 1862
Missal, the American court reports and newspapers, the United Kingdom official
report, and all tracked Bible text. \textbf{One distinction here is easy to get
backwards and is stated plainly: an encyclopedia article read from the volume
facsimile may be quoted, while the same article read from a per-article web
transcription may be summarised but not reproduced, because the transcription
and its markup carry their own copyright over the public-domain article
underneath.} They are the same words under opposite rights. \textbf{Two
encyclopedia articles this formulary would most want --- on the Gospel of
St~Matthew and on the Epistle to the Galatians --- were retrieved by no lane, so
nothing is asserted from what either says}, though the chronology corpus carries
labelled claims sourced to those very volumes and those labels are printed on
page~2.

The Latin transcription behind the medieval layer is an optical transcription of
public-domain Migne and is quotable at the state recorded above. The Greek text
layers carry a modern digitiser's copyright over ancient Greek that is public
domain by age: \textbf{the ancient Greek is what is quoted, and no bytes of
either were written into this repository.}

Restricted, and therefore summarised and nowhere reproduced: the registered
modern Galatians introduction, which is the only limb of that translation
bearing on this proper. Restricted by reason of a modern apparatus over an
ancient text: Anthony of Padua's sermon, \textbf{whose thirteenth-century Latin
is public domain by age and is what is quoted here, the modern apparatus being
paraphrased and never reproduced}; and the Migne facsimile of Honorius, fetched,
read, and retained nowhere. Modern translations under copyright are never
reproduced at any length that would substitute for the book, and \textbf{the one
copyrighted item the cultural sweep returned is not published here at all.}

\textbf{And a fact about this repository that changes what a green build
means.} \textbf{No mechanical gate here can check a rights claim against a
guide's prose.} The preflight checks that touch quotation are a self-consistency
check between this guide's own References and its own quotation environments and
a check of the leaf's declared bindings against the library's rights fields;
neither reads the prose against a source. \textbf{The rights discipline above is
enforced by authorship}, and it is recorded here so that a reader does not
mistake a passing build for a rights audit.

\subsection*{Review state and what is deliberately not here}

The synthesis companion omits the complete appointed texts and the
element-by-element sweep, and replaces the sweep with an integrated commentary
organised by cross-proper questions; the complete Latin, the full per-element
reception and the collation records stand in the canonical edition and in
\texttt{propers/verified.md}.

This guide treats the reusable temporal formulary only. Occurrence in a
particular civil year, commemorations, particular calendars and celebration-mode
branches are outside it. \textbf{No Sacramental Appendix is imported}: this is
an ordinary Sunday of the temporal cycle, the formulary appoints no ritual text,
no blessing and no sacramental rite, and no non-Eucharistic sacrament is
celebrated with it, so the profile's imported summary is not required.

\textbf{The interpretive section is exploratory throughout} and is labelled so
at its head; its novelty verdicts are bounded to the corpus named above and
\textbf{no connection anywhere in this guide is claimed to be unknown,
unprecedented, first, or authored by the model.}
