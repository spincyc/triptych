\section*{Appendix: Scope and Qualifications}

\editionnote

\textbf{Edition and formulary.} The governing text is the 1962 Vatican
\latin{editio typica}, \latin{Dominica Decima Quarta post Pentecosten}, II
classis, printed pp.~394--396, marginal nos.~1572--1581. The complete ten-part
formulary, its boundaries, rank, references, rubrics, wording, accents, and
punctuation were visually collated against the identified facsimile. Green is
derived from the general color rubric rather than printed at the formulary.
The detailed collation, secondary-witness discrepancies, retrieval routes, and
hashes are recorded in \texttt{propers/verified.md} and
\texttt{propers/retrieved.txt}.

\textbf{English and rights.} Scriptural English is quoted from the registered
public-domain Douay--Rheims Bible in the Challoner revision. The Collect,
Secret, and Postcommunion remain in controlled Latin with descriptions of
their petitions: their Cummiskey passage is cataloged, acquired, and inspected
but not verified, and the publication inventory marks all three translations
unavailable. No English wording from that passage is published here. Because the Communion
adapts rather than quotes Matthew~6:33, its controlled Latin is printed beside
the complete Douay verse and a description of the differences; no composite
English antiphon is supplied. The short protected phrase from \work{Fahrenheit
451} is limited to inherited biblical wording, and the protected scene is
paraphrased. Hesbert's scanned expression has unresolved rights, so only a
concise citation and paraphrase are used.

\textbf{Research extent.} Complete biblical contexts were checked for the four
psalms, Galatians, and Matthew. The reception sweep includes Latin and Greek
patristic witnesses, medieval Doctors, Benedict, Bellarmine, and later
liturgical commentary at the exact loci listed in the References. It samples
major accessible traditions but is not an exhaustive census. Some witnesses
were inspected in public transcriptions or historical English translations
rather than critical editions; Bellarmine's English is abridged, Aquinas's
Psalm~33 exposition is a \latin{reportatio}, and the attribution of Cyril's
\work{Mystagogical Catecheses} remains disputed. No original-language Hebrew
Psalter and no complete later Greek commentary corpus was available.

\textbf{Historical and critical bounds.} The chronology labels on page~2 come
only from \texttt{research/chronology.toml}. The supplied modern Catholic
introduction covers Galatians alone; no modern-critical dossier was available
for Matthew or the four psalms. The chronology corpus supplies no narrated-event
date for the Sermon on the Mount. The checked transmission witnesses identify
neither the original compiler nor the cause or date of the prayer trio's
movement, and they do not settle the date or mechanism of the Communion
substitution. The exact Communion recasting received a targeted sweep in the
named Gospel and monastic loci, with no direct interpretation located there.

\textbf{Cultural and exploratory bounds.} The gallery contains exactly the
five afterlives whose wording, context, locus, and rights position were checked:
Bosch, Bradbury, Butler, two American judicial opinions, and the signed
newspaper letter. Screen and television transcript leads were excluded because
their witnesses were inadequate. The six proposals are editorial hypotheses;
their precedents were searched across the recorded Triptych and liturgical OCR
corpus. ``Not located in the checked corpus'' is limited to that corpus and
asserts no universal novelty.

\textbf{Audit and review.} The passage matrix, exact search boundaries,
negative results, disagreements, source roles, proposal audits, and
claim-by-claim dispositions are in \texttt{research/scope.md}; the publication
bindings are in \texttt{research/source-bindings.toml}. The publication is a
study aid and not an official liturgical book, critical edition, homily, or
ecclesiastical approval.
