\section*{The Appointed Formulary in Full}

Everything the 1962 \latin{Missale Romanum} prints for this Mass, in the order
it prints it: the Latin of the Vatican \latin{editio typica} exactly as the
controlling facsimile has it, each element under its own printed heading,
scriptural reference and marginal number, and, where a publishable controlled
witness exists, its identified public-domain English. This is the canonical
full research edition; a reader who came for the texts has them here.

\textbf{Formulary.} \latin{DOMINICA DECIMA QUARTA post Pentecosten},
\latin{II classis}, printed pp.~394--396, marginal nos.\ 1572--1581. Green is
not printed at this Mass; it follows the general rubrics, and the exact locus
is given in the commentary and the terminal appendix. The formulary begins
immediately after the Postcommunion of the Thirteenth Sunday (no.\ 1571) and
ends immediately before the heading \latin{DOMINICA DECIMA QUINTA post
Pentecosten} (no.\ 1582). Nothing between those boundaries is omitted here. It
appoints no Tract, Sequence, second oration, blessing or ritual text, and no
second Collect, Secret or Postcommunion.

\textbf{The Latin.} Received liturgical text of the Roman Rite, older than the
edition that prints it. No public-domain conclusion is claimed for the 1962
typical edition itself; what permits the Latin here is 17 U.S.C.\ \S103(b),
under which a revised edition's copyright reaches only that edition's own
contribution and not the preexisting material it reprints. All ten appointed
elements of this formulary are printed in the Pustet Missal of Ratisbon, 1862,
which this repository tracks as public-domain text --- including the
Communion's recasting, which is the one substantive departure this formulary
makes from its Bible text --- and the terminal appendix states the basis and
its bounds in full. The project claims no rights in this text and has not
composed, translated, modernised, conflated or adapted any of it. Every form
below was read off the CMAA facsimile of the Vatican typical edition as page
images at 300~dpi, with every disputed letter, accent and point re-read at
500~dpi, the scan's native resolution. It was then compared against the 1962
Benziger \latin{editio iuxta typicam} --- and that comparison, unlike the
Thirteenth Sunday's, was made against an uncorrected OCR text layer
and not against page images. Where the two differ, the Vatican typical reading
is what appears here and the difference is tabulated in the commentary as a
difference between one book and the other book's text layer.

\textbf{The English.} Scriptural elements are the Douay--Rheims as revised by
Bishop Challoner (1749--1752) --- the English of the Clementine Vulgate the
missal prints --- in the Project Gutenberg e-text of that revision. No English
is published for the three orations: the registered Cummiskey passage has been
inspected but not verified, and the publication inventory marks those
translations unavailable. Each oration therefore remains in controlled Latin
and is followed by a description of its petition, not by a translation.

\subsection*{1. Introit \cue{Int.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Ant. ad Introitum} --- Ps. 83, 10-11 --- marginal no.\ 1572}
Protéctor noster, áspice, Deus, et réspice in fáciem Christi tui: quia mélior
est dies una in átriis tuis super mília. \textnormal{Ps. ibid., 2-3} Quam
dilécta tabernácula tua, Dómine virtútum! concupíscit, et déficit ánima mea in
átria Dómini. ℣.~Glória Patri.
\end{appointedlatin}

\begin{namedtranslation}{English: Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps. 83:10--11 and 2--3 printed whole, with the portions the chant sings in bold}
\textbf{v.~10} \textbf{Behold, O God our protector: and look on the face of thy
Christ.} \textbf{v.~11} \textbf{For better is one day in thy courts above
thousands.} I have chosen to be an abject in the house of my God, rather than
to dwell in the tabernacles of sinners. \textit{Ps.} \textbf{v.~2} \textbf{How
lovely are thy tabernacles, O Lord of hosts!} \textbf{v.~3} \textbf{my soul
longeth and fainteth for the courts of the Lord.} My heart and my flesh have
rejoiced in the living God. ℣.~\latin{Glória Patri.}
\end{namedtranslation}

\englishgap{The antiphon is a plain quotation, not a centonisation, but it
stops in the middle of both its verses. It cuts v.~11 at \latin{super mília},
immediately before the psalm's own two-way choice between the house of God and
the tents of sinners, and it cuts the psalm verse at \latin{in átria Dómini},
before the heart and the flesh rejoice. The Douay renders both verses whole, so
they are printed whole with the sung portion marked rather than trimmed into an
English antiphon the project would itself have composed. The doxology cue
\latin{Glória Patri} belongs to the Ordinary and is not translated here.}

\subsection*{2. Collect \cue{Coll.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Oratio} --- marginal no.\ 1573}
Custódi, Dómine, quǽsumus, Ecclésiam tuam propitiatióne perpétua: et quia sine
te lábitur humána mortálitas; tuis semper auxíliis et abstrahátur a nóxiis, et
ad salutária dirigátur. Per Dóminum.
\end{appointedlatin}

\englishgap{No publishable English is supplied. In the controlled Latin, the
Church asks God for perpetual propitiation: because human mortality fails
without him, his help must withdraw it from what harms and direct it toward
what saves. The noun \latin{propitiatio} occurs here and again in the Secret.}

\subsection*{3. Epistle \cue{Ep.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Gálatas.} --- Gal. 5, 16-24 --- marginal no.\ 1574}
Fratres: Spíritu ambuláte, et desidéria carnis non perficiétis. Caro enim
concupíscit advérsus spíritum, spíritus autem advérsus carnem: hæc enim sibi
ínvicem adversántur, ut non quæcúmque vultis, illa faciátis. Quod si spíritu
ducímini, non estis sub lege. Manifésta sunt autem ópera carnis, quæ sunt
fornicátio, immundítia, impudicítia, luxúria, idolórum sérvitus, veneficia,
inimicítiæ, contentiónes, æmulatiónes, iræ, rixæ, dissensiónes, sectæ,
invídiæ, homicídia, ebrietátes, comessatiónes, et his simília: quæ prædíco
vobis, sicut prædíxi: quóniam, qui tália agunt, regnum Dei non consequéntur.
Fructus autem Spíritus est: cáritas, gáudium, pax, patiéntia, benígnitas,
bónitas, longanímitas, mansuetúdo, fides, modéstia, continéntia, cástitas.
Advérsus huiúsmodi non est lex. Qui autem sunt Christi, carnem suam
crucifixérunt cum vítiis et concupiscéntiis.
\end{appointedlatin}

\begin{namedtranslation}{English: Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Gal. 5:16--24}
[\latin{Fratres:}] Walk in the spirit: and you shall not fulfill the lusts of
the flesh. For the flesh lusteth against the spirit: and the spirit against the
flesh: For these are contrary one to another: so that you do not the things
that you would. But if you are led by the spirit, you are not under the law.
Now the works of the flesh are manifest: which are fornication, uncleanness,
immodesty, luxury, Idolatry, witchcrafts, enmities, contentions, emulations,
wraths, quarrels, dissensions, sects, Envies, murders, drunkenness, revellings,
and such like. Of the which I foretell you, as I have foretold to you, that
they who do such things shall not obtain the kingdom of God. But the fruit of
the Spirit is, charity, joy, peace, patience, benignity, goodness, longanimity,
Mildness, faith, modesty, continency, chastity. Against such there is no law.
And they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh, with the vices and
concupiscences.
\end{namedtranslation}

\englishgap{The liturgical address \latin{Fratres:} does not supplement Paul's
opening here; it \emph{replaces} it. Paul's own words at v.~16 are
\latin{Dico autem:}, which the Douay renders ``I say then:'' --- the phrase that
ties this verse back to the freedom of v.~13 and the law fulfilled in love at
v.~14. Paul's own vocative \latin{fratres} stands at v.~13, inside the sentence
the pericope drops. The missal's address is the missal's own and no
registered English witness renders it, \textbf{so it stands bracketed above in
the missal's own Latin and untranslated}, which is what makes the substitution
visible without the guide supplying English of its own for appointed text.}

\subsection*{6. Gospel \cue{Gosp.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthǽum.} --- Mt. 6, 24-33 --- marginal no.\ 1577}
In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Nemo potest duóbus dóminis
servíre: aut enim unum ódio habébit, et álterum díliget: aut unum sustinébit,
et álterum contémnet. Non potéstis Deo servíre, et mammónæ. Ideo dico vobis,
ne sollíciti sitis ánimæ vestræ quid manducétis, neque córpori vestro quid
induámini. Nonne ánima plus est quam esca: et corpus plus quam vestiméntum?
Respícite volatília cæli, quóniam non serunt, neque metunt, neque cóngregant
in hórrea: et Pater vester cæléstis pascit illa. Nonne vos magis pluris estis
illis? Quis autem vestrum cógitans potest adícere ad statúram suam cúbitum
unum? Et de vestiménto quid sollíciti estis? Consideráte lília agri quómodo
crescunt: non labórant, neque nent. Dico autem vobis, quóniam nec Sálomon in
omni glória sua coopértus est sicut unum ex istis. Si autem fænum agri, quod
hódie est, et cras in clíbanum míttitur, Deus sic vestit: quanto magis vos
módicæ fídei? Nolíte ergo sollíciti esse, dicéntes: Quid manducábimus, aut
quid bibémus, aut quo operiémur? Hæc enim ómnia gentes inquírunt. Scit enim
Pater vester, quia his ómnibus indigétis. Quǽrite ergo primum regnum Dei, et
iustítiam eius: et hæc ómnia adiciéntur vobis.
\end{appointedlatin}

\begin{namedtranslation}{English: Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Mt. 6:24--33}
[\latin{In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis:}] No man can serve two
masters. For either he will hate the one, and love the other: or he will
sustain the one, and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.
Therefore I say to you, be not solicitous for your life, what you shall eat,
nor for your body, what you shall put on. Is not the life more than the meat:
and the body more than the raiment? Behold the birds of the air, for they
neither sow, nor do they reap, nor gather into barns: and your heavenly Father
feedeth them. Are not you of much more value than they? And which of you by
taking thought, can add to his stature one cubit? And for raiment why are you
solicitous? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they labour not,
neither do they spin. But I say to you, that not even Solomon in all his glory
was arrayed as one of these. And if the grass of the field, which is to day,
and to morrow is cast into the oven, God doth so clothe: how much more you, O
ye of little faith? Be not solicitous therefore, saying: What shall we eat: or
what shall we drink, or wherewith shall we be clothed? For after all these
things do the heathens seek. For your Father knoweth that you have need of all
these things. Seek ye therefore first the kingdom of God, and his justice, and
all these things shall be added unto you.
\end{namedtranslation}

\textbf{Rubric as printed, immediately after the Gospel:} \latin{Credo.}
\textperiodcentered{} printed on a line of its own below the Gospel's last line.

\englishgap{The incipit \latin{In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis:}
corresponds to nothing in Matthew and therefore to nothing in the Douay. Verse
24 begins bare, in the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, and the missal has to
name a speaker and an audience to let the pericope stand alone. No registered
English witness renders it, so \textbf{it stands bracketed above in the missal's
own Latin and untranslated} --- neither rendered as though it were Matthew's nor
supplied with English of the guide's own.}

\subsection*{7. Offertory \cue{Off.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Ant. ad Offertorium} --- Ps. 33, 8-9 --- marginal no.\ 1578}
Immíttet Angelus Dómini in circúitu timéntium eum, et erípiet eos: gustáte et
vidéte, quóniam suávis est Dóminus.
\end{appointedlatin}

\begin{namedtranslation}{English: Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Ps. 33:8--9 printed whole, with the portion the chant sings in bold}
\textbf{v.~8} \textbf{The angel of the Lord shall encamp round about them that
fear him: and shall deliver them.} \textbf{v.~9} \textbf{O taste, and see that
the Lord is sweet:} blessed is the man that hopeth in him.
\end{namedtranslation}

\englishgap{The antiphon stops at \latin{Dóminus} and does not sing v.~9b,
``blessed is the man that hopeth in him.'' The Douay renders the verse whole,
so it is printed whole with the sung portion marked. The clause that is dropped
here is the clause the Eighth Sunday's Communion keeps, which is why the
omission is discussed in the commentary rather than passed over.}

\subsection*{8. Secret \cue{Sec.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Secreta} --- marginal no.\ 1579}
Concéde nobis, Dómine, quǽsumus, ut hæc hóstia salutáris, et nostrórum fiat
purgátio delictórum, et tuæ propitiátio potestátis. Per Dóminum.
\end{appointedlatin}

\englishgap{No publishable English is supplied. The controlled Latin asks that
the saving victim may cleanse the faithful from their offenses and be a
propitiation of God's power. Its parallel genitives make purification and
propitiation predicates of the same \latin{hóstia salutáris}.}

\textbf{Rubric as printed, immediately after the Secret:}
\latin{Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.} \textperiodcentered{} the Preface of the
Most Holy Trinity. \latin{Ssma} is printed under a tilde abbreviation for
\latin{Sanctissima} and is expanded here from the same book's own abbreviation
practice.

\subsection*{9. Communion \cue{Comm.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Ant. ad Communionem} --- Mt. 6, 33 --- marginal no.\ 1580}
Primum quǽrite regnum Dei, et ómnia adiciéntur vobis, dicit Dóminus.
\end{appointedlatin}

\begin{namedtranslation}{English: Douay--Rheims (Challoner), Mt. 6:33 printed whole and unaltered, with the phrases the antiphon reuses in bold}
\textbf{Seek ye} therefore \textbf{first the kingdom of God,} and his justice,
\textbf{and all these things shall be added unto you.}
\end{namedtranslation}

\englishgap{The Communion is an adaptation of Mt.\ 6:33, not a quotation of it,
and it is the one substantive recasting in this formulary. The Douay verse above
is printed exactly as that edition has it, with nothing inserted, and the
antiphon's departures are described rather than shown inside the quotation. Four
things happen:
\latin{ergo} is dropped and \latin{Primum} promoted to the head of the clause;
\latin{et iustítiam eius} is omitted; \latin{hæc} is dropped before
\latin{ómnia}; and \latin{dicit Dóminus} is added, which stands nowhere in
Matthew at either this verse or its Lucan parallel. The Douay verse is
therefore printed whole with the reused phrases marked, rather than joined into
an English antiphon the project would itself have composed.}

\subsection*{10. Postcommunion \cue{Postcomm.}}

\begin{appointedlatin}{\latin{Postcommunio} --- marginal no.\ 1581}
Puríficent semper et múniant tua sacraménta nos, Deus: et ad perpétuæ ducant
salvatiónis efféctum. Per Dóminum nostrum.
\end{appointedlatin}

\englishgap{No publishable English is supplied. The controlled Latin asks that
God's sacraments may continually purify and strengthen the faithful and bring
them to the effect of everlasting salvation.}
