# Fourteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical full research edition at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/54-fourteenth-after-pentecost` and
its mechanical `-synthesis` PDF companion. The canonical edition prints the
complete Latin below beside the identified English; the synthesis companion
omits the appointed-text component.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1572

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 83, 10-11`
(Vulgate numbering; the common modern English numbering of the same verses is
**Ps. 84:9–10**, and the Masoretic numbering, which counts the superscription as
a verse, is Ps. 84:10–11). The psalm verse is cited `Ps. ibid., 2-3` — English
Ps. 84:1–2, Masoretic 84:2–3. **Corrected 2026-08-27 by the authoring stage:**
this record previously gave only `84:10–11` and labelled it "the Hebrew
numbering". That figure is right as Masoretic and wrong as the common modern
number the profile asks for; see the note under Rights below.

> Protéctor noster, áspice, Deus, et réspice in fáciem Christi tui: quia mélior
> est dies una in átriis tuis super mília. *Ps. ibid., 2-3* Quam dilécta
> tabernácula tua, Dómine virtútum! concupíscit, et déficit ánima mea in átria
> Dómini. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column, below the
formulary heading; PDF p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-26, with the drop-cap `P`
and the verse re-read at 500 dpi.

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** This antiphon is a plain quotation,
not a centonisation: v. 10 entire, then v. 11 as far as `super mília`, the rest
of v. 11 (`elégi abiéctus esse in domo Dei mei…`) being left off. The psalm
verse is vv. 2–3a, likewise cut clean at `in átria Dómini`. The one word that
is not the Clementine's is orthographic — `mília` for `millia` — and the one
capital that is not the Clementine's is `Christi` for its `christi`. Recorded
in the divergence table below; nothing is emended.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation,
or reference issue. No repetition cue is printed after `℣. Glória Patri.`

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1573

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Custódi, Dómine, quǽsumus, Ecclésiam tuam propitiatióne perpétua: et quia
> sine te lábitur humána mortálitas; tuis semper auxíliis et abstrahátur a
> nóxiis, et ad salutária dirigátur. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 395, left column, at the head
of the page; PDF p. 476. **Checked:** 2026-08-26 at 500 dpi for the semicolon
after `mortálitas`, the colon after `perpétua`, and every accent.

**Note on the pointing.** The semicolon is load-bearing and is not a comma: it
divides the prayer's causal clause (`quia sine te lábitur humána mortálitas`)
from the double petition it grounds, and the two limbs of that petition are
then yoked by the `et … et` that straddles `abstrahátur` and `dirigátur` — the
Church is to be *drawn back from* what harms and *steered toward* what saves,
by the same `auxíliis`. The first `et` sits before `abstrahátur`, not before
`tuis`, which is what makes the pair read as a pair.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1574

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Gálatas.` · `Gal. 5, 16-24`.

> Fratres: Spíritu ambuláte, et desidéria carnis non perficiétis. Caro enim
> concupíscit advérsus spíritum, spíritus autem advérsus carnem: hæc enim sibi
> ínvicem adversántur, ut non quæcúmque vultis, illa faciátis. Quod si spíritu
> ducímini, non estis sub lege. Manifésta sunt autem ópera carnis, quæ sunt
> fornicátio, immundítia, impudicítia, luxúria, idolórum sérvitus, veneficia,
> inimicítiæ, contentiónes, æmulatiónes, iræ, rixæ, dissensiónes, sectæ,
> invídiæ, homicídia, ebrietátes, comessatiónes, et his simília: quæ prædíco
> vobis, sicut prædíxi: quóniam, qui tália agunt, regnum Dei non consequéntur.
> Fructus autem Spíritus est: cáritas, gáudium, pax, patiéntia, benígnitas,
> bónitas, longanímitas, mansuetúdo, fides, modéstia, continéntia, cástitas.
> Advérsus huiúsmodi non est lex. Qui autem sunt Christi, carnem suam
> crucifixérunt cum vítiis et concupiscéntiis.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 395, left column; PDF p. 476.
**Checked:** 2026-08-26, the vice-list and the fruit-list re-read at 500 dpi
word by word, since both are long strings of near-identical endings.

**Notes.** `veneficia` is printed **without** an accent, alone among the
polysyllables in the vice-list; read at 500 dpi, the `fi` is a plain ligature
and carries no acute. It is reproduced unaccented, as printed.

The liturgical incipit `Fratres:` does more work here than the same word does
on the Thirteenth Sunday. There it was prefixed to a verse that began
`Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes`; here it **replaces** Paul's own opening of
v. 16, `Dico autem:`. The lesson therefore opens with an address the apostle
did not write at this point, and drops the `Dico autem` that in Galatians ties
v. 16 back to the freedom of v. 13.

The pericope stops at v. 24. Paul's `Si spíritu vívimus, spíritu et ambulémus`
(v. 25) is not in this lesson — it is the incipit of the *next* Sunday's, which
begins at Gal. 5:25 and is printed on the facing pages.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1577

**Printed heading and reference:** `✠ Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum
Matthǽum.` · `Mt. 6, 24-33`.

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Nemo potest duóbus dóminis
> servíre: aut enim unum ódio habébit, et álterum díliget: aut unum sustinébit,
> et álterum contémnet. Non potéstis Deo servíre, et mammónæ. Ideo dico vobis,
> ne sollíciti sitis ánimæ vestræ quid manducétis, neque córpori vestro quid
> induámini. Nonne ánima plus est quam esca: et corpus plus quam vestiméntum?
> Respícite volatília cæli, quóniam non serunt, neque metunt, neque cóngregant
> in hórrea: et Pater vester cæléstis pascit illa. Nonne vos magis pluris estis
> illis? Quis autem vestrum cógitans potest adícere ad statúram suam cúbitum
> unum? Et de vestiménto quid sollíciti estis? Consideráte lília agri quómodo
> crescunt: non labórant, neque nent. Dico autem vobis, quóniam nec Sálomon in
> omni glória sua coopértus est sicut unum ex istis. Si autem fænum agri, quod
> hódie est, et cras in clíbanum míttitur, Deus sic vestit: quanto magis vos
> módicæ fídei? Nolíte ergo sollíciti esse, dicéntes: Quid manducábimus, aut
> quid bibémus, aut quo operiémur? Hæc enim ómnia gentes inquírunt. Scit enim
> Pater vester, quia his ómnibus indigétis. Quǽrite ergo primum regnum Dei, et
> iustítiam eius: et hæc ómnia adiciéntur vobis.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Credo.` (printed on a line of its own below
the last Gospel line).

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 395, right column; PDF p. 476.
**Checked:** 2026-08-26 at 500 dpi for `nec Sálomon` (the PDF's own OCR layer
misreads it `nee`), `adícere`, `fænum`, and every question mark in the
passage — there are six, and they carry the argument.

**Notes.** The pericope is the second half of the Sermon's treatment of
mammon and anxiety: v. 24 states the alternative, and v. 33 resolves it. The
appointed lesson stops one verse before Matthew's own conclusion, `Nolíte ergo
sollíciti esse in crástinum` (v. 34), so the Sunday ends on the promise rather
than on the counsel about tomorrow.

The incipit `In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis:` is supplied
entirely by the missal. Matthew has no such words here; v. 24 begins bare with
`Nemo potest duóbus dóminis servíre`, in the middle of the Sermon on the Mount,
and the missal must name a speaker and an audience to make the pericope stand
alone. The audience it names — `discípulis suis` — is the missal's own
specification: Matthew 5:1 has Jesus teaching the disciples with the crowds

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording issue. The Gospel's

## 8. Secret — marginal no. 1579

**Printed heading:** `Secreta`.

> Concéde nobis, Dómine, quǽsumus, ut hæc hóstia salutáris, et nostrórum fiat
> purgátio delictórum, et tuæ propitiátio potestátis. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, left column, at the head
of the page; PDF p. 477. **Checked:** 2026-08-26 at 500 dpi.

**Note on the construction.** The two `et` clauses are parallel predicates of
the one subject `hæc hóstia salutáris`, and the second genitive is objective in
a different way from the first: the victim is `purgátio` *of our offences* and
`propitiátio` *of thy power* — the second phrase means the appeasing of God's
power, not a power belonging to the propitiation. The word order, with
`nostrórum` held back across the line from `delictórum`, is as printed.

**Rubric immediately following:** `Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.` — the Preface
of the Most Holy Trinity, printed with `Ssma` under a tilde abbreviation for
`Sanctissima`. Expanded here from the same book's own abbreviation practice.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1580

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Communionem` · `Mt. 6, 33`.

> Primum quǽrite regnum Dei, et ómnia adiciéntur vobis, dicit Dóminus.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 396, left column; PDF p. 477.
**Checked:** 2026-08-26 at 500 dpi.

**Note.** The antiphon is an adaptation of the Gospel's own last verse, not a
quotation of it, and it is the one substantive recasting in this formulary.
Four things happen to Mt. 6:33: `ergo` is dropped and `Primum` promoted to the
head; `et iustítiam eius` is omitted; `hæc` is dropped before `ómnia`; and
`dicit Dóminus` is added, which stands nowhere in Matthew. The effect is to
turn a clause of the Sermon into a saying attributed to the Lord at the moment
of Communion. The details are in the divergence table below and in the declared
English gaps that follow it.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.
