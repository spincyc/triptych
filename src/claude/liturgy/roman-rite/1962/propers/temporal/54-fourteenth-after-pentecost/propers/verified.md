## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1572

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 83, 10-11`
(Vulgate numbering; the common modern English numbering of the same verses is
**Ps. 84:9–10**, and the Masoretic numbering, which counts the superscription as
a verse, is Ps. 84:10–11). The psalm verse is cited `Ps. ibid., 2-3` — English
Ps. 84:1–2, Masoretic 84:2–3. **Corrected 2026-08-27 by the authoring stage:**
this record previously gave only `84:10–11` and labelled it "the Hebrew
numbering". That figure is right as Masoretic and wrong as the common modern
number the profile asks for; see the note under Rights below.

> Protéctor noster, áspice, Deus, et réspice in fáciem Christi tui: quia mélior
> est dies una in átriis tuis super mília. *Ps. ibid., 2-3* Quam dilécta
> tabernácula tua, Dómine virtútum! concupíscit, et déficit ánima mea in átria
> Dómini. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 394, right column, below the
formulary heading; PDF p. 475. **Checked:** 2026-08-26, with the drop-cap `P`
and the verse re-read at 500 dpi.

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** This antiphon is a plain quotation,
not a centonisation: v. 10 entire, then v. 11 as far as `super mília`, the rest
of v. 11 (`elégi abiéctus esse in domo Dei mei…`) being left off. The psalm
verse is vv. 2–3a, likewise cut clean at `in átria Dómini`. The one word that
is not the Clementine's is orthographic — `mília` for `millia` — and the one
capital that is not the Clementine's is `Christi` for its `christi`. Recorded
in the divergence table below; nothing is emended.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation,
or reference issue. No repetition cue is printed after `℣. Glória Patri.`

**Witness:** Vatican typical edition, printed p. 395, left column, at the head

**Note on the pointing.** The semicolon is load-bearing and is not a comma: it
divides the prayer's causal clause (`quia sine te lábitur humána mortálitas`)
from the double petition it grounds, and the two limbs of that petition are
then yoked by the `et … et` that straddles `abstrahátur` and `dirigátur` — the

**Notes.** `veneficia` is printed **without** an accent, alone among the

type, not a peculiarity of this one word, and reading it as a peculiarity cost
this record two accents it should never have printed.** The typical edition's
text face sets `fi` as a ligature with no room for an acute above the `i`, so
**every** word whose accent would fall on that `i` is printed bare. It happens
three times in this formulary — `veneficia` here at Gal. 5:20, `confidere`
twice in the Gradual, and `fidei` in the Gospel at Mt. 6:30 — and this record
carried the first correctly while silently accenting the other two. Both are
corrected at their own elements below, and the rule is stated once here so that
no later reading of this formulary has to rediscover it. The evidence is read,
not inferred: at 500 ppi the same glyph stands in all four occurrences, with the
`f`'s hood closing over a dotless `i` and nothing above it, while `impudicí-`
and `sérvitus` on the same two lines, and
`princípibus`, `hómine` and `Dómino` in the Gradual and `Nolíte` and
`sollíciti` in the Gospel, carry their acutes plainly. Off this formulary the
same face does the same thing wherever it was looked at — `fidei` at Gal. 6:10
in the Fifteenth Sunday's Epistle on p. 396, and `sacrificium`, `sacrificia`
and `confido` on printed p. 390 (PDF p. 471) — so this is the book's habit and
not damage to these three pages. The Benziger text layer independently shows no
accent at `confidere` or `fidei` either, which is corroboration and not the
ground: the ground is the page image of the controlling witness. Nothing about
the *words* changes; what changes is that this record now prints the
accentuation the typical edition prints.

The liturgical incipit `Fratres:` does more work here than the same word does
on the Thirteenth Sunday. There it was prefixed to a verse that began
`Abrahæ dictæ sunt promissiónes`; here it **replaces** Paul's own opening of
v. 16, `Dico autem:`. The lesson therefore opens with an address the apostle
did not write at this point, and drops the `Dico autem` that in Galatians ties
v. 16 back to the freedom of v. 13.

The pericope stops at v. 24. Paul's `Si spíritu vívimus, spíritu et ambulémus`
(v. 25) is not in this lesson — it is the incipit of the *next* Sunday's, which
begins at Gal. 5:25 and is printed on the facing pages.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved issue.
