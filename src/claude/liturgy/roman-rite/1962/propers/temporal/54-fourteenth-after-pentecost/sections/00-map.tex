\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & A psalm asks God to look on the face of his Christ and prefers one day in his courts to thousands \cue{Int.}. Paul sets seventeen works of the flesh against twelve fruits of the Spirit \cue{Ep.}. Christ forbids the service of two masters and anxiety about food and clothing, and commands that the kingdom be sought first \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Allegorical & Augustine refuses the surface of \latin{réspice in fáciem Christi tui} and reads it as a prayer that God make his Christ known to all; Guéranger reads it of the Church presenting the face of the Christ who is also hers, and both are kept \cue{Int.}. He reads \latin{gustáte et vidéte} of the Eucharist \cue{Off.}. \textbf{The limit belongs here:} on this Gospel he forbids allegorising the birds and lilies \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Moral & The either/or of \latin{Non potéstis Deo servíre, et mammónæ} \cue{Gosp.} is answered by order, not refusal: \latin{Primum} \cue{Comm.}. Chrysostom argues from the vice-list's own contents that \latin{caro} here is not the body; Aquinas answers that objection by another route \cue{Ep.}.\\
Anagogical & \latin{Mélior est dies una in átriis tuis super mília} \cue{Int.}: Augustine reads that one day as \latin{diem sempitérnum, cui non cedit hestérnus, quem non urget crastínus} --- though his exposition runs on past the antiphon's cut. \latin{Perpétuæ \dots\ salvatiónis efféctum} \cue{Postcomm.} is the Mass's only reach past this life.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
