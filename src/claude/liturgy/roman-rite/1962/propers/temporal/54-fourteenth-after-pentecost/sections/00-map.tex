\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & The psalms voice longing, trust, praise, protection, and tasting; Paul contrasts the works of the flesh with the fruit of the Spirit; Christ names the rivalry between God and mammon and commands the kingdom to be sought first.\\
Allegorical & Augustine and Cassiodorus read the psalmic courts and saving God in Christ and his pilgrim Church; the saving victim and the strengthening sacraments make the movement sacramental. Hilary's allegory of the birds and lilies remains distinct from Jerome's insistence on their creaturely force.\\
Moral & Holy desire is ordered toward the kingdom. Grace enables the walk in the Spirit, trust refuses to make creatures ultimate, and the Father's care frees prudent labor from anxious servitude.\\
Anagogical & The one better day in God's courts opens toward beatitude, while the final prayer asks that purification and fortitude lead to the effect of perpetual salvation.\\
\end{sensestable}
