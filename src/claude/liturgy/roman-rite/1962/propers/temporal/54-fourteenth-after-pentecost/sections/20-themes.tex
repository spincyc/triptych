\section*{The Propers: Themes and Movement}
\label{triptych:brief-synthesis:start}

This Mass was assembled from three strands counted on different schedules and
settled at different dates, and one of its ten elements was exchanged rather
than inherited: the Communion. \textbf{That element is where the
formulary's single thrice-repeated substantive word, its single
dropped word and its single contested adverb all stand}, and around it the
propers state an absolute either/or without contempt for created goods.

\subsection*{1. The kingdom, said three times}

\latin{Regnum Dei} is rare in Matthew --- four verses against the thirty-two
that carry \latin{regnum caelorum}, thirty-four times over. It stands
verbatim in three appointed elements, and \textbf{no other exact form reaching
three or more of the ten is anything but a divine name or a function word}.
Negative and positive are one claim: \latin{qui tália agunt, regnum
Dei non consequéntur}; \latin{Quǽrite ergo primum regnum Dei}; then the same
imperative made an address at reception, \latin{dicit Dóminus}.

\textbf{And the element that repeats the phrase is the element that cuts it.}
\latin{Iustítia} stands once in the whole appointed Latin, in the Gospel's last
verse --- and the Communion, quoting that verse, drops it. \textbf{That clause
closes an arc the Vulgate opens at Mt.\ 6:1}, where Robinson--Pierpont's
Byzantine text reads \emph{alms}, and \latin{dicit Dóminus} stands nowhere in
Matthew. \textbf{Chrysostom rests the promise on the verb the antiphon keeps} ---
``he said not, \emph{shall be given}, but \emph{shall be added},'' in the
\work{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers} English, first series --- and
\textbf{his own citation drops \latin{et iustítiam eius} as the antiphon does},
so the abbreviation is ordinary, as is a Communion drawn from the day's Gospel;
on that side the spine may be two elements and an echo, the Epistle's standing
alone.

\subsection*{2. The flesh that is crucified is not the body that is fed}

\latin{Carn-} stands five times in the Epistle and nowhere else, \latin{corp-}
twice in the Gospel and nowhere else. \textbf{That makes the two lessons one
unit, not a contrast}, and a reading setting a mortifying Epistle against a
providing Gospel has inverted it. \textbf{The Introit's cut keeps the division
clean:} its psalm verse stops one clause before \latin{cor meum et caro mea
exsultaverunt in Deum vivum}, the one clause that would have given \latin{caro}
a positive sense.

\textbf{Theodoret and Augustine reach the same conclusion about that flesh by
three different middle terms.} Theodoret
argues, verse by verse, that idolatry and sorcery belong to the soul, so Paul
accuses not the flesh but the slothful cast of mind, and that a list of the
soul's works would otherwise stand beside them. \textbf{Augustine argues from the ascetic
idolater}, convicted of works of the flesh while abstaining from bodily
pleasure, and from ordinary speech, nobody saying \latin{Malam carnem} to an enemy. \textbf{Nothing shows that either knew the other.}

\textbf{Four further positions stand on \latin{caro} at v.~17}, and Jerome
defends the missal's own \latin{vitia} against a reading of the body, \latin{ut non
naturam corporis videretur in spiritualibus viris negare, sed vitia}. The
disjunction holds of these two pericopes and is no law of the two words;
Ambrosiaster's lemma is not the missal's, so he does not speak to the two
lists; and \latin{luxúria}, \latin{modéstia} and \latin{cástitas} answer nothing
in the Greek these Fathers read.

\subsection*{3. Divided at the images, and the most-repeated sentence withdrawn by the man who wrote it}

\textbf{The Latin tradition is divided by name about whether the Gospel's images
may be figures.} \textbf{Augustine states the refusal in his own exposition of
these verses}: the birds and the lilies ``are not to be treated as
allegories,'' in the English of the \work{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers},
first series. \textbf{Christian of Stavelot} refuses it at the lilies on
aesthetic grounds, Jerome forbids reading the birds as angels, and Bruno of
Segni reads them literally. At the far end
\textbf{Godfrey of Admont} makes the birds good angels, the lilies evil spirits
and Solomon Christ; \textbf{Hilary allegorises every image into doctrine of the
risen body}; and \textbf{Chrysostom refuses a different over-reading}, Christ
abolishing not the sowing but the anxiety.

\textbf{And the sentence the tradition repeats most often at this Gospel's first
verse was withdrawn by its author.} Augustine's \latin{Nullius enim fere
conscientia Deum potest odisse} is retracted at \work{Retractationes} I.19,
naming his own exposition of this verse; five later witnesses repeat it \emph{without his qualifier} \latin{fere}; and
\textbf{Bruno of Segni denies it outright}, the covetous man serving idols and
therefore hating God.
\textbf{At Mt.\ 6:33 the argument narrows to a single adverb}: Augustine reads
\latin{primum} as an ordering of importance and not of time, which the
compilations transmit under his name as \latin{non témpore, sed dignitáte},
where \textbf{Paschasius denies that it marks order at all.}

\subsection*{4. Three excerpts stop short of one formula, and three chants divide the Fathers}

\latin{Beatus \dots\ qui sperat} stands in exactly two verses of the Clementine,
and they are this Mass's Offertory and Introit psalms, Ps.\ 33:9b and
Ps.\ 83:13 --- past both the Introit's cuts and one clause past the
Offertory's, while
\textbf{the Gradual alone is uncut and sings the hope vocabulary outright},
\latin{sper-} and \latin{confid-} twice each and in no other element.
\textbf{The Introit belongs because the formula lies past its boundaries, not
because it uses hope vocabulary}, and those extents are inherited.

Three chants carry divisions the Mass prints without adjudicating. \textbf{The
Offertory's angel has four accounts} --- Augustine's Christ, Cassiodorus's moral
sense, Arnobius's angel of the sepulchre and Godfrey's gift of grace.
\textbf{The Gradual's princes have four positions and a question}: Augustine's
good angels, Cassiodorus's devil and good angel, Theodoret's temporary earthly
rulers, Godfrey's interior and exterior senses --- while \textbf{Bruno the
Carthusian asks whether the verse is comparative at all}, where
\textbf{Chrysostom answers that it is not}. \textbf{So the angel who rescues at one chant is the excluded
object of trust at the other}, and Augustine softens that only by taking the
Offertory's \latin{Angelus} for Christ. The Alleluia is still the least
integrated: Cassiodorus hears in \latin{iubilémus} a wordless jubilus and
\textbf{Theodoret the shout of victors}; its medieval readers read the psalm
penitentially through a verse it does not appoint, \textbf{but Augustine
draws right joy against wrong joy from the appointed verse itself} --- the
Gospel's and the Gradual's own shape.

\subsection*{5. Three strands, one exchange, and the goods the Mass never asks for}

\textbf{The strands were counted from different feasts.} The Gregorian Hadrianum
heads this Mass \latin{Dominica XV post Pentecosten}, one later than 1962;
Menard's book and the Frankish Gelasian books stand two later.
\textbf{The chants did not move with them}: in the Roman antiphonary of PL~78
the Introit and Offertory agree with 1962 across twelve weeks, the first of
which that book's own body marks absent from the \latin{Antiphonarium Romanum},
while the Gradual runs a week behind and breaks at this Sunday and its
neighbour.
\textbf{And the readings never moved}: the \work{Liber Comitis} pairs this
Epistle with this Gospel, cut at the verses 1962 cuts. \textbf{Four mechanisms stand and no two agree}: Wilson's numeration base;
Schuster's Roman cycle counted from St~Lawrence, whose reckoning varied between
books; \textbf{Berno of Reichenau's two compilers}, on the embolismic and the
common years; and the \work{Micrologus}'s vacant Sundays after the Ember
fasts.

\textbf{And the arrangement 1962 does not have is a lectionary tradition, not
one commentator's displacement.} Honorius sets this Sunday's Introit, Collect,
Epistle and Offertory beside the ten lepers of Luke~17, as do Rupert, Sicard
and Durandus; the Comes of Murbach pairs them so, one instance of a whole
pattern of epistle~$N$ with gospel~$N{-}1$; and Sarum and the 1662 Book of
Common Prayer still do.
\textbf{When or why the Roman books moved to the pairing 1962 prints, none
says.}

\textbf{The one exchange is at the Communion.}
\latin{Primum quaerite} is an ancient Roman antiphon standing at the
\emph{Ninth} Sunday, where this Sunday's Communion is
\latin{Panis quem ego dedero} --- which travels with the Offertory at
\emph{both} its occurrences. \textbf{All three legs
of that rotation stand in the body of one printed chant book}, PL~78 giving
\latin{Primum quaerite} to the ninth, \latin{Panis quem ego dedero} to the
fourteenth and \latin{Qui manducat carnem meam} to the fifteenth, exactly
reversing 1962. \textbf{So the antiphon is secondary in this slot only and not
in the repertory}, the substitution separating two chants the older books kept
together. \textbf{That book dates the state and not the change}, and when the
exchange was made no witness says.
\textbf{What the Mass asks for and what it promises do not meet.}
\latin{Salut-} and \latin{salv-} stand once each in the Collect, the Alleluia,
the Secret and the Postcommunion, and in none of the other six elements. \textbf{No oration of the fifteen collated 1962 formularies
asks for food, drink or clothing}, so this Sunday's silence about its Gospel's
three goods is a genre fact --- though four of them ask for peace, health or
the world's ordering. \textbf{The singularity lies on the reading side:} this
Sunday alone of the fifteen names eating, drinking \emph{and} clothing together.
\textbf{And the orations withhold agency from the assembly:} in all three the
acting subject is God, the victim or the sacraments --- the settled habit of
the Roman collect, with no concordance of other orations behind the contrast.
\textbf{The assembly acts in its own right in five elements, every one a reading
or a chant} --- the Epistle, the Alleluia, the Gospel, the Offertory and the
Communion --- \textbf{and the Gospel states the same morphology}: the
active denied of the man who cannot add a cubit, the passive promised him.
