\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

{\footnotesize Six passages are appointed; the Communion adapts a verse inside
one and shares its row. Psalms carry the missal's Vulgate number, the common
modern number in parentheses. Summaries are \emph{by choice},
\emph{summarise-only} (rights unresolved) or \emph{compelled} (rights
restricted), as the scope appendix sets out. \textbf{One modern critical horizon
is held, for the Epistle alone}; no NABRE artifact is registered for Ps.~34, 84,
95 or 118, or for Matthew, so the other rows mark the absence. \textbf{Not held}
and \textbf{no locus} are bounded negatives.\par}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps. 33:8--9a (Eng. 34:7--8) & Title: before Achimelech, 1~Kings~21 & David's reign 1055--1015~BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The superscription anchors the psalm to a named escape,
\latin{Davidi, cum immutavit vultum suum coram Achimelech}; the tracked Douay
prints the cross-reference ``[1 Kings 21.]'' --- modern \textbf{1~Samuel~21} in
Douay book-naming --- and \latin{eripiet eos} at v.~8 repeats \latin{eripuit
me} at v.~5. \textbf{Read whole, the chapter does not fit the name:}
Achimelech the priest stands at Nobe in vv.~1--9 and the change of countenance
falls at v.~13 before \textbf{Achis, king of Geth}; the CPDV prints
\latin{Abimelech}. The divergence is recorded, not explained. Corbett puts the
reign at 1055--1015~BC and a pre-royal flight before it. Bellarmine is held and
verified, but in abridged Victorian English at vv.~2--3, reaching no appointed
verse; Haydock's A.M.~2944 is rights-\emph{unresolved}, summarise-only, with no
passage here. \textbf{The alphabetic acrostic is now carried, as documented
reception and not as a fact about the Hebrew:} Cassiodorus states it in his
\latin{Divisio psalmi} --- the Hebrew letters, the sixth excepted, heading the
verses in order --- and tags the Offertory's two verses \textbf{HETH} and
\textbf{TETH}. \textbf{He is a sixth-century Latin reader reporting about the
Hebrew}; no Hebrew psalter is held here. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 83:10--11a, 2--3a (Eng. 84:9--10a, 84:1--2a) & Sons of Core; movement toward the temple & Korahite; no date held\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus filiis Core} is
tracked in Clementine and Douay, and Scripture names the sons of Core
\emph{both} singers and doorkeepers (Num.\ 26:11; 1~Par.\ 6 and 9), so the
temple-singer horizon rests on held text. \textbf{Those support movement toward
the temple; that the psalm is \emph{for} pilgrims the text does not state, and
neither does this guide.} The Biblical Commission's 1910 responsa affirm the
antiquity of the psalm titles and David as principal author --- a criterion
about titles generally, \textbf{not a ruling on this psalm} (\work{AAS}~2
(1910) 354--355, verified). \textbf{Newly read:} Theodoret on Ps.~83 was
retrieved this run and \textbf{read in Greek} at PG~80:1541--1545 ---
inspected, no PG page image collated, still no registered passage record. He
sets the psalm among the Babylonian captives; \textbf{that setting is his own
reconstruction and not something the psalm states}, so no Date cell is built on
it. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 94:1 (Eng. 95:1) & No place or episode named & Davidic; no year\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Clementine prints the title \latin{Laus cantici ipsi David}
\emph{inside} v.~1, which is why the two numberings agree here --- though this
repository's Douay and Douay-American print \emph{no} title at Ps.~94. The
attribution does not rest on the title: \textbf{Heb.\ 4:7 quotes the psalm
\latin{in David dicendo}}, by name, in tracked text, and the responsa treat
psalms expressly attributed to David in Scripture as settled. Brenton carries
the Clementine's numbers at all four psalms, so the one-verse offset elsewhere
is the \emph{English} convention's and not the Vulgate's. \textbf{Read in Greek
this run}, on the Introit row's footing and with the same bounds: Theodoret
reports the psalm \textbf{untitled among the Hebrews} and composes it in the
person of King Josiah foreseeing his reform --- \textbf{his own reconstruction
from 4~Kgs 22--23 and not what the psalm states}. \textbf{No modern critical
horizon.}}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps. 117:8--9 (Eng. 118:8--9) & No superscription; thanksgiving & \textbf{None held here}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{This row's load-bearing negative rests entirely on
tracked public-domain Bible text.} No superscription stands at Ps.~117 in any
of the seven tracked witnesses, all counting 29 verses, so Vulgate and modern
numbers agree throughout. Clementine and CPDV do print \latin{Alleluja} at the
head of v.~1, a rubric-word and not an ascription, not numbered separately ---
and \textbf{Theodoret's own psalm heading, read in Greek this run, is the same
word} (\emph{Allelouia}, transliterated), inspected, no registered passage
record. The scene is in the verses: rescue (5), the gates of justice (19--20),
the altar (27), inside an inclusio at vv.~1 and 29. \textbf{What the text does
not supply is the speaker as king or the occasion as a victory.} \textbf{The
Date cell is a real negative:} the New Testament quotes this psalm \emph{as
Scripture and never as David's} --- \latin{caput anguli} standing at exactly
five New Testament verses besides v.~22, and v.~6 word for word at Heb.\ 13:6.
This guide does not deny that the psalm is David's; nothing held here says it
is. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}

Gospel, Communion & Mt. 6:24--33; v.~33 recast & Judaea, in Hebrew letters, for Jews & No year held for the Sermon\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: within the Sermon on the Mount, on an unnamed mountain
(Mt.\ 5:1) in the Galilean ministry around Capharnaum (4:12--13, 23), before
crowds from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan (4:25).
The pericope opens on the discourse's hinge --- v.~24 closes the treasure
saying, and v.~25's \latin{Ideo dico vobis} depends on it --- and stops one
verse before Matthew's own conclusion. The one ministry chronology
held here, Maas's, does not reach the Sermon, so no year is given and no
present-day equivalence is asserted for the place.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{The traditional side of this row is thinner than it
looks, and the reason is printed.} Held and citable: Maas, \work{Catholic
Encyclopedia}~VIII pp.~378, 380--381, with Matthew's tracked text.
\textbf{The four witnesses a Matthaean dossier would rest on are not held in
the forms it needs.} \textbf{Papias:} no source record of any kind, and
\work{Hist.\ eccl.}\ III.39.16, the locus transmitting him, unregistered.
\textbf{Eusebius} III.24.6 and III.39.16: only Book~VI is held, its English
witness rights-restricted and its public-domain Greek witness Books~VI--X.
\textbf{Irenaeus} III.1.1: tracked public-domain bytes, no registered passage
record. \textbf{Jerome} \work{De viris illustribus}~3: in a held public-domain
artifact, but registered at ch.~2, in English only against a claim of Latin
reading, and no longer byte-reproducible. \textbf{Nothing is asserted from any
of the four}, nor from the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} articles ``Gospel of
St. Matthew'' and ``Epistle to the Galatians,'' citable at exact loci but
retrieved by no lane. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal. 5:16--24 & Galatia; region named in Acts & AD~58 / AD~54--55\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The letter supplies its own author and destination (Gal.\ 1:1--2;
4:13--15; 6:11), with Acts 16:6 and 18:23 for the region. \textbf{Cornelius a
Lapide} reports the ancient subscriptions and the Fathers for \textbf{Rome},
Chrysostom and Baronius for \textbf{Ephesus}, holds no certain determination of
time or place possible, and dates it \textbf{AD~58} after Baronius
(\latin{Argumentum} p.~457, \latin{prooemium} p.~19, Antwerp 1614 --- verified).
The one horizon held here favours a northern audience near Ancyra, composition
at Ephesus about AD~54--55, a southern alternative at perhaps AD~48--50, and
pagan-convert recipients pressed to add Mosaic observances; rights-restricted,
so its \textbf{summary is compelled} and it is nowhere quoted. \textbf{Not
held:} Jerome, \work{Comm. in Gal.} lib.~II prol.\ --- \textbf{though lib.~III
on the appointed verses was read in Latin this run} --- so who the Galatians
were is unsourced here and not given.}
