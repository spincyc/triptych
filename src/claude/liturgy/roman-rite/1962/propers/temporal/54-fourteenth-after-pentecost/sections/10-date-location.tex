\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

{\footnotesize Six passages are appointed; the Communion adapts a verse inside
the Gospel, so seven liturgical elements appear below. Psalms carry the
missal's Vulgate number, the common modern number in parentheses.\par}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps. 33:8--9a (Eng. 34:7--8) & Title: before Achimelech, 1~Kings~21 & \chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The superscription anchors the psalm to a named escape,
\latin{Davidi, cum immutavit vultum suum coram Achimelech}, cross-referenced in
the Douay to ``[1 Kings 21.],'' modern \textbf{1~Samuel~21}. \textbf{The title
and the chapter name different men.} The title names Achimelech; in the chapter
Achimelech stands at Nobe and the change of countenance falls before
\textbf{Achis, king of Geth}, and the Catholic Public Domain Version prints
\latin{Abimelech} at the title. The A.M.~2944 date is the edition's
uninitialled, Ussher-attributed marginal apparatus, admitted through the
chronology corpus's Ussher reporting exception. The composition date is only a
whole-Psalter latest boundary: no individual psalm is securely dated by it.}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 83:10--11a, 2--3a (Eng. 84:9--10a, 84:1--2a) & Sons of Core; movement toward the temple & \chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title reads \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus filiis Core},
and Scripture names the sons of Core \emph{both} singers and doorkeepers
(Num.\ 26:11; 1~Par.\ 6 and 9). \textbf{The text does not state that the psalm
is \emph{for} pilgrims}, and the received chronology attaches it to no event and
no superscription setting. The Biblical Commission's 1910 responsa
affirm the psalm titles' antiquity and David as principal author ---
\textbf{a criterion about titles generally, not a ruling on this psalm}
(\work{AAS}~2 (1910) 354--355, verified). Theodoret's
Greek at PG~80:1541--1545 sets the psalm among the Babylonian
captives: \textbf{his own reconstruction.} The narrower traditional Korahite
composition claim resolves the same relation, so the source hierarchy does
not substitute the critical profile's generic whole-Psalter boundary.}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 94:1 (Eng. 95:1) & No place or episode named & \chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Clementine prints the title \latin{Laus cantici ipsi David}
\emph{inside} v.~1, so the two numberings agree here; the Douay and the
Douay-American print \emph{no} title at Ps.~94.
\textbf{Hebrews quotes this psalm \latin{in David dicendo} at Heb.\ 4:7, and
that is a New Testament attribution and not a date}; it quotes vv.~8--11, which
the Alleluia does not appoint. Theodoret's Greek reports the
psalm \textbf{untitled among the Hebrews} and composes it in the person of King
Josiah --- \textbf{his own reconstruction.} The critical-profile source gives
only the whole-Psalter boundary before c.~165~B.C. and expressly declines to
date an individual psalm securely.}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps. 117:8--9 (Eng. 118:8--9) & No superscription; thanksgiving & \chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{No superscription stands at Ps.~117}, a psalm
of 29 verses; the Clementine and
the Catholic Public Domain Version print \latin{Alleluja} at the head of v.~1,
a rubric-word and not an ascription, and \textbf{Theodoret's Greek heads the
psalm with that same word.} \textbf{The text supplies neither the speaker
as king nor the occasion as a victory}, and the New Testament quotes this psalm
\emph{as Scripture and never as David's}. The critical-profile source gives
only the whole-Psalter boundary before c.~165~B.C. and expressly declines to
date an individual psalm securely.}
\midrule
Gospel & Mt. 6:24--33 & Judaea, in Hebrew letters, for Jews & \chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: within the Sermon on the Mount, on an unnamed mountain
(Mt.\ 5:1) in the Galilean ministry around Capharnaum (4:12--13, 23), before
crowds from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan (4:25).
\textbf{The received chronology dates that occasion nowhere}, and Maas's
ministry chronology, the one that would reach it, stops short of the Sermon.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The six dates above stand in volumes~X and~XIV of \work{The
Catholic Encyclopedia} (New York: Robert Appleton, 1911--1912), in those
volumes' own words, and they disagree with one another about when the Gospel
was written. No separate critical-profile Matthew composition claim is
returned.}
\midrule
Communion & Mt. 6:33 recast & The Gospel's setting; no separate occasion & \chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Communion adapts the Gospel's final appointed verse, so its
composition candidates are Matthew's. It supplies no separate narrated
occasion to date.}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal. 5:16--24 & Galatia; region named in Acts & \chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The letter names its own author and destination (Gal.\ 1:1--2;
4:13--15), with Acts 16:6 and 18:23 for the region. \textbf{Cornelius a Lapide}
reports the subscriptions and Fathers for \textbf{Rome}, Chrysostom and Baronius
for \textbf{Ephesus}. He reproduces Baronius's A.D.~58 chronology (Antwerp
1614), while his separate \emph{Argumentum} says that time and place cannot be
determined with certainty.}
\end{dossiertable}
