\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

{\footnotesize Six passages are appointed; the Communion adapts a verse inside
one and shares its row. Psalms carry the missal's Vulgate number, the common
modern number in parentheses.\par}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps. 33:8--9a (Eng. 34:7--8) & Title: before Achimelech, 1~Kings~21 & \chronodate{offertory}{The occasion the superscription names: \chronology{israel.monarchy.david-at-geth}{superscription-setting}{A.~M. 2944, A.~C. 1060}. \textbf{Not the writing of the psalm.}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The superscription anchors the psalm to a named escape,
\latin{Davidi, cum immutavit vultum suum coram Achimelech}, cross-referenced in
the Douay to ``[1 Kings 21.],'' modern \textbf{1~Samuel~21}. \textbf{The title
and the chapter name different men.} The title names Achimelech; in the chapter
Achimelech stands at Nobe and the change of countenance falls before
\textbf{Achis, king of Geth}, and the Catholic Public Domain Version prints
\latin{Abimelech} at the title. The date above is \textbf{Haydock's}, given in
his commentary on that chapter. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 83:10--11a, 2--3a (Eng. 84:9--10a, 84:1--2a) & Sons of Core; movement toward the temple & \chronodate{introit}{\chronology{composition.psalms-of-the-sons-of-korah}{composition}{between the days of Isaias and the return from exile}. \textbf{Relative, and said of the whole Korahite group.}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title reads \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus filiis Core},
and Scripture names the sons of Core \emph{both} singers and doorkeepers
(Num.\ 26:11; 1~Par.\ 6 and 9). \textbf{The text does not state that the psalm
is \emph{for} pilgrims}, and the received chronology attaches it to no event and
no superscription setting. The Biblical Commission's 1910 responsa
affirm the psalm titles' antiquity and David as principal author ---
\textbf{a criterion about titles generally, not a ruling on this psalm}
(\work{AAS}~2 (1910) 354--355, verified). Theodoret's
Greek at PG~80:1541--1545 sets the psalm among the Babylonian
captives: \textbf{his own reconstruction.} \textbf{No modern critical
horizon.}}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 94:1 (Eng. 95:1) & No place or episode named & \chronodate{alleluia}{\textbf{No date:} the received chronology settles this psalm's authorship and declines to date its writing.}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Clementine prints the title \latin{Laus cantici ipsi David}
\emph{inside} v.~1, so the two numberings agree here; the Douay and the
Douay-American print \emph{no} title at Ps.~94.
\textbf{Hebrews quotes this psalm \latin{in David dicendo} at Heb.\ 4:7, and
that is a New Testament attribution and not a date}; it quotes vv.~8--11, which
the Alleluia does not appoint. Theodoret's Greek reports the
psalm \textbf{untitled among the Hebrews} and composes it in the person of King
Josiah --- \textbf{his own reconstruction.} \textbf{No modern critical
horizon.}}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps. 117:8--9 (Eng. 118:8--9) & No superscription; thanksgiving & \chronodate{gradual}{\textbf{No date here either:} the received chronology dates the writing of no psalm save in a few separately settled cases.}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{No superscription stands at Ps.~117}, a psalm
of 29 verses; the Clementine and
the Catholic Public Domain Version print \latin{Alleluja} at the head of v.~1,
a rubric-word and not an ascription, and \textbf{Theodoret's Greek heads the
psalm with that same word.} \textbf{The text supplies neither the speaker
as king nor the occasion as a victory}, and the New Testament quotes this psalm
\emph{as Scripture and never as David's}. The received chronology declines a
date here because the historical superscriptions give occasions and not
compositions. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Gospel, Communion & Mt. 6:24--33; v.~33 recast & Judaea, in Hebrew letters, for Jews & \chronodate{gospel,communion}{Six dates the \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} carries for Matthew, and they disagree: \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{about A.D. 38--45}, \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{about the year 40--42}, \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{the years 40--45}, \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{about the year 50}, \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{about the year 60--68}, \chronology{composition.gospel-of-matthew}{composition}{about the years 64--67}. \textbf{No date for the narrated occasion.}}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: within the Sermon on the Mount, on an unnamed mountain
(Mt.\ 5:1) in the Galilean ministry around Capharnaum (4:12--13, 23), before
crowds from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan (4:25).
\textbf{The received chronology dates that occasion nowhere}, and Maas's
ministry chronology, the one that would reach it, stops short of the Sermon.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The six dates above stand in volumes~X and~XIV of \work{The
Catholic Encyclopedia} (New York: Robert Appleton, 1911--1912), in those
volumes' own words, and they disagree with one another about when the Gospel
was written. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal. 5:16--24 & Galatia; region named in Acts & \chronodate{epistle}{Four disputed dates for Galatians: \chronology{composition.epistle-to-the-galatians}{composition}{A.D. 49--50}, \chronology{composition.epistle-to-the-galatians}{composition}{about A.D. 53, 54}, \chronology{composition.epistle-to-the-galatians}{composition}{(1 and 2 Corinthians; Galatians), 56}, \chronology{composition.epistle-to-the-galatians}{composition}{A.D. 57 or 58}.}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The letter names its own author and destination (Gal.\ 1:1--2;
4:13--15), with Acts 16:6 and 18:23 for the region. \textbf{Cornelius a Lapide}
reports the subscriptions and Fathers for \textbf{Rome}, Chrysostom and Baronius
for \textbf{Ephesus}, and \textbf{dates the letter AD~58 after Baronius}
(Antwerp 1614): his dating, reported as his. \textbf{The modern critical horizon
here} favours a northern Galatian audience near Ancyra and composition at
Ephesus about AD~54--55.}
\end{dossiertable}
