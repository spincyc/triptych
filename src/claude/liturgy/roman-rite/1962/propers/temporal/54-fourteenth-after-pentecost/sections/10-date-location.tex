\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

{\footnotesize Six passages are appointed; the Communion adapts a verse inside
one and shares its row. Psalms carry the missal's Vulgate number, the common
modern English number in parentheses. Summaries are \emph{by choice}
(quotable matter left unquoted), \emph{summarise-only} (rights unresolved) or
\emph{compelled} (rights restricted); the classes are set out in the scope
appendix. \textbf{No modern critical horizon is held for any row}: no NABRE
artifact is registered for Ps.~34, 84, 95 or 118, or for Matthew at all, so
there is nothing to cite or to summarise from, and each row marks the absence.
\textbf{Not held} and \textbf{no locus} are bounded negatives.\par}

\begin{dossiertable}
Offertory & Ps. 33:8--9a (Eng. 34:7--8) & Title: before Achimelech, 1~Kings~21 & David's reign 1055--1015~BC\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The superscription anchors the psalm to a named escape,
\latin{Davidi, cum immutavit vultum suum coram Achimelech}; the tracked Douay
prints the cross-reference ``[1 Kings 21.]'' --- modern \textbf{1~Samuel~21} in
Douay book-naming --- and \latin{eripiet eos} at v.~8 repeats \latin{eripuit
me} at v.~5. Corbett puts the reign at 1055--1015~BC and a pre-royal flight
before it; Theodoret's exposition is registered at PG~80:1101--1109 (both
public domain, summarised by choice). \textbf{Not available:} Haydock's A.M.~2944, bound to the 2014 Loreto printing
with rights \emph{unresolved} --- summarise-only, its public-domain sibling
being uncorrected optical text, not textual control, with no passage here.
Bellarmine is held and verified, but in abridged Victorian English at vv.~2--3,
reaching no appointed verse. \textbf{The alphabetic acrostic commonly ascribed
to this psalm cannot be asserted:} no Hebrew psalter is held, and no tracked
Latin or English carries acrostic apparatus. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Introit & Ps. 83:10--11a, 2--3a (Eng. 84:9--10a, 84:1--2a) & Sons of Core; movement toward the temple & Korahite; no date held\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The title \latin{In finem, pro torcularibus filiis Core} is
tracked in Clementine and Douay, and Scripture names the sons of Core
\emph{both} singers and doorkeepers (Num.\ 26:11; 1~Par.\ 6 and 9), so the
temple-singer horizon rests on held text and the setting is in vv.~2--8.
\textbf{Those support movement toward the temple; that the psalm is \emph{for}
pilgrims the text does not state, and neither does this guide.} The Biblical
Commission's 1910 responsa affirm the antiquity of the psalm titles and David
as principal author --- a criterion about titles generally, \textbf{not a
ruling on this psalm}. \textbf{Not available:} Theodoret on Ps.~83; PG~80 is held as
public-domain bytes, but no passage record registers that locus and no lane
retrieved it, so no reading of him stands here and no column is printed. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}
(PBC responsa I--VIII,
\work{AAS}~2 (1910) 354--355 --- verified, by choice.)}
\midrule
Alleluia & Ps. 94:1 (Eng. 95:1) & No place or episode named & Davidic; no year\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The Clementine prints the title \latin{Laus cantici ipsi David}
\emph{inside} v.~1, which is why the two numberings agree here --- though this
repository's Douay and Douay-American print \emph{no} title at Ps.~94. The
attribution does not rest on
the title: \textbf{Heb.\ 4:7 quotes the psalm \latin{in David dicendo}}, by
name, in tracked text, and the responsa treat psalms expressly attributed to
David in Scripture as settled. Brenton's Septuagint carries the Clementine's verse numbers
and title treatment at all four psalms, so the one-verse offset elsewhere is a
property of the \emph{English} convention, not of the Vulgate. \textbf{Not available:} Theodoret on Ps.~94, on the
Introit row's footing. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Gradual & Ps. 117:8--9 (Eng. 118:8--9) & No superscription; thanksgiving & \textbf{None held here}\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{This row's load-bearing negative rests entirely on
tracked public-domain Bible text, which makes it the best-supported on the
sheet.} No superscription stands at Ps.~117 in any of the seven tracked
witnesses, all counting 29 verses --- what follows from having no title to
number, and why Vulgate and modern numbers agree throughout. Clementine and
CPDV do print \latin{Alleluja} at the head of v.~1, a rubric-word and not an
ascription, not numbered separately. The scene is in the verses: rescue (5),
the gates of justice (19--20), the altar (27), inside an inclusio at vv.~1 and
29. \textbf{What the text does not supply is the speaker as king or
the occasion as a victory.} \textbf{The Date cell is a real negative:} the New
Testament quotes this psalm \emph{as Scripture and never as David's} at vv.~6,
22 and 26 --- this guide does not say the psalm is not David's, only that
nothing held here says it is. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Gospel, Communion & Mt. 6:24--33; v.~33 recast & Judaea, in Hebrew letters, for Jews & No year held for the Sermon\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierevent{Event: within the Sermon on the Mount, on an unnamed mountain
(Mt.\ 5:1) in the Galilean ministry around Capharnaum (4:12--13, 23), before
crowds from Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan (4:25).
The pericope opens on the discourse's hinge --- v.~24 closes the treasure
saying, and v.~25's \latin{Ideo dico vobis} depends on it --- and stops one
verse before Matthew's own conclusion. The one ministry chronology
held here, Maas's, does not reach the Sermon, so no year is given and no
present-day equivalence is asserted for the place.}
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{\textbf{The traditional side of this row is thinner than it looks, and
the reason is printed rather than covered.} Held and citable: Maas,
\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}~VIII pp.~378, 380--381, with Matthew's tracked
text. \textbf{The four witnesses a Matthaean dossier would ordinarily rest on
are not held here in the forms it needs.} \textbf{Papias:} no source record of
any kind, and Eusebius \work{Hist.\ eccl.}\ III.39.16, the locus transmitting
him, unregistered --- so the testimony that keeps this dossier from flattening
its witnesses into one tradition has nothing behind it. \textbf{Eusebius} III.24.6 and III.39.16: only Book~VI is held, its English
witness rights-restricted and its public-domain Greek witness Books~VI--X.
\textbf{Irenaeus} III.1.1: tracked public-domain bytes, no registered passage
record for the locus. \textbf{Jerome} \work{De viris illustribus}~3: inside a held public-domain
artifact, but the registered passage is ch.~2, only an English edition is
registered against a claim of Latin reading, and the artifact is no longer
byte-reproducible. \textbf{Nothing
is asserted from any of the four}, nor from ``Gospel of St. Matthew,'' citable at an exact locus but
retrieved by no lane. \textbf{No modern critical horizon.}}
\midrule
Epistle & Gal. 5:16--24 & Galatia; region named in Acts & AD~58 / AD~54--55\\
\cmidrule(lr){1-4}
\dossierprose{The letter supplies its own author and destination (Gal.\ 1:1--2;
4:13--15; 6:11), with Acts 16:6 and 18:23 for the region. \textbf{Cornelius a
Lapide} reports the ancient subscriptions and the Fathers for \textbf{Rome},
Chrysostom and Baronius for \textbf{Ephesus}, and holds that no certain
determination of time or place is possible, calling it the most uncertain of
Paul's letters in that respect; his \latin{prooemium} dates it \textbf{AD~58}
after Baronius (\latin{Argumentum} p.~457, \latin{prooemium} p.~19, Antwerp
1614 --- verified, by choice). The registered modern introduction favours a
northern audience near Ancyra, composition at Ephesus about AD~54--55, a
southern alternative at perhaps AD~48--50, and recipients who are pagan
converts pressed to add Mosaic observances; rights-restricted, so its
\textbf{summary is compelled} and it is nowhere quoted. \textbf{Not available:}
Jerome, \work{Comm. in Gal.} lib.~II prol., has no record of any kind here, so
the patristic answer to who the Galatians were is unsourced and not given;
``Epistle to the Galatians'' is citable in form but retrieved by no lane.}
\end{dossiertable}
