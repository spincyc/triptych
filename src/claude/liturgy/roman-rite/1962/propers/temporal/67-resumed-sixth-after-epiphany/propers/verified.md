the formulary *Dominica sexta quae superfuit post Epiphaniam*, II classis, in

15 November 2026*). Nothing is commemorated and no oration is added (RG 111 b).

proper-study run `decbd44ee5bcce3b`; the Communion's comparison note was
reworded in research iteration 1 of the same run, without a new reading of the

## Which printing the study's Latin follows

The 1962 Missal prints this formulary's three orations and two lessons twice:
in the Epiphany season (nos. 337, 338, 341, 343, 345) and in this resumed
formulary (nos. 1760, 1761, 1764, 1766, 1768). The bodies are word for word the
same. They differ in setting variables only:

- the **Collect's conclusion and pointing**: no. 337 writes the conclusion out
  (*Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat
  in unitáte.*) and sets *exsequámur, et factis*; no. 1760 prints the cue *Per
  Dóminum.* and sets *exsequámur et factis* without the comma;
- the **Postcommunion's conclusion**: no. 345 *Per Dóminum nostrum.*; no. 1768
  *Per Dóminum.*;
- the **Epistle's citation line**: no. 338 *1 Thess. 1, 2-10*; no. 1761
  ***1 Thess. 1, 1-20***, a misprint (the chapter has ten verses; the text
  printed under it runs from within v. 2 to v. 10, word for word as no. 338);
- the **pointing of the lessons**: no. 1761 sets *in Macedónia, et in Acháia*
  with a comma at both occurrences, where no. 338 sets none; no. 1764 sets
  *cæli véniant et hábitent* without the comma no. 341 has after *véniant*.

The Secret is identical in both printings.

**The study follows the resumed printing, nos. 1759-1768**, because that is the
formulary appointed on 15 November 2026. It cites the Epistle as 1 Thess
1:2-10, the extent of the text both printings carry, and reports the resumed
citation line as the page's misprint (research/scope.md, section 1). Every
oration ends with the one fixed conclusion of the Missal whichever cue a
printing gives; a reader who says the Collect aloud says it in full.

## The publication basis of the Latin

The repository's holding
(`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`) is
that the 1962 printing is a collation witness and that 17 U.S.C. 103(b) frees
only preexisting material: an element's Latin may be published where a
public-domain printing carries the same words. Orthography, pointing,
capitalisation, accents and the length of a conclusion cue are transformations
and do not change a verdict (`guidance/propers-for-agents.md`).

- **The three orations and the two lessons.** Pustet 1862, *Dominica VI. post
  Epiphaniam*, printed pp. 44-45, read on its page images, prints every word of
  the Collect (p. 44, left column: *Præsta, quæsumus omnípotens Deus: ut semper
  rationabília meditántes, quæ tibi sunt plácita, et dictis exequámur, et
  factis. Per Dóminum.*), the Epistle (p. 44, foot of the left column and the
  whole right column; citation *1. c. 1.*), the Gospel (p. 45, left column;
  *c. 13.*), the Secret (p. 45, left column) and the Postcommunion (p. 45, foot
  of the left column to the head of the right column). The differences are
  orthography (*exequámur* for *exsequámur*; *expectáre* for *exspectáre*;
  *Jesu*, *Jesum*, *Jesus*, *ejus*, *majus* for the 1962 *i*; *cœlis*,
  *cœlórum*, *cœli*, *Cœléstibus* for *cælis* and its forms; *charitátis* for
  *caritátis*), pointing (among other places: *quæsumus omnípotens Deus: ut semper* without the
  1962's commas; *Fratres.* with a full stop; *Sciéntes fratres*; *annúntiant,
  qualem*; *(quem suscitávit ex mórtuis), Jesum*; *véniant, et hábitent*;
  *implerétur, quod*; *Hæc nos oblátio, Deus, mundet, quæsumus, et rénovet,
  gubérnet, et prótegat*; *quæsumus, ut semper* for *quǽsumus; ut semper*),
  capitalisation (*Spíritu sancto*, *Spíritus sancti*) and conclusion cues.
  The *exequámur*/*exsequámur* pair is a spelling of the one verb *exsequor*.
  The 1862 Epistle carries the Missal's departures from the Clementine word for
  word: *Fratres* in place of v. 2's greeting and *ex mórtuis* in v. 10. **No
  word of any body differs.**
- **The five chants.** Pustet 1862, *Dominica XXIII. post Pentecosten*, printed
  pp. 357-358, read on its page images, prints every word of the Introit
  antiphon and its verse (p. 357, left column; *Introitus. Jeremiæ 29.*; *Ps.
  84.*), the Gradual and its verse (p. 357, right column; *Ps. 43.*), the
  Alleluia verse (p. 358, left column; *Ps. 129.*), the Offertory (p. 358,
  left column; *Ps. 129.*) and the Communion (p. 358, right column; *Marci
  11.*). The differences are orthography (*Jacob*, *Allelúja*), pointing
  (*Benedixísti Dómine terram tuam* without the comma; *Liberásti nos Dómine*
  without commas in the Gradual; *clamávi ad te Dómine, Dómine exaúdi* in the
  Alleluia verse; *crédite, quia accipiétis* in the Communion) and citation
  form. No word differs, including every place where a chant departs from the
  Clementine Vulgate (below).
- **The assignment of the chants.** The same 1862 page, p. 358 right column to
  p. 359 left column, prints the rubric for the Sundays left over after the
  Epiphany: they are resumed *quoad Orationes, Epistolas et Evangelia*; *Si
  fuerint xxvi. in Dominica xxiv. dicitur Missa Dominicæ v. post Epiphaniam et
  in vigesimaquinta, Missa Dominicæ sextæ*; *Et ultimo loco semper ponitur
  Missa Dominicæ xxiv. post Pentecosten*; and *Introitus autem, Graduale,
  Offertorium, et Communio in prædictis Dominicis semper dicuntur ut in
  Dominica vigesimatertia*. The 1862 rubric does not name the Alleluia; the
  1962 RGMR 298 adds *graduale et Alleluia cum suo versu*. The 1862 Alleluia of
  the Twenty-third Sunday is *De profúndis* (p. 358), so the 1962 resumed
  Alleluia stands word for word in the 1862 book whichever rule a reader takes.
  The 1862 prints no resumed formulary of its own.

**Result: every word of all ten elements stands in the public-domain Pustet
1862 printing, read on its page images.** The Latin of the whole formulary may
be published on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) with that witness, in the 1962 page's
orthography. The Latin provenance ledger
(`src/sources/inventories/roman-1962-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml`) carries
`permitted` rows for the three orations under `mass = "epiphany-6"` on the same
1862 witness; it has no row for the five chants (`pentecost-23`), the two
lessons or the resumed printing. This record is the collation for those
elements; the missing ledger rows are reported to their owner in
research/scope.md, section 11, not added here.

## Appointed text, as printed in 1962

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — no. 1759, printed p. 421, right column

*Antiphona ad Introitum*, printed citation *Ierem. 29, 11, 12 et 14*; verse
headed *Ps. 84, 2*.

> Dicit Dóminus: Ego cógito cogitatiónes pacis, et non afflictiónis:
> invocábitis me, et ego exáudiam vos: et redúcam captivitátem vestram de
> cunctis locis. *Ps. 84, 2* Benedixísti, Dómine, terram tuam: avertísti
> captivitátem Iacob. ℣. Glória Patri.

### 2. Collect (`collect`) — no. 1760, printed p. 421, right column

*Oratio*

> Præsta, quǽsumus, omnípotens Deus: ut, semper rationabília meditántes, quæ
> tibi sunt plácita, et dictis exsequámur et factis. Per Dóminum.

### 3. Epistle (`epistle`) — no. 1761, printed p. 421, right column, to p. 422, left column

*Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Thessalonicénses.* — printed *1 Thess.
1, 1-20* (a misprint; the text is 1 Thess 1:2-10)

> Fratres: Grátias ágimus Deo semper pro ómnibus vobis, memóriam vestri
> faciéntes in oratiónibus nostris sine intermissióne, mémores óperis fidei
> vestræ, et labóris, et caritátis, et sustinéntiæ spei Dómini nostri Iesu
> Christi, ante Deum et Patrem nostrum: sciéntes, fratres, dilécti a Deo,
> electiónem vestram: quia Evangélium nostrum non fuit ad vos in sermóne
> tantum, sed et in virtúte, et in Spíritu Sancto, et in plenitúdine multa,
> sicut scitis quales fuérimus in vobis propter vos. Et vos imitatóres nostri
> facti estis, et Dómini, excipiéntes verbum in tribulatióne multa, cum
> gáudio Spíritus Sancti: ita ut facti sitis forma ómnibus credéntibus in
> Macedónia, et in Acháia. A vobis enim diffamátus est sermo Dómini, non solum
> in Macedónia, et in Acháia, sed et in omni loco fides vestra, quæ est ad
> Deum, profécta est, ita ut non sit nobis necésse quidquam loqui. Ipsi enim
> de nobis annúntiant qualem intróitum habuérimus ad vos: et quómodo convérsi
> estis ad Deum a simulácris, servíre Deo vivo et vero, et exspectáre Fílium
> eius de cælis (quem suscitávit ex mórtuis) Iesum, qui erípuit nos ab ira
> ventúra.

## The chants and lessons against the Clementine Vulgate

Checked on 2026-11-11. These are textual observations about wording and
boundary; no origin is asserted for any form (research/scope.md, section 2.3).

- **Introit.** Not a continuous Vulgate text. Clementine Jer 29:11 *Ego enim
  scio cogitationes quas ego cogito super vos, ait Dominus, cogitationes pacis
  et non afflictionis, ut dem vobis finem et patientiam*; v. 12 *Et invocabitis
  me, et ibitis: et orabitis me, et ego exaudiam vos*; v. 14 *… et reducam
  captivitatem vestram, et congregabo vos de universis gentibus et de cunctis
  locis ad quae expuli vos*. The Missal opens *Dicit Dóminus: Ego cógito
  cogitatiónes pacis*, has nothing of *ut dem vobis finem et patientiam*, of
  *et ibitis: et orabitis me*, of v. 13, or of *et congregabo vos de universis
  gentibus*, and joins *redúcam captivitátem vestram* directly to *de cunctis
  locis*. The psalm verse agrees with Ps 84:2.
- **Epistle.** The Clementine's v. 2 opens *Gratia vobis, et pax.*, which the
  Missal replaces with *Fratres:*; in v. 10 the Missal reads *ex mórtuis* where
  the Clementine reads *a mortuis*. Every other word of vv. 2-10 agrees.
- **Gradual.** *Liberásti nos, Dómine, ex affligéntibus nos: et eos, qui nos
  odérunt, confudísti* where the Clementine Ps 43:8 reads *salvasti enim nos
  de affligentibus nos, et odientes nos confudisti*; *in sǽcula* where v. 9
  reads *in saeculum*.
- **Alleluia and Offertory.** *Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam* where the
  Clementine Ps 129:2 reads *Domine, exaudi vocem meam*; both omit the title
  *Canticum graduum* and stop before v. 2b; the Offertory repeats its first
  line.
- **Gospel.** The liturgical opening *In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus turbis
  parábolam hanc:* stands for the Clementine's *Aliam parabolam proposuit eis
  dicens:* at the head of v. 31. Every other word of vv. 31-35 agrees. The
  evangelist's citation at v. 35 is not the wording of the Vulgate psalm it
  quotes: Clementine Ps 77:2 reads *Aperiam in parabolis os meum; loquar
  propositiones ab initio* (Douay *I will utter propositions from the
  beginning*), where Mt 13:35 reads *eructabo abscondita a constitutione
  mundi*. This is a difference inside Scripture, not a liturgical adaptation.
- **Communion.** *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orántes pétitis … et fiet vobis*
  where the Clementine Mk 11:24 reads *Propterea dico vobis, omnia quaecumque
  orantes petitis, credite quia accipietis, et evenient vobis*. The antiphon
  differs at both edges. The preceding verse, Mk 11:23, also opens *Amen dico
  vobis*; it ends *fiet ei*, not the antiphon's *et fiet vobis*. Whether the
  antiphon was drawn from another Latin text or shaped from vv. 23-24 is not
  established.

## English

English is quoted from registered public-domain witnesses and never composed.

- **Scripture.** Douay-Rheims (Challoner), at the Vulgate loci. It carries the
  Epistle's body (vv. 2-10, but v. 2 opens *Grace be to you and peace. We give
  thanks …* where the Missal opens *Fratres:*), the Gospel's body (vv. 31-35,
  but *Another parable he proposed unto them, saying* at the head) and Ps 84:2.
  It does not carry the Missal's form of the Introit antiphon, the Gradual
  (*But thou hast saved us …* and *for ever*), the Alleluia and Offertory
  (*Lord, hear my voice*) or the Communion (*Therefore I say unto you, all
  things, whatsoever you ask … and they shall come unto you*).

## Unresolved discrepancies

None in the 1962 text. On the 1962 page itself, the resumed Epistle's citation
line *1 Thess. 1, 1-20* is a misprint for *1, 2-10*. Outside it: the finding
aid's expanded Collect conclusion and *Per Dominum nostrum* Postcommunion for
this formulary, the missing ledger and collation rows, and the Lasance
*Mark 11, 34* and Latin-Epistle slips are reported to their owners in
research/scope.md, section 11. None changes a word published here.
