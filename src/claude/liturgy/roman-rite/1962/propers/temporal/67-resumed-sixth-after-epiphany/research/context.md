## Occurrence on 15 November 2026

**Computation (finding aid only).** `tools/tpt calendar-days day --calendar
roman-1962 --date 2026-11-15 --json` returns the candidate
`resumed-epiphany-6`, "Sixth Sunday after the Epiphany, resumed after
Pentecost", rule "resumption slot 25 of 26 Sundays after Pentecost", season
after Pentecost, no Lectionary value and nothing unresolved, with a second
candidate, the third-class feast `s-alberti-magni-episcopi-confessoris-ecclesiae`
(the day layer leaves `appointed` null and ranks nothing; precedence is the
rubrics layer below). The computed 2026 anchors are: Septuagesima 1 February,
Easter 5 April, Pentecost 24 May, Trinity Sunday 31 May (the first Sunday
after Pentecost), Sunday before Advent 22 November, First Sunday of Advent
29 November; liturgical year 30 November 2025 - 28 November 2026. Recomputed
here: 31 May plus twenty-five weeks is 22 November, so there are 26 Sundays
after Pentecost; the twenty-third falls on 1 November, the twenty-fourth on
8 November, the twenty-fifth on 15 November, the twenty-sixth on 22 November.
15 November 2026 is a Sunday. Septuagesima on 1 February left room for only
three Sundays after the Epiphany (11, 18 and 25 January), so the Fourth, Fifth
and Sixth were impeded.

**The rubric that places it.** RG 18, printed p. XII (PDF 10), read:
*Dominicae post Epiphaniam quae, superveniente Septuagesima, impediuntur,
transferuntur post dominicam XXIII post Pentecosten, hoc ordine: … b) si
dominicae fuerint 26, dominica XXIV erit quae inscribitur V post Epiphaniam; et
XXV, quae inscribitur VI; … Ultimo tamen loco semper ponitur ea quae in ordine
est XXIV post Pentecosten, omissis, si opus sit, ceteris, quae aliquando locum
habere non possunt.* In 2026, therefore: slot 24, 8 November, the resumed
Fifth Sunday; slot 25, **15 November, the resumed Sixth Sunday after the
Epiphany**, this formulary; slot 26, 22 November, the Twenty-fourth and last.
The Fourth Sunday after the Epiphany finds no place this year under the same
clause. RG 14 (same page, read) says the Office and Mass of an impeded Sunday
are neither anticipated nor resumed; RG 18 is the express provision for these
Sundays. RG 10 and 12 (same page, read): Sundays are of I or II class, and every
Sunday not named in RG 11 is of II class, as the printed heading also says.

**The calendar of the day.** The *Calendarium*, printed p. LII (PDF 54), read:
15 November carries dominical letter *d*, *XVII* [Kal. Dec.], and the entry
*S. Alberti Magni Ep., Conf. et Eccl. Doct., III classis*, with no
commemoration. The year 2026 begins on a Thursday, so its dominical letter is
*D* and 15 November is a Sunday. The feast stands in the Sanctorale under *Die
15 novembris*, *S. Alberti Magni*, III classis, *Missa In medio, de Communi
Doctorum*, printed p. 719 (PDF 800, located on the text layer).

**Precedence (finding aid only).** `tools/tpt calendar-rubrics day --calendar

second-class Sunday (RG 12; RG 91 row 15); St Albert, a third-class feast of

### The resumed printing against the Epiphany-season printing

The 1962 edition prints the orations and lessons of this Mass twice: in the
Epiphany season (identity `17`, pp. 48-49, PDF 129-130) and here (pp. 421-422,
PDF 502-503). Both printings were read on page images in this stage. The
words of every oration and lesson agree; the printings differ only as follows.

- **Epistle citation.** Resumed: *1 Thess. 1, 1-20*; Epiphany season: *1 Thess.
  1, 2-10*. The lesson text is identical in both. This is a misprint in the
  resumed printing's citation line and not a different appointment.
- **Collect.** Resumed: *et dictis exsequamur et factis. Per Dominum.*;
  Epiphany season: *et dictis exsequamur, et factis. Per Dominum nostrum Iesum
  Christum, Filium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitate.*
- **Postcommunion.** Resumed: *Per Dominum.*; Epiphany season: *Per Dominum
  nostrum.*
- **Secret.** No difference.
- **Pointing in the lessons.** The resumed Epistle sets *in Macedonia, et in
  Achaia* with a comma at both occurrences where the Epiphany-season printing
  sets none; the resumed Gospel sets *caeli veniant et habitent* where the
  Epiphany-season printing sets *veniant, et habitent*.

The finding aid resolves this Mass's orations through `epiphany-6`, carrying
the Epiphany-season Collect conclusion in full and the Postcommunion conclusion
*Per Dominum nostrum.*; the resumed printing at nos. 1760 and 1768 prints the
short cue *Per Dominum.* The study's Latin follows the printing it reproduces,
and the research stage records which.

The formulary prints no Tract, Sequence, prophecy, proper *Communicantes* or
*Hanc igitur* (RGMR 501, read: such variations are noted in the proper Mass,
and none is noted here), *Oratio super populum*, second or third oration of its
own, blessing, or alternative reading. The 1862 antecedent's *Oratio ii* (*A
cunctis*), *Alia Secreta* (*Exaudi*), *Alia Postcommunio* (*Mundet*) and
*Tertia ad libitum* belong to the pre-1960 rubrics and are not part of the 1962
formulary.

### Wording against the Clementine Vulgate

The comparisons below were made against the tracked Clementine Vulgate
(`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`, its verse-text
artifacts) through `tools/tpt mass-propers show --calendar roman-1962 --mass
resumed-epiphany-6 --bible clementine-vulgate`, with the Epistle and Gospel
compared token by token by script against a reading of the page images, after
normalising i/j, ligatures and accents and ignoring pointing. They are textual
observations about wording and boundary. Whether a chant or lesson follows an
older Latin text, and which, is left to research, and no origin is asserted
here.

- **Introit.** The antiphon is not a continuous Vulgate text. The Clementine at
  Jer 29:11 reads *Ego enim scio cogitationes quas ego cogito super vos, ait
  Dominus, cogitationes pacis et non afflictionis, ut dem vobis finem et
  patientiam*; v. 12 *Et invocabitis me, et ibitis: et orabitis me, et ego
  exaudiam vos*; v. 14 *et reducam captivitatem vestram, et congregabo vos de
  universis gentibus et de cunctis locis ad quae expuli vos …*. The Missal opens
  *Dicit Dominus: Ego cogito cogitationes pacis*, has nothing of *ut dem vobis
  finem et patientiam*, *et ibitis: et orabitis me*, v. 13, or *et congregabo
  vos de universis gentibus*, and joins *reducam captivitatem vestram* directly
  to *de cunctis locis*. The psalm verse agrees with Ps 84:2.
- **Epistle.** The tracked Clementine's 1 Thess 1:2 opens *Gratia vobis, et
  pax.* before *Gratias agimus*; the Missal has *Fratres:* in its place, so the
  lesson begins within the Vulgate's v. 2. In v. 10 the Missal reads *quem
  suscitavit ex mortuis* where the Clementine reads *a mortuis*. Every other
  word of vv. 2-10 agrees.
- **Gradual.** The Missal reads *Liberasti nos, Domine, ex affligentibus nos:
  et eos, qui nos oderunt, confudisti* where the Clementine at Ps 43:8 reads
  *salvasti enim nos de affligentibus nos, et odientes nos confudisti*, and *in
  saecula* where v. 9 reads *in saeculum*.
- **Alleluia and Offertory.** Both read *Domine, exaudi orationem meam* where
  the Clementine at Ps 129:2 reads *Domine, exaudi vocem meam*; both stop before
  v. 2b and omit the title *Canticum graduum*. The Offertory repeats its first
  line at the end.
- **Gospel.** The liturgical opening *In illo tempore: Dixit Iesus turbis
  parabolam hanc:* stands for the Clementine's *Aliam parabolam proposuit eis
  dicens:* at the head of v. 31. Every other word of vv. 31-35 agrees.
- **Communion.** The Missal reads *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orantes petitis …
  et fiet vobis* where the Clementine at Mk 11:24 reads *Propterea dico vobis,
  omnia quaecumque orantes petitis … et evenient vobis*; *credite quia
  accipietis* agrees.
