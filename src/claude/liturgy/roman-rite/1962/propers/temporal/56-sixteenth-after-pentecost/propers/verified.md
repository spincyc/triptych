# Sixteenth Sunday after Pentecost — Verified Propers

Facsimile-collated appointed text in liturgical order. This record is the text
control for the canonical full research edition at
`liturgy/roman-rite/1962/propers/temporal/56-sixteenth-after-pentecost` and its
mechanical `-synthesis` PDF companion. The canonical edition prints the complete
Latin below beside the identified English; the synthesis companion omits the
appointed-text component.

### A limitation of the controlling artifact, stated once

The 1962 typical edition is printed in **red and black**. The CMAA facsimile is
**bitonal**: one bit per component, CCITT G4, no colour plane at all, as the
artifact's own page images show. Every rubric, heading, versicle sign and
initial on these two pages therefore arrives here in black, and **the colour of
no mark on pp. 397–398 can be read from the controlling witness.** Where this
record calls something a rubric it is naming its function and its type, never
its ink. The Benziger images do carry the two-colour printing, and they show its
rubrics and headings in red; that is evidence about the Benziger and not about
the typical edition.

### The running head, and why no search will find one

**No page of the 1,088-page facsimile carries a running head naming this
Sunday.** Printed p. 397 is headed `Dominica XV post Pentecosten` and printed
p. 398 is headed `Dominica XVII post Pentecosten`. Both heads were read on the
page images, and those two pages are the only ones the formulary touches, so the
absence is established and not merely unattested; a sweep of the whole embedded
text layer agrees, returning `Dominica XVI post Pentecosten` **zero times**.

The heads of the surrounding pages run 392 XII, 393 XIII, 394 XIV, 395 XIV,
396 XV, 397 XV, 398 XVII, 399 XVII. There is no clean rule in that sequence:
pp. 394 and 396 take the head of the Sunday that *begins* on them, and p. 397
does not. The likeliest explanation — offered as inference, not as a reading — is
that the head names whichever formulary holds the greater part of the page, and
that on p. 397 the Fifteenth's full left column beat the Sixteenth's right one.
Whatever the cause, the consequence is practical: **a finding aid that locates a
formulary by its running head will report this one absent**, and any lane that
searches the text layer for a heading must search the formulary's own words
instead. The Benziger, whose paging differs, does print
`Dominica XVI post Pentecosten` over its printed p. 392.

## 1. Introit — marginal no. 1592

**Printed heading and reference:** `Ant. ad Introitum` · `Ps. 85, 3 et 5`, with
the psalm verse cited `Ps. ibid., 1`. Vulgate numbering; the common modern
English numbering of the same psalm is **Ps. 86**, so the antiphon is Ps. 86:3, 5
and the verse Ps. 86:1 in a modern Bible.

> Miserére mihi, Dómine, quóniam ad te clamávi tota die: quia tu, Dómine,
> suávis ac mitis es, et copiósus in misericórdia ómnibus invocántibus te.
> *Ps. ibid., 1* Inclína, Dómine, aurem tuam mihi, et exáudi me: quóniam inops
> et pauper sum ego. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Witness:** printed p. 397, right column, immediately below the formulary
heading; artifact PDF p. 478. A four-line drop-capital `M` opens it.
**Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 200 and 400 dpi.

**Note on the antiphon's construction.** A centonisation of two verses that are
not adjacent — v. 3 entire, then v. 5 — with the psalm verse drawn from v. 1.
The join is silent: nothing on the page marks that v. 4 has been passed over.
Every one of the antiphon's five departures from the Clementine is tabulated
under *Appointed text against the Bible text* below; none is emended here.

**Verification result:** collated; no unresolved wording, accent, punctuation or
reference issue. **No repetition cue** is printed after `℣. Glória Patri.`

## 2. Collect — marginal no. 1593

**Printed heading:** `Oratio`.

> Tua nos, quǽsumus, Dómine, grátia semper et prævéniat et sequátur: ac bonis
> opéribus iúgiter præstet esse inténtos. Per Dóminum.

**Witness:** printed p. 397, right column; artifact PDF p. 478. A two-line
drop-capital `T`. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 200 and 400 dpi.

**Conclusion.** `Per Dóminum.` — the short form, not written out.

**Verification result:** collated. The formulary appoints **one** Collect. It
prints no second and no third oration; the 1862 Pustet at the same Sunday prints
`Secunda Oratio. A cunctis nos. fol. 315.` and `Tertia ad libitum.`, which are
the pre-1955 discipline and are no part of this formulary.

**A correction owed to a registered source record.** The passage
`passage.eugene-cummiskey.roman-missal-english-laity.philadelphia-1861.post-pentecosten-16.collect`
states in its `notes` that this Collect stands "at marginal number 1575 of the
1962 Vatican typical edition". **That number is wrong.** No. 1575 in this
facsimile is the *Gradual of the Fourteenth Sunday*, `Bonum est confidere in
Domino`, printed p. 396 and read here at artifact PDF p. 477. This Collect is
**no. 1593**, as the calendar index has it. The error was confined to that one
`notes` field; the passage's own locus, text, page and leaf are right, and the
formulary correspondence it asserts is right. **The source record was repaired
on 2026-09-05** and now cites no. 1593, with the old reading recorded there as
the error it was — see *Discrepancies and open items carried forward* at the
end.

## 3. Epistle — marginal no. 1594

**Printed heading and reference:** `Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad
Ephésios.` · `Ephes. 3, 13-21`.

> Fratres: Obsecro vos, ne deficiátis in tribulatiónibus meis pro vobis: quæ est
> glória vestra. Huius rei grátia flecto génua mea ad Patrem Dómini nostri Iesu
> Christi, ex quo omnis patérnitas in cælis et in terra nominátur, ut det vobis
> secúndum divítias glóriæ suæ, virtúte corroborári per Spíritum eius in
> interiórem hóminem, Christum habitáre per fidem in córdibus vestris: in
> caritáte radicáti, et fundáti, ut possítis comprehéndere cum ómnibus sanctis,
> quæ sit latitúdo, et longitúdo, et sublímitas, et profúndum: scire étiam
> supereminéntem sciéntiæ caritátem Christi, ut impleámini in omnem
> plenitúdinem Dei. Ei autem, qui potens est ómnia fácere superabundánter quam
> pétimus, aut intellégimus, secúndum virtútem, quæ operátur in nobis: ipsi
> glória in Ecclésia, et in Christo Iesu, in omnes generatiónes sǽculi
> sæculórum. Amen.

**Witness:** begins printed p. 397, right column, at a two-line drop-capital `F`;
runs over to printed p. 398, left column, ending at `Amen.`; artifact PDF
pp. 478–479. **Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 200 and 400 dpi, the turn of the page
read at both.

**Verification result:** collated. Two things are worth naming so that neither is
later "corrected" into an error:

- The pericope opens `Fratres: Obsecro vos, ne deficiátis`, where the Vulgate
  reads `Propter quod peto ne deficiatis`. This is the ordinary liturgical
  incipit adaptation — the vocative that tells the hearer whom Paul addresses,
  substituted for a connective that points back to a verse the Mass does not
  read.
- The missal prints **`intellégimus`**, with an `e`. The Clementine prints
  `intelligimus`. The Benziger prints `intellégimus` too. Both 1962 witnesses
  agree against the Bible text, and the reading is not a scan artifact.

## 6. Gospel — marginal no. 1597

**Printed heading and reference:** a cross, then `Sequéntia sancti Evangélii
secúndum Lucam.` · `Luc. 14, 1-11`.

> In illo témpore: Cum intráret Iesus in domum cuiúsdam príncipis pharisæórum
> sábbato manducáre panem, et ipsi observábant eum. Et ecce homo quidam
> hydrópicus erat ante illum. Et respóndens Iesus dixit ad legisperítos et
> pharisǽos, dicens: Si licet sábbato curáre? At illi tacuérunt. Ipse vero
> apprehénsum sanávit eum, ac dimísit. Et respóndens ad illos, dixit: Cuius
> vestrum ásinus, aut bos in púteum cadet, et non contínuo éxtrahet illum die

## 9. Communion — marginal no. 1600

**Printed heading and reference:** `Antiphona ad Communionem` — spelled out in
full, where the same page abbreviates `Ant. ad Offertorium` — with the citation
`Ps. 70, 16-17 et 18` printed on its own line before the text. Vulgate
numbering; modern **Ps. 71:16–18**.

> *Ps. 70, 16-17 et 18* Dómine, memorábor iustítiæ tuæ solíus: Deus, docuísti me
> a iuventúte mea: et usque in senéctam et sénium, Deus, ne derelínquas me.

**Witness:** printed p. 398, right column; artifact PDF p. 479. No drop capital.
**Checked:** 2026-09-05 at 200 and 400 dpi, and again at native 500 dpi.

**The citation form is as printed, and it is exact.** `Ps. 70, 16-17 et 18` is
not a mangled `16-18` and must not be normalised into one. The antiphon takes
the **second half of v. 16**, the **first half of v. 17** and the **first part of
v. 18**, so the missal's own form — two verses run together, then a third named
separately — describes the centonisation more honestly than a range would. The
calendar index and the generated chronology record both carry it as
`Psalm 70:16-18`; the loci are the same and the citation form is not. The
Benziger prints `Ps. 70, 16-17 et 18` as well.

**Verification result:** collated. Every word stands in the Clementine
unaltered; the whole of this antiphon's distance from the psalter is where it
starts and stops.

## The `et` before `renova`: a recorded gap, now closed

`src/sources/inventories/roman-1962-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml` carries this
Postcommunion at `provenance_confidence = "medium"` and states the reason in
capitals: *the 1862 text layer has no `et` before `renova`.* The layer breaks the
line `mentes nostras benignus,` / `renova coelestibus sacra-` and the conjunction
is simply not in those bytes. The ledger's author read the Venice 1570 (lines
53,740–53,752) and the Vatican typica 1604 (lines 38,821–38,831), found the
conjunction in both, and then wrote down honestly what could not be done: *"I
have NOT read an 1862 page image, because that book's page images are registered
`storage = remote` and this lane may not download… on the 1862 alone the honest
verdict for that single conjunction is unproven."*

**This audit read the page image, and the conjunction is printed.**

- Item `bub_gb_E7sPAAAAIAAJ`, leaf **n426** = printed **p. 341**, 2500 × 4097 px,
  SHA-256 `50a5e6c35bc4685d31ddf1aa66632fc6cb34a18422cf6ddb95d611210e9e9b43`,
  fetched 2026-09-05 from
  `https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_E7sPAAAAIAAJ/page/n426_w2500.jpg`,
  re-fetched once from the same URL and byte-identical. The leaf-to-printed-page
  offset is 85, derived from the two leaf images this repository already tracks
  (n303 = p. 218, n347 = p. 262) and confirmed by the folio printed on the page

## Appointed text against the Bible text: the divergences

The missal's chants are older than the Gallican psalter the Clementine prints,
and they diverge from it in ways that are textual history and not error. Every
divergence found is listed; the tracked Clementine
(`src/sources/bibles/clementine-vulgate`) is the comparand for the Latin, and
consonantal `i`/`j`, the `æ`/`œ` ligatures and stress accents are disregarded
throughout as orthography.

**Introit, Ps. 85:3 and 5, with the verse from Ps. 85:1** — five departures, the
most of any element here:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Miserére **mihi**, Dómine` | `Miserere **mei**, Domine` | different word; the chant asks mercy *for me* in the dative |
| `**quia** tu, Dómine` | `**Quoniam** tu, Domine` | different conjunction |
| `suávis **ac** mitis **es**` | `suavis **et** mitis` | different conjunction, and the chant supplies the verb the psalter leaves out |
| `et **copiósus in misericórdia**` | `et **multæ misericordiæ**` | a different construction of the same sense — *plenteous in mercy* against *of much mercy* |
| verse: `aurem tuam **mihi**` | `aurem tuam` | the chant adds the dative |

The antiphon also passes over v. 4 without a mark, and the verse omits the psalm
title `Oratio ipsi David`.

**Gradual, Ps. 101:16–17** — three:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Timébunt gentes` | `**Et** timebunt gentes` | the chant drops the connective that opened the verse |
| `**Quóniam** ædificávit` | `**quia** ædificavit` | different conjunction |
| `vidébitur in **maiestáte** sua` | `videbitur in **gloria** sua` | different noun; the substantive one |

**Alleluia, Ps. 97:1** — one, plus a stop:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `quia mirabília fecit **Dóminus**` | `quia mirabilia fecit` | the chant names the subject the psalter leaves implicit |

It stops there, leaving off `Salvavit sibi dextera ejus, et brachium sanctum
ejus`, and omits the title `Psalmus ipsi David`.

**Offertory, Ps. 39:14–15** — two, plus the refrain:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Dómine, **in auxílium meum** réspice` | `Domine, **ad adjuvandum me** respice` | a different construction of the same petition |
| `confundántur et revereántur, qui quærunt` | `Confundantur et revereantur **simul**, qui quærunt` | the chant drops `simul` |

The chant also opens at the second half of v. 14, stops at the first half of
v. 15, and then repeats the second half of v. 14 — a refrain the psalm does not
have.

**Communion, Ps. 70:16–18** — **none.** Every word is the Clementine's. The
antiphon is made entirely by where it starts and stops: v. 16 from `Domine,
memorabor`, v. 17 as far as `a iuventute mea`, v. 18 as far as `ne derelinquas
me`.

**Epistle, Eph. 3:13–21** — two:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `**Fratres: Obsecro vos, ne** deficiátis` | `**Propter quod peto ne** deficiatis` | the liturgical incipit adaptation |
| `quam pétimus, aut **intellégimus**` | `quam petimus aut **intelligimus**` | a different received spelling of one word; not a different word |

**Gospel, Luke 14:1–11** — one substantive, one of pointing:

| Missal | Clementine | Kind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `**In illo témpore: Cum** intráret Iesus` | `**Et factum est cum** intraret Jesus` | the liturgical incipit adaptation |
| `ab illo, et véniens is qui te…` | `ab illo. **Et** veniens is, qui te…` | the missal runs vv. 8–9 into one sentence, lowercasing the `Et` and replacing the full stop with a comma |

Nothing else in the Gospel departs from the Clementine.

### Scriptural elements — Douay–Rheims (Challoner)

The introit, gradual, alleluia, offertory, communion, epistle and gospel take the
Douay–Rheims as revised by Bishop Challoner (1749–1752), the English of the
Vulgate the missal prints, in the Project Gutenberg e-text of that revision:
`edition.english-college-of-douay.douay-rheims-bible.challoner-gutenberg-1581`,
indexed at `src/sources/bibles/douay-rheims` and declaring `numbering: vulgate`,
`psalter: gallican`, `psalm_titles: numbered`, `rights: public-domain`,
`publishable: true`. Challoner translated the Clementine text, which is why the
Clementine is the right comparand for the divergences above.

**Every locus resolves in the missal's own Vulgate psalm numbering**, checked
element by element on 2026-09-05:

| Element | Missal citation (Vulgate) | Modern number | Douay–Rheims text present |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introit | Ps. 85, 3 et 5; Ps. ibid., 1 | Ps. 86:3, 5; 86:1 | yes |
| Gradual | Ps. 101, 16-17 | Ps. 102:16–17 | yes |
| Alleluia | Ps. 97, 1 | Ps. 98:1 | yes |
| Offertory | Ps. 39, 14 et 15 | Ps. 40:14–15 | yes |
| Communion | Ps. 70, 16-17 et 18 | Ps. 71:16–18 | yes |
| Epistle | Ephes. 3, 13-21 | Eph. 3:13–21 | yes |
| Gospel | Luc. 14, 1-11 | Luke 14:1–11 | yes |

**Four warnings for the authoring stage**, all of them consequences of the
divergences tabulated above:

1. **The psalm numbers are Vulgate and must be resolved as Vulgate.** Ps. 85, 97,
   101, 70 and 39 here are Ps. 86, 98, 102, 71 and 40 in a modern Bible. A guide
   that crosses the two systems publishes the wrong verses, and the resolution
   above is the one to use.
2. **The Douay renders the psalter, not the chant.** Where the chant departs from
   the Clementine — `mihi` for `mei`, `copiósus in misericórdia` for `multæ
   misericordiæ`, `maiestáte` for `gloria`, `in auxílium meum` for `ad
   adjuvandum me` — the Douay English answers the psalter's word and not the
   antiphon's. The Douay reads *Have mercy on me*, which happens to fit `mihi`;
   it reads *he shall be seen in his glory*, which does **not** answer
   `maiestáte`; it reads *look down, O Lord, to help me*, which does not answer
   `in auxílium meum réspice`. Each such place must be marked at the element, not
   smoothed over.
3. **Ps. 97:1 and Ps. 70:16–18 carry more than the chant sings.** The Douay
   Ps. 97:1 includes the title *A psalm for David himself* and the whole second
   half of the verse; Ps. 70:18 runs on past `ne derelinquas me` for two more
   clauses. Print the verses whole with the sung portion marked, as the
   sibling guides do, or mark the cut.
4. **The Epistle's and Gospel's opening words are not in the Douay at all.**
   `Fratres: Obsecro vos` and `In illo témpore` are liturgical formulae; the
   Douay gives *Wherefore I pray you* and *And it came to pass*. The gap belongs
   at the element.
