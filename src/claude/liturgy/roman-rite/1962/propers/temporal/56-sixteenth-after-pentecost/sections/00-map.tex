\subsection*{The four senses}

\begingroup
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & A psalmist asks mercy because he has cried all the day, and calls himself needy and poor \cue{Int.}. Paul bows his knees to the Father, asking that Christ dwell by faith in the heart \cue{Ep.}. At a chief Pharisee's sabbath table Christ heals a man with the dropsy, then tells the guests to take the lowest place \cue{Gosp.}.\\
Allegorical & Augustine reads the Gradual's Sion as building now --- \latin{Hoc agitur nunc} --- and its builder as seen at the Judgment, having been seen at the first coming in weakness \cue{Grad.}. \textbf{At the Epistle's four measures the tradition divides three ways}: Augustine reads them as the four members of the Cross, \textbf{Chrysostom for the extent of God's love figured by a solid body, and not for the Cross at all}, and Guéranger for the dimensions of Christ's indwelling \cue{Ep.}.\\
Moral & Bede ties the first place at table to \latin{de meritis gloriando} and argues that the parable must be read \latin{typice}; Cyril of Alexandria makes seizing an honour not due a theft with restitution owed; Ambrose has the lesson taught gently \cue{Gosp.}. Augustine at the Introit's psalm verse: God draws near the humbled and departs far from the exalted \cue{Int.}.\\
Anagogical & Augustine, Cassiodorus and Bellarmine read the Gradual's \latin{vidébitur} of the Judgment; \textbf{Theodoret reads it of Sion rebuilt in this world} \cue{Grad.}. \textbf{Bede holds \latin{Tunc erit tibi glória} in an eschatological and a daily sense together and does not choose} \cue{Gosp.}. In \latin{usque in senéctam et sénium} Augustine hears the Church's voice, her youth the age of the apostles; Theodoret hears the age of Moses, and old age the end of the Law \cue{Comm.}.\\
\end{sensestable}
\endgroup
