\section*{Scriptural Date and Location}

{\footnotesize Seven passages are directly appointed; the orations quote no
Scripture. Psalms carry the missal's Vulgate number, with the modern number in
parentheses. \textbf{An attributed author's regnal era dates neither composition
nor occasion}: the Communion dossier distinguishes all three.\par}

\textbf{Offertory --- Ps. 39:14--15 (Hebr. 40:14--15; Eng. 40:13--14)}. Title names David, no place or occasion\\*
\chronodate{offertory}{\chronologyannotation{offertory}}\\*
The Clementine title at v.~1, \latin{In finem. Psalmus ipsi David}, names no place, episode or audience, nor does the psalm. Individual composition remains insecure within the whole-Psalter critical bound. Hebrew numbers the title; English omits its number, so English 40:14--15 would begin one verse too late.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Communion --- Ps. 70:16-17 et 18 (Eng. 71:16--18)}. Title: the sons of Jonadab, the former captives\\*
\chronodate{communion}{\chronologyannotation{communion}}\\*
The title reads \latin{Psalmus David, filiorum Jonadab, et priorum captivorum}. Absalom's setting comes through Ps.~62 and Ps.~3's title; Joakim's third year from Dan.~1:1. The A.M. alternative is Haydock's reported Ussher figure, excluded from the corpus's own basis. The settings remain unresolved. These traditional settings remain distinct from the attributed reign and the critical whole-Psalter composition bound.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Introit --- Ps. 85:3, 5, with v.~1 (Eng. 86:3, 5, 1)}. Title names David, no occasion\\*
\chronodate{introit}{\chronologyannotation{introit}}\\*
The Clementine's \latin{Oratio ipsi David}, inside v.~1 and omitted by the chant, names no place, episode or audience. The composition bound is the Psalter's. Augustine's sermon asks no chronological question; his v.~1 lemma has \latin{egenus et inops ego sum} against the Missal's \latin{inops et pauper sum ego}.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Alleluia --- Ps. 97:1 (Eng. 98:1)}. Title names David, no place\\*
\chronodate{alleluia}{\chronologyannotation{alleluia}}\\*
The chant omits the Clementine's \latin{Psalmus ipsi David} within v.~1. Both disputed Nativity dates are prophetic referents from the grouping of Pss.~67, 95, 96 and 97, not dates of composition or claims about Ps.~97 alone. Composition has only the Psalter's bound.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Gradual --- Ps. 101:16--17 (Hebr. 102:16--17; Eng. 102:15--16)}. Title: a poor man; no name, no place\\*
\chronodate{gradual}{\chronologyannotation{gradual}}\\*
The unsung title, \latin{Oratio pauperis, cum anxius fuerit, et in conspectu Domini effuderit precem suam}, names a condition, not an author, place or occasion. Davidic attribution does not apply. Hebrew numbers the title; English does not, so English 102:16--17 begins one verse too late. Augustine, \work{Enarratio in Ps.~101} I §§16--17, reads \latin{aedificabit} and \latin{in gloria sua}.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Gospel --- Luke 14:1--11}. Written by Luke; no place, no audience\\*
\chronodate{gospel}{\chronologyannotation{gospel}}\\*
\textit{Event: a sabbath meal in the house of one of the chief of the
Pharisees (14:1), the third and last of the sabbath-healing controversies in
Luke after 6:6--11 and 13:10--17, inside the journey narrative and closing
immediately before the parable of the great supper (14:16--24). \textbf{The
corpus makes no dated assertion about this sabbath dinner}: it carries no
narrated-event claim for the pericope at all.}\\*
Composition alternatives are traditional: \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} XIV, ``The New Testament'' (14530a), and the Biblical Commission's 1912 response on Mark and Luke, which dates writing relative to Acts and the Roman imprisonment. Place, first audience and life stage are unresolved; no critical-profile claim is returned.\\
\midrule
\textbf{Epistle --- Eph. 3:13--21}. Paul in captivity; Rome or Caesarea\\*
\chronodate{epistle}{\chronologyannotation{epistle}}\\*
The Missal attributes the letter to Paul. The pericope's \latin{tribulatiónibus meis pro vobis} names his affliction. Both disputed dates are traditional (\work{Catholic Encyclopedia}, V, 05485a; XI, 11567b), the selected default profile. Ladeuze favours Rome over Caesarea and considers a circular letter to north-eastern Asia Minor beside the traditional Ephesian destination.\\
\bottomrule
