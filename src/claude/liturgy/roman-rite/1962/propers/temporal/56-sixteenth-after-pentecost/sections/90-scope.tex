\subsection*{Edition, formulary identity and text-verification state}

The controlling text is the \work{Missale Romanum}, editio typica (Vatican City:
Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis, 1962), printed pp.~397--398, the ten appointed
elements carrying marginal reference numbers 1592--1601. The formulary is
bounded by no.~1591, the Fifteenth Sunday's Postcommunion, and by the heading of
the Seventeenth Sunday at no.~1602;%
\ifdefined\TriptychSynthesisEdition{} the complete text between those boundaries
is printed in the canonical full edition and not in this one, the ten elements
reaching this edition through the map and the page-2 dossier sheet.%
\else{} nothing between those boundaries is omitted
here.%
\fi{} Rank \latin{II classis}; \latin{Credo}; Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.
\textbf{Green follows the general rubrics and is not printed at the formulary},
being recorded as an inference from RG~127~b rather than as a reading of
pp.~397--398. \textbf{No page of the 1,088-page facsimile carries a running head
naming this Sunday}, so a finding aid that locates a formulary by its running
head reports this one absent.

\textbf{Text-verification state.} Every published Latin form, rubric, citation,
marginal number and boundary was read as page images rendered from the
identified facsimile of the typical edition at 200~dpi and again at 400~dpi,
with the Secret's conclusion and the Communion antiphon re-read at the images'
native 500~ppi. No transcription, no optical layer and no web text supplied a
reading. \textbf{The facsimile's own embedded text layer misled once in this
formulary}, giving \latin{extranet} for \latin{éxtrahet} at Luke~14:5, and the
page image settles it. Collated 2026-09-05; no unresolved reading remains in the
controlling edition.

\textbf{The second 1962 witness was read on page images too, and is recorded and
never adopted.} The Benziger \latin{editio iuxta typicam} differs from the
typical edition at eight places, two of them substantive --- a repetition cue
after the Introit's doxology, and a longer conclusion to the Secret --- and six
of pointing, abbreviation or paging. Where the two books differ the difference
is an edition difference and never an uncertain reading in the typical edition;
nothing is blended and no Benziger form is substituted.

\textbf{The two books that carry the century chain back from 1962 stand at a
weaker state than either.} The 1920 typical edition and the pre-1955 Benziger
were opened at this formulary in uncorrected optical layers of small-type altar
books, with no page image of either behind them, and those layers damage the
formulary's own numerals: the 1920 gives the Gospel's reference as \texttt{huc.
24, 2-22} and the Offertory's as \texttt{Ps. 39, 24 et 15}. The stability
claimed of the century before 1962 is therefore claimed of the ten texts and
their order, and is an inference and not a collation at the psalm citations.

\textbf{The controlling facsimile is bitonal.} The 1962 typical edition is
printed in red and black; the scan carries one bit per component and no colour
plane, so the colour of no mark on these two pages can be read from it. Where
this guide calls something a rubric it names its function and its type, never
its ink.

\subsection*{English, and the translations of the witnesses}

Scriptural elements take the Douay--Rheims as revised by Bishop Challoner
(1749--1752), the English of the Clementine Vulgate the missal prints. The three
orations take the anonymous English of \work{The Roman Missal translated into
the English language for the use of the laity}, first revised edition
(Philadelphia: Eugene Cummiskey, 1861), cited by formulary rather than by page;
all three were collated against that book's page images at printed pp.~429 and
431 and stand there character for character, including the abbreviated
conclusion ``Thro'.'' \textbf{That English is a free devotional rendering and not
a construe of the Latin}, and the book names no translator, so the edition is
cited and no translator is. \textbf{The 1861's own English of the scriptural
elements is not used}: it prints free renderings of the Introit, Offertory and
Communion and its own Epistle and Gospel, and the profile appoints the
Douay--Rheims for those. \textbf{Nothing of the appointed text is composed,
translated, adapted or paraphrased here}, and no element of this formulary lacks
public-domain English. Neither witness is an official liturgical translation and
neither is the text of the 1962 books.

\subsection*{Dates, and the psalm numbering}

\textbf{Every biblical date printed in this guide comes from the Scripture
chronology corpus and from nowhere else}, carried in
\texttt{research/chronology.toml} and printed through the generated projection
in the Date column of page~2. Seven appointed Scriptures each have one cell, and
what stands in it is the deterministic concise projection of the corpus's own
structured date, each source's raw wording and profile being kept in the
generated file's audit form rather than on the page.
\textbf{The Gospel's narrated event has no date in the corpus} --- the sabbath
dinner is dated nowhere --- and the cell states that absence. \textbf{Composition
place, first audience and the writer's life stage are not stated for any of the
seven}: the three \work{Catholic Encyclopedia} articles the corpus cites for
those two elements --- vol.~V, article 05485a and vol.~XI, article 11567b for
the Epistle, and vol.~XIV, article 14530a for the Gospel --- and the Biblical
Commission responsa beside them are the nearest sources that would carry them,
and they were opened for their chronology alone; nothing comparable was
identified for the five psalms, whose only composition claim is a boundary
shared by the whole Psalter.%
\ifdefined\TriptychSynthesisEdition{} \textbf{Where a psalm commentator quoted
above gives a psalm a speaker and an occasion} --- Theodoret's Babylonian
captives at Ps.~101 --- it stands above as what that source says, never as the
date or setting of the passage.%
\else{} \textbf{The psalm commentators quoted above give
each psalm a speaker and an occasion} --- Theodoret's Sennacherib at Ps.~85 and
Babylonian captives at Ps.~101, Bellarmine's David with the bear and the lion at
Ps.~70, Cassiodorus's Christ in the assumed humanity at Ps.~85 --- and each
stands above as what that source says, never as the date or setting of the
passage.%
\fi

\textbf{Psalms are cited by the Vulgate numbering the missal prints}, with the
modern number in parentheses on page~2 and the convention named where the two
modern English series part. Ps.~39 and Ps.~101 carry their titles as numbered
first verses, so an English Bible runs one verse behind at the Offertory and the
Gradual; Ps.~70, 85 and 97 fold their titles into verse one, so the Introit, the
Alleluia and the Communion agree in both series. The same offset governs the
marginal verse figures on the New Advent pages of the \work{Enarrationes}, and
that series titles each exposition by the Hebrew psalm number.

\subsection*{Source scope, and what was not reached}

\textbf{Languages: Latin, Greek and English, with two Greek authors read in
translation and one at his own text.} No Greek New Testament and no Septuagint
is tracked in this repository, so the Gospel's shorter clause at 14:3 is not
settled against the Greek; and no Old Latin, Roman, Ambrosian or Mozarabic
psalter is tracked, so no chant reading above is traced to an older psalter.
Chrysostom is read in English and Cyril in an English of a Syriac version;
Theodoret is read in Migne's Greek with Migne's own facing Latin beside it.

\textbf{Four commentators stand at each of the five appointed psalms.}
Augustine, Cassiodorus, Theodoret and Bellarmine expound all five; Aquinas
reaches the Offertory's alone, his \work{Postilla super Psalmos} breaking off
inside Psalm~54; and Jerome adjudicates the wording at the Offertory and the
Communion. \textbf{What is not established is whether the psalm works of
Chrysostom, Jerome and Hilary reach these five at all}: their
\work{Expositiones in psalmos}, \work{Tractatus in psalmos} and
\work{Tractatus super psalmos} were not opened and no extent record for them is
tracked, so nothing above rests on their silence. The Latin original of
Bellarmine's \work{Explanatio in Psalmos} was not opened, and Theodoret is the
one Greek psalm voice held. Ambrosiaster, Jerome on Ephesians, Marius Victorinus
and Gregory of Nyssa were not opened at the Epistle; Theophylact, the Basilian
ascetica and Gregory's \work{Moralia} were not opened at the Gospel. The Second
Council of Orange, the other conciliar place the Collect's doctrine stands, was
not opened.

\textbf{No patristic or saintly reception of the Secret or the Postcommunion was
found.} The search ran over the Latin corpora retrieved for this formulary ---
Augustine's \work{Enarrationes} on Pss.~31--40, 61--70, 81--90, 91--100 and
101--110 and his \work{Quaestiones euangeliorum}, Ambrose's \work{Expositio}
VII--IX, Bede's \work{In Lucam} entire, Benedict's \work{Regula} entire,
Aquinas's \work{Catena in Lucam} 14--18, \work{Super Ephesios} entire and
\work{Super Psalmos} 31--40, and the Tridentine text --- for \latin{sacrificii
praesentis}, \latin{mereamur esse participes}, \latin{caelestibus sacramentis}
with its \latin{coelestibus} spelling, and \latin{Munda nos}, and returned no
hit. It is not a search of the patristic corpus at large, and a phrase search
cannot find a Father who paraphrases an oration without quoting it. The medieval
\latin{expositores missae}, where such reception would show up if anywhere, were
not walked, and nothing from Amalarius, Honorius Augustodunensis, Sicardus or
Durandus is cited in the commentary.

\textbf{One formulary-level commentator reads this Mass as a whole, and he
carries no liturgical history.} Guéranger's chapter stands at printed

sentence and the Introit were read on a rendered page image of the registered

text --- CCSL~98 or PL~70 at a column --- would settle the four Cassiodorus
lemmata on which four of the agreements above rest.

\subsection*{The gallery and the exploratory proposals}

The five entries of \work{The Propers: Notable and Quotable} are documented
reuses of wording from the Epistle and the Gospel. \textbf{The five psalm chants
produced none over a corpus of ninety volumes and a newspaper collection}, and
the obstacle was checked rather than assumed at each: the Alleluia's clause is
not distinctive to its verse, \latin{Miserére mihi} is not the famous
\work{Miserere}, and the Offertory's petition is too near the versicle that
opens every Hour. \textbf{The corpora were English and Latin, with German and
Spanish opened once each because a named candidate led there}, so a French or
Italian afterlife would not have been found, and musical settings were not
counted --- a real exclusion, all five chants of this Mass having substantial
chant and polyphonic afterlives. Two hosts could not be opened. \textbf{Eight
further candidates were reached and set aside}, and the reasons are of five
kinds: four of them turn on Eph.~3:21's ``world without end,'' a clause that
also closes the \latin{Gloria Patri} this same formulary cues at its own
Introit, so no entry on it could show the Epistle rather than the doxology as
the channel; one turns Luke~14:10 from inside the tradition rather than out of
it, where the gallery prefers the secular or ironic turn; one is the Spanish
proverbial form of Luke~14:11 and not the appointed clause --- Cervantes at
\work{Don Quijote} I cap.~XI, whose \latin{Dios le ensalza} adds an agent the
Vulgate and the Douay have not; one stands in a 1958 newspaper that names no
author and is under ninety-five years old; and one was by 1889 an ordinary
English idiom carrying no sign of quotation. The remark above
about Luther's German at Luke~18:14 rests on the standing Luther text: no
public-domain German Bible could be opened here.

The six proposals of \work{The Propers: Interpretive Possibilities} are
exploratory editorial and AI proposals. The precedent search behind them swept
the repository's own published guides and research records, the tracked 1962
calendar registry, and twelve full-text missal payloads; \textbf{it did not reach
the chant repertories, the pre-Tridentine Latin commentary tradition, the
sacramentary editions themselves, or any page image}. \textbf{No guide to this
formulary stands anywhere else in this collection}, so the control a companion
guide to the same formulary would give was unavailable, and every precedent
result rests on neighbouring identities and on the appointed-text registry,
which is a weaker control.

\subsection*{Rights, and the state of this guide}

\textbf{The Latin.} All ten elements are publishable, and none of them on any
property of the 1962 typical edition. The route is settled once for this
repository at
\texttt{src/sources/inventories/missale-\allowbreak romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml}:
17 U.S.C.~103(b) together with a public-domain witness. The three orations stand
as preexisting material corroborated word for word in the tracked public-domain
\work{Missale Romanum} (Ratisbon: Pustet, 1862), whose pp.~340--341 carry them,
\textbf{and the one gap in that argument --- the conjunction \latin{et} before
\latin{rénova}, absent from the 1862 optical layer at a line break --- is closed
on the 1862 page image, where it is printed.} The seven scriptural elements are
the Vulgate. The facsimile's pagination, marginal numbering and arrangement as
such are not reproduced; its numbers and page locations are facts and are cited
freely.

\textbf{The English} of both witnesses is public domain by age. No translation
under copyright is used, quoted or consulted: not the ICEL Missal, the Knox
Bible, the Jerusalem or New Jerusalem Bibles, the RSV or NRSV, the NABRE, or the
Grail psalms. \textbf{Unresolved rights questions for any text of this
formulary: none.}

\textbf{The witnesses.} New Advent's presentation of the public-domain
\work{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers} translations is registered with its rights

\textbf{Two translations of witnesses quoted above are under copyright or
unestablished, and neither is reproduced}: R.~C. Hill's

attributed; and Helen Zimmern's 1910 English of Nietzsche, public domain in the

\textbf{Four items in this guide's own source records were carried forward for
repair; three of them now stand repaired and none of the four blocks a claim

\textbf{Review state.} This is a study companion for use beside a hand missal,
not an official liturgical book, a critical edition, a homily or a substitute
for the cited sources. It has had no specialist, clerical, physical-use or
ecclesiastical review, and none is claimed.
