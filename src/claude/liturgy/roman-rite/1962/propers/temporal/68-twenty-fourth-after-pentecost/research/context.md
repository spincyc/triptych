## Occurrence on 22 November 2026

**Computation (finding aid only).** `tools/tpt calendar-days day --calendar
roman-1962 --date 2026-11-22 --json` returns the candidate
`last-sunday-after-pentecost`, "Twenty-fourth and Last Sunday after
Pentecost", rule "the Sunday before the First Sunday of Advent; the
twenty-fourth formulary always keeps the last place", season after Pentecost,
no Lectionary value, nothing unresolved, no commemorations, with a second
candidate, the third-class feast `s-caeciliae-virginis-martyris` (the day
layer leaves `appointed` null and ranks nothing; precedence is the rubrics
layer). Recomputed here: Easter 5 April 2026, Pentecost 24 May, Trinity Sunday
(the first Sunday after Pentecost) 31 May; 31 May plus twenty-five weeks is

twenty-sixth and last of them; the First Sunday of Advent is 29 November, the
Sunday nearest 30 November. 22 November 2026 is a Sunday: the year begins on a
Thursday, its dominical letter is *D*, and the *Calendarium* gives 22 November
the letter *d*.

### Wording against the Clementine Vulgate

The comparisons below were made against the tracked Clementine Vulgate
(`edition.catholic-church.vulgata-clementina.ebible-latvuc`, its verse-text
artifacts) through `tools/tpt mass-propers show --calendar roman-1962 --mass
last-sunday-after-pentecost --bible clementine-vulgate`, with the Epistle and
Gospel compared token by token by script against a reading of the page
images, after normalising i/j, ligatures and accents and ignoring pointing and
capitals. They are textual observations about wording and boundary. Whether a
chant or lesson follows an older Latin text, and which, is left to research,
and no origin is asserted here.

- **Introit.** The antiphon is not a continuous Vulgate text. The Clementine at
  Jer 29:11 reads *Ego enim scio cogitationes quas ego cogito super vos, ait
  Dominus, cogitationes pacis et non afflictionis, ut dem vobis finem et
  patientiam*; v. 12 *Et invocabitis me, et ibitis: et orabitis me, et ego
  exaudiam vos*; v. 14 *Et inveniar a vobis, ait Dominus: et reducam
  captivitatem vestram, et congregabo vos de universis gentibus et de cunctis
  locis …*. The Missal opens *Dicit Dominus: Ego cogito cogitationes pacis*,
  has nothing of *ut dem vobis finem et patientiam*, *et ibitis: et orabitis
  me*, v. 13, *Et inveniar a vobis* or *et congregabo vos de universis
  gentibus*, and joins *reducam captivitatem vestram* directly to *de cunctis
  locis*. The psalm verse agrees with Ps 84:2.
- **Epistle.** The Missal's *Fratres: Non cessamus* stands for the
  Clementine's *Ideo et nos ex qua die audivimus, non cessamus*, so the lesson
  begins within v. 9; and in v. 9 the Missal reads *agnitione voluntatis Dei*
  where the Clementine reads *voluntatis ejus*. Every other word of vv. 9-14
  agrees; the Clementine's v. 14 ends with a colon, the sentence continuing
  into v. 15, where the Missal ends with a full stop.
- **Gradual.** The Missal reads *Liberasti nos, Domine, ex affligentibus nos:
  et eos, qui nos oderunt, confudisti* where the Clementine at Ps 43:8 reads
  *salvasti enim nos de affligentibus nos, et odientes nos confudisti*, and *in
  saecula* where v. 9 reads *in saeculum*.
- **Alleluia and Offertory.** Both read *Domine, exaudi orationem meam* where
  the Clementine at Ps 129:2 reads *Domine, exaudi vocem meam*; both stop before
  v. 2b and omit the title *Canticum graduum*. The Offertory repeats its first
  line at the end.
- **Gospel.** The liturgical opening *In illo tempore: Dixit Iesus discipulis
  suis: Cum videritis* stands for the Clementine's *Cum ergo videritis* at the
  head of v. 15, dropping *ergo*, which in Matthew ties the saying to the
  question of vv. 3-14. The Missal spells *intellegat* where the Clementine has
  *intelligat* (orthography). Every other word of vv. 15-35 agrees, with
  pointing differences only (for instance *prope est in ianuis* against *prope
  est, in januis*).
- **Communion.** The Missal reads *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orantes petitis …
  et fiet vobis* where the Clementine at Mk 11:24 reads *Propterea dico vobis,
  omnia quaecumque orantes petitis … et evenient vobis*; *credite quia
  accipietis* agrees.
