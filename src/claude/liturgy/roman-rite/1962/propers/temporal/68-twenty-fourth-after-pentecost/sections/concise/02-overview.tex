\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:start}
\begin{conciseoverview}
Literal\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:literal} & God promises the exiles in
Babylon peace, a hearing and return; one psalm thanks God for deliverance,
another cries from the depths; Paul thanks the Father who delivered the
Colossians from darkness; Jesus bids those in Judea flee, which Chrysostom
reads of the Roman war, foretells his coming and the gathering of the elect,
and says that his words will not pass.\\
Allegorical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:allegorical} & The body to which
the eagles gather is the Passion of Christ (Jerome), the place of the Passion
(Hilary), or his body taken into heaven (Augustine); the captivity turned
back is sin (Augustine); the housetop is the height of one new by rebirth
(Hilary), the abomination every perverse doctrine standing in the Church
(Jerome); the generation that does not pass is that of believers
(Chrysostom).\\
Moral\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:moral} & Not to go down from the roof by
desire for worldly things (Hilary), nor let faith and charity grow cold or lie
idle in the work of God (Jerome); to confess the captivity one is in, that one
may be set free (Augustine); to believe what is to come from what is already
fulfilled (Chrysostom, Gregory); to pray that one be heard (Hilary), and to
forgive others first (Bede).\\
Anagogical\zlabel{triptych:concise:sense:anagogical} & The angels assemble
the saints in the place of the Passion, where the glory returns (Hilary);
after the confusion of this world, their penance accepted, sinners are brought
back to their own place, the Church (Jerome); the just have a spiritual summer
after the tempest (Chrysostom), when the clouds of grief pass (Gregory).\\
\end{conciseoverview}
\zlabel{triptych:concise:overview:end}
