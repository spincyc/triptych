in the last place after Pentecost (research/context.md, *Occurrence on
22 November 2026*). Nothing is commemorated and no oration is added (RG 111 b).
Read and recorded on 16 November 2026 in the research stage (iteration 0) of
proper-study run `11c5af05fe374203`.

## The publication basis of the Latin

The repository's holding
(`src/sources/inventories/missale-romanum-1962-facsimile-rights-v1.toml`) is
that the 1962 printing is a collation witness and that 17 U.S.C. 103(b) frees
only preexisting material: an element's Latin may be published where a
public-domain printing carries the same words. Orthography, pointing,
capitalisation, accents and the length of a conclusion cue are transformations
and do not change a verdict (`guidance/propers-for-agents.md`).

Pustet 1862, *Dominica XXIV. et ultima post Pentecosten*, printed pp. 359-360,
read on its page images, prints every element of this formulary written out
under its own heading:

- p. 359, left column: Introit (*Introitus. Jeremiæ 29.*; verse *Ps. 84.*),
  Collect (*Oratio.*), the Epistle's heading and opening (*Léctio Epístolæ
  beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Colossénses. c. 1.*); right column: the rest of the
  Epistle, Gradual (*Ps. 43.*), Alleluia (*Ps. 129.*), and the Gospel's heading
  (*Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthæum. c. 24.*) and opening;
- p. 360, left column: the Gospel to *donec ómnia hæc fiant*; right column: the
  Gospel's last sentence, *Credo.*, Offertory (*Ps. 129.*), Secret, Communion
  (*Marci 11.*), Postcommunion.

The 1862 also prints, between the Collect and the Epistle, *Secunda Oratio. A
cunctis nos. fol. 315.* and *Tertia ad libitum.*, and after the Secret and the
Postcommunion *Alia Secreta. Exaúdi nos Deus.* and *Alia Postcommúnio. Mundet et
múniat.* with *Tertia ad libitum.*: the pre-1960 additional orations that
research/context.md already records as no part of the 1962 formulary.

The differences between the 1862 and 1962 printings of the ten elements are:

- **orthography**: *exequéntes* (1862) for *exsequéntes* (1962), one verb
  *exsequor*; *intélligat* for *intéllegat*; *Jesus*, *Judǽa*, *ejus*,
  *majóra*, *majestáte*, *jam*, *jánuis*, *Jacob*, *Allelúja* for the 1962 *i*
  forms; *cœlo*, *cœlórum*, *cœli*, *cœléstia* for *cælo* and its forms;
- **pointing**: among other places, *quæsumus Dómine,* and *voluntátes: ut
  divíni* without the 1962 commas, *exequéntes, pietátis* for *exsequéntes;
  pietátis*; *Fratres.* with a full stop; *orántes et postulántes, ut*;
  *claritátis ejus, in omni patiéntia*; *dilectiónis suæ; in quo*;
  *clamávi ad te Dómine, Dómine exaúdi* in the Alleluia verse and the
  Offertory; *Christus: aut illic*; *pseudoprophétæ, et dabunt*; *cum tuba, et
  voce*; *esto,Dómine*; *convérte: ut a terrénis* for *convérte; ut, a
  terrénis*; *quæsumus Dómine: ut per hæc*;
- **capitalisation**: *ecce in desérto* (1862) for *Ecce in desérto*; *dixit
  Jesus* after the colon;
- **conclusion cue**: the Secret ends *Per Dóminum.* in 1862 and *Per Dóminum
  nostrum.* in 1962;
- **citation form**: *Jeremiæ 29.*, *Ps. 84.*, *c. 1.*, *Ps. 43.*, *Ps. 129.*,
  *c. 24.*, *Marci 11.* against the 1962's verse-exact citations.

**No word of any element's body differs.** Every place where an element departs
from the Clementine Vulgate (below) stands word for word in 1862. The Gospel
citation line reads *c. 24.* on the 1862 page image (PDF p. 445, right column);
the tracked text layer's *c. 21.* is an OCR error (`propers/retrieved.txt`, pull
2).

**Result: every word of all ten elements stands in the public-domain Pustet
1862 printing, read on its page images.** The Latin of the whole formulary may
be published on 17 U.S.C. 103(b) with that witness, in the 1962 page's
orthography. The Latin provenance ledger
(`src/sources/inventories/roman-1962-proper-latin-provenance-v1.toml`) carries
collated `permitted` rows for the three orations (`mass =
"last-sunday-after-pentecost"`), with the 1862 text layer as their publication
witness; on the 1962 page images read here their bodies and conclusions agree
with the ledger. It has no row for the five chants or the two lessons. This
record is the page-image collation for those elements; the missing ledger rows
are reported to their owner in research/scope.md, section 11, and not added
here.

## Appointed text, as printed in 1962

### 1. Introit (`introit`) — no. 1769, printed p. 422, right column

*Antiphona ad Introitum*, citation *Ierem. 29, 11, 12 et 14*

> Dicit Dóminus: Ego cógito cogitatiónes pacis, et non afflictiónis:
> invocábitis me, et ego exáudiam vos: et redúcam captivitátem vestram de
> cunctis locis. *Ps. 84, 2* Benedixísti, Dómine, terram tuam: avertísti
> captivitátem Iacob. ℣. Glória Patri.

### 2. Collect (`collect`) — no. 1770, printed p. 423, left column

*Oratio*

> Excita, quǽsumus, Dómine, tuórum fidélium voluntátes: ut, divíni óperis
> fructum propénsius exsequéntes; pietátis tuæ remédia maióra percípiant. Per
> Dóminum.

### 3. Epistle (`epistle`) — no. 1771, printed p. 423, left column

*Léctio Epístolæ beáti Pauli Apóstoli ad Colossénses.* — *Col. 1, 9-14*

> Fratres: Non cessámus pro vobis orántes, et postulántes ut impleámini
> agnitióne voluntátis Dei, in omni sapiéntia et intelléctu spiritáli: ut
> ambulétis digne Deo per ómnia placéntes: in omni ópere bono fructificántes,
> et crescéntes in sciéntia Dei: in omni virtúte confortáti secúndum
> poténtiam claritátis eius in omni patiéntia, et longanimitáte cum gáudio,
> grátias agéntes Deo Patri, qui dignos nos fecit in partem sortis sanctórum
> in lúmine: qui erípuit nos de potestáte tenebrárum, et tránstulit in regnum
> Fílii dilectiónis suæ, in quo habémus redemptiónem per sánguinem eius,
> remissiónem peccatórum.

### 4. Gradual (`gradual`) — no. 1772, printed p. 423, left column

*Graduale* — *Ps. 43, 8-9*

> Liberásti nos, Dómine, ex affligéntibus nos: et eos, qui nos odérunt,
> confudísti. ℣. In Deo laudábimur tota die, et in nómine tuo confitébimur in
> sǽcula.

### 5. Alleluia (`alleluia`) — no. 1773, printed p. 423, left column

> Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. *Ps. 129, 1-2* De profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine:
> Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam. Allelúia.

### 6. Gospel (`gospel`) — no. 1774, printed p. 423, left column foot and right column, to p. 424, left column

*Sequéntia sancti Evangélii secúndum Matthǽum.* — *Mt. 24, 15-35*

> In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Cum vidéritis abominatiónem
> desolatiónis, quæ dicta est a Daniéle prophéta, stantem in loco sancto: qui
> legit, intéllegat: tunc qui in Iudǽa sunt, fúgiant ad montes: et qui in
> tecto, non descéndat tóllere áliquid de domo sua: et qui in agro, non
> revertátur tóllere túnicam suam. Væ autem prægnántibus et nutriéntibus in
> illis diébus. Oráte autem, ut non fiat fuga vestra in híeme vel sábbato. Erit
> enim tunc tribulátio magna, qualis non fuit ab inítio mundi usque modo,
> neque fiet. Et nisi breviáti fuíssent dies illi, non fíeret salva omnis
> caro: sed propter eléctos breviabúntur dies illi. Tunc si quis vobis
> díxerit: Ecce hic est Christus, aut illic: nolíte crédere. Surgent enim
> pseudochrísti, et pseudoprophétæ: et dabunt signa magna et prodígia, ita ut
> in errórem inducántur (si fíeri potest) étiam elécti. Ecce prædíxi vobis. Si
> ergo díxerint vobis: Ecce in desérto est, nolíte exíre: ecce in
> penetrálibus, nolíte crédere. Sicut enim fulgur exit ab Oriénte, et paret
> usque in Occidéntem: ita erit et advéntus Fílii hóminis. Ubicúmque fúerit
> corpus, illic congregabúntur et áquilæ. Statim autem post tribulatiónem
> diérum illórum sol obscurábitur, et luna non dabit lumen suum, et stellæ
> cadent de cælo, et virtútes cælórum commovebúntur: et tunc parébit signum
> Fílii hóminis in cælo: et tunc plangent omnes tribus terræ: et vidébunt
> Fílium hóminis veniéntem in núbibus cæli cum virtúte multa, et maiestáte. Et
> mittet Angelos suos cum tuba et voce magna: et congregábunt eléctos eius a
> quátuor ventis, a summis cælórum usque ad términos eórum. Ab árbore autem
> fici díscite parábolam: cum iam ramus eius tener fúerit, et fólia nata,
> scitis quia prope est æstas: ita et vos cum vidéritis hæc ómnia, scitóte
> quia prope est in iánuis. Amen dico vobis, quia non præteríbit generátio
> hæc, donec ómnia hæc fiant. Cælum et terra transíbunt, verba autem mea non
> præteríbunt.

Printed cue after the Gospel: *Credo.*

### 7. Offertory (`offertory`) — no. 1775, printed p. 424, left column

*Ant. ad Offertorium* — *Ps. 129, 1-2*

> De profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine: Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam: de
> profúndis clamávi ad te, Dómine.

### 8. Secret (`secret`) — no. 1776, printed p. 424, left column to right column

*Secreta*

> Propítius esto, Dómine, supplicatiónibus nostris: et, pópuli tui
> oblatiónibus precibúsque suscéptis, ómnium nostrum ad te corda convérte;
> ut, a terrénis cupiditátibus liberáti, ad cæléstia desidéria transeámus. Per
> Dóminum nostrum.

Printed direction after the Secret: *Præfatio de Ssma Trinitate.*

### 9. Communion (`communion`) — no. 1777, printed p. 424, right column

*Ant. ad Communionem* — *Marc. 11, 24*

> Amen dico vobis, quidquid orántes pétitis, crédite quia accipiétis, et fiet
> vobis.

### 10. Postcommunion (`postcommunion`) — no. 1778, printed p. 424, right column

*Postcommunio*

> Concéde nobis, quǽsumus, Dómine: ut per hæc sacraménta quæ súmpsimus,
> quidquid in nostra mente vitiósum est, ipsórum medicatiónis dono curétur.
> Per Dóminum.

Every oration ends with the one fixed conclusion of the Missal whichever cue
the page prints (*Per Dóminum.* or *Per Dóminum nostrum.*).

## The chants and lessons against the Clementine Vulgate

research/context.md, *Wording against the Clementine Vulgate*, records the
comparison; this stage re-read Col 1:9-14, Mt 24:15, Ps 43:8 and Ps 129:2 in
the Clementine verse-text TSVs and confirms it on the 1962 page images:

- **Introit.** A cento of Jer 29:11-12, 14 opened by *Dicit Dóminus*, leaving
  out *ut dem vobis finem et patientiam*, *et ibitis: et orabitis me*, v. 13,
  *Et inveniar a vobis* and *et congregabo vos de universis gentibus*; *et
  redúcam captivitátem vestram* joined to *de cunctis locis*. The verse agrees
  with Ps 84:2.
- **Epistle.** *Fratres: Non cessámus* for the Clementine *Ideo et nos ex qua
  die audivimus, non cessamus*; *voluntátis Dei* for *voluntatis ejus*. Every
  other word agrees; pointing differs (the Clementine sets a comma after
  *claritatis ejus* and none after *patientia*).
- **Gradual.** *Liberásti nos … ex affligéntibus nos: et eos, qui nos odérunt,
  confudísti* for the Clementine *salvasti enim nos de affligentibus nos, et
  odientes nos confudisti*; *in sǽcula* for *in saeculum*.
- **Alleluia and Offertory.** *oratiónem meam* for the Clementine *vocem
  meam*; the Offertory repeats its first line.
- **Gospel.** *In illo témpore: Dixit Iesus discípulis suis: Cum vidéritis* for
  *Cum ergo videritis*; *intéllegat* for *intelligat*; otherwise pointing only.
- **Communion.** *Amen dico vobis, quidquid … et fiet vobis* for the Clementine
  *Propterea dico vobis, omnia quaecumque … et evenient vobis*.

One datum on the Gradual's form was read in this stage (research/scope.md,
section 2.3): Augustine's lemma of Ps 43:8-9 in *Enarrationes in Psalmos* 43.8-9
reads *Salvos enim fecisti nos ex affligentibus nos, et eos qui oderunt nos
confudisti* and *confitebimur in saecula*. It is a textual observation, not a
derivation; no origin is asserted for any form.

## English

### Lasance 1945, printed pp. 736-740

Lasance prints this formulary under the heading *Twenty-fourth and Last Sunday
after Pentecost (Green)*, with the rubric that it "is always said on the last
Sunday after Pentecost, that is, the Sunday preceding the first Sunday of
Advent", in Latin and English columns. Read on its page images (PDF pp.
734-738):

- **Introit** (p. 736): "The Lord saith, I think thoughts of peace, and not of
  affliction; you shall call upon Me and I will hear you; and I will bring back
  your captivity from all places. *Ps. 84, 2.* Lord, Thou hast blessed Thy land:
  Thou hast turned away the captivity of Jacob. ℣. Glory."
- **Collect** (p. 737, *Prayer*): "Arouse, we beseech Thee, O Lord, the wills of
  Thy faithful, that, by more earnestly following after the fruit of the divine
  work, they may the more abundantly partake of Thy mercies. Through our Lord."
- **Epistle** (p. 737): "Lesson from the Epistle of blessed Paul the Apostle to
  the Colossians. Brethren, We cease not to pray for you, and to beg that you may
  be filled with the knowledge of the will of God, in all wisdom and spiritual
  understanding; that you may walk worthy of God, in all things pleasing, being
  fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God;
  strengthened with all might according to the power of His glory, in all
  patience and long-suffering with joy; giving thanks to God the Father, Who
  hath made us worthy to be partakers of the lot of the saints in light; Who
  hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the
  kingdom of the Son of His love, in Whom we have redemption through His blood,
  the remission of sins."
- **Gradual** (pp. 737-738): "Thou hast delivered us, O Lord, from them that
  afflict us: and hast put them to shame that hate us. ℣. In God we will glory
  all the day: and in Thy name we will give praise for ever."
- **Alleluia** (p. 738): "Alleluia, alleluia. ℣. *Ps. 129, 1, 2.* From the
  depths I have cried to Thee, O Lord: Lord, hear my prayer. Alleluia."
- **Gospel** (pp. 738-739): "Continuation of the holy Gospel according to St.
  Matthew. At that time, Jesus said to His disciples, When you shall see the
  abomination of desolation …" through "Heaven and earth shall pass away, but My
  words shall not pass away." It is the Douay text with the liturgical opening
  and light revisions: for instance "such as has not been found from the
  beginning of the world" where the Challoner verse text reads "such as hath not
  been from the beginning of the world", "show" for "shew", and capitalised
  divine pronouns.
- **Offertory** (p. 739): "From the depths I have cried out to Thee, O Lord:
  Lord, hear my prayer: from the depths I have cried out to Thee, O Lord."
- **Secret** (p. 740): "Be propitious, O Lord, to our supplications, and accept
  the offerings and prayers of Thy people: turn all our hearts unto Thee, that,
  being delivered from earthly desires, we may pass on to the enjoyments of
  heaven. Through our Lord."
- **Communion** (p. 740): "Amen, I say to you, whatsoever you ask when you pray,
  believe that you shall receive, and it shall be done unto you."
- **Postcommunion** (p. 740): "Grant us, we beseech Thee, O Lord, that, through
  this sacrament which we have received, whatever is evil in our hearts may be
  restored by its gift of healing. Through our Lord."

Lasance renders the Missal's forms where the Douay cannot: *voluntátis Dei* ("the
will of God"), *Liberásti* ("Thou hast delivered us"), *oratiónem meam* ("my
prayer"), *Amen dico vobis, quidquid* ("Amen, I say to you, whatsoever"), and the
liturgical openings of both lessons. Its oration English is its own and not a
literal rendering: the Postcommunion's plural *hæc sacraménta* is "this
sacrament" and *in nostra mente* "in our hearts"; the Collect's *pietátis tuæ
remédia maióra* is "the more abundantly … Thy mercies". Its oration rows in the
English overlay (`src/sources/inventories/roman-1962-proper-translations-v1.toml`,
`mass = "last-sunday-after-pentecost"`, Collect p. 737, Secret and
Postcommunion p. 740, `collation_result = "confirmed"`) agree with the page
images read here. The edition is public domain in the United States by
non-renewal on the repository's record
(`src/sources/inventories/lasance-new-roman-missal-rights-v1.toml`); these pages
stand in the body of the book, outside the excluded material that record names.

### Douay-Rheims (Challoner)

The Douay verse texts carry the Epistle's body except its opening and *Dei*
("Therefore we also, from the day that we heard it, cease not to pray for you
… the knowledge of his will"), the Gospel's body except its opening ("When
therefore you shall see"), and Ps 84:2. They do not carry the Missal's form of
the Introit antiphon, the Gradual ("But thou hast saved us … for ever"), the
Alleluia and Offertory ("Lord, hear my voice") or the Communion ("Therefore I
say unto you, all things, whatsoever you ask … and they shall come unto you").

The Douay source verses to set beside the chants whose Missal Latin is not the
Vulgate verse, read in the tracked Douay-Rheims (Challoner) verse texts
(iteration 1):

- **Jer 29:11-12, 14** (Introit antiphon): "For I know the thoughts that I
  think towards you, saith the Lord, thoughts of peace, and not of affliction,
  to give you an end and patience. And you shall call upon me, and you shall
  go: and you shall pray to me, and I will hear you. … And I will be found by
  you, saith the Lord: and I will bring back your captivity, and I will gather
  you out of all nations, and from all the places to which I have driven you
  out, saith the Lord: and I will bring you back from the place to which I
  caused you to be carried away captive."
- **Ps 43:8-9** (Gradual): "But thou hast saved us from them that afflict us:
  and hast put them to shame that hate us. In God shall we glory all the day
  long: and in thy name we will give praise for ever."
- **Ps 129:1-2** (Alleluia and Offertory): "Out of the depths I have cried to
  thee, O Lord: Lord, hear my voice. Let thy ears be attentive to the voice of
  my supplication."
- **Mk 11:24** (Communion): "Therefore I say unto you, all things, whatsoever
  you ask when ye pray, believe that you shall receive: and they shall come
  unto you."

The Introit verse (Ps 84:2) is the Vulgate's and its Douay is the display text:
"Lord, thou hast blessed thy land: thou hast turned away the captivity of
Jacob."

### Recommended display

Revised in iteration 1 to follow the 1962 profile
(`guidance/liturgy/roman-1962-propers.md`, *English*): the scriptural elements
take the Douay-Rheims (Challoner), "the English of the Vulgate the missal
prints"; the orations take a public-domain hand missal cited by formulary.

- **Epistle and Gospel.** The Douay-Rheims (Challoner) body of each lesson,
  attributed (Col 1:9-14; Mt 24:15-35). The Missal's liturgical openings and
  its *voluntátis Dei* are disclosed as Missal forms and not blended into the
  Douay: the Epistle opens *Fratres: Non cessámus* where the Douay renders the
  Vulgate's *Ideo et nos ex qua die audivimus, non cessamus* ("Therefore we
  also, from the day that we heard it, cease not"); the Missal reads
  *voluntátis Dei* where the Douay has "his will"; the Gospel opens *In illo
