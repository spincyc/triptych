**Audience and argument.** An adult parish assembly at the Mass said on
Sunday 22 November 2026, the speech following the Gospel. It follows the
third interpretation, *My Words Shall Not Pass: What God Has Said Is as Good
as Done*, and takes the literal sense of the commands of flight from the
second, which sets that sense first. It tells the scene of Mt 24:1-3 and the
disciples' double question, and notes that the Gospel stops before v. 36. It
glosses "abomination" in the preacher's words and gives Chrysostom's reading
of the flight as a warning to the Jews before the Roman war, with his proof
from the sabbath (*Hom. in Mt.* 76.1), and Bede's account of the Christians'
withdrawal to Pella (*In Marc.* IV on 13:14). From the fulfilled first part it
draws, with Chrysostom's sentence on v. 35 (77.1), belief in the coming that
remains (vv. 30-31). It takes the false christs and the lightning that needs
no herald (vv. 23, 27; 76.3) as a warning against anyone who names a place or
a day. It meets the difficulty of "this generation" (v. 34) with
Chrysostom's generation of believers (77.1, Ps 23:6), and gives Gregory on
heaven and earth and the passing word (*Hom. in Ev.* I.1.4). It hears the
Gradual's "Thou hast delivered us" with Augustine on the future told as past
(*En. in Ps.* 43.8), and the Epistle's thanks for deliverance and the
remission of sins (Col 1:13-14) with Chrysostom on the things already theirs
as the causes of the rest (*Hom. in Col.* 2). It joins the Introit's promise
to the command to call on God (Jer 29:10-14) with Jerome (*In Hier.* V, PL 24
col. 893), and reads the Communion (Mk 11:24) with the verse after it (Mk
11:25) and Bede's condition (*In Marc.* III on 11:25). The one response,
spoken once, is to forgive before Communion and to ask forgiveness believing
that it will be received. It closes on the Secret's petition and Gregory's
gladness at the world's end (I.1.3). The Collect, Alleluia, Offertory and
Postcommunion are not used; no prayer is recited.

**What the text changes.** The governing question is the disciples' own,
"when shall these things be?" (Mt 24:3), which a congregation hearing this
Gospel at the end of the year also brings to it. The decisive textual detail
is that the Lord answers with signs, warnings, a parable and the saying
"Heaven and earth shall pass: but my words shall not pass" (24:35), while the
appointed Gospel stops before "of that day and hour no one knoweth" (24:36);
set beside it, the Gradual's past tense *Liberásti* for a deliverance still
to come, read with Augustine, and the Communion's future *accipiétis* with
its condition in Mk 11:25. These move the question from a date to the
certainty of the speaker's word: the part already fulfilled (Chrysostom,
Bede) and God's certainty of what is to come (Augustine, Gregory) ground the
rest, so that the hearers need not believe those who name a place or a day,
pray for what is promised (Jerome), forgive as the Communion's verse
requires (Bede), and hear of the end with gladness rather than dread
(Gregory). The speech states this as what the texts say, in the preacher's
voice. With unrelated readings the argument does not survive: each step
rests on Mt 24:15-36, Ps 43:8, Col 1:11-14, Jer 29:10-14, Mk 11:24-25 or the
Secret.
