# Calendar Inventory

## What this record covers, and what it does not

This leaf's corpus is the **norms, the temporal cycle, and the precedence machinery of the 1962
Missal, with the I class and II class layers of the universal calendar**. This inventory is
therefore complete for:

- every season and sub-period boundary;
- every class assignment the code makes to Sundays, ferias, vigils, and octaves;
- every closed enumeration the code states (I class Sundays, the four classes of feria, the seven
  vigils, the three octaves, the privileged commemorations, the proper feasts entering a particular
  calendar by law, the twenty-eight lines of the table of precedence);
- every movable identity derivable from Easter that the publication names;
- every fixed I class and II class entry of the universal calendar as the 1962 Missal prints it;
- every itemised change recorded in *Variationes in Calendario*, nn. 1-12.

It is **not** an inventory of the III class feasts or of the commemorations of the universal
calendar, and no completeness is claimed for either. It is not an inventory of any territorial,
diocesan, religious, or church layer. Nothing here or in the publication is a "complete calendar"
within the meaning of `guidance/liturgy/roman-calendar-references.md`, and nothing here is an Ordo.

Unqualified numbers are numbers of the 1960 *Rubricae generales*. "Missal rubrics" marks part three
of the same code. "Calendar" marks the universal *Calendarium* printed at pages XLV-LIII of the 1962
typical edition. Transcription checks are recorded in the rightmost column: *image* means the entry
was read in a rendered page image, *text* means it was read in an identified text layer and checked
against a quoted locus.

Fourteen I class Sundays every year. Sundays have First Vespers (13). An impeded Sunday is neither
anticipated nor resumed (14). A I class Sunday outranks any feast, except that the Immaculate
Conception is preferred to an occurring Sunday of Advent (15).

All five take the Sunday's place with all its rights and privileges, no commemoration of the Sunday
being made (17, closing sentence; and 16 a for the general rule).

after None (27). On Saturdays carrying a IV class feria the Office is of Holy Mary on Saturday
(78-79), which stands at line 27 of the table, immediately above the IV class ferias at line 28.

Seven, and no more. The rubric's four II class vigils and the calendar's three fixed II class
*Vigilia* entries account for each other exactly, the fourth being the movable vigil of the
Ascension. The Paschal Vigil is excluded from the category by 28 as a *pervigilium*. A II or III
class vigil is entirely omitted if it falls on a Sunday or a I class feast, or if its feast is
transferred or reduced (33).

## Octaves, exhaustively

| Octave | Class | Days within | Locus | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nativity | II | II class; the octave day, 1 January, is I class | 65, 67 | text |
| Easter | I | I class | 65, 66 | text |
| Pentecost | I | I class | 65, 66 | text |

All others excluded, in universal and particular calendars alike (64). The Christmas octave has an
order no other octave has (68): 26, 27, 28 December are simultaneously II class feasts and days
within the octave; 29 and 31 December carry commemorations; 30 December is a day within the octave
only; and for those six days only I class particular feasts of Saints inscribed universally on
those days are admitted. A Sunday falling 26-31 December takes the Office, unless a I class feast
occurs (69).

## Ember and Rogation provisions

| Provision | Rule | Locus | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ember days of Advent | Wednesday, Friday, Saturday after Advent III | Missal front matter, *De anno et eius partibus*; class at 24 b | text |
| Ember days of Lent | after Lent I | same | text |
| Ember days of Pentecost | after Pentecost Sunday | same; class is I by 66, since they fall within the octave | text |
| Ember days of September | after the third Sunday of September, which by 19 is the Sunday falling 15-21 September, so the Ember Wednesday falls 18-24 September | same; class at 24 b; colour at 127-128; privileged commemoration at 109 d | text |
| Greater Litanies | 25 April; transferred to the following Tuesday if Easter Sunday or Easter Monday falls there | 80 | text |
| Lesser Litanies (Rogations) | Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday before the Ascension, transferable by the local Ordinary to three other continuous days | 87 | text |

Nothing is done about either set of Litanies in the Office, only in the Mass (81, 88). The
obligation to say the Litany of the Saints in Latin binds on 25 April alone (84, and 90 for the
contrary rule on Rogation days). The greater Litanies' commemoration is privileged in the Mass
(109 f) and is not counted a commemoration "of the season" (81), so the bar of 112 c is not
triggered.

**Unresolved:** the pre-1955 books date the September Ember week from the Exaltation of the Holy
Cross, and the act that changed it is not identified. The change is corroborated as a change by two
independent 1960-code books, and the *Variationes in Calendario* do not mention the Ember Days.

## The two counts

| Quantity | Definition | Range | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *E* | Sundays after the Epiphany actually occurring, from the Sunday after 6 January to the Sunday before Septuagesima | 1 to 6 | recomputed 1900-2100: distribution 2, 42, 44, 47, 52, 14 |
| *P* | Sundays after Pentecost, from Trinity to the Sunday before Advent I | 23 to 28 | recomputed: distribution 4, 44, 51, 46, 44, 12; perpetual *Tabula paschalis* column agrees |
| *S* | surplus (6 − *E*) − (*P* − 24) | 0 or 1 | recomputed: 0 in 64 years, 1 in 137 |

Extreme years 1900-2100: *E* = 1 in 1913 and 2008 only; *P* = 23 in 1943, 2011, 2038, 2095 only;
*P* = 24 in exactly 44 years.

## Resumption

Four resumable identities, and no others (18, and the Missal printing exactly four such
formularies):

| Printed heading | Class | Resumed when | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Dominica tertia quae superfuit post Epiphaniam* | II | *P* = 28 only | image |
| *Dominica quarta quae superfuit post Epiphaniam* | II | *P* ≥ 27 | image |
| *Dominica quinta quae superfuit post Epiphaniam* | II | *P* ≥ 26 | located, not collated |
| *Dominica sexta quae superfuit post Epiphaniam* | II | *P* ≥ 25 | located, not collated |

All four take the Twenty-third Sunday's Introit, Gradual, Alleluia with verse, Offertory, and
Communion, and keep their own orations, Epistle, and Gospel (Missal rubrics 298). The formulary
headed *Dominica XXIV et ultima post Pentecosten* always occupies the last place. The First and
Second Sundays after the Epiphany are never resumed; an unused Second is simply omitted.

**Unresolved:** the case *P* = 23 is unlegislated. Both readings are published at full strength; a
dated Ordo for 1943, 2011, 2038, or 2095 under the 1960 rubrics would settle it, and none was read.

## III class and commemorative layers

Not inventoried. Structurally: III class feasts are the residue of *duplex maius*, *duplex minus*,
*semiduplex*, and the post-1955 *simplex* (*Variationes* nn. 3-4); they have no First Vespers
(37 b); they stand at line 24 when universal and line 23 when particular, so a particular III class
feast outranks a universal one. Commemorations inscribed in the calendar are not feasts of a lower
class but the mode of non-full celebration defined at 5. No count of either layer is asserted; a
machine tally of the Acta calendar was attempted and was not reliable enough to publish.

## The table of precedence

Twenty-eight lines, reproduced entire in the publication and read line by line in the Acta text.
Line 8 carries its own exception (All Souls yields to an occurring Sunday). Lines 14/15 place II
class feasts of the Lord above II class Sundays. Lines 22 and 25 separate Lenten from Advent III
class ferias. Lines 23/24 place particular III class feasts above universal ones. The movable-first
tie-break appears at lines 13, 14, 20, 23, and 24 and nowhere else. Precedence is governed by this
table alone (7, 91).

## Unresolved issues, collected

1. The year of twenty-three Sundays after Pentecost — unlegislated; two readings published.
2. Which II class feasts are *festa Domini* — no controlling list established.
3. The instrument and date of the September Ember change — not identified.
4. Whether the pre-1960 Breviary rubrics carried an exclusivity clause — the Missal side of the test
   was run and found none; the Breviary side is untested.
5. The Acta's tables of occurrence and concurrence — described, not collated; out of scope.
