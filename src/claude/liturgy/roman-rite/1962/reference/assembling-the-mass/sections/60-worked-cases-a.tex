\section{Worked cases, I}

Each case runs the five decisions in order and shows the working. Civil dates
were computed from the Gregorian Easter and checked against the code's own
date rules before use; each case states the dates it depends on so that the
arithmetic can be re-run. None of these results is an Ordo. Where a particular
calendar would change the answer, the case says so.

One rule is needed before the first case and is not printed in the Missal at
all. \rgm{431}\,a makes the Gloria depend on whether the Te Deum was said at
Matins --- and the rule for the Te Deum is nn.~237--238 of the code's second
part, \work{Rubricae generales Breviarii Romani}, cited here as \rgb{n}, which
the motu proprio directed to be printed in the Breviary and not in the Missal.
It runs: Te Deum is said on Low Sunday,
Pentecost and Easter Matins; on second-class Sundays except Septuagesima,
Sexagesima and Quinquagesima; \emph{on all feasts}; through the octaves of
Christmas, Easter and Pentecost; in the ferial Office of Christmastide and
Paschaltide; on the vigils of the Ascension and Pentecost; and in the Office
of Our Lady on Saturday. It is omitted in Offices \latin{de Tempore} from
Advent Sunday to the vigil of Christmas and from Septuagesima to Holy
Saturday; on second- and third-class vigils except the Ascension; on all
ferias \latin{per annum}; and in the Office of the dead. A reader who owns
only the 1962 Missal cannot apply \rgm{431}\,a from his own book.

\subsection{Case 1: a third-class saint against a feria --- twice, with
opposite results}

\begin{casebox}{Case 1a. Wednesday 17 March 2027 --- St Patrick on a
Passiontide feria}

\textbf{Calendar facts.} Easter 2027 falls on 28 March; Ash Wednesday on 10
February; Passion Sunday (the First Sunday of the Passion) on 14 March; Palm
Sunday on 21 March. So 17 March 2027 is the Wednesday after Passion Sunday. It
is not an Ember day: the Lenten Ember days of 2027 are 17, 19 and 20 February.

\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Day.} Two competitors. The feria: a feria of Passiontide between
the Thursday after Ash Wednesday and the Saturday before Palm Sunday, and so a
third-class feria under \rg{25}\,a. The feast: St Patrick, Bishop and
Confessor, third class in the calendar of the universal Church.

\item \textbf{Precedence.} Row 22 (ferias of Lent and Passiontide, Ember days
excepted) against row 24 (third-class feasts of the universal calendar). Row 22
is higher. \rg{25}\,a says the same in words: these ferias ``praeferuntur
festis III classis.'' \textbf{The feria takes the day.}

\item \textbf{Impediment.} St Patrick's feast is third class, so it has no
right of transfer (\rg{95}); it is commemorated or dropped. \rg{25} requires
the feria to be commemorated when \emph{it} is impeded; here it is not. The
day is third class, so two commemorations are admitted (\rg{111}\,d), and St
Patrick's ordinary commemoration fits --- \emph{at a Low Mass or at the
conventual Mass}. At a sung Mass that is not conventual, \rg{108} and
\rg{111}\,a between them exclude every ordinary commemoration, and St Patrick
is not commemorated at all.

\item \textbf{Mass category.} The Mass of the Office. A third-class votive
would be admissible on a third-class day (\rgm{384}), but none is requested. A
third-class Requiem is barred only on first- and second-class days
(\rgm{416}), so an anniversary Mass would in fact be admissible here; a
fourth-class daily Requiem would not, since \rgm{423} confines those to
fourth-class ferias.

\item \textbf{Assembly.}
\afield{Formulary}{The proper Mass of the Wednesday after Passion Sunday
(Missal, printed p.~122).}
\afield{Colour}{Violet (\rg{128}\,b).}
\afield{Prayers at the foot}{Confiteor as usual; \emph{Psalm \latin{Iudica me}
omitted}, because this is a Mass \latin{de Tempore} between Passion Sunday and
Maundy Thursday (\rgm{425}\,a).}
\afield{Introit}{Its antiphon and psalm verse, but \emph{no \latin{Gloria
Patri}} (\rgm{428}), and the antiphon then repeated.}
\afield{Gloria}{Omitted --- the Office is \latin{de Tempore} in the stretch of
\rgb{238}\,a, so no Te Deum, so \rgm{431}\,a does not fire; and \rgm{432}\,b
omits it independently because violet is worn.}
\afield{Orations}{Two. First the collect of the feria under its own
conclusion; then, under a second conclusion, the collect of St Patrick
(\latin{Deus, qui ad praedicandam gentibus gloriam tuam beatum Patricium
\dots{}}) taken from his own entry in the Proper of Saints. Secrets and
postcommunions in the same number, order and conclusions (\rgm{480},
\rgm{505}).}
\afield{Chants and readings}{The lesson (Leviticus 19), Gradual and Tract of
the feria; the Gospel is John 10, 22--38, \latin{Facta sunt Encaenia in
Ierosolymis}.}
\afield{Creed}{Omitted: none of the five cases of \rgm{475} applies.}
\afield{Preface}{Of the Holy Cross --- and as the \emph{proper} Preface, not
merely the seasonal one, since this is a Mass \latin{de Tempore} between
Passion Sunday and Maundy Thursday (\rgm{487}\,a). The Missal prints the
direction in the formulary.}
\afield{After the postcommunion}{The prayer over the people, preceded by
\latin{Oremus. Humiliate capita vestra Deo}, under its own conclusion
(\rgm{506}). The Missal prints it.}
\afield{End}{\latin{Ite, missa est}; blessing; the Last Gospel of St John.}
\end{steps}

\textbf{Conditional variant.} If the calendar actually binding the celebrant is
one in which St Patrick has been duly constituted principal Patron of the
nation, his feast is first class there by \rg{42}\,a and sits at row 12. It
then defeats the third-class feria, and everything changes:

\begin{substeps}
\item Mass of St Patrick, taken from the Common of a Confessor Bishop, first
formulary (\latin{Statuit}), with his own collect, secret and postcommunion,
exactly as his Missal entry directs.
\item Colour white (\rg{120}\,e).
\item \emph{Psalm \latin{Iudica me} is said} and the \latin{Gloria Patri} at
the Introit is said: \rgm{425}\,a and \rgm{428} suppress them only in Masses
\latin{de Tempore}, and this Mass is not one.
\item Gloria said (Te Deum on all feasts, \rgb{237}\,c). Creed said (first-class
feast, \rgm{475}\,b).
\item One commemoration only, and it must be privileged (\rg{111}\,a): the
Passiontide feria, which is privileged under \rg{109}\,e and which \rg{25}
requires to be commemorated. It goes first (\rg{113}).
\item \emph{No} prayer over the people: \rgm{506} attaches it to Masses
\emph{of} the ferias of Lent and Passiontide, and this Mass is of a feast.
\item Preface of the Holy Cross even so --- the Common supplies no proper
Preface, and \rgm{487}\,b makes the Holy Cross the seasonal Preface for
\emph{all} Masses from Passion Sunday to Wednesday of Holy Week that lack one.
\end{substeps}

\noindent
That variant is a derivation from the universal norms on a stated hypothesis.
It is not a statement about any actual national calendar; the approved proper
must be produced before it becomes an answer.
\end{casebox}

\begin{casebox}{Case 1b. Friday 3 December 2027 --- the same contest, reversed}

\textbf{Calendar facts.} Advent Sunday 2027 falls on 28 November, so 3
December is the Friday of the first week of Advent, before 17 December, and not
an Ember day (the Advent Ember days of 2027 are 15, 17 and 18 December).

\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Day.} The Advent feria (third class, \rg{25}\,b) and St Francis
Xavier, Confessor, third class in the universal calendar.

\item \textbf{Precedence.} Row 24 (third-class universal feasts) against row 25
(Advent ferias to 16 December). \textbf{The feast takes the day} --- and
\rg{25}\,b says so in words: these ferias ``cedunt festis III classis.''
Identical class numbers, identical kinds of competitor, opposite result from
Case 1a, because the two clauses of \rg{25} are opposite.

\item \textbf{Impediment.} The Advent feria is impeded, and \rg{25} closes:
``Hae feriae, si impediuntur, commemorari debent.'' Its commemoration is
privileged (\rg{109}\,e).

\item \textbf{Mass category.} The Mass of the Office. See Case 4 for what a
First Friday does to this day.

\item \textbf{Assembly.}
\afield{Formulary}{The proper Mass of St Francis Xavier in the Proper of
Saints (Introit \latin{Loquebar de testimoniis tuis}).}
\afield{Colour}{White, a Confessor not a Martyr (\rg{120}\,e).}
\afield{Prayers at the foot}{Complete, psalm included: this is not a Mass
\latin{de Tempore} in the Passiontide stretch.}
\afield{Gloria}{Said. The Office is of a feast, so Te Deum is said
(\rgb{237}\,c) notwithstanding \rgb{238}\,a, which suppresses it only in Offices
\latin{de Tempore}; and white, not violet, is worn.}
\afield{Orations}{Two. Collect of St Francis Xavier; then the collect of the
Advent feria --- that is, of the preceding Sunday, since \rgm{299} gives the
non-privileged Advent ferias the Mass of the preceding Sunday --- under a
second conclusion. Because the feria's commemoration is \emph{of the season},
\rg{113} puts it first: the order at the altar is feria, then saint. At a sung
non-conventual Mass the same two survive, because both here are privileged or
the Mass's own; at a Low Mass both are said in any event.}
\afield{Creed}{Omitted (third-class feast; \rgm{476}\,b).}
\afield{Preface}{\textbf{Common.} No Preface is assigned \latin{de Tempore}
for the ferial days of Advent --- \rgm{494}\,b gives Trinity to the
\emph{Sundays} of Advent only --- and the Mass of a Confessor from the Proper
of Saints has no proper Preface. So step three of \rgm{482} is reached and the
Common Preface is said.}
\afield{End}{\latin{Ite, missa est}; blessing; Last Gospel of St John.}
\end{steps}
\end{casebox}

\subsection{Case 2: the Annunciation when Easter falls on 25 March}

\begin{casebox}{Case 2. Sunday 25 March 2035 and Monday 2 April 2035}

\textbf{Calendar facts.} Easter 2035 falls on 25 March, the earliest date the
Gregorian computus allows. Palm Sunday is 18 March, Low Sunday 1 April, and
the Monday after Low Sunday is 2 April.

\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Day, 25 March.} Easter Sunday --- row 1, a first-class Sunday and
a first-class feast with an octave (\rg{11}) --- and the Annunciation of the
Blessed Virgin Mary, a first-class feast of the universal Church at row 11.

\item \textbf{Precedence.} Row 1 against row 11. Easter takes the day, and by a
wide margin.

\item \textbf{Impediment.} This is \emph{accidental} occurrence: a movable day
meeting a fixed one only in some years (\rg{92}). The Annunciation is
first class, so \rg{95} gives it the right of transfer. \rg{96}'s general rule
would send it to the next day that is not first or second class --- which,
with the whole octave of Easter at row 10 and Low Sunday at row 6, would be
Monday 2 April anyway. But \rg{96}\,a does not leave it to arithmetic: ``festum
Annuntiationis B. Mariae Virg., quando est transferendum post Pascha,
transfertur, tamquam in sedem propriam, in feriam II post dominicam in
albis.'' \textbf{2 April 2035.} By \rg{99} it arrives with its own first-class
rank intact.

\item \textbf{What 2 April 2035 then is.} In the universal calendar the date
carries St Francis of Paola, Confessor, third class. He is displaced. Is he
commemorated? \rg{111}\,a admits on a first-class day one commemoration and it
must be privileged; his is ordinary. \textbf{He is omitted}, and because
\rg{96}\,a made 2 April the Annunciation's \emph{proper seat}, the omission is
not an accident of the year's arithmetic but the ordinary consequence of the
feast being at home there.

\item \textbf{Mass category.} The Mass of the Office. Votive Masses of Our
Lady are barred that day by \rgm{317}, since a first-class day occurs whose
Office is of the same Person.

\item \textbf{Assembly.}
\afield{Formulary}{The proper Mass of the Annunciation, 25 March, said on 2
April.}
\afield{Colour}{White (\rg{120}\,b).}
\afield{Introit}{\latin{Vultum tuum deprecabuntur} --- and the antiphon's
printed ``(T.\,P. Alleluia, alleluia.)'' is used, since 2 April 2035 is in
Paschaltide (\rgm{429}). The same applies at the Offertory (\rgm{478}) and the
Communion antiphon, which prints ``(T.\,P. Alleluia.)'' (\rgm{504}).}
\afield{Gloria}{Said (first-class feast; Te Deum by \rgb{237}\,c).}
\afield{Orations}{One. The collect \latin{Deus, qui de beatae Mariae Virginis
utero} alone; no commemoration is admitted. The Missal's own direction ``Et,
tempore quadragesimali, fit commemoratio feriae'' does not apply: the feast has
been transferred out of Lent.}
\afield{Between the readings}{Not the printed Gradual and Tract. The Missal
directs: ``Tempore paschali, omissis graduali et tractu, dicitur: Alleluia,
alleluia \dots{}'' --- the two Alleluia verses \latin{Ave, Maria} and
\latin{Virga Iesse floruit}.}
\afield{Creed}{Said (\rgm{475}\,b). And \rgm{518}\,b names this Mass expressly:
at the words \latin{Et incarnatus est}, the celebrant always genuflects when
he recites them, and \emph{all} genuflect while they are sung --- a privilege
the rubric grants only to the three Masses of Christmas and to the Mass of the
Annunciation.}
\afield{Preface}{\textbf{Of the Blessed Virgin Mary}, with the proper
embolism \latin{Et te in Annuntiatione}, which the Missal prints in the
formulary. Note what this defeats: the Paschal Preface is assigned
\latin{tamquam de Tempore} in Paschaltide only ``in ceteris Missis quae
\dots{} praefatione propria carent'' (\rgm{489}\,b), and this Mass does not
lack one. Step one of \rgm{482} is reached and step two is never consulted.}
\afield{End}{\latin{Ite, missa est}; blessing; Last Gospel of St John. No
double Alleluia at the dismissal: \rgm{507}\,b confines that to Masses
\latin{de Tempore} within the octave of Easter, and 2 April is outside the
octave in any case.}
\end{steps}

\textbf{Residual uncertainty.} \rgm{439} forbids altering the words
\latin{hanc}, \latin{hodiernam} or \latin{praesentem diem} in the orations of a
transferred Office. The Annunciation's own orations do not contain them, so
nothing turns on it here; but the rule is the reason a transferred feast may
be heard saying ``this day'' of a day that is not its date.
\end{casebox}
