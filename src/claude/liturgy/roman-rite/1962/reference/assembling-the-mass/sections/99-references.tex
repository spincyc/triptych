\section{References}

\subsection*{Controlling liturgical books and norms}

\begin{itemize}
\item \work{Missale Romanum ex decreto Sacrosancti Concilii Tridentini
restitutum, Summorum Pontificum cura recognitum}, editio typica. Vatican City:
Typis Polyglottis Vaticanis, 1962. Front matter: \work{Rubricae generales}
(nn.~1--137, printed pp.~XII--XX); \work{Rubricae generales Missalis Romani}
(nn.~269--530, pp.~XXI--XXXVI); \work{Ritus servandus in celebratione Missae};
\work{De defectibus}. Body: \work{Ordo Missae}; \work{Proprium de Tempore};
\work{Proprium Sanctorum}; \work{Commune Sanctorum}; \work{Missae votivae};
\work{Missae defunctorum}; \work{Praefationes}. Read in the Church Music
Association of America facsimile of that edition. \emph{Controlling witness.}

\item John XXIII, \work{Litterae apostolicae motu proprio datae}
``\work{Rubricarum instructum}'', 25 July 1960. \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis}
52 (1960) 593--595. \emph{Approving act; establishes the three-part division
and the 1 January 1961 effective date.}

\item Sacred Congregation of Rites, \work{Decretum generale quo novus
rubricarum Breviarii ac Missalis Romani codex promulgatur}, 26 July 1960.
\work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 52 (1960) 596. \emph{Promulgating decree.}

\item \work{Rubricae Breviarii et Missalis Romani}. \work{Acta Apostolicae
Sedis} 52 (1960) 597--705. \emph{The promulgated text of the code, in three
parts, with the enumerations of days and the tables of occurrence and
concurrence that the Missal does not reprint. Used here as textual control and
as the source of \rgb{237}--\rgb{238}.}

\item \work{Variationes in Breviario et Missali Romano ad normam novi codicis
rubricarum}. \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 52 (1960) 706--740. \emph{Used only
for the abolition of the seasonal orations (cap.~IV, n.~18) and of the Suffrage
and commemoration of the Cross (cap.~II, n.~13).}

\item Sacred Congregation of the Council, \work{Decretum: Novus index
statuitur dierum festorum, quibus inest obligatio litandi Sacrum pro populo},
3 December 1960. \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 52 (1960) 985--986.
\emph{Cited once, for the contemporaneous official list of \latin{festa de
praecepto} for the \latin{pro populo} obligation under cann.~339 \S1 and 466
\S1 of the 1917 Code. Not a rubrical definition.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Identified English witnesses}

\begin{itemize}
\item \work{The Roman Missal translated into the English language for the use
of the laity}, first revised edition. Philadelphia: Eugene Cummiskey, 1861.
Published with the approbation of the Bishop of Philadelphia; the translator is
not named. \emph{Quoted once, for the collect of the Fifth Sunday after
Epiphany. A pre-1955 witness: its rubrics, seasonal second and third orations
and Holy Week are not those of the 1962 book, and none of them is used here.}

\item \work{The Holy Bible}, Douay-Rheims, in Bishop Richard Challoner's
revision. \emph{Quoted once, for John 1, 1--3 and 14. An identified historical
English translation of the Vulgate, offered as a witness to the sense of the
Latin the celebrant reads, not as a liturgical translation and not for
recitation.}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Non-controlling aids}

\begin{itemize}
\item \work{Missale Romanum \dots{} editio iuxta typicam}. New York: Benziger
Brothers, 1962. Consulted only as an uncorrected optical transcription for
locating passages in the front matter. It controls no wording here and is a
distinct edition from the Vatican typical edition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Note on what is not cited}

No manual, commentary, Ordo or rubrical handbook is cited, and none was used
to reach a result in this book. That is a deliberate limitation and a real
one: a competent commentator would resolve some of the questions left open in
Section~10 from a tradition of authorised responses that this study has not
examined, and the reader who needs a settled answer should seek it there and
from the Ordo binding his own church.
