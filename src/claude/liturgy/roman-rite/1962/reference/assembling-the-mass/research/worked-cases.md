# Worked Cases — ledger

Six cases, chosen to exercise the branches most likely to be got wrong rather than the ones
most likely to occur. Each entry records the calendar facts, the competitors and their rows,
the controlling rubric, the impeded-item result, the Mass-category test, the assembly, the
independent check, and the residual uncertainty.

Every civil date below was computed from the Gregorian Easter and then cross-checked against
the code's own date rules (RG 17 d for Christ the King; RG 18 for the resumed Sundays; RG 20
for Advent Sunday). **No date or result was taken from, or checked against, a published
Ordo.**

Independent check on the Sundays-after-Pentecost counts: the number equals (Advent I − Dom I
post Pentecosten) in days divided by seven, because the last Sunday after Pentecost is the
Sunday one week before Advent I. 2026: (29 Nov − 31 May) = 182; 182/7 = 26. 2027: (28 Nov −
23 May) = 189; 189/7 = 27. 2035: (2 Dec − 20 May) = 196; 196/7 = 28. 2036: (30 Nov − 8 Jun) =
175; 175/7 = 25. Advent I was fixed independently by RG 20 (the Sunday falling on or nearest
30 November): 30 November is a Monday in 2026, a Tuesday in 2027, a Friday in 2035 and a
Sunday in 2036, giving 29 November, 28 November, 2 December and 30 November respectively.
The counts then select RG 18's branches: 2026 branch (b), two resumed; 2035 branch (d), four
resumed; 2036 branch (a), one resumed.

## Case 1a — Wednesday 17 March 2027

- **Why chosen.** The single most-misremembered rule in the code: a third-class universal
  feast loses to a third-class Lenten or Passiontide feria.
- **Competitors and rows.** Feria of Passiontide, third class (RG 25 a) — row 22. St Patrick,
  Bishop and Confessor, third class in the universal calendar — row 24.
- **Check that it is a III-class feria and not an Ember day.** Lenten Ember days 2027 are the
  Wednesday, Friday and Saturday after Dom I Quadragesimae (14 Feb): 17, 19, 20 February. RG
  25 a covers Thursday after Ash Wednesday (11 Feb) to the Saturday before Palm Sunday (20
  March) inclusive; 17 March is inside it.
- **Controlling row.** 22 over 24. RG 25 a states the same result in words.
- **Impeded item.** St Patrick, third class, has no right of transfer (RG 95). Commemorated at
  Low Mass and at the conventual Mass; **not commemorated at a sung non-conventual Mass**
  (RG 108, RG 111 a).
- **Mass category.** Mass of the Office. Noted in the case: a III-class Requiem would be
  admissible (RGMR 416 bars it only on I- and II-class days), a IV-class daily Requiem would
  not (RGMR 423).
- **Assembly.** Missal, Feria IV post dominicam I Passionis, printed p. 122 (artifact pp.
  203–204): lesson Lev. 19, 1–2, 11–19 and 25; Gradual Ps. 42; Tract *Domine, non secundum*;
  Gospel **Io. 10, 22–38** (*Facta sunt Encaenia*). The neighbouring Feria III, printed
  pp. 121–122, has Io. 7, 1–13; the two are easily transposed. Violet. Psalm
  *Iudica me* omitted (RGMR 425 a). No *Gloria Patri* at the Introit (RGMR 428). No Gloria (Te
  Deum omitted, RGBR 238 a; and violet, RGMR 432 b). Two orations. No Creed. Preface of the Holy
  Cross as the **proper** Preface (RGMR 487 a; the Missal prints the direction at the
  formulary). Prayer over the people (RGMR 506; the Missal prints it). Last Gospel of St John.
- **Independent check.** The Missal's own printed page for that feria carries
  `Praefatio de sancta Cruce` and `Super populum: Oremus. Humiliate …`, which confirms both
  slot results without relying on the general rubrics alone.
- **Conditional variant.** If a national calendar makes St Patrick principal Patron, RG 42 a
  puts him at row 12 and he wins. The variant's assembly is derived from the universal norms
  on that hypothesis and is expressly not a claim about Irish law.
- **Residual uncertainty.** None on the universal calendar. The variant is conditional.

## Case 1b — Friday 3 December 2027

- **Why chosen.** The mirror image of 1a with identical class numbers, to show that the class
  numbers are not what decide.
- **Competitors and rows.** Advent feria before 17 December, third class (RG 25 b) — row 25.
  St Francis Xavier, Confessor, third class in the universal calendar — row 24.
- **Check.** Advent Sunday 2027 = 28 November (RG 20: the Sunday falling on or nearest 30
  November; 28 Nov is two days before, 5 Dec five days after). Advent Ember days 2027 fall in
  the third week: 15, 17, 18 December. 3 December is neither.
- **Controlling row.** 24 over 25. RG 25 b states the same result.
- **Impeded item.** The Advent feria must be commemorated (RG 25); its commemoration is
  privileged (RG 109 e).
- **Assembly.** Proper Mass of St Francis Xavier, Introit *Loquebar de testimoniis tuis*.
  White (RG 120 e). Gloria said (Te Deum on all feasts, RGBR 237 c). Two orations, the feria
  first (RG 113). No Creed (RGMR 476 b). **Common Preface** — no Preface is assigned *de
  Tempore* for the ferial days of Advent, and RGMR 494 b gives Trinity to the Sundays of Advent
  only.
- **Residual uncertainty.** None.

## Case 2 — Sunday 25 March 2035 and Monday 2 April 2035

- **Why chosen.** The extreme accidental occurrence: a first-class feast of the universal
  calendar against the highest row of the table, resolved by a named proper seat rather than by
  arithmetic, with a Paschaltide assembly at the far end.
- **Check.** 25 March 2035 is the earliest date the Gregorian computus produces for Easter.
  Palm Sunday 18 March; Low Sunday 1 April; *feria II post dominicam in albis* = 2 April.
- **Competitors and rows.** Easter Sunday — row 1. The Annunciation — row 11.
- **Controlling rule.** RG 95 (right of transfer, I-class feasts only), then RG 96 a: the
  Annunciation, when it must be transferred after Easter, goes *tamquam in sedem propriam* to
  the Monday after Low Sunday.
- **What 2 April carries.** S. Francisci de Paula Conf., third class (Missal, artifact p. 583).
  Displaced, and **not** commemorated: RG 111 a admits one privileged commemoration only on a
  first-class day, and his is ordinary.
- **Assembly.** Annunciation formulary (Missal, artifact pp. 576–577). White. Introit,
  Offertory and Communion take their printed `(T. P. Alleluia)` (RGMR 429, 478, 504). Gloria
  and Creed. One oration only; the Missal's own `Et, tempore quadragesimali, fit commemoratio
  feriae` does not apply. **Gradual and Tract replaced**: the Missal directs `Tempore paschali,
  omissis graduali et tractu, dicitur: Alleluia, alleluia …` with the verses *Ave, Maria* and
  *Virga Iesse floruit*. Preface **of the Blessed Virgin** with the proper embolism *Et te in
  Annuntiatione*, which the Missal prints — defeating the Paschal Preface, which is *de
  Tempore* only for Masses lacking a proper one (RGMR 489 b). All genuflect at *Et incarnatus
  est* (RGMR 518 b names this Mass).
- **Independent check.** The Missal prints both the Paschaltide chant substitution and the
  Preface direction in the formulary itself; neither result depends on the general rubrics
  alone.
- **Residual uncertainty.** RGMR 439 (words *hanc*, *hodiernam* not altered in a transferred
  Office) is cited but does not bite: the Annunciation's orations contain none of them.

## Case 3 — Sunday 2 November and Monday 3 November 2036

- **Why chosen.** The one row of the table that carries its own defeat, and the fullest
  Requiem assembly in the book.
- **Check.** Dom I p. Pent 2036 = 8 June; (2 Nov − 8 Jun) = 147 days = 21 weeks, so 2 November
  is the **22nd** Sunday after Pentecost. Christ the King, the last Sunday of October (RG
  17 d), is 26 October 2036 — the 21st slot. All Saints, 1 November, is the Saturday.
- **Competitors and rows.** The 22nd Sunday after Pentecost, second class — row 15. All Souls
  — row 8, with the clause `quae tamen locum cedit dominicae occurrenti`.
- **Controlling rule.** Row 8's own clause, and RG 16 b. The Sunday takes the day.
- **Impeded item.** All Souls is not a feast, so RG 95 does not reach it; RG 96 b transfers it
  *tamquam in sedem propriam* to Monday 3 November. The Missal prints the same result in its
  heading: `Die 2 novembris vel, si in dominicam inciderit, die 3 sequenti`. No commemoration
  on the Sunday (RG 111 b).
- **Sunday assembly.** 22nd Sunday after Pentecost; green; Gloria (RGBR 237 b); one oration;
  Creed (RGMR 475 a); Preface of the Trinity (RGMR 494 b).
- **Monday assembly.** I-class Requiem (RGMR 402 a). Three Masses (Missal rubric at the day;
  RGMR 403–404); the first is the conventual Mass (RGMR 295). Black, with the RG 130 d
  exception. Psalm *Iudica me* omitted (RGMR 425 b); no *Gloria Patri* (RGMR 428); no Gloria
  (RGMR 432 d, RGBR 238 d); one oration and it must be for the dead (RGMR 391, 398 c); *Dies
  irae* obligatory (RGMR 399 a) with the Missal's own relief for three Masses said without
  interruption; no Creed (RGMR 476 f); Preface of the dead (RGMR 499); no pax (*Ordo Missae*);
  *Requiescant in pace* and no blessing (RGMR 507 c, 508); Last Gospel omitted if the
  absolution follows (RGMR 510 e, with RGMR 401 a).
- **Also barred.** The *Pro Sponsis* Mass, its commemoration and the nuptial blessing within
  Mass, all three (RGMR 381 d).
- **Residual uncertainty.** RGMR 422's octave. The rubric measures it `a die Commemorationis
  omnium Fidelium defunctorum`, which in 2036 is 3 November, so the octave appears to run 3–10
  November. Recorded in the publication as a **necessary inference**, not a direct rule.

## Case 4 — Friday 3 December 2027, the First Friday votive

- **Why chosen.** To show that the fourth decision has its own conditions. The day's class
  only tells you the votive is *not excluded*; what admits it is a fact about the church.
- **Day.** As Case 1b: third class.
- **Category test.** RGMR 385 b: two Masses of the Sacred Heart, on the first Friday, **in
  churches and oratories in which the special exercises in honour of the Sacred Heart are
  performed that day**. Then RGMR 384: a III-class votive requires a III- or IV-class day.
  Both satisfied. RGMR 317 and RGMR 326 a checked and clear.
- **Assembly.** Formulary from the feast (RGMR 314): *Cogitationes Cordis eius*. White
  (RG 121 a). **Gloria said** (RGMR 431 d — white, not violet). **No Creed** (RGMR 386 a,
  *semper*). Three orations, the maximum RGMR 435 allows: Sacred Heart; Advent feria
  (privileged, RG 109 e, obligatory RG 25, first by RG 113); St Francis Xavier. Preface of the
  Sacred Heart (RGMR 491). Solemn tone if sung — **but** RGMR 434 a then reduces the
  commemorations to one privileged, so the sung Mass has two orations where the Low Mass has
  three.
- **Independent check.** The same day yields three different oration counts depending on
  category and manner of celebration (2 in Case 1b, 3 here at Low Mass, 2 here sung). That
  spread is itself the check that the rules were applied separately rather than merged.
- **Residual uncertainty.** Whether a Mass at which the choir sings and the celebrant reads is
  a *Missa in cantu* for RGMR 434 a. Recorded as contested in §10.2.

## Case 5 — the autumn of 2026, with the 2035 variant

- **Why chosen.** The signature 1962 assembly rule: a Sunday Mass built out of two different
  Sundays' formularies; plus a Sunday that vanishes; plus a collision the rubrics do not
  resolve.
- **Check.** Epiphany 2026 is Tuesday 6 January; Septuagesima is 1 February; so the First,
  Second and Third Sundays after Epiphany are celebrated (11, 18, 25 January) and the Fourth,
  Fifth and Sixth are impeded. There are 26 Sundays after Pentecost, so RG 18 b resumes the
  Fifth and Sixth only.
- **Result.** 23rd Sunday after Pentecost = 1 November; slot XXIV (8 November) = *Dominica
  quinta quae superfuit post Epiphaniam*; slot XXV (15 November) = *sexta*; slot XXVI (22
  November) = *Dominica XXIV et ultima post Pentecosten*, always last by RG 18's closing
  paragraph. Christ the King 2026 is 25 October — the 22nd slot — so it does not collide.
- **The Sunday that vanishes.** The **Fourth** after Epiphany. The count of Sundays after
  Pentecost, not the count of Sundays actually impeded, decides how many are resumed.
- **Assembly.** RGMR 298: Introit, Offertory and Communion antiphons and the Gradual and
  Alleluia come from the 23rd Sunday after Pentecost; the orations, Epistle and Gospel are the
  Epiphany Sunday's own. The Missal prints the composite formulary in full (artifact pp.
  499–500), which is the independent check.
- **The 2035 variant.** 28 Sundays after Pentecost, so RG 18 d fixes four nominal assignments
  at slots XXIV–XXVII. Slot XXIV is 28 October 2035, the last Sunday of October, hence Christ
  the King (RG 17 d). Occurrence displaces the Third after Epiphany in that slot; the Fourth,
  Fifth, and Sixth remain at XXV–XXVII. The sequence does not compress. This is a normative
  rule application, not dated-Ordo verification.
- **English witness.** The collect of the Fifth Sunday after Epiphany is quoted from the 1861
  Cummiskey hand missal, row `epiph-05 / collect`, as a witness only.

## Case 6 — a funeral requested for Monday 3 November 2036

- **Why chosen.** To test the funeral-Mass prohibition list from the direction opposite to
  §10.1, and to show that RGMR 406 is a list of named rows and named feasts rather than a class
  threshold.
- **Result.** All Souls is row 8; it is not in RGMR 406's list, and RGMR 393 expressly excepts
  the Masses of All Souls from the exposition bar. The funeral Mass is **admitted**, and
  RGMR 409 positively provides for it: the first of the day's three Masses with the funeral
  orations, or the second or third if the first is being said for the Office of the day.
- **Orations.** RGMR 395 for a person who is not a priest; RGMR 394 a routes a deceased pope,
  cardinal, bishop or priest to the first Mass of All Souls with the proper orations. One
  oration only: RGMR 458 confines the imposed prayer for the dead to IV-class ferias and
  IV-class read votive and Requiem Masses.
- **Assembly.** As Case 3, with *Dies irae* obligatory (I class, RGMR 399 a) and the Last
  Gospel omitted because the absolution *fieri debet* after a funeral Mass (RGMR 401 a,
  510 e).
- **If barred or impossible.** RGMR 408 permits transfer to the nearest similarly unimpeded
  day.
- **Residual uncertainty.** None on this day. The neighbouring question — a funeral on an
  ordinary Sunday — is contested and is argued in §10.1.
