# Origen — chronology record

Provider: Anthropic Claude. All web checks 2026-07-24. Evidence classes as in
the publication appendix (A/N/E/L/M/K/S).

Dates are claims. This record states, for each event, the source basis
actually checked, competing views, and the confidence used in the
publication. Regnal synchronisms are Eusebius's; conversions to years CE use
standard regnal dates (Severus from 193, Alexander Severus 222–235, Philip
244–249, Decius 249–251, Gallus 251–253) and are marked as such.

## Anchors (checked at the source)

1. **202/203 — persecution at Alexandria; Leonides beheaded.** Basis:
   Eusebius places the arrest in "the tenth year of the reign of Severus,"
   with Laetus governor and Demetrius newly bishop (HE 6.2.2, checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/250106.htm) (E); Leonides "was beheaded while his
   son was still young" (HE 6.1). Severus's dies imperii 193 ⇒ tenth year
   202/203. Status: secure.

2. **Birth c. 184–186 (inference only).** Origen "was not quite seventeen
   years old" at his father's death (HE 6.2.12) ⇒ born c. 185/186. HE 7.1
   has him die "being sixty-nine years of age" under Gallus (251–253) ⇒ born
   c. 182–184 if death in 251–253. The two Eusebian data do not perfectly
   cohere; the publication uses c. 184–186 and says so. No ancient source
   states a birth year. Status: range only.

3. **c. 203 — takes charge of catechetical instruction.** "He was in his
   eighteenth year when he took charge of the catechetical school," during
   the persecution under Aquila (HE 6.3.3); Demetrius later entrusted the
   school "to him alone" (HE 6.3.8). Status: secure as sequence; age is
   Eusebius's.

4. **198–217 — Rome visit under Zephyrinus.** "Adamantius — for this also
   was a name of Origen — when Zephyrinus was bishop of Rome, visited Rome"
   (HE 6.14.10). Status: secure event; datable only to Zephyrinus's
   episcopate.

5. **c. 215/216 — flight to Caesarea; lay preaching; recall.** "A
   considerable war broke out in the city" and Origen went to Palestine,
   where the bishops had him preach though unordained; letter of Alexander
   and Theoctistus quoted; Demetrius recalled him (HE 6.19.16–19). The link
   of the "war" to Caracalla's massacre of 215/216 is standard scholarship
   (K), not Eusebius. Status: plausible.

6. **222–235 — Julia Mamaea audience at Antioch.** HE 6.21.3–4; within
   Alexander Severus's reign; Eusebius gives no year. Status: secure event;
   range only.

7. **Before 231/232 — Alexandrian works.** "He wrote also the books De
   Principiis before leaving Alexandria," with the Stromateis "during the
   reign of Alexander" (HE 6.24.3, checked). Status: secure relative order.

8. **c. 231 — ordination at Caesarea.** Sent to Greece "on account of a
   pressing necessity," ordained "in Caesarea by the bishops of that
   country" (HE 6.23.4). Status: secure; year approximate (tied to the
   departure below).

9. **231/232 — removal to Caesarea; proceedings; Heraclas.** "In the tenth
   year of the above-mentioned reign" (Alexander Severus ⇒ 231/232) Origen
   "removed from Alexandria to Caesarea"; Demetrius died "not long
   afterward" after forty-three years in office; Heraclas succeeded (HE
   6.26). Photius (cod. 118, checked at tertullian.org) supplies the two
   stages: a synod decreeing banishment from Alexandria but retention of
   priesthood, then Demetrius "and some Egyptian bishops" depriving him of
   the priesthood. Jerome, Ep. 33.5 (checked): condemnation accepted at
   Rome, rejected by Palestine, Arabia, Phenicia, Achaia; "not because of
   the novelty or heterodoxy of his doctrines." Status: proceedings secure;
   details unresolved (no acts survive).

10. **235–238 — Maximin's persecution; Exhortation to Martyrdom.** HE 6.28
    (checked): edict against church leaders; Ambrose and Protoctetus
    confessors; the work dedicated to them. Status: secure.

11. **c. 233–238 — Gregory (Thaumaturgus) at Caesarea.** Panegyric (N,
    checked at newadvent.org/fathers/0604.htm) attests the school and the
    departure; HE 6.30 names Theodore/Gregory and Athenodorus. The
    five-year span and c. 233 arrival are later report and scholarly
    convention (Prat, checked) (K). Status: plausible.

12. **c. 238–244 — Beryllus conference; Arabian soul dispute.** HE 6.33 and
    6.37 (checked); Eusebius gives sequence, not years. Status: secure
    events; approximate dates.

13. **c. 246–249 — stenographers permitted; Against Celsus; letters to
    Philip and Severa.** Origen "over sixty years old" (HE 6.36.1) ⇒ after
    c. 245; Contra Celsum placed by Eusebius in Philip's reign (244–249),
    conventionally c. 248 (K). Status: plausible–secure.

14. **250–251 — Decian imprisonment and torture.** HE 6.39.5 (checked),
    explicitly sourced by Eusebius to Origen's letters: bonds, iron collar,
    dungeon, feet "stretched four spaces in the stocks," threats of fire,
    the judge striving that his life not be taken; letters "full of
    comfort" afterwards. Status: secure (as Eusebius's report of A-class
    letters he possessed).

15. **c. 253/254 — death aged sixty-nine; Tyre.** "When Decius had reigned
    not quite two years, he was slain... and Gallus succeeded him. At this
    time Origen died, being sixty-nine years of age" (HE 7.1, checked at
    newadvent.org/fathers/250107.htm). Jerome, De vir. ill. 54 (checked):
    "lived until the time of Gallus and Volusianus, that is, until his
    sixty-ninth year, and died at Tyre, in which city he also was buried."
    Photius, cod. 118 (checked): Pamphilus and others made him a martyr at
    Caesarea under Decius; "others say" Tyre under Gallus at sixty-nine —
    "this is the truer account, unless the letters supposed to have been
    written by him after the Decian persecution are spurious." Publication
    follows the Tyre account, preserves the variant, and asserts no exact
    year (commonly 253 or 254). The causal link of death to the tortures is
    plausible inference (so already Prat), not attested. Status: year
    approximate; place from Jerome; variant preserved.

## Posthumous (checked)

16. **c. 307–310 — Pamphilus and Eusebius, Defense of Origen** (five books
    by Pamphilus in prison "in the company of Eusebius," sixth by Eusebius
    after Pamphilus's martyrdom): Photius, cod. 118 (checked); Jerome, De
    vir. ill. 75 (checked) on Pamphilus's hand-copies at Caesarea.

17. **c. 374–377 — Epiphanius, Panarion 64** against Origen: dates standard
    (K); the work itself was not inspected in this research (ceiling
    recorded).

18. **393–404 — first Origenist controversy.** Rufinus's translation of De
    Principiis with its preface (398; preface checked in NPNF2-3 at
    newadvent.org/fathers/2712.htm); Jerome, Ep. 84 (checked; written
    399/400, standard dating K); Theophilus's synodal condemnation (400)
    and Anastasius's censure: standard dates (K), narrated at survey level.

19. **543 — Justinian's edict and anathemas.** Text of "The Anathemas of
    the Emperor Justinian Against Origen" checked at
    newadvent.org/fathers/3812.htm (M); adoption by the standing synod
    under Menas and the patriarchs' and Vigilius's assent per Prat 1911
    (checked) (K). Status: secure.

20. **553, 5 May–2 June — Constantinople II.** Sessions and dates per the
    conciliar extracts (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/3812.htm) and Prat.
    Anathema 11 (with Origen's name) checked in Percival's text and in
    Tanner's as presented at papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum05.htm;
    Tanner's introduction excludes the fifteen anathemas as not attributable
    to the council. The fifteen anathemas' text checked in Percival. The
    pre-conciliar-subscription reconstruction is Diekamp's (via Prat) and is
    the general modern view; Price's contrary emphasis reported at survey
    level (ceilings recorded in source-audit). Status: facts secure;
    conciliar status disputed — preserved, not resolved.

21. **681 — Constantinople III's Exposition of Faith** describes the Fifth
    Synod as against "Theodore of Mopsuestia, Origen, Didymus, and
    Evagrius" (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/3813.htm).

22. **1941 — Tura papyri; 2012 — Munich Cod. graec. 314** (29 Greek Psalm
    homilies identified by M. Molin Pradel, edition L. Perrone, GCS N.F. 19,
    2015): checked at the level of published identification and edition
    records (K). 

23. **2007, 25 Apr and 2 May — Benedict XVI's catecheses on Origen**:
    checked at vatican.va (M).

## Competing chronologies preserved

- Birth c. 185/186 (from age at Leonides's death) vs. c. 182–184 (from age
  sixty-nine at death under Gallus): the publication uses c. 184–186 and
  flags the tension rather than harmonizing.
- Death as Decian martyrdom at Caesarea (Pamphilus, via Photius) vs. death
  at Tyre c. 253/254 (Eusebius's letters, Jerome): both stated, with
  Photius's own test (authenticity of the post-Decian letters) quoted.
- Condemned by the Fifth Ecumenical Council (Byzantine reception,
  Constantinople III's description, Price's reading) vs. extra-conciliar
  condemnation mistaken for conciliar (Diekamp, Prat): both stated; the
  publication decides neither.
