## Reader and governing question

A serious general reader, student, or researcher who wants one coherent,
evidence-controlled life. Governing questions: (1) what can be established
about the life when nearly everything rests on Eusebius, an avowed defender
writing three generations later; (2) what Origen built (Hexapla, commentaries
and homilies, *On First Principles*, *Against Celsus*) and in what condition
it survives; (3) what exactly happened to his name after death — what the
543 anathemas and the Fifth Ecumenical Council actually did, whether Origen
himself was anathematized, and why the modern era largely reversed the older
verdict.

## Thesis and boundary

Origen's achievement and his condemnation must be held together, and the
question "was Origen anathematized by the Church?" admits only a divided
answer: certain (543 imperial-synodal anathema by name with papal assent;
name in anathema 11 of 553; fifteen anti-Origenist anathemas subscribed
around the council), disputed (whether any of this constitutes a personal
ecumenical-conciliar condemnation of the historical Origen and his actual
teaching). This is historical synthesis, not an ecclesiastical verdict: the
study does not adjudicate the self-mutilation report, the canonical validity
of the Alexandrian proceedings or the Caesarean ordination, the doctrinal
merits of the condemned propositions, the authority of the anathemas, or
Origen's sanctity or eternal state, and it claims no theological, canonical,
or rights review.

## Included

- Severan Alexandria, the catechetical milieu (Pantaenus/Clement succession
  treated cautiously), and the 202/203 persecution;
- Leonides's martyrdom, the letter ("Take heed not to change your mind on our
  account"), confiscation, the patroness and Paul the heretic, grammar
  teaching (HE 6.1–2);
- the boy catechist: age eighteen, Demetrius's confirmation, martyr pupils,
  sale of the library, four obols, ascetic regime, Heraclas (HE 6.3–5, 6.15);
- the self-mutilation report (HE 6.8) with its full historiographical
  problem: single-source status, Eusebius's polemical use, Epiphanius's
  inability to verify (via Edwards/SEP), the Commentary on Matthew 15
  counter-evidence (via scholarship), named modern skeptics and defenders —
  presented as contested, not resolved;
- Ammonius and Porphyry's notice; the Rome visit; Adamantius nickname;
- Ambrose's patronage and the production apparatus (HE 6.18, 6.23); the
  Hexapla and Tetrapla (HE 6.16; Jerome 54); genres of exegesis; the Letter
  to Gregory ("spoiling the Egyptians");
- *On First Principles*: preface's rule-of-faith-plus-open-questions method,
  1.6 restoration passage in its exploratory register, and the Rufinus
  transmission problem stated from Rufinus's own preface;
- the lay-preaching dispute (c. 215/216) with the Alexander/Theoctistus
  letter; Mamaea audience; ordination at Caesarea c. 231; the Alexandrian
  proceedings (HE 6.23, 6.26; Photius 118; Jerome Ep. 33) with their
  evidentiary ceiling; Caesarean school and Gregory's Panegyric; Beryllus and
  the Arabian disputes;
- Maximin persecution and *Exhortation to Martyrdom* (HE 6.28); stenographers
  and late homilies; *Against Celsus* (preface quoted) and letters to Philip
  (HE 6.36);
- Decian torture (HE 6.39.5), death at sixty-nine (HE 7.1), Tyre and burial
  (Jerome 54), the Photius death-dilemma (martyrdom variant vs. letters);
- posthumous: Pamphilus/Eusebius *Defense*; Methodius; fourth-century use;
  first Origenist controversy (Epiphanius, Rufinus's preface, Jerome Ep. 84,
  Theophilus, Anastasius, Chrysostom's fall); Justinian 543 (nine anathemas
  + personal anathema, quoted); 553 in detail (capitula 11, the fifteen
  anathemas, Tanner's editorial exclusion, Prat/Diekamp vs. Price,
  Constantinople III's reading); loss of the Greek corpus;
- reception: Vincent of Lérins 17; not a saint (bounded negative); Tyre tomb
  tradition; survival by translation; Tura 1941 and Munich Cod. graec. 314
  (2012); de Lubac, Daniélou, Balthasar, Crouzel, Sources chrétiennes; CCC
  2745 and 2847; Benedict XVI's audiences of 25 April and 2 May 2007
  (paraphrased).

## Excluded or bounded

- no catalogue of works, homilies, catena fragments, or Hexaplaric readings,
  and no manuscript-family or papyrological study;
- no independent reconstruction of Celsus, Demetrius's case, the Alexandrian
  synods' acts (lost), the sixth-century Palestinian parties, or Evagrian
  metaphysics beyond what the condemnation question requires;
- no full doctrinal exposition of Origen's Trinitarian theology, Christology,
  or eschatology beyond identified loci; no verdict on the orthodoxy of any
  proposition;
- no medical or psychological conclusion about the mutilation report, the
  torture, or the cause of death;
- no archaeological or relic research on the Tyre tomb beyond the recorded
  tradition; no item-level iconography or patronage census;
- no ecclesiastical, canonical, or independent rights judgment.

## Evidence classes

A (Origen's works and letters, marked by transmission layer), N (Gregory's
Panegyric; Porphyry via Eusebius), E (Eusebius as biographical spine;
Pamphilus via Photius; Jerome; Rufinus; Vincent), L (Photius's epitomes,

documents, CCC citations, papal audiences — each limited to the act's actual
object), K (named modern reconstructions: Diekamp, Prat, Crouzel, de Lubac,
McGuckin, Edwards, Price, Perrone), S (project synthesis). Defined in the
publication's terminal appendix and used consistently across the research
records.

## Material uncertainties

- birth year and place; the mother's name and status; the family's legal
  condition after confiscation;
- the institutional reality of the pre-Origen "catechetical school" and
  Clement's role in Origen's formation;
- the historicity, motive, and date of the self-castration report; identity
  of Origen's Ammonius with Ammonius Saccas;
- dates of the Rome visit, the first Arabian journey, the Mamaea audience,
  and the stages of Hexaplaric work;
- the charges, legal theory, votes, and geographic effect of the Alexandrian
  proceedings; whether "deposition" was received anywhere outside Egypt and
  Rome; the ordaining bishop's name in Photius's epitome (Theotecnus vs.
  Eusebius's Theoctistus);
- the relation of the Decian ordeal to the death two or three years later;
  Tyre as death-place (Jerome) vs. Pamphilus's martyrdom account (Photius's
  dilemma over the letters' authenticity);
- how much of *On First Principles* as transmitted is Origen's wording;
  whether anathema 11's name-list was in every 553 exemplar (interpolation
  thesis now generally abandoned but occasionally revived);
- whether the fifteen anathemas were subscribed by the same bishops as the
  council (generally accepted) and their canonical weight.
