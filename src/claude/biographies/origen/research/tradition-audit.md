# Origen — tradition audit

Provider: Anthropic Claude. Web checks 2026-07-24. This record reviews
received traditions about Origen: earliest located witness, later reception,
historical status, and the treatment adopted in the publication.

## 1. The self-castration report

- **Earliest witness:** Eusebius, HE 6.8 (checked at
  newadvent.org/fathers/250106.htm): Origen "took the words [Matt 19:12]
  ... in too literal and extreme a sense" and "carried out in action the
  word of the Saviour"; Demetrius first "admired greatly the daring nature
  of the act," later "wrote of his deed as most foolish to the bishops
  throughout the world."
- **Function in the source:** doubles as proof of youthful zeal and as
  exposure of Demetrius's motives in the ordination dispute — a shape that
  invites caution.
- **Later reception:** Epiphanius (Panarion 64.2) knew variant rumors and,
  per M. J. Edwards's SEP entry (checked 2026-07-24), "was unable to verify
  the story." The report became a standing item in polemic for and against
  Origen from the fourth century onward.
- **Counter-evidence:** Origen's own Commentary on Matthew 15.1–5 treats the
  literal reading of "made themselves eunuchs" as a misunderstanding —
  pressed by modern skeptics (McGuckin and others, as reported in the survey
  literature; the passage and the Handbook were not inspected at first hand
  in this research — ceiling recorded).
- **For historicity:** Eusebius's documentary access (letters, living
  witnesses) and apologetic motive to omit rather than explain; the act's
  attested reality in the period (Nicaea later legislated on self-mutilated
  clergy); compatibility of a repented youthful act with the mature
  exegesis.
- **Status:** contested; single-source. **Publication treatment:** reported
  as an early, contested report with both cases stated; no resolution; no
  medical/psychological conclusion; the historiographical irony (the enemy
  of literalism remembered for literalism) noted as reception, not fact.

## 2. "Adamantius"

- **Witnesses:** Eusebius, HE 6.14.10 ("for this also was a name of
  Origen"); Jerome, Ep. 33 ("man of brass, or, rather, man of adamant");
  Photius, cod. 118 ("because his arguments were linked together like
  chains of adamant") (all checked).
- **Status:** the name is securely ancient; Photius's etymology is later
  explanation. Publication quotes the witnesses and asserts no origin.

## 3. The catechetical-school succession (Pantaenus → Clement → Origen)

- **Earliest witness:** Eusebius (HE 5.10–11, 6.6, 6.14.8–9); repeated by
  Photius, cod. 118 (checked) with "it is said" qualifiers.
- **Status:** the tidy institutional succession is doubted in modern
  scholarship (the "school" before Origen may have been private teaching
  circles). Publication describes the figures and marks the institutional
  question as undeterminable.

## 4. The Alexandrian condemnation as envy

- **Earliest witness:** Eusebius's framing (HE 6.8: Demetrius "overcome by
  human weakness"); Jerome, Ep. 33.5: condemned "not because of the novelty
  or heterodoxy of his doctrines, but because men could not tolerate the
  incomparable eloquence" (checked).
- **Status:** partisan aetiology from Origen's defenders; no acts survive to
  test it. Publication quotes it as the defenders' claim and preserves the
  evidentiary ceiling (witnesslimit box in the rupture section).

## 5. Death as martyrdom vs. death at Tyre

- **Martyrdom tradition:** Pamphilus "and many others who have written an
  accurate account of Origen, as given by those who knew him, assert that he
  quitted this life by a glorious martyrdom at Caesarea" under Decius
  (Photius, cod. 118, checked).
- **Tyre tradition:** Eusebius, HE 7.1 (aged sixty-nine, under Gallus);
  Jerome, De vir. ill. 54: "died at Tyre, in which city he also was buried"
  (checked). Photius's own test: the Tyre account "is the truer account,
  unless the letters supposed to have been written by him after the Decian
  persecution are spurious."
- **Status:** Tyre account generally accepted (ancient and modern);
  martyrdom variant is the earliest defender tradition. Publication follows
  Tyre while quoting the variant and Photius's dilemma.

## 6. The tomb at Tyre

- **Witness:** medieval tradition of a tomb of Origen at Tyre, placed in the
  crusader era behind the high altar of the cathedral and shown to pilgrims;
  recorded in Prat 1911 (checked at newadvent.org/cathen/11306b.htm), who
  notes the cathedral is ruined and the tomb's location lost.
- **Status:** L-class tradition; evidence of continued fascination and
  respect at Tyre, not of the third-century burial's location. Publication
  says exactly that (witnesslimit box in the death section) and makes no
  relic or shrine claim. No current shrine or relic claim was located
  (bounded negative).

## 7. "Origen was condemned by the Fifth Ecumenical Council"

- **Received tradition:** Byzantine and much later usage treats Origen as
  conciliarly anathematized in 553; Constantinople III (681) already
  describes the Fifth Synod as against "Theodore of Mopsuestia, Origen,
  Didymus, and Evagrius" (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/3813.htm);
  the fifteen anathemas circulated as conciliar.
- **Documentary record:** name in anathema 11 of the 553 capitula (checked,
  Percival and Tanner texts); fifteen anathemas absent from the acts;
  Tanner's edition: these "cannot be attributed to this council" (checked at
  papalencyclicals.net); 543 imperial-synodal anathema by name with papal
  assent (checked, Percival; Prat).
- **Modern positions:** Diekamp/Prat (extra-conciliar subscription before
  the opening; papal confirmation covered the Three Chapters only; "many
  learned writers believe so; an equal number deny") vs. Price (anathema 11
  genuine and knowing; the ancient reception correct in substance) — Price
  at reported level (ceiling recorded).
- **Status:** the underlying facts are secure; the juridical
  characterization is genuinely disputed. **Publication treatment:** a
  dedicated subsection states both certainties and both readings, with a
  witnesslimit box; no resolution and no judgment on the condemned
  propositions or the Church's authority.

## 8. "Origenism = Origen"

- **Tradition:** the fifteen anathemas and much later polemic attribute the
  sixth-century system (pre-existent intellect-henad, animated stars,
  spherical resurrection bodies, Christ crucified for demons) to Origen by
  name.
- **Audit:** several condemned propositions correspond to nothing in the
  surviving corpus and are Evagrian; others (pre-existence, restoration) do
  have Origenian roots stated hypothetically in De Principiis (preface and
  1.6 checked in the Rufinian layer). Publication distinguishes the layers
  and quotes Origen's own investigation-not-decision rule.

## 9. "Not a saint"

- **Search:** no feast, cult, canonization, or liturgical commemoration of
  Origen located in any period or rite (ancient notices, Prat 1911, general
  searches 2026-07-24). CCC citations (2745, 2847) and Benedict XVI's 2007
  catecheses are reception of texts, not cult.
- **Status:** bounded, correctable negative; stated as such in the
  publication and appendix.

## 10. Famous tags and set pieces

- "Take heed not to change your mind on our account" (letter to Leonides) —
  Eusebian report of a letter he did not quote from directly; also in
  Photius's epitome of Pamphilus; quoted as Eusebius's report (checked).
- The mother hiding his clothes; Leonides kissing the sleeping boy's breast
  — Eusebian anecdotes "related of Origen when a boy" (HE 6.2.11); told as
  such, not as established fact.
- "His manner of life was as his doctrine, and his doctrine as his life"
  (HE 6.3.7) — Eusebius's summary ("they say"); quoted with attribution;
  reused by Benedict XVI (paraphrased in the reception section).
- "Would rather be wrong with Origen than right with others" — Vincent of
  Lérins's report of "that saying" (Commonitorium 17, checked); quoted as
  Vincent's, not as a verified saying of any individual.
- Four obols a day; barefoot; sleeping on the floor — HE 6.3 (checked);
  Eusebian asceticism report, partly "he is said" — marked in prose.
