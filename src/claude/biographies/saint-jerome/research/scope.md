## Reader and governing question

A serious general reader, student, or researcher who wants one coherent,
evidence-controlled life. Governing questions: (1) what can be established
about a life documented almost entirely by its subject's purposeful pen,
once self-presentation, hostile counter-statement, and later legend are kept
distinct; (2) what exactly Jerome did to the Latin Bible — revised, newly
translated, declined to translate — and what the composite "Vulgate" does
and does not owe him; (3) how the Church's official reception (medieval
doctorate through three twentieth/twenty-first-century papal documents)
relates to the documented life and to the legends (lion, cardinalate,
relics).

## Thesis and boundary

The Church's greatest biblical doctor and the tradition's hardest temper are
one man; the achievement and the wounds come from the same pen, and a
truthful biography reports both while preserving the disputes the sources
cannot close (birth year 331 vs c. 347; the dream's binding force; the 385
proceedings; the 416 responsibility; the Vulgate's scope). Historical
synthesis only: no ecclesiastical verdicts, no doctrinal adjudication, no
relic authentication, no rights or specialist review claimed.

## Included

- Stridon origins (De vir. ill. 135), the vanished town, family; the
  birth-year dispute presented as a dedicated claim with both bases;
- Roman education under Donatus, baptism at Rome (c. 366), Trier and
  Aquileia, the turn east;
- Antioch illness and the dream tribunal (Ep. 22.30 quoted at length), with
  its afterlife (Rufinus Apology 2.6–8; Jerome, Against Rufinus 1.30–31;
  Ep. 70's positive defense) treated as part of its evidence;
- desert of Chalcis (Epp. 14, 22.7), Hebrew begun under a convert (Ep.
  125.12), the Maronia-estate revision (Rebenich via BMCR, reported), the
  hypostases appeal to Damasus (Ep. 15), withdrawal;
- ordination by Paulinus on monastic condition (Against John 41);
  Constantinople, Gregory Nazianzen "my instructor in the Scriptures" (De
  vir. ill. 117), Chronicle and Origen translations;
- Rome 382–385: Damasus's commission and the Gospel preface (quoted), the
  Aventine circle (Marcella, Paula, Blaesilla, Eustochium; Epp. 22, 45,
  127), Against Helvidius (383), Damasus's death, the 385 storm and
  departure (Ep. 45) with the proceedings' formal character left open;
- Bethlehem: Paula's monasteries and hospice, her Hebrew (Ep. 108); the
  workshop and self-catalogue (De vir. ill. 135); Postumianus's six-month
  report (Sulpicius Severus, Dial. 1.8–9);
- the Vulgate labor: Gallican psalter stage, Hebraica veritas program,
  prologus galeatus (canon, 22 books, apocrypha; quoted), Tobit/Judith
  prologues, and a dedicated audit of what "Jerome translated the Vulgate"
  overstates (Gospels-only documented NT revision; Hebrew psalter never in
  the Vulgate; Old Latin deuterocanon retained — ISBE 1915 quoted);
- the Augustine exchange on the Hebrew verity (Aug. Epp. 71, 82; Jerome
  Epp. 102, 112; the Oea gourd);
- controversies in sequence: Jovinian (propositions quoted; the withheld
  book), the Origenist rupture with Rufinus (both Apologies; Ep. 84),
  Vigilantius (406);
- Pelagian conflict (Ep. 133; Dialogue, 415; Diospolis) and the 416 attack
  (Augustine, De gestis Pelagii 66; Innocent, Epp. 136–137; Jerome Ep. 139)
  with responsibility kept as attribution;
- deaths (Paula 404; Marcella after 410; Eustochium 418/419; Jerome 419/420)
  and burial by the Nativity cave;
- reception: 1298 doctorate act, Trent (reported via Benedict XVI), Spiritus
  Paraclitus 1920, CCC 133, Benedict XVI 2007 audiences, Scripturae Sacrae
  affectus 2020; tradition audit of lion (Golden Legend; Gerasimus
  parallel; Rice transfer thesis reported), cardinal anachronism, relic
  translation to Santa Maria Maggiore (Fremantle's "are said"), iconography;
- modern scholarship's rebalancing (Kelly, Rebenich; collaborators
  restored), at recorded ceilings.

## Excluded or bounded

- no textual history or stemma of the Vulgate, its books, or its editions;
  no manuscript, papyrological, or epigraphic research;
- no verdict on the birth year, the dream's supernatural status, the 385
  process, or the 416 culprits;
- no full exposition of the Origenist or Pelagian theological questions
  beyond what the life requires (the Origen questions are treated in this
  series' Origen biography);
- no medical claims (Blaesilla's death is mentioned without causal
  assignment); no psychological diagnosis of Jerome;
- no archaeological or relic authentication (Stridon, Bethlehem tomb, Santa
  Maria Maggiore); no current shrine or patronage census;
- no modern-nationality claim from the unlocated Stridon;
- no ecclesiastical, canonical, or independent rights judgment.

## Evidence classes

A (Jerome's letters, prefaces, catalogue, polemics — advocacy and
self-fashioning), N (Augustine's letters and De gestis Pelagii; Rufinus's
Apology; Innocent I; Sulpicius Severus), E (Prosper's notices as reported;

iconography), M (1298 decretal; Trent as reported; 1920 encyclical; CCC;
2007 audiences; 2020 apostolic letter — each limited to the act's object),
K (Kelly, Rebenich, Rice, ISBE, Fremantle's prolegomena, encyclopedia
records — with reported-level ceilings marked), S (project synthesis).
Defined in the publication's terminal appendix and used consistently across
the research records.

## Material uncertainties

- birth year (331 vs c. 347) and every dependent age; site of Stridon;
  the mother's name; family details;
- exact dates of baptism, travels, desert years, ordination, and most
  letters;
- the dream's date, its binding force, and the consistency of Jerome's own
  accounts;
- the formal character of the 385 Roman proceedings;
- the extent of Jerome's own NT revision beyond the Gospels; the degree of
  his Hebrew competence and reliance on Jewish assistance;
- perpetrators, casualties beyond the slain deacon, and legal aftermath of
  the 416 attack;
- Eustochium's death date; Jerome's death year (419 vs 420);
- the transmission history of the lion and cardinal legends and of the
  relic-translation tradition; the "Sophronius" name expansion.
