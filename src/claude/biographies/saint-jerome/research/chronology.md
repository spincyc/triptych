# Saint Jerome — chronology record

Provider: Anthropic Claude. All web checks 2026-07-24. Evidence classes as in
the publication appendix (A/N/E/L/M/K/S).

Dates are claims. This record states, for each event, the source basis
actually checked, competing views, and the confidence used in the
publication. Jerome supplies almost no absolute dates; the skeleton is built
from his own recollections (A), the one regnal date he gives (De vir. ill.
135: "the fourteenth of the Emperor Theodosius" = 392/393), external
witnesses, and the received editorial chronology (Fremantle's NPNF
prolegomena, checked, used as K).

## The birth-year dispute (dedicated claim)

- **Early tradition — 331.** Basis: the chronicle of Prosper of Aquitaine,
  whose notices yield birth 331 and death 420 at about ninety-one. Prosper
  was not inspected directly (outstanding review); his data are used as
  reported in two checked public-domain witnesses: the ninth-edition
  Britannica article "St Jerome" ("Some authorities, following Prosper's
  chronicle, give 330 or 331 as the date of his birth," checked at
  1902encyclopedia.com) and Fremantle's prolegomena ("The Chronicle of
  Prosper of Aquitaine gives September 20, 420, as the day of his death,"
  checked at ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf206.iv.iii.html). J. N. D. Kelly's
  standard biography (1975) retained the 331–420 frame (publisher/review
  records only; not inspected — ceiling recorded).
- **Late tradition — c. 345–347.** Basis: Jerome's own recollections that he
  was a schoolboy at grammar school when Julian died (363) and "yet a boy"
  in related contexts (reported in the same Britannica article and in
  Fremantle, who adopts 345); the 1912 Catholic Encyclopedia gives "about
  340-2" (checked); Benedict XVI's catechesis of 7 Nov 2007 uses c. 347
  (checked); Rebenich (2002) is catalogued with c. 347 in the reference
  literature (reported).
- **Publication treatment:** c. 347 as working date; 331 preserved as a
  serious alternative in the opening, the dedicated witnesslimit, and the
  chronology table; dependent ages flagged as shifting by ~15 years.

## Anchors (checked at the source)

1. **392/393 — De viris illustribus.** "Up to the present year, that is,
   the fourteenth of the Emperor Theodosius" (De vir. ill. 135, checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm) (A). Theodosius from January 379 ⇒
   14th year 392/393. Status: secure; the anchor for everything relative.
2. **c. 366 — baptism at Rome.** Jerome's baptism at Rome is reported by
   Spiritus Paraclitus 2 ("after his baptism here in Rome," checked,
   vatican.va) following Jerome's own statements; Fremantle infers 366
   under Liberius (checked) (K). Status: event plausible-secure; date
   editorial.
3. **373/374 — journey east; Antioch.** Fremantle's chronology from the
   letters (checked) (K); Ep. 22.30's "many years ago" frame (A). Status:
   approximate.
4. **c. 374/375 — the dream.** Told in Ep. 22.30 (checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/3001022.htm), written 384 at Rome; placed at the
   Antioch illness before the desert. Status: reported by subject; date
   approximate.
5. **c. 375–377 — desert of Chalcis.** Epp. 14 and 22.7 (checked); Hebrew
   begun under "a brother who before his conversion had been a Hebrew"
   (Ep. 125.12, checked). The two-to-three-year span is editorial (K).
   Rebenich's Maronia-estate context reported via BMCR 2003.05.06
   (checked). Status: episode secure; dates approximate.
6. **c. 376/377 — appeal to Damasus.** Ep. 15 (checked): "I communicate
   with none but your blessedness, that is with the chair of Peter"; the
   "three hypostases" pressure. Status: secure as episode; date editorial.
7. **c. 378/379 — ordination by Paulinus at Antioch.** Jerome's own
   recollection, Against John of Jerusalem 41 (checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/3004.htm): "Did I ask to be ordained by you?...
   you have suffered no loss by ordaining me," told to "Bishop Paulinus of
   blessed memory." Status: event secure; date editorial (Britannica 9th
   ed. gives 379).
8. **380–382 — Constantinople; Gregory Nazianzen.** De vir. ill. 117
   (checked): "my instructor in the Scriptures"; Chronicle continued to
   378; Origen homilies translated (De vir. ill. 135). Status: secure.
9. **382–385 — Rome with Damasus.** Gospel preface to Damasus (checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/3002.htm, headed 383): "You urge me to revise the
   old Latin version..."; Against Helvidius 383 (checked page headnote);
   Ep. 22 written 384. Damasus died December 384 (received chronology, K).
   Status: secure.
10. **August 385 — departure from Rome.** Ep. 45 (checked): "I write this
    in haste, dear Lady Asella, as I go on board." The scandal and informal
    condemnation are in the same letter; no formal record survives
    (negative result). Status: event secure; proceedings unresolved.
11. **386 — settlement at Bethlehem.** Ep. 108 (checked): Paula's
    monasteries (one for men, virgins "divided into three companies and
    monasteries"), hospice ("the welcome which Mary and Joseph had
    missed"). Status: secure; month editorial.
12. **c. 390–405/406 — Hebrew-based Old Testament.** Prologus galeatus
    (checked at CCEL): 22 books/letters; apocrypha rule. Tobit "the work
    of one day"; Judith "a single night's work" (prologues checked at
    tertullian.org). Program dates per ISBE survey (checked at
    bible-researcher.com): Samuel/Kings c. 390, Psalms c. 392, Job and
    Prophets 393, Pentateuch 401, completion c. 405/406. Status: program
    secure; book dates approximate (K).
13. **393 — Against Jovinianus; the withheld book.** Adv. Jov. 1.3
    (checked): Jovinian's propositions; the gold/silver-vessels defense.
    NPNF headnote to Ep. 48 (checked): Pammachius and friends "had
    withheld [the books] from publication." Status: secure; the
    withholding is the editor's report of Jerome's own apology.
14. **c. 393–402 — Origenist controversy and the Rufinus break.** Ep. 84.2
    and 84.7 (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/3001084.htm); Rufinus,
    Apology 2.6–8 (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/27052.htm); Jerome,
    Apology 1.30–31 (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/27101.htm);
    Anastasius's censure and Rufinus's death (411) at received-chronology
    level (K). Status: sequence secure; individual dates editorial.
15. **404 — death of Paula.** Ep. 108 (checked; page headnote dates 404).
    Burial "close to the cave of the Lord." Status: secure.
16. **406 — Against Vigilantius.** Checked at
    newadvent.org/fathers/3010.htm ("Vigilantius, or, more correctly,
    Dormitantius"); the work says it was dictated in one night; date 406
    per its headnote. Status: secure.
17. **410–412 — sack of Rome; Marcella's death; Ep. 127.** Ep. 127
    (checked): "The City which had taken the whole world was itself
    taken"; Marcella beaten, sheltered in the basilica of Paul, died soon
    after; letter written c. 412. Status: secure.
18. **415 — Pelagian engagements.** Ep. 133 (checked): "the philosophers
    are the patriarchs of the heretics"; Dialogue Against the Pelagians
    415; Diospolis December 415; Jerome's "miserable synod" epithet
    reported in NPNF editorial material (K). Status: secure.
19. **416 — attack on the monasteries.** Augustine, De gestis Pelagii 66
    (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/1505.htm): beatings, "slain his
    deacon, and burnt his monastic houses," Jerome escaping in "a
    well-defended fortress," with "are said to have" attribution; Innocent
    I to Jerome (Ep. 136, checked): no named individuals, judges offered;
    Jerome, Ep. 139 (checked): house "completely destroyed by the
    onslaughts of the heretics" (letter dated 417 by headnote). Status:
    event secure; culprits attributed only.
20. **418/419 — death of Eustochium.** Fremantle's prolegomena (checked)
    (K). Status: approximate.
21. **30 September 419/420 — death of Jerome.** Year 420 from Prosper as
    reported in Fremantle (checked; Fremantle prints "September 20, 420"
    as Prosper's day — the received commemoration day is 30 September, and
    the publication does not derive the day from Prosper). Britannica 9th
    ed. (checked): "died on September 30, 420." Benedict XVI (checked):
    died 30 September 419-420. Commentary on Jeremiah left unfinished
    (Fremantle, checked). Status: year disputed 419/420; day traditional.

## Posthumous (checked)

22. **Medieval — lion, cardinalate, relics.** Golden Legend, Life of
    S. Jerome (checked at sourcebooks.fordham.edu): the retold dream,
    "ordained cardinal priest" at twenty-nine, the lion narrative, death
    at "eighty-eight years and six months" (a third age tradition, noted).
    Relic translation to Santa Maria Maggiore: Fremantle (checked): "are
    said to have been transferred." Status: L-class tradition.
23. **1298 — Boniface VIII's decretal Gloriosus** ordering the feasts of
    the four Latin doctors as doubles: 1912 Catholic Encyclopedia,
    "Doctors of the Church" (checked at newadvent.org/cathen/05075a.htm).
24. **1546 — Trent on the Vulgate**: reported via Benedict XVI's audience
    (checked); the decree itself not inspected (ceiling).
25. **1920 — Spiritus Paraclitus** (checked at vatican.va): "her 'Greatest
    Doctor'"; fifteenth centenary.
26. **2007 — Benedict XVI's audiences of 7 and 14 November** (checked at
    vatican.va): born c. 347; died 30 September 419-420.
27. **2020 — Scripturae Sacrae affectus** (checked at vatican.va):
    sixteenth centenary; dream cited; iconography read.

## Competing chronologies preserved

- Birth 331 (Prosper tradition; Kelly's frame) vs c. 345–347 (internal
  recollections; Fremantle, CE 1912's 340–2, Benedict XVI, Rebenich): both
  stated; c. 347 used as working date; no resolution.
- Death 419 vs 420: official biographies give both; Prosper's 420 reported;
  the publication prints 419/420.
- Golden Legend's ages (cardinal at 29; death at 88½) noted as a third,
  legendary chronology, not harmonized.
