# Saint Jerome — tradition audit

Provider: Anthropic Claude. Web checks 2026-07-24. This record reviews
received traditions about Jerome: earliest located witness, later reception,
historical status, and the treatment adopted in the publication.

## 1. The dream tribunal (Ep. 22.30)

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome himself, Ep. 22.30 (checked at
  newadvent.org/fathers/3001022.htm), written 384 about an experience of
  c. 374: "You lie. You are a follower of Cicero and not of Christ";
  the scourging; the oath "Lord, if ever again I possess worldly books, or
  if ever again I read such, I have denied You"; the insistence that "this
  was no sleep nor idle dream" with bruises as proof.
- **Contemporary contest:** Rufinus, Apology 2.6–8 (checked), charges
  perjury from the oath and reports Jerome teaching "his own Maro and the
  comedians and lyrical and historical writers" to boys at Bethlehem;
  Jerome, Against Rufinus 1.30–31 (checked), replies "it was a promise for
  the future, not the abolition of my memory of the past" and that dreams
  cannot be made crimes; Ep. 70 (checked) defends secular learning
  positively (captive-woman figure).
- **Later reception:** retold in the Golden Legend (checked) and in
  Scripturae Sacrae affectus (checked, paraphrase).
- **Status:** single-witness self-report whose binding force its own teller
  first asserted and later minimized. **Publication treatment:** quoted in
  full context; dedicated witnesslimit presents Rufinus's charge and
  Jerome's demotion; no supernatural or psychological adjudication.

## 2. The lion

- **Negative early record (bounded):** no lion in Jerome's own works as
  checked, in De vir. ill. 135, in Sulpicius Severus's Dialogue 1.8–9, in
  Innocent's letters, or in Fremantle's survey of early material; Fremantle
  (checked): the desert descriptions "no doubt gave rise to the tradition
  that he was always attended by a lion... With such traditions a
  historical work must not be burdened."
- **Earliest located witness in this research:** the medieval vita
  tradition as represented by the Golden Legend (13th c.; Caxton text
  checked at sourcebooks.fordham.edu): thorn, healing, the lion set to
  guard the ass, the theft, the penance, the returning camels. (Earlier
  medieval vitae exist but were not located in inspectable form —
  outstanding lead.)
- **Parallel and transfer thesis:** the same story-pattern is told of abbot
  Gerasimus of the Jordan (d. 475) in early Byzantine monastic literature
  (John Moschus, Pratum Spirituale 107 — not inspected; reported); the
  name-confusion transfer (Gerasimus/Geronimus) is E. F. Rice's argument
  (Saint Jerome in the Renaissance, 1985), used at reported level via
  museum/reference literature (ceiling recorded).
- **Status:** late legend; probably transferred. **Publication treatment:**
  narrated as Golden Legend content in the reception section; bounded
  negative stated; Rice thesis attributed and marked reported; closing
  synthesis (lion as image of tamed ferocity) marked as reception reading.

## 3. The cardinalate

- **Earliest located witness:** Golden Legend (checked): "when he was
  nine-and-twenty years old he was ordained cardinal priest in the church
  of Rome" (with the woman's-clothing trick and the flight to Gregory
  Nazianzen — a legendary sequence).
- **Reception:** panel painting tradition of Jerome in cardinal's dress,
  e.g. Master of Grossgmain, "Saint Jerome as a Cardinal" (1498), Thyssen
  catalogue entry (checked; cardinal dress + Golden Legend lion).
- **Audit:** the cardinalate as an office did not exist in the fourth
  century; the legend back-projects Jerome's documented service as
  Damasus's secretary/adviser (A: Gospel preface "the command is given by
  you who are the supreme bishop"; Ep. 123-era recollections not used).
- **Status:** anachronistic legend. **Publication treatment:** stated as
  reception with the anachronism explained; no claim that any papal act
  underlies it.

## 4. "Jerome translated the Vulgate" (authorship-scope tradition)

- **Jerome's own claim:** De vir. ill. 135 (checked): "I translated the
  New Testament from the Greek, and the Old Testament from the Hebrew."
- **Documented scope:** prefaced NT work = the four Gospels only (Gospel
  preface to Damasus, checked); Hebrew OT with prefaces (prologus
  galeatus, checked); Tobit and Judith hastily (prologues checked);
  Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, Maccabees declined (galeatus and ISBE).
- **Modern survey (checked):** ISBE 1915 (bible-researcher.com): NT-beyond-
  Gospels revision disputed ("whether Jerome revised the whole New
  Testament or only the Gospels"); "His Psalter from the Hebrew never
  ousted the Gallican which still holds its place in the Vulgate"; the
  received Vulgate includes "the unrevised Old Latin (Wisdom,
  Ecclesiasticus, 1 and 2 Maccabees and Baruch)."
- **Status:** the solitary-translator tradition overstates; the received
  Vulgate is composite. **Publication treatment:** dedicated witnesslimit
  in the Vulgate section; Jerome's own claim quoted and weighed; Trent's
  decree described as concerning the composite, at reported level.

## 5. Relics at Santa Maria Maggiore

- **Witness:** Fremantle (checked): remains "are said to have been
  transferred... to the Church of Santa Maria Maggiore at Rome, and
  miracles to have been wrought at his tomb."
- **Status:** L-class tradition; no primary translation record located
  (bounded negative; outstanding lead). **Publication treatment:** "are
  said" preserved; no authentication or current-custody claim; cult noted
  as independent of relic verification.

## 6. The 385 departure as formal trial

- **Witness:** Ep. 45 (checked) attests circulated charges, scandal
  around Paula, and a departure under pressure; no source records a formal
  process or sentence (bounded negative).
- **Status:** modern "trial" narratives are reconstruction. **Publication
  treatment:** witnesslimit states the storm and leaves the formal
  character open.

## 7. The 416 attack as Pelagian crime

- **Witnesses:** Augustine, De gestis Pelagii 66 (checked): "certain
  followers of Pelagius are said..."; Innocent, Ep. 136 (checked): no
  individuals named; Jerome, Ep. 139 (checked): "the heretics."
- **Status:** event multiply attested; responsibility only ever
  attributed. **Publication treatment:** witnesslimit keeps attribution
  distinct from proof; Pelagius never charged with ordering it.

## 8. Death-day and age traditions

- 30 September: the received commemoration day (feast) and Britannica 9th
  ed. (checked); Fremantle prints Prosper's day as "September 20, 420"
  (checked) — divergence noted, not resolved; Golden Legend's "eighty-eight
  years and six months" noted as legendary. Publication prints 30 September
  419/420 with the bases separated.

## 9. Doctor of the Church

- **Acts checked:** Boniface VIII's 1298 decretal Gloriosus (via CE 1912,
  checked); Spiritus Paraclitus 1920 (checked): "her 'Greatest Doctor'";
  CCC 133 (checked); Benedict XVI 2007 audiences (checked); Scripturae
  Sacrae affectus 2020 (checked).
- **Audit:** each act limited to its object (feast rank; biblical
  devotion; quotation of one sentence); none asserts legends or dates as
  fact. Publication treats reception in its own section with a
  witnesslimit on those limits.
