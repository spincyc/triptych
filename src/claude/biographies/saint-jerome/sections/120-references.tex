\subsection*{Primary sources: Jerome}

\begin{itemize}
\item Jerome, letters and principal works in English translation:
\textit{A Select Library of Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers}, series 2,
vol.~6, ed.\ P.~Schaff and H.~Wace, trans.\ W.~H.~Fremantle, G.~Lewis, and
W.~G.~Martley (New York: Christian Literature Company, 1893). Quotations
follow the transcriptions at New Advent
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/}): \work{Letters} 14
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001014.htm}), 15, 22, 45, 48
(headnote), 70, 84, 102, 108, 112, 125, 127, 133, 136, 139 (same pattern,
\texttt{3001NNN.htm}); Preface to the Four Gospels
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3002.htm}); \work{The Perpetual
Virginity of Blessed Mary (Against Helvidius)}
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3007.htm}); \work{Against
Jovinianus}, book 1 (\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/30091.htm});
\work{Against Vigilantius}
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3010.htm}); \work{To Pammachius
Against John of Jerusalem} 41
(\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3004.htm}); and, at CCEL, the

(\url{https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf206.vii.iii.iv.html}) and the
prolegomena Life of Jerome by Fremantle
(\url{https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf206.iv.iii.html}). The translation is
public domain; the underlying Latin is ancient and public domain.
\item Jerome, \work{On Illustrious Men} 117 and 135, and \work{Apology

\url{https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/27101.htm}).
\item Jerome, prologues to Tobit and Judith, public-domain translations at
the Tertullian Project
(\url{https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/jerome_preface_tobit.htm};
\url{https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/jerome_preface_judith.htm}).

\subsection*{Later tradition}

\begin{itemize}
\item \work{The Golden Legend} (Jacobus de Voragine, 13th c.), Life of
S.~Jerome, in Caxton's English version, Temple Classics text at the Internet
Medieval Sourcebook, Fordham University
(\url{https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/goldenlegend/GoldenLegend-Volume5.asp});
source of the lion story, the cardinal-priest notice, and the retold dream.
\item Master of Grossgmain, \textit{Saint Jerome as a Cardinal} (1498),
catalogue entry, Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza
(\url{https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/master-grossgmain/saint-jerome-cardinal});
cited for the iconographic convention.
\item E.~F. Rice, \textit{Saint Jerome in the Renaissance} (Baltimore,
1985): the Gerasimus-transfer explanation of the lion legend, cited as
reported in museum and reference literature. Not independently inspected.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Modern scholarship and reference}

\begin{itemize}
\item J.~N.~D. Kelly, \textit{Jerome: His Life, Writings, and
Controversies} (London: Duckworth, 1975). Cited for its standing as the
standard English biography and its retention of the 331--420 frame, at the
level of publisher and review records. Not independently inspected.
\item S.~Rebenich, \textit{Jerome} (The Early Church Fathers; London:
Routledge, 2002). Cited through the review by V.~Hunink, \textit{Bryn Mawr
Classical Review} 2003.05.06
(\url{https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2003/2003.05.06}), from which the quoted
phrases on Jerome's Hebrew and the Maronia estate are taken. The book was
not independently inspected.
\item S.~Angus, ``Latin Vulgate,'' \textit{International Standard Bible
Encyclopedia} (1915), as presented at Bible Research
(\url{https://www.bible-researcher.com/vulgate1.html}); public domain;
source of the quoted judgments on the Vulgate's composite character and the
Psalter.
\item ``St Jerome,'' \textit{Encyclop\ae dia Britannica}, 9th ed.\ (1881),
at 1902encyclopedia.com
(\url{https://www.1902encyclopedia.com/J/JER/st-jerome.html}); public
domain; quoted on the birth-year traditions.
\end{itemize}
