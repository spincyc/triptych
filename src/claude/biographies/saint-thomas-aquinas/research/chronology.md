# Saint Thomas Aquinas — chronology record

Provider: Anthropic Claude. All web checks 2026-07-25. Evidence classes as
in the publication appendix (A/N/E/L/M/K/S).

Dates are claims. Thomas supplies almost no autobiographical dates; the
skeleton is institutional (university and papal acts) and retrospective
(canonization-era memory, modern reconstruction). For each event this
record states the basis actually checked, competing views, and the
confidence used in the publication.

## The birth year (dedicated claim)

- **1224/5.** Benedict XVI's catechesis of 2 June 2010 (checked, vatican.va
  EN): born "between 1224-1225" at the castle of Roccasecca near Aquino.
  The Stanford Encyclopedia entry "Thomas Aquinas" (2022, checked): "circa
  1225." The Commissio Leonina site (checked) uses c. 1226.
- **Older literature.** The 1912 Catholic Encyclopedia (Kennedy, checked):
  "1225 or 1227"; "the end of 1225 is usually assigned," with Prümmer
  preferring 1227.
- Basis of all reconstructions: later age notices and educational
  chronology, not a contemporary vital record.
- **Publication treatment:** 1224/5 as working range; the older 1225/1227
  span preserved; every dependent age flagged as approximate.

## Anchors and events

1. **c. 1230/31 — Monte Cassino oblature.** CE 1912 (checked): "At the age
   of five... sent to receive his first training from the Benedictine monks
   of Monte Cassino"; Benedict XVI (checked) reports the same placement.
   Basis: canonization-era memory as received by the modern literature (E
   via K/M). Approximate.
2. **Mid/late 1230s — University of Naples.** CE: "about 1236" sent to
   Naples; Benedict XVI: moved to Naples, where Frederick II's foundation
   exposed him to Aristotle. Modern accounts place the move c. 1239; this
   study asserts only "in the later 1230s." Approximate.
3. **c. 1244 — Dominican habit at Naples.** SEP (checked): joined the order
   at about nineteen. CE argues "between 1240 and August, 1243." Both
   preserved; publication uses "probably 1244, and not later than the
   mid-1240s." Disputed year.
4. **1244/45 — Seizure and detention.** CE (checked): brothers "captured
   the novice near the town of Aquapendente and confined him in the
   fortress of San Giovanni at Rocca Secca. Here he was detained nearly two
   years." SEP (checked): his parents "detained him at Roccasecca for a
   year before relenting." Event secure (E, multiply remembered); duration
   and legal character disputed. The temptation-and-girdle story attached
   to the detention is audited in `tradition-audit.md`.
5. **1245–52 — Studies under Albert the Great.** CE (checked): arrived at
   Cologne "in 1244 or 1245," went with Albert to Paris in 1245, "in 1248
   both returned to Cologne." SEP (checked): sent to Paris, then in 1248 to
   Cologne. The Paris-first and Cologne-first itineraries are both
   preserved; secure that 1248–52 was Cologne under Albert. The "dumb ox"
   anecdote is audited in `tradition-audit.md`.
6. **1252–56 — Paris, bachelor of the Sentences.** SEP: nominated by Albert
   in 1252 for the Paris theology degree; the Scriptum belongs to these
   years. Secure as period; work dates approximate.
7. **Spring 1256 — Master (regent 1256–59).** SEP: held the Dominican chair
   1256–59. CE: William of Saint-Amour's *De periculis novissimorum
   temporum* condemned by Alexander IV at Anagni, 5 October 1256; Thomas
   answered with *Contra impugnantes*. Secure.
8. **1259/61–65 — Italy; Orvieto lector.** CE: successively at Anagni,
   Orvieto, Rome, Viterbo (list without year-by-year assignment); the
   Catena aurea's Matthew part dedicated to Urban IV. Urban IV's bull
   *Transiturus* instituting Corpus Christi: 1264 (CE "Corpus Christi"
   article, checked, which prints "8 September, 1264"; modern accounts give
   August — day left open here). Office attribution audited separately.
9. **1265–68 — Rome (Santa Sabina); Summa begun.** CE: the first part of
   the Summa written at Rome, 1265–69 span reported; SEP: Italian decade
   1259–68 in Orvieto and Rome. Secure as period.
10. **1268/69–72 — Second Paris regency.** SEP: 1268–72. The SEP
    "Condemnation of 1277" entry (Thijssen, rev. 2023, checked) records
    Tempier condemnations in "1270 and 1277"; De unitate intellectus c.
    1270 (standard dating; not independently anchored here). Secure as
    period.
11. **1272–73 — Naples.** SEP: returned to Naples 1272; Benedict XVI
    (23 June 2010, checked): the Lent 1273 Naples sermons on the Creed,
    Our Father, Ten Commandments, Hail Mary. Secure.
12. **6 December 1273 — Cessation of writing.** CE (checked): "On 6
    December, 1273, he laid aside his pen and would write no more," with
    the reply to Reginald rendered "I can do no more. Such secrets have
    been revealed to me that all I have written now appears to be of little
    value." SEP (checked): stopped writing, saying his work "seems to me
    like straw." Earliest witness: Bartholomew of Capua's deposition at the
    1319 Naples inquiry, at second hand (reported level; process text not
    inspected — see source audit). Event secure; wording and character
    open.
13. **January–March 1274 — Journey toward Lyon; death.** CE: Gregory X
    convoked the council to open 1 May 1274 and directed Thomas to attend
    and bring *Contra errores Graecorum*; he set out in January 1274,
    collapsed near Terracina, was taken to the castle of Maenza (niece
    Francesca), then to Fossanova, where at the monks' request he dictated
    a brief commentary on the Canticle (tradition), made the recorded
    profession before Viaticum, and died 7 March 1274. Death date secure
    (CE, SEP, and all reception acts); deathbed details are E/L class.
14. **7 March 1277 — Paris condemnation.** SEP "Condemnation of 1277":
    Tempier prohibited 219 theses. Relation to Thomas bounded: Wippel
    ("some of the positions were taken from his writings") vs Hissette
    (most articles aim at Arts-faculty readings); Giles of Rome censured
    before 28 March 1277; Wielockx's thesis that a distinct process
    against Thomas was begun and halted by the curia between 20 May and 25
    November 1277. The coincidence with the third anniversary of the death
    is arithmetical (S), not a sourced contemporary observation.
15. **1277 — Oxford prohibitions.** CE "Thomism" (checked): Tempier and
    Kilwardby "censured certain philosophical propositions, embodying
    doctrines taught by St. Thomas," especially individuation and the
    unity of substantial form. Year secure; day not asserted.
16. **1278–88 — Dominican defense.** CE: chapters (Milan 1278, Paris 1279
    and 1286, Bordeaux 1287, Lucca 1288) impose or defend his doctrine.
    Reported level.
17. **1319/1321 — Canonization inquiries.** Naples 1319; a second inquiry
    at Fossanova 1321. Reported level only (the process editions were
    identified, not inspected). The 1319 Naples inquiry date is multiply
    reported in the modern literature checked.
18. **18 July 1323 — Canonization by John XXII.** CE (checked). Secure.
19. **1324/25 — Paris revocation.** CE "Thomism" (checked): "in 1324
    Stephen de Bourret, Bishop of Paris, revoked the censure," declaring
    Thomas "had never believed, taught, or written anything contrary to
    the Faith or good morals." Modern accounts date the act 14 February
    1325; the discrepancy (probably old-style reckoning) is preserved, not
    resolved.
20. **28 January 1369 — Translation to Toulouse.** CE (checked). Secure as
    reported; ceremony details in Räsänen (publisher-record level).
21. **1567 — Doctor of the Church (Pius V).** CE (checked). Secure.
22. **1789–1974 — Saint-Sernin interval; return to the Jacobins.** Reported
    level only (University of Mary "Prime Matters" article, checked
    2026-07-25; no official custodial document checked). Not asserted
    beyond reported status.
23. **4 August 1879 — Aeterni Patris (Leo XIII).** Checked at vatican.va
    EN. Distinct from Pius IX's 1868 bull of the same incipit (repository
    family `pius-ix/aeterni-patris-1868`, not used).
24. **18 January 1880 — Leonine edition ordered; 4 August 1880 — patron of
    Catholic schools.** CE (checked): the edition begun by Leo XIII's
    direction (motu proprio of 18 Jan 1880 for the Contra Gentiles volume);
    Brief of 4 Aug 1880 for the patronage. The Commissio Leonina continues
    the edition (site checked; founding-year statement not located there —
    bounded).
25. **1914–23 — Doctoris Angelici (29 June 1914, reported via the Sacred
    Congregation of Studies decree preamble and Studiorum Ducem footnote);
    24 theses decree (27 July 1914, checked at franciscan-archive.org);
    1917 code canon 1366 §2 (as quoted in Studiorum Ducem, checked);
    Studiorum Ducem (29 June 1923, checked LA, "Doctor Communis").**
26. **28 October 1965 — Optatam totius 16** (checked EN): speculative
    theology "under the guidance of St. Thomas."
27. **20 November 1974 — Lumen Ecclesiae** (Paul VI to Vincent de
    Couesnongle, checked IT at vatican.va).
28. **1983 — CIC canon 252 §3** (checked EN at vatican.va): dogmatic
    theology "especially with St. Thomas as a teacher."
29. **1992/97 — CCC 460, 1381, 2763** (checked EN, vatican.va archive).
30. **14 September 1998 — Fides et ratio 43–44** (checked EN).
31. **2, 16, 23 June 2010 — Benedict XVI's three catecheses** (checked EN).
32. **1 November 2025 — Leo XIV declares Newman co-patron, with Thomas, of
    the Church's educational mission** (reported: America Magazine report
    checked 2026-07-25; the formal act's text not checked).

## Competing chronologies preserved

- birth 1224/5 vs 1225/1227;
- Dominican entry 1244 vs 1240–43;
- detention about one year vs nearly two;
- Paris-first vs Cologne-first for 1245–48;
- revocation 1324 (old literature) vs 14 February 1325 (modern accounts);
- Transiturus day (CE: 8 September 1264; modern accounts: August).

No date supplied only by later tradition is promoted to the historical
skeleton; deathbed and detention details keep their E/L classes.
