# Saint Thomas Aquinas — tradition audit

Provider: Anthropic Claude. Web checks 2026-07-25. This record reviews
received traditions: earliest located witness, later reception, historical
status, and the treatment adopted in the publication.

## 0. The witness base itself

The oldest substantial biographical witnesses are all canonization-era:
the Naples inquiry (1319) and Fossanova inquiry (1321), William of Tocco's
*Ystoria sancti Thome de Aquino* (composed 1318–1323; PIMS critical edition
record checked: "the oldest important biographical document we possess"),
Bernard Gui's Legenda, and Peter Calo's Life (c. 1300 per the 1912
Encyclopedia's notice of the Fontes vitae fascicle). All stand two
generations after the events of Thomas's youth; Tocco was promoter of the
cause. The Fontes vitae S. Thomae Aquinatis (Prümmer/Laurent, 1911–1937)
was located in scan (archive.org) but not page-inspected; the English
documents volume (Foster 1959) is in copyright and was identified, not
quoted. Every tradition below therefore has, at best, an E-class earliest
witness reaching events of 1230–1274 across forty to ninety years, and the
publication says so.

## 1. The temptation and the girdle

- **Story as received:** during the family detention his brothers sent a
  woman to seduce him; Thomas drove her out with a brand snatched from the
  fire; angels then girded him, in the words the 1912 Encyclopedia
  (checked) reports, "We gird thee with the girdle of perpetual virginity."
- **Earliest located witnesses:** the canonization-era Lives and inquiry
  materials; the tradition's own chain runs through Reginald of Piperno,
  to whom (per the same Encyclopedia notice) Thomas confided "the secret of
  a remarkable favour received" — i.e. the story reaches the record as a
  reported deathbed confidence, not as any bystander's testimony.
- **Later reception:** girdle relic and confraternity devotion (identified
  only; not researched item-level here); standard element of iconography
  and popular Lives.
- **Status:** unverifiable private event with late, promotional witnesses;
  the detention itself is historically secure, the bedroom scene is not
  controllable. **Publication treatment:** narrated in the tradition
  section with its chain stated; the historical narrative asserts only the
  detention and the family's failure to break the vocation; no girdle
  relic claim asserted.

## 2. The "dumb ox"

- **Story as received:** fellow students called the silent Neapolitan "the
  dumb ox"; Albert, after hearing him defend a thesis, exclaimed (CE 1912
  wording, checked): "We call this young man a dumb ox, but his bellowing
  in doctrine will one day resound throughout the world."
- **Earliest located witnesses:** the canonization-era Lives; no
  contemporary Cologne or Paris record of the nickname or the prophecy was
  located in this study's checked sources (bounded negative result: not in
  the checked SEP entries' biographical summaries; the 1912 Encyclopedia
  transmits it without chain).
- **Status:** classic retrospective master-recognizes-genius anecdote,
  possible but unverifiable; its polish and its prophecy-fulfilled shape
  are hagiographic. **Publication treatment:** told in the tradition
  section as canonization-era memory; the historical narrative asserts
  only the studies under Albert.

## 3. The 6 December 1273 cessation and the "straw" remark

- **Event:** after Mass on St. Nicholas's day 1273 at Naples, Thomas
  stopped writing and dictating. Event multiply received (CE 1912; SEP
  2022; Benedict XVI 2 June 2010, all checked) and treated as secure.
- **Earliest witness for the remark:** Bartholomew of Capua's sworn
  deposition at the Naples inquiry of 1319 — forty-six years after the
  event, and at second hand: Bartholomew did not claim presence; the
  deposition transmits a chain running through a Dominican intermediary
  (John del Giudice, in the standard modern reconstructions) back to
  Reginald of Piperno, to whom the words were spoken. This study verified
  the chain only at reported level (search-located modern accounts;
  Torrell at publisher-record ceiling); the process text itself was not
  inspected (outstanding).
- **Wording varies by transmission:** SEP: everything written "seems to me
  like straw"; CE 1912: "all I have written now appears to be of little
  value" (no straw); Benedict XVI's catechesis: all he had written "was
  worthless." The famous Latin (omnia quae scripsi videntur mihi paleae)
  was not verified at source here and is not printed in the publication.
- **Status:** secure cessation; famous wording carried by one late,
  second-hand, sanctity-promoting deposition and rendered variously.
  **Publication treatment:** cessation in the historical narrative; the
  remark quoted in the CE rendering with a witnesslimit stating the chain;
  no medical or mystical adjudication.

## 4. The speaking crucifix and the levitations

- **Story as received (CE 1912, checked):** at Naples in 1273 "three of
  the brethren saw him lifted in ecstasy, and they heard a voice
  proceeding from the crucifix on the altar, saying 'Thou hast written
  well of me, Thomas; what reward wilt thou have?'" with the reply "None
  other than Thyself, Lord"; the Encyclopedia notes that "similar
  declarations are said to have been made at Orvieto and at Paris" — the
  multiplication is itself an audit datum.
- **Earliest located witnesses:** canonization-era (inquiry and Lives);
  named seers reach the record through the promotional dossier.
- **Later reception:** retold by Benedict XVI (2 June 2010 audience,
  checked) as spiritual exemplum — reception, not historical
  certification.
- **Status:** miracle narratives of the cause; evidence of how Thomas's
  sanctity and his theology were remembered as one thing. **Publication
  treatment:** tradition section, with the multiplication noted; no
  natural explanation invented, no supernatural causation certified.

## 5. The deathbed traditions

Journey collapse, Maenza, the monks' request for a Canticle commentary,
the Eucharistic profession before Viaticum ("I submit all to the judgment
and correction of the Holy Roman Church..." in the CE rendering), all from
canonization-era memory (CE, checked). The death and its date are secure;
the speeches are E-class received tradition and are labeled as such in the
narrative. No cause-of-death claim is made; the poisoning legend is
excluded as extraneous (see scope).

## 6. Relic history

- **Secure core:** burial at Fossanova 1274; translation of the remains to
  the Dominican church (Jacobins) at Toulouse, 28 January 1369 (CE,
  checked; ceremony scholarship: Räsänen 2017, publisher-record level).
- **Reported extensions (not independently verified):** removal to
  Saint-Sernin at the Revolution and return to the Jacobins in 1974 for
  the seventh centenary (University of Mary "Prime Matters" article,
  checked 2026-07-25; no official custodial document checked).
- **Competing claims:** the same checked article asserts head and arm
  relics at San Domenico Maggiore, Naples; other traditions (e.g. a skull
  claim at Priverno) are treated in the modern relic scholarship
  (Räsänen; record level). Item-level custody is unadjudicated here.
- **Status and treatment:** the publication reports the secure core, marks
  the Revolution-era and 1974 movements as reported, presents the
  competing head/arm claims as unresolved, and asserts no current
  authentication. The cult honors the saint; no claim depends on a
  verified skeleton.

## 7. Titles and patronages

"Angelic Doctor" and "Common Doctor" are reception titles, not
contemporary descriptions: the Doctor declaration is 1567 (Pius V; CE
checked); "Doctor Communis" is used programmatically by Pius XI in 1923
(Studiorum Ducem, Latin checked); patron of Catholic schools 1880 (Leo
XIII's Brief, CE checked); co-patron, with Newman, of the Church's
educational mission per Leo XIV's 2025 act (reported). Each is limited to
its act; none authenticates any anecdote above.
