## Reader and governing question

A serious general reader, student, or researcher who wants one coherent,
evidence-graded life. Governing questions (stated in the opening section):
(1) what the controlled sources establish about the life; (2) where memory
is contested — Sixtine Index, Bruno, the 1616 Galileo documents, the
cause's three-century delay — what each document says and where document
ends and interpretation begins; (3) how the Church's own judgment moved
from the vetoes of 1677/1753 to the acts of 1930–1931, and what those acts
claim and do not claim.

## Thesis boundary

The organizing synthesis — a massively documented life whose contested
episodes are each governed by a small number of identifiable documents,
around which interpretation has accreted — is historical. The study does
not adjudicate the theology of grace, papal temporal power, or the larger
science-and-faith questions; asserts no position on the course of the
morning of 26 February 1616 beyond the documents; does not allocate
personal responsibility in the Bruno case; and claims no theological,
canonical, archival, or rights review and no ecclesiastical approval.
Galileo's 1633 trial is outside scope except as reception of the 1616
documents and of the certificate.

## Included

- family, Jesuit entry (1560), formation (Roman College, Florence,
  Mondovì, Padua), the 1569 journey north;
- Louvain 1569–76: the anomalous preaching before orders, the compressed
  ordination sequence of 1570 (Liège/Ghent, Cornelius Jansenius the
  elder), six years lecturing on the Summa, Hebrew;
- the Roman College chair of Controversies; the making, structure
  (verified in the 1599 and 1721 Venice printings), publication history,
  and reception of the De controversiis, including the counter-chairs
  report;
- the 1590 French legation; the Sixtine Index episode stated precisely
  (1590 unpromulgated Index, donec corrigantur, removal after Sixtus's
  death); the Vulgate revision and Bellarmine's preface with the pia
  fraus debate preserved as debate;
- the catechisms of 1597/98 and their three-century career;
- cardinalate 1599 (the elogium in both transmitted forms); Holy Office
  and Index membership; the Bruno case at its documentary ceiling;
- the De auxiliis controversy and its deliberate non-resolution (1607);
- Capua 1602–05: the consecration-day discrepancy (20 vs 21 April), the
  autobiography's pastoral ledger, the 1605 conclaves, resignation;
- the Venetian interdict and the James I / oath of allegiance exchange
  (documents in McIlwain), De potestate papae against Barclay, the
  indirect-power doctrine with anti-modernizing ceilings;
- the Galileo entanglements 1611–1616 at document level (Opere XI, XII,
  XIX), with the 1616 dossier table, the certificate in two witnesses,
  the 1633 reception of the certificate, and the authenticity
  historiography (von Gebler) with ceilings;
- the spiritual works (works table), autobiography 1613, death, burial;
- the cause 1622–1931 with the delay historiography (Döllinger–Reusch,
  CE 1907, Lux illa) honestly presented; canonization 1930; doctorate
  1931 (Providentissimus Deus, verified at source in AAS 23); feast
  history 13 May → 17 September;
- traditions, relics (1923 translation, Sant'Ignazio), patronages
  (Capua 1967 verified; catechists etc. unlocated), sayings,
  iconography; reception including 19th-century adversarial memory and
  20th-century re-evaluations.

## Excluded or bounded

- no complete commentary on the Disputationes or any work; loci asserted
  only where read;
- no reconstruction of the Bruno trial beyond the named witnesses; no
  allocation of his personal role;
- no assertion about the unsigned 26 February 1616 minute beyond its
  text, signatures, and the named historiography;
- no coverage of Galileo's 1633 trial except as certificate reception;
- no modernization of the indirect-power doctrine in either direction;
- no independent judgment of sanctity, miracle, or relic identity;
- no patronage claims beyond the verified 1967 Capua act;
- Brodrick's biography (in copyright) not consulted; named only.

## Principal corpus

- Autobiography (1613) in Döllinger–Reusch, *Die Selbstbiographie des
  Cardinals Bellarmin* (Bonn 1887), public scan, Latin read directly;
  the commentary is also the control for the beatification-process
  history and the 1590 Index bibliography.
- *Disputationes de controversiis*: Venice 1599 (four-tome contents
  lists; dedication to Sixtus V) and Venice 1721 tomus I (book
  divisions), public scans.
- Favaro, *Le Opere di Galileo Galilei, Edizione Nazionale*, vols. XI,
  XII, XIX, public scans: nos. 515, 520, 709, 1110; XIX pp. 278,
  320–323, 342, 348, 404–405.
- Von Gebler, *Galileo Galilei and the Roman Curia* (Sturge trans.,
  1879), public scan: 1616 decree translation; precept-authenticity
  debate; 1633 deposition and sentence translations.
- McIlwain, *The Political Works of James I* (1918), public scan: oath
  text, breves, Triplici nodo, Premonition, Blackwell letters
  (introduction).
- AAS 22 (1930) 278–279, 593–604; AAS 23 (1931) 433–438; AAS 60 (1968)
  12–13 — all read in the vatican.va OCR scans; the 1931 act's incipit
  *Providentissimus Deus* verified directly against the scan (it is a
  distinct act from Leo XIII's 1893 encyclical of the same incipit).
- Catholic Encyclopedia: Smith, "St. Robert Francis Romulus Bellarmine"
  (vol. 2, 1907, read in full); Astrain, "Congregatio de Auxiliis"
  (1908); Turner, "Giordano Bruno" (1908).
- Benedict XVI, General Audience 23 February 2011 (vatican.va English).
- Current calendar: 2002 Missale Romanum General Roman Calendar page
  verified for the 17 September optional memorial (repository source
  record; controlling artifact restricted, checked directly).

## Material uncertainties (preserved)

- exact teaching-years count of the controversies chair (11 years per
  Lux illa vs 1576–86/88 per CE and Benedict XVI);
- print history of the Institutiones linguae hebraicae and full revision
  history of the Disputationes;
- Bellarmine's individual acts in the Bruno proceedings;
- the course of events on 26 February 1616 and the status of the
  unsigned minute;
- the Capua consecration day (20 April per Lux illa vs Good Shepherd
  Sunday = 21 April per autobiography and Benedict XVI);
- Clement VIII's motive in the Capua appointment;
- how near the 1605 conclave came to electing him;
- the morality of the Clementine Vulgate preface (pia fraus debate);
- the exact interplay of Passionei's votum and the French court in
  1753;
- current shrine and relic status beyond the 1930/1967 record.
