## Reader and governing question

A serious general reader, student, or researcher who wants one coherent,
evidence-controlled life. Governing questions: (1) what the sources actually
establish about the life once the late notices are audited; (2) how a
converted Carthaginian rhetorician supplied Latin Christianity's working
theological vocabulary and its first inside portrait of church practice;
(3) whether he really left the Church — presenting the modern reassessment of
the "Montanist schism" without resolving it.

## Thesis and boundary

Tertullian's achievement (Latin apologetic, theological vocabulary,
documentation of practice, the prescription argument) and his estrangement
(New Prophecy allegiance, war on the *psychici*, contempt for the penitential
edict) flow from one rigorist temperament; his formal ecclesiastical fate is
unresolved, with current scholarship weighing against a formal personal
schism. This is historical synthesis, not an ecclesiastical verdict: the study
does not decide his eternal state, the *On Modesty* bishop's identity, New
Prophecy institutional history, or opponents' actual teachings, and claims no
theological, canonical, or rights review.

## Included

- Roman Carthage and the African church's documented beginnings (Scillitan
  Acts 180; Perpetua 203);
- the audited biographical record: self-witness (lay self-placement, marriage,
  conversion, Greek works) versus Jerome/Eusebius portrait versus the
  Barnes-line modern critique, with Frend's caution preserved;
- apologetic works (197 and 212 anchors; religious-liberty passage; famous
  epigrams at verified loci);
- church practice (baptism, prayer, penance/exomologesis, unwritten customs,
  marriage);
- boundary-drawing and polemic (prescription argument; *Against Marcion*
  edition history and the 207/208 anchor);
- theological coinage (*trinitas*, one substance/three persons, two natures in
  one person, soul, flesh/resurrection) with the pre-Nicene semantic caveat;
- the New Prophecy: origins per Eusebius's dossier, Tertullian's adherence,
  discipline-not-doctrine self-description, *psychici*, the *On Modesty*
  edict with its disputed bishop;
- the schism question: ancient claims (Jerome, Augustine, Praedestinatus)
  versus modern reassessment (Powell 1975, Barnes, Rankin 1995, Tabbernee),
  ending unresolved;
- death-silence, the Tertullianists and their end under Aurelius;
- reception: Cyprian's *Da magistrum*, Lactantius, Vincent, Gelasian Decree,
  medieval near-loss, not-a-saint status, Catechism citation (2761), Benedict
  XVI's 2007 catechesis, Chapman 1912, contested pages ("devil's gateway,"
  *The Shows* 30), and the modern rebalancing.

## Excluded or bounded

- no work-by-work chronology, manuscript-family study, textual criticism, or
  inventory of lost/spurious works beyond what the argument needs;
- no independent reconstruction of Marcion, Praxeas, Hermogenes, Valentinians,
  or of Montanism beyond Eusebius's located dossier;
- no Vetus Latina / biblical-text analysis of Tertullian's citations;
- no archaeological, epigraphic, relic, or cult research beyond the recorded
  negative searches;
- no whole-corpus census of gendered or adversarial rhetoric — the contested
  pages are treated at identified loci;
- no ecclesiastical, canonical, or independent rights judgment.

## Evidence classes

A (Tertullian's works, as advocacy), N (Scillitan Acts, Passio Perpetuae),
E (Lactantius, Eusebius, Jerome, Augustine, Vincent), L (Praedestinatus,
Gelasian Decree, later portraits), M (Catechism, papal catechesis, limited to

Defined in the publication's terminal appendix and used consistently across
the four research records.

## Material uncertainties

- birth year/place, family, education route, conversion date and
  circumstances, baptism, household beyond the addressed wife;
- Jerome's presbyterate versus lay self-placement (*Exhortation to Chastity*
  7; *On Monogamy* 12); centurion father (Barnes: textual corruption); jurist
  identification (rejected by Barnes; not asserted);
- Roman residence; Greek works' content; relative chronology of most works;
- the stages and institutional character of New Prophecy adherence; the
  bishops of *Against Praxeas* 1 and *On Modesty* 1; any formal breach;
- death date/place/manner, final communion, burial; continuity (if any)
  between Tertullian and the fourth-century Tertullianistae;
- authorship/editorship of the *Passion of Perpetua* (not asserted in the
  publication).

## Rights boundary

translations and other public-domain translations identified in the
References. Muller's 1956 Augustine translation and the Praedestinatus

is used within ordinary scholarly quotation. No images are reproduced. Online
access is not treated as a reuse license.
