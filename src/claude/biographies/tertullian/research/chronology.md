# Tertullian — chronology record

Provider: Anthropic Claude. All web checks 2026-07-24. Evidence classes as in
the publication appendix (A/N/E/L/M/K/S).

Dates are claims. This record states, for each event, the source basis
actually checked, competing views, and the confidence used in the publication.

## Anchors (checked at the source)

1. **180 Jul 17 — Scillitan martyrs tried at Carthage.** Basis: consular date
   in the opening line of the *Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs* ("When Praesens,
   for the second time, and Claudianus were the consuls, on the seventeenth
   day of July, at Carthage…"), checked at
   earlychristianwritings.com/text/scillitan.html (N). Status: secure.
   Function: earliest dated African Christian document; setting, not an event
   in Tertullian's life.

2. **203 — Perpetua and Felicity die at Carthage.** Basis: Passio tradition
   (N/E); the year 203 is the standard scholarly dating, not verified from an
   internal consular date in this research; the day 7 March is the traditional
   feast date. Tertullian's citation of Perpetua checked at *On the Soul* 55
   (A; CCEL anf03.iv.xi.lv.html). Status: year secure in scholarship; day
   traditional; marked accordingly in the publication table.

3. **207/208 — *Against Marcion* book 1 (third edition) in progress.** Basis:
   Tertullian dates Marcion's activity "up to the fifteenth of the Emperor
   Severus" (*Against Marcion* 1.15, checked at newadvent.org/fathers/03121.htm)
   (A). Severus's dies imperii 193 ⇒ fifteenth year 207/208. Status: secure
   (the one absolute self-date in the corpus). The third-edition preface
   (1.1) checked at the same page.

4. **212, after 14 Aug — *To Scapula*.** Basis: the darkened sun "in the
   metropolis of Utica" (*To Scapula* 3, checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/0305.htm) (A), commonly identified with the eclipse
   visible in Africa on 14 August 212 (K: standard scholarship; the
   astronomical identification was not independently recomputed). Scapula's
   proconsulship c. 212–213 (K). Status: plausible–secure.

## Reconstructed or conventional dates

5. **c. 155–170 — birth.** No ancient evidence. Modern estimates: traditional
   c. 155–160 (long career, Jerome's old age); Barnes c. 170 (compressed
   career 196–212, death soon after, "perhaps a little more than forty" —
   reported in Frend, CR 24 (1974) 72–76, checked at
   tertullian.org/articles/frend_on_barnes.htm) (K). Publication uses the
   range only.

6. **before c. 195 — conversion.** Event certain from the convert's voice
   (e.g. *On Repentance* 4 "O sinner, like myself", checked at
   newadvent.org/fathers/0320.htm) and from the pagan-past assumption
   throughout (A). No date, place, motive, or baptism record exists; the
   customary "c. 193–195" is inference from the start of the datable works.
   Publication: event secure, details unknown.

7. **c. 196/197 — *To the Nations* and *Apology*.** Basis: internal allusions
   to the Severan civil wars; standard scholarly dating (K), stated as such
   in the publication ("generally placed in 197"); not independently
   re-derived. Status: plausible.

8. **c. 205–212+ — New Prophecy adherence visible; disciplinary works.**
   Basis: self-alignment in *On Fasting* 1, *On Monogamy* 1, *Against
   Praxeas* 1 (all checked; A). No dated "Montanist conversion" exists in any
   source; the publication asserts a tendency across the later corpus, not a
   dated event. Ordering of the late disciplinary works is disputed and not
   asserted.

9. **211 — the soldier's crown incident (*The Chaplet* 1).** Basis: "very
   lately… while the bounty of our most excellent emperors was dispensed in
   the camp" (checked at newadvent.org/fathers/0304.htm) (A); the link to the
   donative of Caracalla and Geta after Severus's death (Feb 211) is standard
   scholarship (K). Status: plausible.

10. **after 212 — later works, lost writings, old age.** Basis: Jerome, *On
    Illustrious Men* 53 ("He is said to have lived to a decrepit old age";
    six books *On Ecstasy* plus a seventh against Apollonius, lost), checked
    at newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm (E, hearsay marked by Jerome himself).
    Status: unresolved.

11. **c. 220/225 — death (convention only).** No ancient witness gives year,
    place, cause, burial, or final communion. The customary date splices the
    end of the datable works (212) with Jerome's old-age report. Barnes's
    alternative (death not long after 212) equally possible (K). Publication
    presents both and asserts neither.

## Posthumous

12. **Late 4th c. — Tertullianists attested.** Augustine, *On Heresies* 86
    (written 428; E for his own time), checked in paraphrase against the
    Latin/English presentation at tertullian.org/tertullianistae/
    de_haeresibus.htm; *Praedestinatus* 86 (c. 430s; L, unreliable) adds a
    minister in Rome under Maximus (383–388) and the Carthage basilica used
    "until Bishop Aurelius" (tertullian.org/tertullianistae/praedestinatus.htm).

13. **c. 390s–420s — last Tertullianists rejoin the Catholic Church at
    Carthage, surrendering their basilica.** Basis: Augustine, *On Heresies*
    86 (E, contemporary). Status: secure. Exact year unrecorded.

14. **392/393 — Jerome's notice written** (*De viris illustribus*); **428 —
    Augustine's *De haeresibus*;** dates are standard for those works (K) and
    used only to class the witnesses as late.

## Competing chronologies preserved

- Long life (birth c. 155–160, death c. 220/225+, Jerome's old age credited)
  versus Barnes's short career (born c. 170, wrote 196–212, died soon after).
  The publication keeps both; no harmonization.
- Traditional "definitive separation from the Church c. 211" (Chapman 1912,
  checked at newadvent.org/cathen/14520c.htm) versus the modern view that no
  datable separation event exists (Powell 1975; Rankin 1995). The publication
  treats the separation date as unfounded.
