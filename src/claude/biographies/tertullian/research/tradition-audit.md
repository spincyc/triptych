# Tertullian — tradition audit

Provider: Anthropic Claude. Web checks 2026-07-24. This record reviews
received traditions about Tertullian: earliest located witness, later
reception, historical status, and the treatment adopted in the publication.

## 1. "Presbyter of Carthage"

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 53 (c. 392/393):
  "Tertullian the presbyter… was presbyter of the church until middle life"
  (checked, newadvent.org/fathers/2708.htm). Repeated by *Praedestinatus* 86.
- **Counter-evidence:** Tertullian's lay self-placement: "Are not even we
  laics priests?… where three are, a church is, albeit they be laics" (*On
  Exhortation to Chastity* 7, checked) and the lay "we" of *On Monogamy* 12
  (checked).
- **Status:** disputed; most modern scholarship (Barnes and others) holds he
  was a layman. Publication reports both and does not call him a priest.

## 2. "Son of a proconsular centurion"

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome, ibid. ("son of a proconsul or Centurion" in
  the NPNF rendering).
- **Modern critique:** Barnes: "Tertullian's father was not a centurion;
  there never was a *centurio proconsularis*" — the office is an elaboration
  of a corrupt reading (reported in Frend's review, checked). Frend cautions
  against total dismissal of Jerome.
- **Status:** unverifiable; treated as tradition under review, not fact.

## 3. "Trained lawyer / the jurist Tertullianus of the Digest"

- **Earliest witness:** Eusebius, HE 2.2.4: "a man well versed in the laws of
  the Romans… one of those especially distinguished in Rome" (checked).
- **Modern critique:** the identification with the Severan jurist excerpted
  in the *Digest* is rejected by Barnes (reported in reviews, checked);
  Eusebius's description may itself be an inference from the *Apology*'s
  forensic manner.
- **Status:** legal training plausible from the works' texture; professional
  advocacy and the Digest identity unverified. Publication says "forensic
  mind," asserts no bar career.

## 4. Cyprian's "Give me the master" (*Da magistrum*)

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome, ibid., on the testimony of the aged Paul of
  Concordia, formerly acquainted with Cyprian's secretary (checked).
- **Status:** anecdote at two removes, c. 140 years after Cyprian; consistent
  with Cyprian's demonstrable literary debts. Publication quotes it as
  Jerome's report, not as a verified saying of Cyprian.

## 5. "Driven to Montanism by the envy and abuse of the Roman clergy"

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome, ibid.
- **Modern critique:** explains nothing in the African evidence; rejected by
  Barnes (reported in Frend, checked); no earlier source connects the
  adherence to Roman clerical conflict.
- **Status:** unsupported aetiology; reported as Jerome's claim only.

## 6. "He seceded from the Church and founded the Tertullianists"

- **Earliest witnesses:** Jerome ("lapsed to the doctrine of Montanus…
  composed, moreover, directly against the church" — doctrinal, not
  juridical); Augustine, *De haeresibus* 86 (428): joined the Cataphrygians,
  then formed his own congregations; remnant reconciled at Carthage in
  Augustine's time (checked in paraphrase).
- **Contemporary record:** *Against Praxeas* 1 "we… withdrew from the
  carnally-minded" (checked); *On the Soul* 9 shows his circle inside the
  common Sunday liturgy (checked); no excommunication, synodal act, rival
  bishop, altar, or ordination anywhere attested.
- **Modern reassessment:** Powell 1975 (no organizational break; "Nowhere
  does he refer to Montanist bishops…", checked at tertullian.org); Rankin
  1995 (probably never left; ecclesiola in ecclesia; publisher description
  checked); Barnes (Tertullianistae = African Montanists); Tabbernee
  (memory-circle hypothesis; survey checked at
  tertullian.org/tertullianistae/tertullianistae.htm).
- **Status:** estrangement certain; formal personal schism unresolved and
  currently doubted. Publication devotes a section to the disagreement and
  resolves nothing.

## 7. "Died c. 220/225 in extreme old age"

- **Earliest witness:** Jerome, ibid., expressly as hearsay ("He is said…").
- **Status:** no death record of any kind (date, place, manner, burial,
  grave, relics). "c. 220/225" is a modern convention; Barnes's short
  chronology equally possible. Publication labels the convention as such and
  records the total silence.

## 8. Cult, feast, relics, images

- **Search:** no feast, cult, canonization, relic, grave, or liturgical
  commemoration located in any witness consulted (ancient notices, Chapman
  1912, Benedict XVI 2007, general searches 2026-07-24). Benedict XVI's
  catechesis and CCC citations are reception of texts, not cult.
- **Status:** negative result, bounded and correctable; stated in the
  publication as such. Iconographic tradition was not researched item-level
  and no image claim is made.

## 9. "Father of Latin theology / first Latin Christian writer"

- **Reception:** Jerome ranks him first of the Latins after (i.e. excepting)
  Victor and Apollonius (checked); Lactantius's stylistic verdict (checked);
  Benedict XVI's catechesis presents him as the beginner of Christian Latin
  literature (checked, paraphrased).
- **Status:** received characterization, qualified in the publication: the
  Scillitan Acts and Latin Bible versions precede him; "creator of Christian
  Latin literature" is used with the near-single-handed qualifier, and the
  Minucius Felix priority question is not adjudicated (not raised in the
  prose; see negative results in source-audit).

## 10. Famous tags

- "The blood of Christians is seed" (*Apology* 50) — checked; often
  misquoted as "…seed of the Church"; publication quotes ANF wording.
- "Credo quia absurdum" — **not in Tertullian in that form**; the actual text
  (*On the Flesh of Christ* 5) reads differently. Not used in the
  publication; recorded here so its absence is deliberate. (Locus not
  re-checked online in this research; the tag is therefore simply not
  quoted at all.)
- "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?" (*Prescription* 7) — checked;
  publication warns against reading it as a ban on philosophy.
- "The flesh is the hinge of salvation" (*caro salutis est cardo*, *De
  resurrectione* 8) — ANF chapter checked; the famous English tag is a
  modern rendering, used in the conclusion as paraphrase, with the ANF
  sentence quoted in the body.
- "You are the devil's gateway" (*On the Apparel of Women* 1.1) — checked;
  treated as contested legacy, neither suppressed nor excused.
