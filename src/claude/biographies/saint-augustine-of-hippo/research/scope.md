## Reader and governing question

A serious general reader, student, or researcher who wants one coherent,
evidence-controlled life. Governing questions: (1) what can be established
about a life whose principal witness is the subject's own retrospective
prayer (the Confessions) plus one devoted eyewitness (Possidius), once
self-presentation, external witness, and later legend are kept distinct;
(2) what the two African controversies actually cost and settled — the
Donatist war including Augustine's self-documented change of mind on
coercion (Ep. 93), and the Pelagian war on grace and original sin; (3) how
the man became the memory — relics to Pavia, Doctor of Grace, medieval and
Reformation appropriations, modern scholarship (Brown at record level).

## Thesis and boundary

The best-documented conversion in ancient literature belongs to a witness
who is also its subject; the Church received both the doctor and his own
published self-corrections (Retractationes), canonizing the Doctor of Grace
without canonizing every inference of his hardest pages or erasing the
coercion he defended. Historical synthesis only: no doctrinal adjudication,
no relic authentication, no miracle certification, no rights or specialist
review claimed.

## Excluded or bounded

- no complete intellectual history or works catalogue; sermons, most
  letters, and the Divjak/Dolbeau discoveries at reported level only;
- no textual history of the Rule of St. Augustine; no verdict on 395 vs 396;
- no archaeological or scientific authentication of relics (Hippo, Pavia,
  Ostia/Rome); no miracle certification;
- no adjudication of Donatist or Pelagian theology, predestination, or the
  later grace controversies (Orange, Jansenism) beyond bounded mention;
- no ethnic-identity claims (Berber/Punic/Roman descent left open);
- no assumption that later Augustinian institutes were founded by Augustine;
- no ecclesiastical, canonical, or independent rights judgment.

## Material uncertainties

- the concubine's name (no ancient witness); Adeodatus's death date;
- the garden scene's descriptive detail (single retrospective witness;
  scholarly debate preserved);
- the consecration year 395 vs 396; the exact dates of most works, sermons,
  and letters;
- the Monnica/Monica spelling and Monica's family background;
- Cassiciacum's exact site;
- the Donatist and Pelagian movements' self-understanding (archives lost or
  hostile);
- the relic chain 430–Sardinia–Pavia before the eighth century; current
  state of the Pavia and Rome shrines beyond the cited official texts;
- Augustine's descent and the reach of Punic/Berber culture in his
  formation.
