# Saint Paul — research scope

Provider: Anthropic Claude.

## Publication identity

- **Publication:** *Saint Paul: The Least of the Apostles*
- **Provider / collection / leaf:** `claude / biographies / saint-paul`
- **Genre:** source-first historical and hagiographic biography
- **Subject:** Paul the Apostle (Saul of Tarsus per Acts), Jew of Benjamin,
  Pharisee, persecutor, apostle by revelation, letter-writer, and martyr
- **Primary geography:** Tarsus and Jerusalem (Acts); Damascus, Arabia
  (Nabataea), Syria and Cilicia, Antioch, Cyprus, southern Asia Minor,
  Macedonia, Achaia, Ephesus/Asia, Caesarea, Malta, Rome; Spain as stated
  intention only
- **Primary life period:** first century AD; birth conventionally c. AD 8
  (Pauline-Year convention); death at Rome under Nero between 64 and 68
- **Reception period:** first century through the research cutoff
- **Language:** English; Greek/Latin tags only where they clarify evidence
- **Research and mutable-fact cutoff:** 2026-07-25
- **Governing guidance:** `guidance/editorial.md`, `guidance/repository.md`,
  `guidance/biographies.md`, `guidance/sources.md`

This Claude edition was researched and written independently of the sibling
GPT edition; per the independence rule, the only file consulted under
`src/gpt/biographies/saint-paul/` was `research/scope.md`, for topic parity
only. No GPT sections, chronology, tradition audit, or prose were read.

Saint Peter has his own publication in this collection; the Antioch
incident, the council, the paired Roman martyrdom tradition, and the joint
feast are treated here strictly from Paul's evidentiary horizon.

## Controlling question

What do the seven undisputed letters establish about Paul's life; what does
Acts add, and where must its narrative be held apart from the letters; and
what can responsibly be said about the martyrdom, tomb, legends, feasts,
and theological legacy, at what actual evidential ceiling?

The publication is a study aid for serious general readers, students,
clergy, and researchers. It is not a critical edition, a Pauline theology,
an archaeological or scientific authentication, or a substitute for
magisterial teaching.

## Required coverage delivered

Sources and weighing (seven letters / disputed letters with the honest
authorship spread / Acts with its tensions worked: Jerusalem visits,
Damascus escape, emphasis and omission, apostle-title and citizenship);
Pharisaic formation and the persecutor (Gal 1/Phil 3 vs Acts); the
Damascus event with the three Acts accounts tabulated and Paul's own

with a rendered grading table; reception (canon, Chrysostom, feasts,
Pauline Year, bounded theological legacy); conclusion; terminal apparatus
with evidence key and full per-event chronology; references.

## Controlling conclusions

- The seven undisputed letters are the documentary core; every
  autobiographical passage is also an argument and is used as testimony.
- Acts is an independent, near-contemporary, designed narrative; it never
  mentions Paul's letters; where it conflicts with the letters the
  participant outranks the narrator (Jerusalem visits; Damascus pursuers).
- The one absolute date is the Gallio anchor (proconsulship from summer
  51, or 52–53 at the outside), carried by the Delphi inscription as
  studied by Deissmann and reported by Benedict XVI; all other dates are
  reconstructions and are labeled.
- Martyrdom at Rome under Nero (64–68) is morally certain on the E-chain
  from 1 Clement 5; beheading is early (Acts of Paul c. 160; Tertullian
  c. 200) but not contemporary; year and day are not fixed; "same day as
  Peter" is calendar piety.
- Spain: intention certain (Rom 15), execution unproven; 1 Clement's
  "extreme limit of the west" is ambiguous; the Muratorian fragment
  assumes the journey; release/second arrest is a coherent, unverifiable
  tradition — asserted neither way.
- The Ostian grave tradition is early (Gaius c. 200), unrivaled, and
  continuous; the 2009 probe results are consistent with, but cannot
  prove, identity; the pope's own ceiling ("would seem to confirm") is
  preserved.
- Thecla, the physical description, the milk, Tre Fontane, and the Seneca
  letters are graded L and never passed backward as biography.

## Exclusions and restraint

- No invented scenes, dialogue, psychology, or continuous itinerary
  beyond Acts' framework labeled as such; no harmonization of the three
  Damascus accounts; no use of Acts speeches as transcripts.
- No birth-year precision; no medical diagnosis of the thorn; no claim
  that Tarsian origin proves philosophical schooling.
- No assertion of Spain, release, or a second imprisonment as fact; no
  use of the Pastorals as sole support for any biographical claim.
- No forensic authentication (or debunking) of the sarcophagus remains.
- No adjudication of confessional justification debates or of the
  reception history of the difficult texts (Israel, women, slavery),
  which is named and marked out of scope.
- No images.

## Live uncertainties requiring review

- Crucifixion year (30/33) and the concurrent-vs-consecutive reading of
  Gal 1–2's year-counts.
- Gal 2 = Acts 15 vs Acts 11; the famine visit's place; the decree's
  relation to Paul's silence about it.
- Galatians' destination (north/south) and date; 2 Corinthians' unity;
  provenance of Philippians/Philemon (Ephesus? Caesarea? Rome?).
- Authorship and settings of the six disputed letters.
- Festus's accession year; the absolute placement of Caesarea–Rome.
- Release, Spain, second arrest; exact year and day of death.
- Critical text of 1 Clement 5's western phrase; the Gallio inscription's
  current critical edition; the sarcophagus laboratory record.
