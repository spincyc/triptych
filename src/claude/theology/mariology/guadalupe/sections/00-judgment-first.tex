\section{What the Church Has Actually Done}

A study of Guadalupe must begin where a reader's reliance actually
rests: not with the story, and not with the arguments about the story,
but with the acts of competent ecclesiastical authority---their exact
wording, date, author, and object. Those acts are fewer, later, and
more carefully worded than the devotional literature suggests, and
they are also more substantial than the sceptical literature
sometimes allows. Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.

\subsection{The fullest act: the beatification of 6 May 1990}

The single most informative document the Holy See has issued on the
subject is the apostolic letter by which John Paul II granted
liturgical cult to Blessed Juan Diego. It is worth reading closely,
because it shows exactly how Rome speaks about Tepeyac when it is
speaking juridically.

\begin{operativetext}{John Paul II, apostolic letter \term{Ad
perpetuam rei memoriam} \work{Cultus liturgicus in honorem Beati
Ioannis Didaci conceditur}, Mexico City, 6 May 1990, extracts (Latin,
as printed in \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis})}
\ldots\ Ioannes Didacus, vir humilis ex Indorum stirpe, tamquam
instrumentum electus est ut, in honorem Beatae Mariae Virginis de
Guadalupe, templum aedificantur [sic]\ldots\par
Uti scripta ferunt, his aliisque similibus Ioannes Didacus usus est
verbis, cum, mense Decembri, anno MDXXXI, Deipara Virgo Maria mirum
in modum sese videndam ei praebuit in colle Tepeiacensi.\par
Haud multae sunt notitiae quae, usque ad annum MDXXXI, de Ioanne
Didaco ad nos pervenerunt\ldots\par
\ldots\ Positio concinnata est super Servi Dei fama sanctitatis,
virtutibus et cultu ab immemorabili eidem praestito\ldots\par
\ldots\ concedimus, ut memoria liturgica Ioannis Didaci tamquam
Beati --- obligatoria in Mexicana archidioecesi, ad libitum vero in
aliis Americae Latinae dioecesibus --- quotannis die~ix Decembris tum
in actione Eucharistica tum in Liturgia Horarum celebrari
possit.\endnote{John Paul II, \work{Cultus liturgicus in honorem
Beati Ioannis Didaci conceditur}, \work{Acta Apostolicae Sedis} 82
(1990), pp.~853--855, read 2026-07-25 in the Holy See's OCR scan
(\url{https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-82-1990-ocr.pdf}),
pages located and read at first hand. The bracketed \emph{sic} marks
the reading of the scan at that word. Ellipses mark omissions.
Editorially: ``\ldots Juan Diego, a humble man of the Indian people,
was chosen as an instrument so that a temple might be built in honour
of Blessed Mary the Virgin of Guadalupe\ldots\ As the writings
relate, Juan Diego used these and similar words when, in the month of
December in the year 1531, the Virgin Mary Mother of God showed
herself to him in a wondrous manner on the hill of
Tepeyac\ldots\ Not many notices concerning Juan Diego down to the
year 1531 have come to us\ldots\ A \term{Positio} was drawn up on the

cult rendered to him from time immemorial\ldots\ We grant that the

Eucharistic action and in the Liturgy of the Hours.'' The letter also
quotes the \work{Nican mopohua} and the \work{Nican motecpana} by
name, in Latin rendering, as the sources of Juan Diego's words and of
the account of his eremitical life. Quoted within ordinary scholarly
limits with citation.}
\end{operativetext}

Five things in that text should be fixed in a reader's mind before
anything else in this study.

\emph{The narrative is used, and its register is marked.}
\term{Uti scripta ferunt}---``as the writings relate.'' The Holy See
narrates the apparition and simultaneously signals that it is
narrating what the writings say. The same reserve appears again a
paragraph later in \term{fertur}, ``he is said to have,'' of Juan
Diego's continence.

\emph{The documentary poverty is admitted.} \term{Haud multae sunt
notitiae}: not many notices have reached us. This is the Holy See
itself, in the operative act, acknowledging the problem that \S3 will
set out in detail.

\emph{The juridical ground is cult, not fact.} The \term{Positio} was
on the reputation for sanctity, the virtues, and the cult rendered
\term{ab immemorabili}. The act is an equipollent beatification: the
Pope confirms an immemorial cult.

\emph{The grant is liturgical and bounded.} A memorial on 9 December,
obligatory in one archdiocese and optional elsewhere in Latin
America. Nothing more is granted.

\emph{Nothing is declared about the events.} There is no clause
determining that the apparitions occurred, and none certifying the
image.

\subsection{The act that does not exist}

No act of any competent authority determining that the events of
December 1531 occurred, or that they are of supernatural origin, was
located by this study. The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith's
documents index contains nothing concerning Guadalupe, Tepeyac or
Juan Diego, although it does carry determinations concerning other
alleged phenomena (\S9.3). The nearest approaches in the record are
two, and they are exactly as far as they go: a liturgical formula of
1894 asserting an ancient and constant \emph{tradition}, and a
provincial council's edict of 1896 stating that the apparition,
\emph{without being a dogma of faith}, is such a tradition.

That is a bounded negative result, correctable by better evidence,
and it is not a finding that the events were judged not to have
occurred. It is a finding about what the record contains.

\studythesis{This is an ecclesial-judgment-first study of Guadalupe,
not a devotional narrative and not a verdict. It establishes what
competent authority has actually done and in what words; it lays out
the documentary strata in the order in which they can be dated,
beginning from the printed witnesses of 1648 and 1649 and working
back; it states the silence problem and the controversy of 1556 from
the sources, including the one moment in the sixteenth century when
the origin of the image was formally at issue; it presents the
historicity dispute as the live scholarly question it is, with each
side stated at its strongest and each named position reported only at
the level at which it was actually checked; it separates what the
beatification and canonization of Juan Diego establish from what they
do not; it binds every claim about the image to a source that was
reached, and records as negative findings the popular claims that
could not be traced; it reads the Nahuatl text's own rhetoric from
the 1649 imprint; and it states what all of this obliges. Public
Revelation is complete in Christ. No event at Tepeyac adds to it, and
even a positively received private revelation asks a prudent human
assent (\term{fides humana}), never the divine and Catholic faith owed
to Revelation. Christ alone is Redeemer and mediator; the Guadalupe
narrative's own self-identification of the apparition subordinates her
to him, and this study keeps that subordination visible. Where the
record is thinner than the received story---and in places it is very
much thinner---the ceiling is stated rather than papered over; and
where the reception is heavier than sceptical summaries allow, that
is stated too.}

\subsection{How the study runs}

In the order the evidence demands: the documentary strata and what
each witness can carry (\S2); the silence problem and the
\work{Informaci\'on} of 1556 (\S3); the historicity dispute stated at
strength on both sides (\S4); Juan Diego's cause, the objections of
the 1990s, and the canonization (\S5); the image and what its
documented examinations establish, with a traceability audit of the
popular claims (\S6); the Nahuatl text's rhetoric and Indigenous
agency (\S7); liturgical and magisterial reception from 1555 to the
present (\S8); and what all of it obliges under the doctrinal frame
and the current norms (\S9). Work-wide bounds, taxonomy, and
qualifications are gathered in the terminal appendix; witnesses and
rights are in the References.
