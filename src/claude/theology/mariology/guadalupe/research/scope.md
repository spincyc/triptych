## Reader and governing question

A serious Catholic reader who wants devotion, historical criticism,
Indigenous dignity, and obedience to ecclesial authority held
together, and who does not want to be told less than the truth in
either direction.

Governing question: what has competent ecclesiastical authority
actually done about Guadalupe, in what words and with what object;
what will the surviving documentary record carry; how does the
historicity dispute stand among the scholars who conduct it; what do
the beatification and canonization of Juan Diego establish; what do
the documented examinations of the image show; how does the Nahuatl
narrative read as Nahuatl; and what does any of it oblige?

## Thesis

Two statements are true at once and must be held together. (1) The
Church's reception of Guadalupe is extraordinary in weight: a papal
confirmation of national patronage (1754), a proper Office and Mass, a
canonical coronation (1895), basilica dignity transferred to a new
shrine (1976), an equipollent beatification (1990) and canonization
(2002) of the reported seer, a continental feast of the rank of
*festum* (1999), inscription in the General Roman Calendar (2008), and
four papal pilgrimages to Tepeyac. (2) No act of any competent
authority determining that the events of December 1531 occurred, or
that they are of supernatural origin, was located. The nearest
approaches are a liturgical formula of 1894 asserting an ancient and
constant *tradition*, a provincial council's edict of 1896 stating
that the apparition is such a tradition *without being a dogma of
faith*, and the beatification letter of 1990 which narrates the event
as "the writings relate" and states in its own text that "not many
notices" about Juan Diego have come down to us.

The historicity question is therefore genuinely open, is disputed
among competent Catholic scholars on historical rather than
theological grounds, and is not adjudicated here.

## Material discrepancies and limits preserved

- **"Non est equidem."** The incipit and the year 1754 are attested
  only in the 1785 Guadalupan compilation; the Latin text was not
  reached; the day of the month is garbled in the OCR read. In the one
  accessible Mechelen *Bullarium* volume whose index lists that
  incipit, the surrounding constitutions are of 1756 and the volume
  contains no Mexican material. Recorded as an anomaly; nothing about
  the brief's wording is asserted, and the Psalm 147 *Non fecit
  taliter* association is not asserted.
- **1887, 1894, 1895 acts.** Searched for directly in *ASS* 19, 20, 26
  and 27; not found. Reported from O'Gorman.
- **1910 patronage of Latin America.** Only the SRC agenda item of 16
  August 1910 is published (*AAS* 2 (1910) 651); the resulting act is
  absent from *AAS* 1910 and 1911. The commonly cited 24 August 1910
  is not verified.
- **Pius XII 1946.** *AAS* 38 (1946) contains no Guadalupan act; the
  "Patroness of the Americas" attribution to 1946 is not asserted.
- **Canonization date.** The consistorial decree at *AAS* 94 (2002)
  313–314 assigns Juan Diego's canonization to 30 July 2002; the same
  volume's index and the dated homily give 31 July 2002. The
  discrepancy is recorded, not harmonized.
- **No Latin canonization formula** for Juan Diego was located in *AAS*
  94 (2002).
- **General Roman Calendar rank.** The 2008 *Variationes* add the 9 and
  12 December entries with no rank qualifier; no rank is asserted.
- **Nahuatl orthography.** The 1649 imprint's cedillas, grave accents
  and the contraction *inniçéquizca* are reproduced or expanded only
  from the imprint's own convention, with the expansion recorded.
- **O'Gorman's Latin variants.** He prints *antiqua et constante
  traditio ducet*; the standard formula is *antiqua et constans
  traditio docet*. Both are shown.
