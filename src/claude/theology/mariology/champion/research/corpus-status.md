# Champion — corpus and status record (controlling inventory)

Provider: Anthropic Claude. This record is the controlling inventory
required by the apparition-judgment addendum of
`guidance/theology/mariology.md` for `theology/mariology/champion`. It
is updated before any narrative status change in the publication.

## Corpus definition

Single-event study. The included event corpus is exactly one dossier:
the events, apparitions, and locutions reported by Adele Brise at
Robinsonville (now Champion), Brown County, Wisconsin, in or about
October 1859. The judged corpus is the 2010 decree's own object: "the
events, apparitions and locutions given to Adele Brise in October of
1859." No day of the month, number of appearances, or sequence is part
of the judged corpus, because the decree fixes none.

No "all approved apparitions" claim is made anywhere; no master
registry is asserted; the publication expressly declines to evaluate
any superlative about American apparition sites, on the ground that
such a claim would require a reproducible register that does not exist.

Related matters inside the narrative scope but **outside** the judged
object, and marked as such wherever they appear: the preservation of
the shrine enclosure in the fires of 8 October 1871; every reported
healing or favour; the national-shrine title; the change of devotional
title and the annual solemnity; and the cause of canonization of Adele
Brise.

## Status terms used

- *do exhibit the substance of supernatural character* — the 2010
  act's own formula. Legacy positive diocesan judgment made under the
  1978 Norms. **Not relabeled under the 2024 taxonomy**, and expressly
  not called a *Nihil obstat*.
- *worthy of belief (although not obligatory)* — the 2010 act's own
  formula for the effect. The parenthesis is the decree's.
- *pro nunc nihil obstare* — the 1978 Norms' intermediate formula,
  named only to describe the regime; never applied to Champion as a
  status.
- *Nihil obstat*, *Prae oculis habeatur*, *Curatur*, *Sub mandato*,
  *Prohibetur et obstruatur*, *Declaratio de non supernaturalitate* —
  the six conclusions of the 2024 Norms, named for completeness. **None
  of them has been applied to Champion by any competent authority, and
  the publication applies none.**
- *national shrine* — CIC (1983) can. 1231: a title of the place
  approved by the conference of bishops. Not an apparition judgment.
- *Servant of God* — a cause of canonization has been introduced.
  Nothing more; and by DDF *Norms* (2024) n. 13, nothing about
  supernatural phenomena in the person's life.
- *reported* — default register for all words and phenomena attributed
  to the apparition.
- *received custodial narrative* — the National Shrine's current
  published account; a custodial statement, never an independent
  historical witness.

## Event dossier (as of 2026-07-25)

- **Stable identity:** the reported apparitions to Adele Brise,
  Robinsonville / Champion, Wisconsin, October 1859; devotion formerly
  titled Our Lady of Good Help, since 2023 Our Lady of Champion.
- **Jurisdiction:** Diocese of Green Bay, Latin Church, United States
  (established 1868; Ricken the twelfth bishop, per the diocese's own
  list). The jurisdiction competent in 1859 is not established here.
- **Claimed type:** apparitions with locutions, three encounters in the
  received narrative, the first silent.
- **Recipient:** Adele Brise (custodial dates: born Dion-le-Val,
  Belgium, 30 January 1831; died Champion, 5 July 1896). Illiterate;
  left no writing; no deposition exists.
- **Witness corpus:** no contemporary witness of any kind was located.
  The earliest published narrative located is Starr's
  (copyright-entered 1871); the second is Pernin's (1874).
- **Authority, office, act, date, language, locus, source:** David L.
  Ricken, Bishop of Green Bay; decree entitled *Decree on the
  Authenticity of the Apparitions of 1859 at the Shrine of Our Lady of
  Good Help*; 8 December 2010; English; operative clause at p. 4 of
  four; read in the signed PDF published by the National Shrine, with
  p. 4 read as a page image.
- **Procedural regime:** CDF *Norms* of 25 February 1978, then in
  force. Whether the Holy See authorized or reviewed the act cannot be
  determined; the 2024 presentation records that bishops who obtained
  such authorization in that period were asked not to mention it.
- **Exact object judged:** the events, apparitions, and locutions of
  October 1859. Nothing else.
- **Excluded from the object:** any day, count, sequence, or setting;
  any description of the figure; any wording of the message; any
  miracle, including the 1871 preservation; the sanctity of Adele
  Brise.
- **Later acts located:** national-shrine designation (reported 19
  March 2016, USCCB; conference act not reached); change of title and
  inaugural annual solemnity 9 October 2023 (shrine attributes word
  from the Dicastery for Divine Worship; instruments not reached);
  introduction of the cause of Adele Brise (dated acts not reached).
- **Current controlling status:** the 2010 decree, unmodified. No act
  of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith concerning Champion,
  before or after 2010, was located. The DDF *Norms* of 17 May 2024
  replaced the 1978 Norms entirely (art. 27) but contain **no
  transitional provision** on concluded cases.
- **What the current status does not establish:** obligation of belief;
  historical accuracy of any narrative detail; the authenticity of any
  transmitted wording; any miracle — the decree states that none of the
  reported favours has been officially declared a miracle.

## Checked reported-message themes and cautions

- **Prayer for the conversion of sinners; general confession;
  Communion offered for sinners.** In the received narrative and quoted
  in the decree; not located in either nineteenth-century witness.
- **Warning of punishment.** In the received narrative; **not** quoted
  in the decree (its ellipsis passes over it); not located in either
  nineteenth-century witness. Read in the publication as a conditional
  scriptural summons to repentance, never as a portrait of Mary
  restraining an angry Son, and never as a prediction of the 1871
  fires.
- **Self-identification as Queen of Heaven.** In the received narrative
  and quoted in the decree; not located in either nineteenth-century
  witness, in both of which the identification is made by the seer.
  Read within *Lumen gentium* 60–62: a queen who prays is a creature
  interceding.
- **Catechetical commission.** The stable core; earliest located form
  is Starr's 1871 English, which the received text reproduces almost
  verbatim.
- **"Go and fear nothing, I will help you."** Earliest located form is
  Pernin's 1874 English; received verbatim.
- **Beatitude on the companions; vineyard rebuke.** In the received
  narrative; not quoted in the decree; not located in either
  nineteenth-century witness.
