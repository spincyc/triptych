## Governing thesis of the publication

Champion is the clearest American instance of a positively judged
private revelation whose event stratum has almost no contemporary
documentation, and that is a statement about the record rather than an
argument against the judgment. The two earliest published narratives
located (Starr, copyright-entered 1871; Pernin, 1874) disagree with
each other and with the received narrative on season, distance,
sequence, and appearance, and neither contains the self-identification
as Queen of Heaven, the command of general confession, or the warning
of punishment. The received message is a composite whose catechetical
core reproduces Starr's printed English almost verbatim and whose
closing clause reproduces Pernin's. The preservation of the enclosure
in the fires of 8 October 1871 is early and attested; the rain, the
refugee crowd, the livestock, and the well are not, and a paragraph
supplying the rain circulates under Pernin's name that is not his text.
The 2010 decree judged "the substance" of the October 1859 events and
obliges no one.

   pp. 297–301**, read in the 1883 Baltimore (John B. Piet & Co.)

3. **Peter Pernin, *The Finger of God Is There!* (Montreal: Lovell,

   the burnt district — read in a **modern reset reproduction**, not
   the 1871 imprint.
5. ***History of Northern Wisconsin* (Chicago: Western Historical
   Company, 1881)** — negative-search corpus only.
6. **The current custodial narrative** published by the National Shrine
   of Our Lady of Champion ("Our Story," "Shrine Historical Timeline,"
   "Solemnity of Our Lady of Champion"), read 2026-07-25.
7. **The signed four-page decree of Bishop David L. Ricken**, 8

   operative page read as a page image because it carries no text

8. **Procedural and doctrinal law:** CDF *Norms* of 25 February 1978;

## Exclusions

- No reconstruction of a Walloon or French original of the reported
  words; no determination of the day of the month or the number of
  appearances; no assertion about the mechanism of perception.
- No authentication of any healing, favour, crutch, relic, or
  pilgrimage story.
- No adjudication of 1871 casualty totals, acreage, fire mechanics, or
  meteorology; no claim that the Champion fire was the identical flame
  front that burned Peshtigo (the publication states the opposite on
  Tilton's and Pernin's own testimony).
- No claim that the 1859 message foretold the 1871 fires. No checked
  source makes that connection.
- No superlative about American apparition sites, and no "all approved
  apparitions" claim: this is a single-event study and asserts no
  registry.
- No use of private revelation to bind conscience, replace catechesis,
  or displace pastoral, medical, or emergency-planning judgment.

## Material disagreements deliberately preserved

- **Date.** Starr's "twelve years ago" from an 1871 copyright entry
  lands on 1859/1858; Pernin's "ten or twelve years ago" from an 1874
  imprint lands on 1862–1864. Left unreconciled.
- **Sequence.** Starr: return, then outward, then return the same
  afternoon. Pernin: three successive Sundays, all returning. Received:
  grist-mill errand, outward, return. Three incompatible sequences.
- **Distance.** Eleven miles (Starr); two and a half leagues / seven
  and a half miles (Pernin); ten miles (shrine).
- **Appearance.** Veiled (Starr); undescribed (Pernin); unveiled with
  crown, sash, and golden hair (received).
- **Identification.** Made by the seer in both nineteenth-century
  witnesses; made by the visitor in the received narrative.
- **Calendar.** 15 August as the apparition commemoration (Starr);
  two unnamed annual dates (Pernin); 9 October (received).
- **Acreage.** Six acres (Pernin, both languages); five acres (shrine
  timeline).
- **Tilton's date.** The reset reproduction prints "October 3d" for the
  east-shore firestorm, contradicting its own preface and map.

## Copyright and translation boundary

Starr (copyright-entered 1871, author d. 1901), Pernin (1874, author
d. 1909), Tilton (1871), and the 1881 *History of Northern Wisconsin*
are public domain in text; the digitized scans consulted are
third-party artifacts and are not vendored or redistributed. The 2010
decree's rights status is unresolved; it is quoted only so far as
identifying its object and effect requires. Holy See texts are quoted
within ordinary scholarly limits. Shrine and diocesan web pages are ©
their publishers and are quoted briefly with citation. No AI-generated
prayer or translation appears; the French of Pernin is quoted in the
original where the wording is doing work, with English renderings
visibly editorial. Where the English scan's optical character recognition is damaged, the
wording is taken from the page images rather than reconstructed.
