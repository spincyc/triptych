## Reader and governing question

A serious Catholic lay reader, catechist, seminarian, or study group
needing exactly what the Church has defined about Mary. Governing
question: for each of the four commonly identified Marian dogmas, what
is the defined text and locus, the defining authority and level, the
doctrinal history to the definition, the scriptural and patristic roots
honestly weighted, what the dogma does and does not assert, the common
misunderstandings, and the current catechetical framing — with the
boundary between defined dogma and other Marian teaching (including the
2025 DDF judgment on cooperation titles) kept explicit.

## Thesis

The four dogmas confess one work of God in Mary for the sake of Christ:
divine motherhood (Ephesus 431 in conciliar reception), perpetual
virginity (universal Tradition, formulated at Constantinople II and
Lateran 649), Immaculate Conception (Pius IX, 1854), and bodily
Assumption (Pius XII, 1950). Their modes of proposal differ and are not
flattened; each definition leaves identified questions open; and no
Marian title may obscure Christ's unique mediation.

## Included

- What "dogma" means (Dei Filius criterion, DS 3011), the differing
  modes of the four, and a definitions table (dogma | act and date |
  operative Latin from the identified witness | level and assent);
- a scriptural-foundations chapter treating the four recurring loci once
  at their own scale: *kecharitomene* (Lk 1:28) with the grammar and the
  translation range, Gen 3:15 with the Vulgate/Hebrew divergence stated
  from the Douay's own annotation, Rev 12's polyvalence as the
  constitutions themselves handle it, and the Cana and Cross scenes;
- per dogma: defined text quoted from an identified witness (with Latin
  where wording matters), authority and level, history to the
  definition, Scripture honestly weighted (Lk 1:43; Mt 1/Lk 1 virginal
  conception; Gen 3:15 with the *ipsa* translation care; Lk 1:28
  *kecharitomene* with translation range), patristic/scholastic
  reception, assertions and non-assertions, misunderstandings,
  catechetical framing (CCC 484–511, 963–975; LG ch. 8), and a
  development-milestone table with an evidentiary-register column;
- per-dogma doctrinal history worked as narrative with more witnesses:
  Ignatius and Irenaeus before the controversy, the *Sub tuum
  praesidium* dating dispute stated rather than omitted, the Nestorian
  controversy's course (Anastasius's sermon, Cyril's second and third
  letters with the twelve anathemas and Nestorius's counter-anathemas,
  Cassian at reported level, the deposition decree, the Formula of
  Reunion 433, Vincent of Lérins); Jerome's four arguments against
  Helvidius and the three ancient solutions to the "brothers" with their
  patrons, Leo's Tome (DS 291), and Lateran 649's Monothelite context;
  Bernard's letter, Thomas exactly from ST III.27, Scotus at reported
  level, the papal steering 1476–1661 with the *Cum praeexcelsa* dating
  puzzle, and *Ineffabilis Deus*'s own argument structure; the dormition
  traditions with the *Decretum Gelasianum*'s classification of the
  *Transitus* as apocryphal, the liturgical argument, the 1946
  *Deiparae Virginis Mariae* consultation, and *MD*'s argument structure;
- a systematic-coherence chapter: the Christological rule, the twofold
  order among the four, and Mary as eschatological icon of the Church
  (LG 63–65, 68) with the cautions that follow;
- a reception-and-ecumenism chapter drawn from the other communions' own
  documents: the 1895 Constantinopolitan encyclical, the *Smalcald
  Articles* (1537), and ARCIC's Seattle Statement with its own status
  paragraph; plus a dated currentness check on *Mater Populi Fidelis*;
- the Thomistic limit on the Immaculate Conception and the Scotist
  resolution; MD §31's own caution on Thomas and the Assumption;
- what 1950 deliberately left open (death vs. dormition);
- the boundary chapter: sinlessness, Mother of the Church, Queenship as
  authoritative non-dogmas; cooperation titles under LG 60–62 and
  *Mater Populi Fidelis* (2025), Co-redemptrix judged always
  inappropriate for constructive use (§22);
- four page-bounded synopsis cards (object, mode/authority, operative
  wording, biblical center, early witnesses, Thomistic support/limit,
  development hinge, doctrinal fruit, required assent, exclusions/open
  questions, ecumenical position).

## Excluded or bounded

- No apparitions, feasts-as-such, prayers, devotional practice, or
  iconography; no proposal or advocacy for any new dogma;
- no critical-edition work on conciliar acts (Schwartz, Riedinger,
  Tanner not collated); no verbatim patristic quotations beyond the
  identified witnesses; no anatomical reconstruction of virginitas in
  partu; no settlement of any question the Church left open;
- historical claims inside the 1854 and 1950 constitutions (Trent Sess.
  V, Sixtus IV, Alexander VII, the consultations, the homiletic and
  scholastic surveys) are reported on the constitutions' authority as
  witnesses to their cited acts, not re-verified in primary archives;
- Eastern/Protestant reception mentioned only where a dogma's history
  requires.

## Terminology controls

Dogma / authoritative doctrine / conciliar synthesis / administrative
doctrinal judgment / theological opinion / typology / apocryphal
reception are kept distinct (terminal appendix). *Theotokos*,
*aeiparthenos*, *kecharitomene* transliterated. DS numbers follow the
revised numbering as used by the CCC's apparatus; older DB numbers
recorded where the online witness uses them.

## Material uncertainties preserved

- Translator of the circulating English *Ineffabilis Deus* unidentified;
- print-edition identity of the patristica.net Latin Denzinger unknown;
  its "eam (eum?)" query at DS 503 disclosed;
- Ephesus procedural history complex, not part of the defined object;
- early silence on Mary's end; death vs. dormition open;
- cousins vs. prior-marriage readings of the "brothers" both undefined;
- *potuit, decuit, fecit* not a checked verbatim Scotus quotation;
- Sub tuum praesidium: from the 2026-07-25 deepening pass the prayer is
  used, but only as undated evidence of early invocation, with the
  palaeographic dispute (P. Ryl. III 470) quoted from Towarek, *Vox
  Patrum* 80 (2021); its English wording is quoted strictly as ARCIC's
  witness and is not offered as a devotional form;
- the Greek doctrine of ancestral sin is reported at the level of a
  widely held theological description; no Orthodox synodal act on it was
  read, and the 1895 encyclical's English is an unattributed private-site
  translation, uncollated;
- specific academic acts of the Immaculate-Conception controversy (e.g.
  a Paris faculty requirement of 1497) were not verified and are not
  asserted;
- the Ignatian recension question, the date and dependence of the
  *Transitus* literature, and the date and authorship of the *Decretum
  Gelasianum* are reported as disputed and not adjudicated.
