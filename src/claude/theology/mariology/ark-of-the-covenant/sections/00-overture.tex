\section{Overture: The Question of the Ark}

The ark of the covenant is the most famous lost object in the world,
and the modern instinct, on hearing its name, is to ask where it is.
The instinct is not foolish. The ark was a real chest of real wood,
carried by real poles on real shoulders through a landscape whose towns
can still be walked. So concrete a thing invites a concrete question,
and the guesses have been concrete accordingly: a cave on Mount Nebo, a
chamber under the temple pavement, a chapel in Aksum. This study
reports those answers, with their dates and their witnesses, and hides
none of them.

But the Bible, which watches the ark more closely than any other
source, declines to end the story that way. The historical books let
the ark vanish without an obituary. At some point no writer marks, it
is simply no longer there, and the traditions that later arose to fill
the silence disagree with one another. The New Testament names the ark
of the covenant only twice: once in the Epistle to the Hebrews, to
describe its furniture and decline to expound it; and once in the
canon's final book, to show it---not found, but seen, in the opened
temple in heaven, one verse before the great sign of the woman clothed
with the sun.\endnote{Hebrews 9:4--5; Apocalypse 11:19
and 12:1; Douay-Rheims (Challoner) numbering and naming, read at
\url{https://drbo.org/chapter/65009.htm},
\url{https://drbo.org/chapter/73011.htm}, and
\url{https://drbo.org/chapter/73012.htm}. All web loci cited in these
notes were read on 2026-08-15 unless another date is stated; full
bibliographic detail and witness identifications are in the
References.}

The canon's answer to ``where did the ark go?'' is an answer of a
different kind, because the question was of a different kind than it
looked. When a king moves his court, the antiquarian hunts for the old
throne; the courtier follows the king. The ark was never important as
furniture. It was important as the appointed meeting place of God and
Israel, and to trace it honestly is to trace not a relic but a
Presence.

\arkthesis{The ark's story is complete only when it is read forward.
The chest built at Sinai carries the Presence of God through Israel's
history, vanishes without an obituary, and is seen again in heaven at
Apocalypse 11:19, immediately before the sign of the woman clothed with
the sun. The Church---in her philology, her Fathers, her liturgy, and
her magisterium---reads the Virgin Mary as the ark of the new covenant.
This study tells the whole journey, presents that reading at its
evidence's true weight, preserves its disputes, and keeps its
devotional application under the primacy of Christ.}

Two warnings, and the road can begin. The first concerns fact.
Questions of fact---who wrote a homily, what a verse says, where a word
occurs---are settled in these pages by evidence and by nothing else: a
pseudonymous sermon is called pseudonymous even when its sentence is
beautiful, and a wrong reference is corrected even when it is
venerable. The second concerns interpretation. The critical
minimalism that reads every unfulfilled expectation as an error is a
school with premises, not a referee; and the Church reading her own
Scripture in her own worship is not a bystander to the question of what
that Scripture means. So two things will be measured throughout, and
measured separately: how anciently a reading is attested, and how the
Church has received it. A reading may be old and never received, or
received and articulated late---and late articulation is what
development looks like, not what error looks like. That is the whole
method. It will do its work from here on without being talked about.

The journey is long, and it starts where every
covenant thing starts, on the mountain where God told a freed people,
in cubits and gold, exactly how He wished to be met. Begin at Sinai.
