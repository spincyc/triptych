## Reader and governing question

A motivated adult Catholic reader without special training, and the pastors, catechists,
and serious inquirers who serve them. The governing question: where did the ark of the
covenant go, and what did God mean by it — answered as one continuous account of the
ark's canonical journey, the theology of presence and holiness it teaches, and the
Church's reading of Mary as the ark of the new covenant, ending in the Assumption.

## Thesis

The ark's story is complete only when read forward: the chest built at Sinai carries the
Presence through Israel's history, vanishes without obituary, and reappears in heaven at
Revelation 11:19, immediately before the sign of the Woman; the Church — in her
philology, her Fathers, her liturgy, and her magisterium — reads the Virgin Mary as the
ark of the new covenant, and this study presents that reading with its evidence graded
on two axes (attestation history and ecclesial reception), its disputes preserved, and
its devotional application governed by Christ's primacy.

## Method rule (owner directive, 2026-08-15)

Facts exact, framework Catholic, labels honest. Questions of fact (authorship, wording,
concordance counts, loci) are settled by evidence, and pseudonymous attributions are
flagged plainly. Questions of interpretive reality are not settled by
historical-critical minimalism: critical scholars' verdicts are reported as a school
with premises; liturgical and magisterial reception counts as real evidence (Dei Verbum
12; CCC 112–118, 128–130); late explicit articulation is development, not debunking. No
apologetic overreach: negatives are preserved, resemblance is never printed as exegesis,
and project synthesis is labeled as such.

## Included

- Panel I, the journey: every canonical ark pericope from Exodus 25 to Revelation 11:19,
  per the corpus catalog in the production research (Sinai design and construction;
  wilderness marches; Jordan, Jericho, Ebal; Shiloh and the capture narrative; the
  Philistine sojourn; Beth-shemesh; Kiriath-jearim; the two processions of 2 Kings
  (2 Samuel) 6 with the Chronicles parallel preserved synoptically; the temple
  installation; the last mentions and the silence of 587; the fate traditions — 2 Macc
  2:4–8, 4 Ezra 10:22, the rabbinic split, cognates — and the Ethiopian tradition as
  reception history), with archaeological identifications carried at their actual
  confidence.
- Panel II, the pattern: presence and glory (the Ichabod-to-Ezekiel Shekinah arc);
  holiness that is not a talisman; the mercy seat and Christ the hilasterion; the ark
  superseded, not lost (Jer 3:16); what typology is and is not.
- Panel III, the new ark: the Luke 1 / LXX philology at verified strength and verified
  limits; the scholarly debate fairly stated on both sides; the patristic florilegium
  with per-text authenticity verdicts; liturgical and magisterial reception (Akathist,
  Dormition Menaion, Litany of Loreto, Munificentissimus Deus, the 1969 lectionary, CCC
  2676–2677, John Paul II); the Assumption (Ps 131/132:8); one derived master table
  carrying the two-axis assessment of every parallel.
- Coda: the spiritual application (Visitation as the pattern of apostolate; reverence
  before the holy; Mary as bearer of Christ), imitating the registers of Chautard and
  Garrigou-Lagrange without quoting their in-copyright translations.
- Terminal apparatus per the mariology profile: Scope, Status, and Qualifications;
  references grouped by source function; generation metadata; rights colophon.

## Excluded or bounded

- No position taken on contested archaeology beyond reporting identifications at their
  scholarly confidence; site disputes are noted, not adjudicated.
- The Kebra Nagast and the Aksum claim are treated as reception history, dated to their
  14th-century composition; modern pseudo-archaeology (Wyatt, Templar theories) is
  declined in at most a sentence.
- Islamic reception is bounded to one paragraph (Qur'an 2:248) for parity with other
  reception traditions.
- No prayer or liturgical text is offered for vocal recitation; the Akathist, Litany of
  Loreto, and Menaion texts are quoted briefly as objects of study from identified
  witnesses. This leaf therefore keeps no prayer-text audit.
- Source hypotheses about the ark narratives (Rost and successors) are reported as live
  scholarship in one candid passage; the narrative stands on the canonical text.
- Mary's cooperation is presented within Christ's unique mediation throughout (Lumen
  gentium 60–62; the profile's shared doctrinal controls); no mediation title beyond the
  profile's boundaries is advanced.

## Material uncertainties preserved

- The ark's fate: destroyed in 587, removed earlier, hidden, or taken to Babylon — all
  four ancient answers carried side by side; no resolution asserted.
- Whether Luke intends the ark typology: the Laurentin school and its critics presented
  as a genuinely open methodological debate; the document's own claim rests on the
  convergence plus reception, stated as such.
- Authenticity disputes: the Turin "Athanasius" homily (disputed, no located scholarly
  defense); Ps.-Gregory Thaumaturgus, Ps.-Ambrose Sermo 42, Ps.-Methodius (pseudonymous);
  the Hesychius locus pending its Aubineau/PG check; the Symmachus reading of 2 Sam 6:16
  pending the Hexapla check.
- Whether Munificentissimus Deus §26's own note cites John Damascene for the ark type —
  under active verification against the constitution's footnotes; the publication will
  state only what the notes actually show.
- 1 Sam 6:19's number (the Beth-shemesh crux) and 1 Sam 14:18 (ark/ephod) as textual
  cruxes, with the Qumran witness reported where establishable.
