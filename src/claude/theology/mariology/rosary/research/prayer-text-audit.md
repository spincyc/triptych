Provider: Anthropic Claude. This record governs every text printed for
vocal recitation in the publication. Collation dates: 2026-07-25
(witness A, initial edition) and 2026-07-25 (witness B, deepening

## Governing witness B — the concluding antiphon and the mystery petitions

**A Manual of Prayers for the Use of the Catholic Laity, Prepared and
Published by Order of the Third Plenary Council of Baltimore** (New
York: The Catholic Publication Society Co., 9 Barclay Street; London:
Burns & Oates, Limited, 1889).

- Identification (from page images of the title leaf and the following
  leaf): "The Prayer Book ordered by the Third Plenary Council of
  Baltimore, having been diligently compiled and examined, is hereby
  approved. James Card. Gibbons, Archbishop of Baltimore, Apostolic
  Delegate. Baltimore, May 17, 1889."; "Imprimatur, ✠ Michael
  Augustinus, Archiep. Neo-Eborac."; "Copyright, 1888, by Clarence E.
  Woodman"; Library of Congress copyright stamp "DEC 23 1889".
- Exemplar: Internet Archive item `manualofprayersf00wood`
  (https://archive.org/details/manualofprayersf00wood), a complete
  page-imaged scan; the item's page-number map was used to resolve
  printed pages to scan leaves, and the images were retrieved through
  IIIF and read visually. The item's OCR was used only as a finding aid
  and was **not** relied on for wording: the Salve Regina is printed in
  parallel Latin and English columns that the OCR interleaves
  unusably.
- Rights: the printing carries an 1888 United States copyright, renewed
  by no traceable act and long expired; the book is public domain in the
  United States. Its printed notice "All rights reserved, and making
  extracts or selections from this book without the proper permission is
  strictly prohibited" is a lapsed nineteenth-century claim and is
  recorded here for completeness, not as a subsisting restriction. The
  Salve Regina itself is an ancient received antiphon of the Church, not
  project-owned expression.
- Relationship to witness A: the two books are companions of the same
  conciliar programme (catechism and prayer book), which is why the
  second witness was preferred to an unrelated manual when a witness for
  the concluding antiphon was needed. They are nonetheless distinct
  witnesses, and no wording is transferred between them.

## Inventory controls

- Exactly five texts are printed for recitation: the Lord's Prayer, the
  Hail Mary, the Doxology, and the Apostles' Creed from witness A, and
  the Salve Regina in English with its versicle and response from
  witness B. Each reproduces its single governing witness verbatim. No
  abbreviation required expansion: witness A prints its four in full
  ("&c." appears only in the Q. 1082 lesson, which is not a printed
  vocal text), and witness B prints the antiphon in full.
- No prayer text was composed, translated, retranslated, modernized,
  conflated with another edition, or adapted by the project or an AI
  contributor. Wording is never combined across the two witnesses.
  Commentary is typographically distinct (the texts sit in framed
  "Received text" blocks naming the witness and its pages).
- **Named but deliberately not printed**, each with its stated reason in
  the publication:
  - the **Litany of Loreto** — its text is not fixed, competent
    authority having repeatedly added invocations (Leo XIII 1883,
    Benedict XV 1917, Pius XII 1950, and the Congregation for Divine
    Worship on 20 June 2020, with *Mater Ecclesiae* and *Regina
    familiae* widely reported but not verifiable at a Holy See source);
    any public-domain nineteenth-century printing would therefore
    misrepresent current usage. The 1889 witness does print a Litany at
    p. 65, which was located but deliberately not used for this reason;
  - the **Fatima decade prayer** — no public-domain, precisely
    identified received witness for an English text was located, and no
    project or AI translation may substitute for one;
  - **Psalm 70's opening versicle** and the **concluding collects** —
    plural by local custom (RVM 35, 37), with no single received witness
    that would govern them;
  - the **mystery clauses** (*clausulae*) and any apparition-derived
    prayer. A future revision printing any of these must bind its own
    received witness first.
- The publication states beside the texts that other received forms
  exist and that no claim of unique correctness or of
  approval-for-indulgence-purposes attaches to these printings. The
  indulgence section's vernacular-approval caveat cross-references this
  boundary and is reinforced by the Enchiridion's own requirement of an
  *approved* prayer (conc. 17 § 2, 3°).
- Latin texts: the publication quotes the Enchiridion's Latin definition
  of the Rosary, the operative Latin clause of *Salutaris ille*, and one
  clausula example, all inside scholarly discussion; none is offered for
  recitation, and the English renderings given beside the Latin are
  editorial identifications, not translations for prayer.
