Provider: Anthropic Claude. This record governs every text printed for

## Governing witness

**A Catechism of Christian Doctrine, Prepared and Enjoined by Order of
the Third Plenary Council of Baltimore, No. 3** (New York: Benziger
Brothers, 1921).

- Identification (from page images of the copyright/imprimatur leaf):
  imprimatur John Cardinal McCloskey, New York, 6 April 1885; approval
  ✠ James Gibbons, Archbishop of Baltimore, Apostolic Delegate,
  6 April 1885; nihil obstat Remigius Lafort with imprimatur Michael
  Augustine, New York, 21 February 1901; nihil obstat Arthur J.
  Scanlan with imprimatur Patrick J. Hayes, New York, 29 June 1921;
  copyright 1885 (J. L. Spalding), 1901 (T. L. Kinkead), 1921
  (Benziger Brothers); "Printed in the United States of America."
- Exemplar: Internet Archive item `catechismofchris00unse`
  (https://archive.org/details/catechismofchris00unse), a complete
  page-imaged scan. Page images were retrieved through the item's IIIF
  delivery and read visually; the item's OCR was used only as a finding
  aid.
- Rights: published in the United States in 1921; public domain in the
  United States. The prayer texts themselves are received prayers of
  the Church, not project-owned expression, and the repository license
  does not relicense them.

## Inventory controls

- Exactly four texts are printed for recitation: Lord's Prayer, Hail
  Mary, Doxology, Apostles' Creed. Each reproduces the single governing
  witness above, verbatim; no abbreviation required expansion (the
  witness prints all four in full; the "&c." abbreviations appear only
  in the Q. 1082 lesson, which is not a printed vocal text).
- No prayer text was composed, translated, retranslated, modernized,
  conflated with another edition, or adapted by the project or an AI
  contributor. Commentary is typographically distinct (the texts sit in
  framed "Received text" blocks naming the witness).
- Named but deliberately not printed (no witness bound): Salve Regina /
  Hail Holy Queen, Litany of Loreto, Psalm 70 opening versicle,
  concluding collects, mystery clauses (clausulae), and any
  apparition-derived prayer. A future revision printing any of these
  must bind its own received witness first.
- The witness is one received American English tradition bearing
  historical imprimaturs. The publication states beside the texts that
  other received forms exist and that no claim of unique correctness or
  of approval-for-indulgence-purposes attaches to this printing. The
  indulgence section's vernacular-approval caveat cross-references this
  boundary.
- Latin texts: the publication quotes the Enchiridion's Latin
  definition of the Rosary and one clausula example inside scholarly
  discussion; neither is offered for recitation.
