\section{Magisterial Chronology}\label{app:chronology}

The acts through which the Church has taught and ruled on the angels, in
order of date, each with what it teaches of the angels and the degree it
supports (\S\ref{sec:faith}). The disciplinary acts on the cult of
the angels stand with them, since they fix the limits of devotion
(\S\ref{sec:devotion}, Appendix~\ref{app:names}).

1972, 15 Nov. & Paul VI, general audience \emph{Liberaci dal male}

spiritual, perverted and perverting; not every sin is directly diabolical

1975, 26 June & CDF, study \emph{Christian Faith and Demonology},
commended by the Congregation (expert study with the Congregation's
approval) & conclusions & Each principal point of Lateran's chapter has
equal dogmatic value; the demonic world's existence is a dogmatic datum
of the ordinary and universal teaching (Church teaching)\\
1983, 24 Sept. & CDF, decisions on the Opus Angelorum movement (as
recalled in the decree of 1992) & AAS 76 (1984) 175--176 & Forbade a

1986, July--Aug. & John Paul II, six general audiences on the angels and

spirits; the nine choirs after Dionysius; guardian angels;
Satan's fall, his power not infinite, Christ's victory (Church

1992, 6 June & CDF, decree on the Opus Angelorum, approved by John Paul

1992; typ.\ 1997 & \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church} (universal

incorporeal, invisible, immortal, personal; their ministry; the fall and
its irrevocability; Satan's power not infinite (Church teaching)\\

Two lines run through the table. The doctrinal line runs through the
creeds and the acts against dualism: Nicaea, Constantinople, Leo I, Braga,
Lateran IV, Florence, Vatican I. In them the Church confesses the
existence, creation, and created goodness of the angels and the
self-willed fall of the demons, and the twentieth-century acts ---
\latin{Humani generis}, the \emph{Credo} of Paul VI, the CDF study, the
catecheses of John Paul II, and the Catechism --- teach the same doctrine
to a new age. The disciplinary line runs from Laodicea through the Roman
synod of 745 to the Directory of 2001 and the Opus Angelorum decree: the
Church keeps the cult and naming of the angels to what Scripture gives.
