\section{Positions on the First Sin}\label{app:first-sin}

Lateran IV fixes what the faith holds: the devil and the other demons
were created good by nature and became evil of themselves
(\latin{ipsi per se facti sunt mali}, DS~800). The witnesses below, in
chronological order, state in their own words what the sin was in its
object and when it was committed; \S\ref{sec:dq-firstsin} expounds their
division.

\medskip
\begin{fourstable}{Witness}{Locus}{Object of the sin}{Moment and cause}
Gregory of Nazianzus & \emph{Or.} 38.9--10 & Pride: Lucifer ``became and
 is called Darkness through his pride''; his hosts ``creators of evil by
 their revolt against good'' & After the angelic world's creation and
 before the visible world's (38.9--10); by their own movement toward
 evil\\
Augustine & \emph{De civ.\ Dei} XI.13--15; XII.6--7 & Pride: forsaking
 God and turning to themselves, ``the beginning of sin'' (XII.6, citing
 Ecclus 10:15) & Not from the beginning of his created existence but
 ``from the beginning of his sin'' (XI.15, on 1 John 3:8); the evil will
 has no efficient cause (XII.7)\\
John of Damascus & \emph{De fide orth.} II.3--4 & Rebellion: set over the
 earthly realm, he ``determined to rise in rebellion'' against God, the
 first to depart from good (II.4) & Of his free choice, after his making
 by the good Creator; for angels the fall is as death, admitting no
 repentance (II.4)\\
Anselm & \emph{De casu diaboli} c.~4 & He willed \latin{aliquod commodum,
 quod nec habebat nec tunc velle debuit}; thereby \latin{voluit
 inordinate similis esse deo}, by a will \latin{nulli subdita}, subject to
 none & By his own will, after creation; the object was what he would
 have reached had he stood fast\\
Peter Lombard & \emph{Sent.} II d.~5 c.~1 & Pride, the mother of envy:
 \latin{voluerunt se parificare Deo} & \latin{Post creationem mox}:
 aversion by free will; the converting grace given to those who stood was
 never given to those who fell\\
Bonaventure & \emph{In II Sent.} d.~5 a.~1 q.~1; d.~4 a.~1 q.~2 &
 Pride: presumption at the sight of his own beauty; ambition to preside
 over the rest by his own authority, subject to none
 (\latin{aequiparantia}); confirmed in envy and hatred & At once upon
 beholding himself; grace had more probably not been given in the first
 instant (d.~4 a.~1 q.~2)\\
Aquinas & \ST{I q.~63 aa.~2--3, 5--6}; \emph{De malo} q.~16 aa.~3--4 &
 Pride: likeness to God in seeking final beatitude \latin{per virtutem
 suae naturae\ldots\ sine Deo gratiam conferente}; not equality, which he
 knew to be impossible & Not in the first instant (rejected by the
 masters as erroneous, a.~5; his reason: the first instant is the turn to
 natural self-knowledge); at once after it, if created in grace (a.~6)\\
Scotus & \emph{Ord.} II d.~6 qq.~1--2 & An inordinate friendship-love of
 self, then immoderate desire of beatitude; \latin{non fuit superbia
 proprie dicta\ldots\ magis videtur reduci ad luxuriam}; equality with
 God only by an inefficacious volition of complacency (q.~1) & The will's
 first inordinate act, an elicited free act following the order of
 \latin{velle amicitiae} before \latin{velle concupiscentiae} (q.~2)\\
Suárez & \emph{De angelis} VII.13--14, 28--30 & De facto, and very
 probably: pride desiring the hypostatic union for his own nature, and
 envious opposition to Christ from the beginning (VII.13 n.~13) & Upon
 the revelation of Christ as the angels' head and the author of grace
 (VII.30); a positive precept laid on them is probable (VII.28)\\
\end{fourstable}

\medskip
All the witnesses hold the frame of \emph{Firmiter}: a spiritual
creature, made good, turning from God by its own will, with pride in the
broad sense as the sin's name. They divide on the object -- likeness to
God in beatitude by natural power (Anselm, Aquinas), equality or
self-sufficient preeminence (Lombard, Bonaventure), inordinate self-love
(Scotus), the hypostatic union (Suárez) -- and on the moment, whether the
sin could fall in the first instant of creation and how soon after it the
fall is to be placed. That the fall was the angels' own is Defined
(DS~800); its object and its moment are Disputed among the schools
(\S\ref{sec:dq-firstsin}).
