\section{Witness Register}\label{app:witnesses}

Every author and every act that this treatise cites as a witness appears
here once. Scripture, the Jewish writings outside the canon, and the
philosophers come first; then the councils, popes, and Roman congregations
by date; the Fathers and Doctors by date; Thomas Aquinas and the later
scholastics; the saints and spiritual writers; and the liturgical books.
``Standing'' gives the Church's recognition of the person, or the kind of
the act. A Doctor of the Church is named with the proclaiming pope and the
year, as the act or another Holy See document records them or, for
proclamations before 1909, the \emph{Catholic Encyclopedia}'s article
``Doctors of the Church''; the year is omitted where that article's date
could not be confirmed. The four Latin Doctors are dated by
Boniface~VIII's decree of 1298 ordering their feasts kept as doubles in
the whole Church, and the four Greek Doctors by the Breviary of Pius~V
(1568), which raised their feasts to the same rank. Where no such title
applies, the column says ``Father'', ``saint'', or what the witness was.
Standing describes the witness; the grade of a teaching stands with the
teaching where it is stated. ``Works read'' names the works read at their
own loci, whose editions and translations are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:references}; ``Where treated'' gives the sections and
appendices that expound or cite each witness.
