# Source Audit: The Linen Cloths at the Empty Tomb

Every witness named in the publication is listed here with what was read, how it
was reached, and the ceiling at which its evidence stops. All online retrievals
were made on 25 July 2026 unless another date is given. Records registered in
the reusable source library are identified by their stable IDs in
`research/source-bindings.toml`; this file is the human-readable companion.

## Evidence ceilings used

- **facsimile** — read as a page image rendered from a hashed scan of the
  printing; the printed page number was read on the image.
- **transcription** — read in an electronic transcription of a printing; a
  transcription error cannot be excluded.
- **OCR** — read in uncorrected optical character recognition; character-level
  damage is present and repairs are recorded.
- **delivery** — read in a dated web delivery of a text whose exemplar the
  delivery does not identify.

## Scripture

**Greek.** Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine Textform, byztxt Unicode CSV, already
registered in this repository. Read directly in the tracked CSV artifacts for
John, Luke, Matthew and Acts. Every transliterated Greek form in the publication
was taken from these files. The apparatus braces were read: at Luke 24:12 the
note `{NA κείμενα > -}` records that the Nestle–Aland text lacks the participle
the Byzantine text has, which is the sole basis for the publication's bounded
statement that the modern eclectic text prints the verse. **Nestle–Aland was not
collated**, and no claim finer than that inference is made.

**Latin.** Clementine Vulgate, Hetzenauer 1914, SacredBible page images.
Four new page images were retrieved and are now tracked: scans 1038, 1054, 1062
and 1063. Five passages were read on the page, transcribed, and registered as
tracked checked transcriptions: John 20:3–10, John 19:39–40, John 11:44, Luke
23:53, Luke 24:12. Ceiling: facsimile. Hetzenauer's marginal summaries and
section headings were read and are identified as his apparatus wherever the
publication mentions them; the marginal summary at John 20 names the other
disciple as John, and the publication records that this is the editor's
identification and not the text's.

**English.** Douay–Rheims, Challoner, Project Gutenberg eBook 1581, already
registered. Read in the tracked per-book verse-text TSV artifacts, not from any
website. Ceiling: the registered electronic edition, which is an identified
working witness and not a critical text.

**Authorized Version.** Project Gutenberg eBook 10, retrieved and hashed.
Read for John 20:5–10 only, to establish that the version supplying the English
word "napkin" reads "wrapped together" and not "folded". Carries no authority in
the publication.

**Thayer's revision of Grimm's *Clavis*.** Internet Archive facsimile
`greekenglishlex00grim`, downloaded and hashed. Three entries read as page

*soudarion* (p. 581). The running heads were read to confirm each page number.

working authority. **No modern lexicon was collated.** A delegated search
reported readings from Liddell–Scott–Jones, the *Diccionario Griego-Español* and
Vincent's *Word Studies*; none of them is relied on for any claim in the
publication, and none is cited there.

## Patristic and medieval witnesses

**Chrysostom, *Homilies on John* 85, 86, 87.** NPNF first series vol. 14,
Internet Archive facsimile `selectlibraryofn14auguuoft`, downloaded and hashed.
Printed pp. 320–321, 323 and 327 rendered at 220 dpi and read; the running heads
were read to confirm the homily numbers and page numbers. Ceiling: facsimile.
The passage was first located in the New Advent transcription and then verified
in the printed volume; the publication cites the printed volume. **The Greek of
Patrologia Graeca 59 was not reached**, and no claim about Chrysostom's Greek
wording is made.

**Augustine, *Tractates on John* 120.8–9.** NPNF first series vol. 7, Internet
Archive facsimile `selectlibraryofn07auguuoft`, downloaded and hashed. Printed
p. 436 rendered at 220 dpi and read; sections 7, 8 and 9 and the heading of
Tractate 121 lower on the same page were all read, so the passage boundary is
explicit. Ceiling: facsimile. The running head on that page names Tractate CXXI
because that tractate begins there; the publication cites by tractate and
section, which is edition-independent, and records the page.

**Augustine, Latin.** augustinus.it, retrieved and hashed. Ceiling: delivery —
the site does not identify its Latin exemplar. Registered because the
publication prints Augustine's Latin in a note. Orthographic accidentals differ
from Migne. **The Corpus Christianorum edition of Willems was not consulted.**

**Cyril of Alexandria, *Commentary on John*, book 12.** tertullian.org
transcription of the Library of Fathers translation, retrieved and hashed.
Ceiling: transcription; **no printed volume was consulted**, and the page
markers |650 and |651 are the transcription's. Material check: the famous lacuna
in Cyril's commentary falls at books 7–8, not at book 12, so the passage is
genuinely extant; this was confirmed by reading the continuous lemma on John
20:1–9 in the file.

**Gregory the Great, *Homilies on the Gospels* 22.** Corpus Corporum
transcription of Migne reproduced at la.wikisource.org, retrieved and hashed.
Ceiling: transcription. The file was checked for the automated Italian spelling
corruption known to affect some files on that site: zero occurrences. The
homily's rubric naming the Lateran basilica, the Saturday after Easter and the
lection John 20:1–9 was read on the file.

**Bede, *In Lucae evangelium expositio* VI on Luke 24:12.** Same site and route,
retrieved and hashed; the transcription embeds Migne's column markers, and the
note stands at PL 92, 625A–B. Ceiling: transcription. **No page image of PL 92
at these columns was reachable**: the only Internet Archive scan of that volume
located contains the Matthew and Mark commentaries and never reaches col. 620.

*Bede's homilies — a bounded negative.* A delegated researcher reported that the
stem `sudari-` does not occur in books I–II of Migne's Bede homiliary and that
Migne's own index gives II.9 as the Ascension homily. **This was not
independently re-checked by this study**, and the publication reports it at that
stated ceiling and confines the negative to Migne's arrangement. The Corpus
Christianorum numbering differs and could not be verified online; that limit is
carried into section 10 as an open question.

**Ambrose, *Expositio in Lucam* X.** Same site and route, retrieved and hashed.
Ceiling: transcription; the file was corruption-checked and was clean. Two
things were established over the exact retrieved bytes: the book's sequence of
verse lemmata runs 23:49, 23:50, 23:53, 23:55 and then jumps to 24:25, with no
lemma on 24:12; and the stems `linteam-`, `sudari-` and `cucurr-` do not occur
in the book at all. Section 179, under a lemma on Luke 24:37 and 45, carries the
assertion that Peter could not have doubted. The counts are counts over a
transcription; an editorial or transcription omission cannot be excluded, and
the publication states the negative with that limit.

**Theophylact, *In Ioannem* 20.** Internet Archive OCR of Migne, Patrologia
Graeca 124, downloaded and hashed; Migne's facing **Latin** column read at cols.
289–292. Ceiling: OCR, and the publication says so at the point of quotation.
Four character-level repairs are recorded in the passage notes. **Theophylact's
Greek was not collated by this study**; a delegated researcher transcribed the
Greek column from page images by eye, and none of that Greek is quoted or relied
on in the publication. The modern English translation is under copyright and was
not consulted.

**Aquinas, *Lectura super Ioannem*, cap. 20, lect. 1.** Corpus Thomisticum
(Busa–Alarcón), retrieved and hashed. Ceiling: delivery of an electronic text
based on the Turin 1952 printing; not a critical edition. Both readings of
"he saw and believed" were read in place, and the publication's account of the
structure — Augustine's given as `ideo dicendum`, Chrysostom's offered as `vel`
— was checked against the retrieved bytes. The *Catena aurea* was reported by a
delegated researcher; the publication identifies it as a quotation collection and
rests nothing on it that the *Lectura* does not carry.

**Jerome, *De viris illustribus* 2.** New Advent transcription of Richardson's
NPNF translation, retrieved and hashed. Ceiling: transcription.

**Gospel of Peter.** Read only by a delegated researcher, in M. R. James's 1924
translation at earlychristianwritings.com. **Not independently re-checked, and no
Greek collated.** The publication reports it at that ceiling in one sentence.

## Rabbinic and Second Temple

All rabbinic material was reached through Sefaria's public text and search
interfaces, and each retrieval was saved and hashed.

**Licence discipline.** Only versions whose recorded licence permits quotation
are quoted: the Torat Emet Hebrew (public domain), A. Cohen's *Tractate Berakot*
(Cambridge, 1921; public domain), and the Sefaria Community Translation (CC0).
**The William Davidson English is CC BY-NC and is quoted nowhere in the
publication.** Tosefta Berakhot 5 was read by a delegated researcher in a version
whose licence Sefaria records as unknown; it is not quoted and is not relied on.

**Read and registered:** Mishnah Berakhot 8 (whole chapter, Hebrew), Bavli
Berakhot 42a and 52b (Cohen), Mishnah Sanhedrin 6:1 (Hebrew and CC0 English).

**Searches re-run independently for this study**, over Sefaria's indexed corpus,
25 July 2026. Phrase queries and totals: `"folded napkin"` 0; `"folds the
napkin"` 0; `"folds his napkin"` 0; `"crumpled napkin"` 0; `"napkin" "servant"
"signal"` 0; `"sign to his servant"` 0; `"I am coming back"` 0; `"I am not
finished"` 0; `soudarion` 0; `napkin` 49; `sudarium` 43. Every hit in the two
non-empty result sets was inspected by its reference. The 49 "napkin" hits are
the m. Berakhot 8:3 dispute and its commentaries, lexicon entries (Klein,
Jastrow) for *mappah*, *mappit* and *mitpahat*, ritual-purity material in Kelim,
Chagigah, Yadayim and Sukkah, and modern halakhic and responsa literature. The
43 `sudarium` hits include Mishnah Sanhedrin 6:1 and b. Sanhedrin 43a — the
judicial *sudarin* signal — which is reported in the publication as a positive
control on the negative.

**Boundaries of the negative, as stated in the publication:** Sefaria's index is
not the whole rabbinic corpus; a phrase search in English cannot find a custom
described in words not guessed; the searches were made on one date. What raises
the bounded negative is that the loci where such a custom would necessarily be
recorded do survive, are indexed, and were read.

**Lightfoot, *Horae Hebraicae et Talmudicae*, Gandell 1859.** All four volumes'
Internet Archive OCR downloaded and hashed. Searched for `napkin`: zero in
volumes 1, 2 and 4; two in volume 3, both in the note on John 11:44. The chapter
of exercitations on John 20 was read for its sequence of verse lemmata: Ver. 1,
5, 12, 17, 23, 24, 25, 26, 29 — **no note on verse 7**. Ceiling: OCR; a
recognition miss cannot be excluded, and the publication says so. Registered as
a `context` binding rather than a `negative-search` binding, because the
artifacts are remote and are not tracked search representations, so no replayed
zero-result receipt is claimed.

**Greco-Roman.** Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Antiquities* (1875),
s.v. "Mantele", and Suetonius, *Nero* 22.2 in Rolfe's Loeb — both read directly
at the LacusCurtius transcriptions and hashed. Ceiling: transcription. Petronius
and the *mappa* passages in the *Satyricon* were read only by a delegated
researcher and are not cited in the publication.

## The modern folded-napkin claim

**Jerusalem Perspective**, article first published 7 October 2006 per the site's
own metadata field. The live page was unreachable (HTTP 403); read in the
Internet Archive capture `20130307140747` and hashed. Ceiling: a capture that
postdates the claimed publication date by more than six years, and the date is
the site's own field. Both limits are printed in the publication.

**Mailing-list forward**, 21 May 2007, mail-archive.com msg14227, retrieved and
hashed. The earliest independently archived instance located.

**TruthOrFiction** and **GotQuestions**, both retrieved and hashed. Registered
because the "originated in 2007" estimate travels from the first to the second
nearly verbatim; the publication corrects it against the 2006 page.

None of these four is transcribed into the repository: all are present-day
copyrighted publications, registered as remote artifacts with hashes, and quoted
in the publication only in short attributed clauses.

## Use of delegated research

Two assisting research processes were used, one for the rabbinic and modern-claim
strand and one for the patristic reception. Their findings were treated as leads.
Everything relied on in the publication was independently re-fetched and re-read
for this study before use: the Sefaria texts and searches, the Jerusalem
Perspective capture, TruthOrFiction, GotQuestions, the 2007 mailing-list page,
Smith's *Mantele*, Suetonius, Cyril, Gregory, Ambrose, Bede on Luke,
Theophylact's Latin column, Aquinas's *Lectura*, Jerome, and all four Lightfoot
volumes. Three findings remain at a delegated ceiling and are marked as such both
here and at their place in the publication: the absence of `sudari-` in Migne's
Bede homiliary, the reading of the Gospel of Peter, and the reported treatments
in modern copyrighted commentaries.
