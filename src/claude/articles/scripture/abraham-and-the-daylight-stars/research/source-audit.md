it, the exact locus, the route by which it was reached, and the limit on what it
establishes. Checks were performed on 2026-07-26 unless stated otherwise.

## 9. Corrections made during the work

- An earlier draft attributed to Cornelius a Lapide a citation of Augustine by
  book **and chapter**. The chapter number is not legible with confidence in
  the text layer consulted, and the rendered study now reports only the book
  number and says that the chapter number is not reported.
- An earlier draft asserted that the Hetzenauer apparatus attaches Exodus 32:13
  to Genesis 22. The apparatus at Genesis 22 was not read; the rendered study
  now says only that Exodus 32:13 does not appear in the list at Genesis 15:18.
- An earlier draft stated that a Lapide's two conflicting notes stand "eight
  columns apart". The exact distance was not measured; the rendered study now
  says only that they stand several columns apart under separate verse headings.
- The English of the CC BY-NC translations (William Davidson Talmud; Strickman
  and Silver on Ibn Ezra) was quoted in three endnotes of an earlier draft.
  Those quotations were removed and replaced by statements that the translations
  were consulted as controls on the study's own glosses.

## 10. Project synthesis, listed

Claims below belong to this study and to no source. Each is labelled in the
rendered text at the point where it is made.

1. That on the daylight reading the sign is a demand rather than an
   illustration, and that Genesis 15:6 stands immediately after a command that
   could not be obeyed.
2. The extension of Rav Pappa's limitation from halakhic derivation to
   narrative chronology.
3. The observation that the above-the-firmament reading would incidentally
   dissolve the daylight problem, and that no source that transmits it connects
   it to 15:12.
4. The classification of the reception into five mutually inconsistent answers.
5. The reading of the Septuagint's three changes as one consistent
   de-temporalizing tendency, with the `ekei` claim explicitly bounded by
   Augustine's contrary Old Latin.
6. The identification of allegory as the cause of the patristic silence.
7. The observation that a Lapide's notes at 15:5 and 15:12 jointly commit him to
   a two-day chronology inside a chapter that ends "in that day".
