## Source classes and rights

- **Public domain:** Hume (1748; davidhume.org critical text used as witness); Comte–Martineau (1896 Bell printing); Douay–Rheims (Challoner); English Dominican Summa translation; Dei Filius Latin (1870 conciliar act).
- **Copyrighted, quoted briefly with attribution:** Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic* (fair quotation; exact scan witness identified); CCC official English and Fides et ratio official English (brief excerpts of Vatican web texts); SEP entry (used as a checked secondary narrative, not quoted).
- **Coined and project content:** the term "instrumentalist" as defined; the presupposition inventory; the compressed contingency shape; all examples and formulations not attributed above.

## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- No claim that the collapse of the verifiability criterion establishes the truth or meaningfulness of any metaphysical assertion.
- No demonstration of God's existence is executed or claimed; the five ways are named as out of scope.
- No gaps-based argument is used; the fourth section expressly distinguishes contingency reasoning from appeals to unexplained phenomena.
- Ayer's later interview remarks disparaging the book (widely quoted in obituaries) were deliberately not used; only his printed 1946 concession is relied on.
- Hempel's 1950 text was not collated; no Hempel wording is quoted, and the narrative is attributed to the named secondary witness.
- Berlin 1939, Church 1949, and Quine 1951 are cited bibliographically and labeled not collated.
- The Dei Filius canon's English is a labeled working gloss; the Latin governs.
- Romans 1 is not presented as Paul doing philosophical method; the culpability context and received-reading status are stated.
- Wisdom's deuterocanonical status is noted for non-Catholic readers.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval, independent review, or immunity of Church history from criticism is made.
