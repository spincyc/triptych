- **Governing question:** Does the exclusivist thesis — that only what is empirically verifiable can be meaningful, known, or real — survive examination, and what standing does metaphysical reasoning about God retain once it does not?
- **Thesis:** the exclusivist thesis fails by self-application under every formulation its own tradition produced (statement, proposal, metalinguistic ascent) and by misdescribing the sciences it honors; knowledge is proportioned to its objects; reasoning from sensible effects toward their ground is admissible and disciplined; and the Catholic Church is dogmatically committed to the competence of natural reason so to reason. The article clears and defends this space; it does not execute any demonstration of God's existence.

## Adopted terms and argument

- **Instrumentalist** is a coined, reader-facing term, defined in the first section as one who converts the instruments of empirical science, and the observational tests they serve, into the sole criterion of what can be known or what is real. It is expressly distinguished from philosophy-of-science instrumentalism (Duhem-style theses about theories), from empiricism as such, and from methodological naturalism. Working scientists as such are not the referent.
- **Verifiability criterion** means the logical-positivist test as formulated by Ayer (1936), with its Vienna Circle antecedent (1929 manifesto) and its 1946 revision treated in the genealogy.
- **Demarcation criterion** means Popper's falsifiability test for the scientific character of theories, kept strictly distinct — on Popper's own published insistence — from any criterion of meaning.
- **Demonstration quia** means Aquinas's demonstration from effect to the existence of its cause (ST I, q. 2, a. 2), as distinct from demonstration propter quid. **Praeambula fidei** means the class of naturally knowable truths about God that precede the articles of faith (ST I, q. 2, a. 2, ad 1).
- The body moves in epistemic order: (1) the instrumentalist case at full strength (Hume's flames passage, Comte's three stages, Ayer's criterion and its theological application); (2) a genealogy of the criterion read from its own documents — Comte at depth (stage architecture, ontogeny argument, theory–fact circle, social program, astronomical exclusions and their spectroscopic sequel), the Vienna Circle manifesto (1929), Ayer's engineering (strong/weak, rejection of conclusive verifiability and falsifiability), Popper's demarcation alternative distinguished from verificationism, the 1946 retreat (Berlin's objection conceded, direct/indirect repair, Church's breach reported, Hempel's audit at reported ceiling), and Quine's holism; (3) the self-application problem in three formulations — statement, proposal, semantic ascent — each with its historically actual rejoinder and the rejoinder's costs; (4) the knowledge presupposed by empirical practice (logic and mathematics, theory-laden observation, induction, testimony and memory, norms of inquiry, the judging knower) and the proportioning of methods to objects; (5) the Thomistic account of knowledge from sensible effects: demonstration quia, the praeambula fidei, the objections of I.2.3 as evidence of method, and structural limits; (6) the received witnesses — Wisdom 13, Romans 1:19–20, Dei Filius ch. 2 with canons (revelation 1; God 2) and ch. 4 (two orders, mutual aid, liberty of the sciences), CCC 31–39, Fides et ratio 67, 83, 88 — cited as the Church's teaching, not as conclusions of the preceding philosophy; (7) the scientistic residue in contemporary discourse, graded and engaged at stated ceilings (Hawking–Mlodinow; Krauss; Rosenberg); (8) restoration of empirical method to its proper scope. A synopsis table of positions and fates stands as a navigational appendix.

## Included scope

1. the strongest form of the exclusivist appeal to scientific success;
2. a genealogy of the verifiability criterion confined to the named documents, including Popper's demarcation alternative and the reported collapse literature;
3. the self-application problem in three formulations with rejoinders and costs;
4. the knowledge presupposed by empirical practice and the proportioning of methods to objects;
5. the Thomistic account of demonstration quia from sensible effects, the praeambula fidei distinction, the I.2.3 objections as method, sense-origin, and structural limits;
6. the scriptural witness of Wisdom 13 and Romans 1:19–20;
7. the dogmatic definition and quoted canons of Dei Filius, its ch. 4 on the two orders, mutual aid, and the liberty of the sciences, CCC 31–39, and Fides et ratio 67, 83, 88;
8. a bounded, exhibit-based treatment of contemporary scientism;
9. the restoration of empirical method to its proper scope, against both scientism and fideism.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded are execution or evaluation of any demonstration of God's existence, including the five ways and Aquinas's replies to the quoted objections; a full history of logical positivism or of the Vienna Circle's membership and internal debates; Comte's later religion of humanity; adjudication of realism versus instrumentalism in philosophy of science; the adequacy of Popper's demarcation criterion beyond the quoted distinctions; a full treatment of Quinean holism or the analytic/synthetic debate; philosophy of mind; foundations of mathematics; probabilistic, fine-tuning, or miracle-based apologetics; arguments from gaps in scientific explanation; assessment of the physics in the contemporary exhibits; biblical criticism of Wisdom beyond canonical status; and claims about the salvation or good faith of particular persons.

The article does not infer that the collapse of the criterion validates any metaphysical claim; does not treat the self-application objection as unanswerable by mere assertion (the definition rejoinder and the semantic ascent are engaged on their merits); does not present the compressed contingency reasoning as a completed proof; does not charge any contemporary exhibit author with Ayer's semantic doctrine; and does not present Dei Filius as canonizing any particular argument. The Quinean holist alternative is acknowledged and answered briefly rather than fully treated.
