- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader who respects empirical science and may hold, explicitly or tacitly, that empirical testability bounds the knowable or the real.
- **Governing question:** Does the exclusivist thesis — that only what is empirically verifiable can be meaningful, known, or real — survive examination, and what standing does metaphysical reasoning about God retain once it does not?
- **Thesis:** the exclusivist thesis fails by self-application and by misdescribing the sciences it honors; knowledge is proportioned to its objects; reasoning from sensible effects toward their ground is admissible and disciplined; and the Catholic Church is dogmatically committed to the competence of natural reason so to reason. The article clears and defends this space; it does not execute any demonstration of God's existence.

## Adopted terms and argument

- **Instrumentalist** is a coined, reader-facing term, defined in the first section as one who converts the instruments of empirical science, and the observational tests they serve, into the sole criterion of what can be known or what is real. It is expressly distinguished from philosophy-of-science instrumentalism (Duhem-style theses about theories), from empiricism as such, and from methodological naturalism. Working scientists as such are not the referent.
- **Verifiability criterion** means the logical-positivist test as formulated by Ayer (1936): literal significance only for the analytic or the empirically verifiable.
- **Demonstration quia** means Aquinas's demonstration from effect to the existence of its cause (ST I, q. 2, a. 2), as distinct from demonstration propter quid.
- The body moves in epistemic order: (1) the instrumentalist case at full strength (Hume's flames passage, Comte's three stages, Ayer's criterion and its theological application); (2) the criterion's self-application problem, Ayer's definition rejoinder, and the documented technical collapse (Berlin, Church, Ayer 1946, Hempel 1950); (3) the knowledge presupposed by empirical practice (logic and mathematics, induction, testimony and memory, norms of inquiry, the judging knower) and the proportioning of methods to objects; (4) the Thomistic account of knowledge from sensible effects and its structural limits; (5) the received witnesses — Wisdom 13, Romans 1:19–20, Dei Filius ch. 2 with canon 1 on revelation and ch. 4, CCC 31–39, Fides et ratio 83 and 88 — cited as the Church's teaching, not as conclusions of the preceding philosophy; (6) restoration of empirical method to its proper scope.

## Included scope

1. the strongest form of the exclusivist appeal to scientific success;
2. the self-application problem of the verifiability criterion and the criterion's technical collapse as documented in the empiricist literature itself;
3. the knowledge presupposed by empirical practice and the proportioning of methods to objects;
4. the Thomistic account of demonstration quia from sensible effects, its sense-origin, and its structural limits (no vision of the essence; existence and relational knowledge only);
5. the scriptural witness of Wisdom 13 and Romans 1:19–20;
6. the dogmatic definition and canon of Dei Filius on natural knowledge of God, the no-true-conflict teaching of its ch. 4, CCC 31–39, and Fides et ratio 83 and 88;
7. the restoration of empirical method to its proper scope, against both scientism and fideism.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded are execution or evaluation of any demonstration of God's existence, including the five ways; a history of logical positivism; adjudication of realism versus instrumentalism in philosophy of science; philosophy of mind; foundations of mathematics; probabilistic, fine-tuning, or miracle-based apologetics; arguments from gaps in scientific explanation; biblical criticism of Wisdom beyond canonical status; and claims about the salvation or good faith of particular persons.

The article does not infer that the collapse of the criterion validates any metaphysical claim; does not treat the self-application objection as unanswerable by mere assertion (Ayer's definition rejoinder is engaged); does not present the compressed contingency reasoning as a completed proof; and does not present Dei Filius as canonizing any particular argument. The Quinean holist alternative is acknowledged and answered briefly rather than fully treated.
