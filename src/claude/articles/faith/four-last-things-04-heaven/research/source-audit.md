## Bounded searches and negative results

All searches were run on 2026-07-26 over the tracked per-verse artifacts of the Challoner electronic Douay–Rheims. Each is a literal English string match within a single verse line. None can exclude synonyms, paraphrase, inflection, another translation's vocabulary, or a sense expressed without the word.

1. `wings`, over the twenty-seven New Testament books: five hits — Matt 23:37 and Luke 13:34 (a hen's, a bird's), Apoc 4:8 (the four living creatures), Apoc 9:9 (the locusts), Apoc 12:14 (the woman given eagle's wings). No human being, living or risen or blessed, is given wings. Reported in the article as a bounded absence, not as a demonstration that no text anywhere does so.
2. `harp`, over the tracked Apocalypse artifact, run with `scripts/source-library search`: returns Apoc 5:8, 14:2, 15:2 plus false positives from the substring inside "sharp." Result: harps are given to the redeemed at 15:2, contrary to the naive form of the popular claim; the article corrects itself accordingly.
3. `cloud`/`clouds`, over the New Testament books: twenty-three verses. Two associate human beings with a cloud in the relevant way — Apoc 11:12 (the two witnesses ascend) and 1 Thess 4:16 in this edition's numbering (the living caught up in the clouds to meet Christ). Both are transits; no text makes a cloud a dwelling.
4. `halo`, over all seventy-three books: seven hits, all substrings of proper names (Mahalon, Chelion, Hethalon and the like). The nimbus has no scriptural occurrence in this edition.
5. Divergence check: the edition's registered `american-edition-divergences` table was filtered for every book and chapter quoted in this article. Two quoted verses appear in it — Job 19:26 ("I shall see my God" here, "I will see my God" in the 1899 American edition) and Apoc 22:2 (a clause-final variation) — and both are disclosed at the point of quotation. No claim depends on either.

## Source classes and rights

- **Public domain:** Douay–Rheims (Challoner) in the tracked electronic edition; the English Dominican *Summa* translation; the NPNF translations of Basil (Jackson), Chrysostom (Marriott), Gregory of Nyssa (Moore), and John of Damascus (Salmond); the Dods 1871 *City of God* in the tracked transcription; the 1907–1912 *Catholic Encyclopedia*; the Latin conciliar and papal acts as acts.
- **Copyrighted, quoted briefly with attribution:** the official Vatican English texts of the Catechism, *Lumen gentium*, and *Unitatis redintegratio*; Nicholas Gendle's 1983 English translation of Palamas's *Triads* and John Meyendorff's introduction to the same volume.
- **Editorial rights retained by a third party:** printed editions of Denzinger's *Enchiridion symbolorum*; the patristica.net web presentation, which does not identify the printing it transcribes; Corpus Thomisticum's site edition, used as a checked Latin witness by link with no bulk text incorporated.
- **Project content:** the three-register discipline; the measuring-reed and postcard framing; the privative reading of the Apocalypse's imagery; both labelled project-synthesis blocks; the weights table; the estimate of how much of the received picture is speculation; all formulations not attributed above.

## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- The acts of the Byzantine councils of 1341, 1347, and 1351, the Hagioritic Tome, and the Synodal Tome of 1351 were sought and not obtained in any readable edition. The article therefore makes no claim about their wording, canons, or anathemas, reports their outcome from a named modern introduction, and holds its judgment about formal contradiction at low confidence for exactly this reason. This is the largest single gap in the evidence and the most actionable follow-up.
- A public-domain English witness for Palamas was sought and not found. Two publicly hosted PDFs of the Gendle translation were retrieved and proved to be image-only scans without extractable text; a Russian Orthodox portal hosting the Sinkewicz translation of the *One Hundred and Fifty Chapters* was blocked by an anti-bot interstitial. The archive OCR of the Gendle volume is the witness actually used, and its limits are stated wherever it is quoted.
- Aquinas's Augustinian citations at ST I, q. 12, a. 7 (`attingere vero mente Deum…`) and at ST I-II, q. 4, a. 8 (*De Genesi ad litteram* viii.25) were not independently checked at their own loci; both are reported as Aquinas gives them, and both are marked in the article.
- The traditional reading of John 14:2 ("many mansions") as teaching degrees of glory was not adopted, because the sense of *mansiones* there is genuinely disputed and no patristic locus for the reading was checked. The article says so and rests the degrees claim on 1 Cor 15:41 and the definition of Florence, which is also where Aquinas rests it.
- The Vienne proposition at DS 895 is a compound, and its condemnation is of a listed error of a named sect. The article states this explicitly and does not treat the condemnation as a positive definition of the Thomistic account.
- The parity-consulted GPT scope record was used once for topic parity only; the GPT edition's sections, prose, and PDF were not read. Its distinctive coverage (boredom and freedom objections, memory, foretaste) was deliberately not replicated.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval or independent review is made.
