# Then Face to Face — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; faith and theology articles.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs. Theology governs the whole argument; no canon-law claim is made and no juridical as-of date applies.
- **Series position:** Part IV, the closing volume, of *The Four Last Things*. The first three volumes take death, judgment, and hell; each is an independent work. This volume neither summarises nor depends on them, refers to them by subject rather than by page or section, and creates no shared TeX fragment.
- **Series relationship:** an independent Claude edition of the leaf `articles/faith/four-last-things-04-heaven`. It shares the leaf identity and subject territory with the GPT edition but is an independent work: its research, argument order, examples, section structure, and prose were produced without reading the GPT edition's sections, prose, or PDF. Only that edition's `research/scope.md` was consulted, once, for topic parity (beatific vision, grace, non-comprehension, personal identity, degrees of glory, communion of saints, intermediate state, resurrection, new creation, boredom and freedom objections, memory, foretaste). This edition deliberately does not replicate the GPT edition's coverage of the boredom and freedom objections, memory, or foretaste; its own spine is the text of *Benedictus Deus*, the Thomistic question on the vision of God, the Eastern essence-and-energies alternative in its own authors, the defined-versus-speculative boundary in the doctrine of the risen body, the defined degrees of glory, the communion of saints as related to the beatifying act, and a bounded audit of devotional imagery against the tracked Scripture text.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. All witnesses were read on 2026-07-26 (UTC).
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader able to follow careful distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** What may be said about heaven on evidence that can be produced, and how much of the received picture is defined doctrine, how much is theological reasoning, and how much is imagery read as description?
- **Thesis:** what the Catholic Church has defined about heaven is a promise of an immediate, unmediated, personal seeing of God himself, given to creatures who cannot achieve it, differing in degree with charity, entailing the resurrection of the very bodies we now bear, and enjoyed in company; this defined core is both smaller and more extreme than the picture that has grown over it; and the great majority of what an ordinary Catholic believes about heaven is theological speculation, a substantial part of it flagged as conjecture by the authors who produced it and un-flagged by their readers.

## Adopted terms and argument

- **Beatitude/happiness** translates *beatitudo*, the complete and final good of a rational creature, not a mood. **Light of glory** renders *lumen gloriae*, and is used strictly in the sense Aquinas gives it at ST I, q. 12, a. 5 (a created disposition of the seer, a *medium quo*, never a *medium in quo*). **Energies** renders the Greek *energeiai*, which the public-domain Basil translation renders "operations"; the article notes the identity of the term.
- Project images and formulations: the three registers (defined / argued / imagined); the measuring reed and the postcard; the reading of the Apocalypse's imagery as systematically privative around one positive clause; the "the eye is changed; nothing is interposed" formulation of the light of glory; the observation that the loser of the Byzantine controversy held something structurally like the winner of the Latin one; the closing "the last of the last things is not a thing." All labelled as synthesis.
- The body moves in the order the evidence has: (1) the gap between the received picture and the text, and the three-register discipline; (2) the definitions, opened through the 1330s controversy — John XXII's sermons, *Ne super his* with its qualifying clause, *Benedictus Deus* construed phrase by phrase, then Florence and Lateran IV, then what the definitions do not say; (3) the question the definitions raise — the texts that deny the vision (Ex 33:20; Jn 1:18; 1 Tim 6:16; Chrysostom; the Damascene) against those that promise it, then ST I, q. 12, aa. 1–8 read at the text, with the Vienne condemnation; (4) the Eastern answer at full strength in Basil, Nyssa, and Palamas, with a labelled project-synthesis judgment on whether it contradicts the definition; (5) the risen body — Lateran IV, 1 Cor 15, ST I-II q. 4 a. 5, Augustine's speculations and his two disclaimers, the Supplement's four gifts with its provenance, the aureoles; (6) degrees — Florence, Trent canon 32, ST I q. 12 a. 6, Augustine XXII.30, Nyssa's alternative; (7) the company — the corporate imagery, CCC 1024–1026 and 1045, LG 49–51, the counterargument from ST I-II q. 4 a. 8, a second labelled synthesis, and ST II-II q. 26 a. 13; (8) the bounded audit of the popular picture; (9) the close.

## Included scope

1. the text and construction of *Benedictus Deus* and the controversy that produced it, with the additions at Florence, Lateran IV, Trent, and the Vienne condemnation;
2. the scriptural texts denying and promising the vision of God, and the Greek Fathers' pressure on the first set;
3. the Thomistic account of the vision, the exclusion of any created likeness, the impossibility of a bodily vision, the *lumen gloriae* as a *medium quo*, non-comprehension, and the limits of the blessed's knowledge;
4. the Eastern essence-and-energies doctrine at full strength in Basil, Nyssa, and Palamas, with its fourteenth-century reception reported at a stated ceiling, and a labelled judgment on contradiction;
5. the defined and undefined content of the resurrection of the body, with Augustine's speculations and disclaimers, the schools' four gifts and their provenance, and the aureoles;
6. the defined degrees of beatitude, their measure in charity, and the three considerations by which equality and difference are held together;
7. the communion of saints in relation to the beatifying act, with the counterargument from ST I-II q. 4 a. 8 at full strength;
8. a bounded audit of the popular picture against the tracked Scripture text, and an account of what kind of language the imagery is.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded: death, particular judgment, purgatory, the general judgment, and hell, which belong to the companion volumes and are touched only where a source under examination mentions them; grace, merit, justification, predestination, and assurance beyond the single canon of Trent cited; Christology and soteriology, presupposed and undeveloped; the sacramental economy and the Eucharist as pledge of glory; the intra-Thomist controversy over the natural desire for the supernatural, named and not adjudicated; angelology beyond what the quoted sources say; private revelation, mystical or near-death testimony, and religious-experience phenomenology; the history and iconography of Christian art; the reception history of *Laetentur caeli* in the East; the boredom, freedom, and memory objections and the theology of foretaste, left to the GPT edition's spine; and any judgment about the state, culpability, degree of glory, or destiny of any person.

Material limits (fully stated in the appendix): every Latin magisterial text was read in a dated web presentation of a standard collection that does not identify the printing it transcribes, and no printed edition was collated; English renderings of Latin acts are labelled working glosses; *Benedictus Deus* is used only within its own boundary; the compatibility of the *lumen gloriae* with the definition depends on the *medium quo* / *medium in quo* distinction, which the article states and does not claim the definition canonises; the Vienne proposition is a compound and its condemnation defines no positive explanation; the natural-desire argument is used only as an explanation of a datum held on other grounds; the Palamas quotations rest on an OCR of a copyrighted 1983 translation, without the Greek and without collation; the acts of the Byzantine councils of 1341, 1347, and 1351 were not reached at all, which is the largest gap in the evidence and the reason the contradiction judgment is held at low confidence; the Supplement's compiler is not settled; Augustine's disclaimers are quoted in full because the argument depends on them; every negative result is a bounded literal English search in one electronic edition and never means "false"; the tracked Douay–Rheims uses Vulgate numbering and differs from the 1899 American edition in 1859 verses, two of which touch verses quoted here (Job 19:26; Apoc 22:2) without affecting any claim; and the estimate that "the great majority" of the received picture is speculation is a qualitative judgment, not a measurement over any sampled corpus.
