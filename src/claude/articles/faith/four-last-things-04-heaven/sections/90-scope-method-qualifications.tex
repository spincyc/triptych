\section{Scope, Method, and Qualifications}

\paragraph{Reader and purpose.} The fourth and closing volume of \emph{The Four Last Things} is spiritual reading for Catholics at the close of the liturgical year and in the annual preparation for death. It sets out the Church's hope of heaven, the vision of God, in the words of Scripture, the liturgy, the Fathers, the Doctors and the saints, and moves from that teaching toward desire, resolution and prayer. It is a study aid, not a catechism or a magisterial act.

\paragraph{Division with the companion volumes.} The moment of death and the preparation for it belong to \emph{The Debt of Nature}; the particular and general judgment, the full reading of \emph{Benedictus Deus}, purgatory and prayer for the dead belong to \emph{What Death Does Not Finish}; the loss of God and its eternity belong to \emph{The Fire That Is Not Extinguished}. Here those subjects are named in a clause and referred to their volumes.

\paragraph{Degrees of teaching.} Each teaching is stated in the body at its degree in a single clause, and Appendix A grades the principal teachings. The graded categories are those of the series: defined; Church teaching; common teaching of the Fathers and Doctors; theological opinion; pious tradition. Where the Church leaves a question open, the body says so once and gives the positions held.

\paragraph{Sources and translations.} Scripture is quoted from the Douay--Rheims (Challoner revision), at the Vulgate chapter and verse numbering. The conciliar and papal acts are quoted in Latin from the \emph{Enchiridion symbolorum}; the Catechism, \emph{Spe salvi}, the \emph{Credo of the People of God} and \emph{Munificentissimus Deus} are quoted briefly from the Holy See's official English, which is copyrighted. The Fathers, Doctors and saints are quoted from public-domain English translations named in the notes and References. Thomas Aquinas's \emph{Collationes in Symbolum} is quoted in Latin; the English renderings beside it are glosses, not a published translation. Thérèse of Lisieux is quoted briefly from the 1912 Taylor edition, public domain in the United States.

\paragraph{Liturgy and received prayer.} The 1962 Missal and the Postconciliar Missal are cited by their own titles and readings; Year A of the Postconciliar Lectionary is so labelled. The closing antiphon is printed in the Latin of the \emph{Rituale Romanum} (Ratisbon, 1872) and in the English of Lasance and Walsh's \emph{New Roman Missal} (Benziger, 1945), whose copyright was not renewed and which is public domain in the United States. No prayer is composed, translated or adapted.

\paragraph{Verification.} Every quotation was read at its locus in the identified witness; the record of those checks, with editions, loci and rights, is kept in the volume's research file and not in the notes.
