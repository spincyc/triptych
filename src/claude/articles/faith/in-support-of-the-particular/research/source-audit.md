Public English translations are working study witnesses; consequential claims are governed by the identified work and exact locus, not by any claim that an online transcription is critical. Eleven endnotes carry loci, witnesses, and claim-local qualifications; the main argument remains complete without them.

## Consequential negative results and verification limits

- **Lessing primary printing not directly inspected.** Attempts on 2026-07-24: zeno.org unreachable (connection refused); projekt-gutenberg.org page for the essay not found; de.wikisource page not found; Deutsches Textarchiv full-text search for "garstige breite Graben" returned zero hits (the essay is not in that corpus); an MDZ (digitale-sammlungen.de) search errored server-side; the USC Scalar Fragmentenstreit record confirmed the 1777 Braunschweig printing's identity but reproduces no text. The two German sentences are therefore quoted as attested in the German Wikipedia article on the essay and multiple independent German transcriptions, and this ceiling is disclosed in the rendered appendix. No public-domain English translation was identified; Chadwick's 1956 translation was deliberately not used or reproduced.
- **No claim of full-work examination** is made for any patristic or Thomistic source; loci were checked individually as listed.
- The New Advent transcription of De incarnatione truncated (in the fetched text) before §41; sections 41–43 were verified instead in the full-text NPNF copy identified above. Wording between the two witnesses was consistent for the sections both carried (8, 17).
- The C.S.M.V. translation of De incarnatione ("The Lord did not come to make a display…"), whose rights status is unclear, was identified during research and deliberately not quoted; Robertson's public-domain wording is used instead.
- "Scandal of particularity" was not found as a scriptural or magisterial formula and is used only as a received modern label.
- No source was found asserting that Aquinas treats fittingness arguments as demonstrations; ST III q. 1 a. 1's own structure (convenientia) supports the retrospective-only use adopted here.
