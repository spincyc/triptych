# In Support of the Particular — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Governing question and document identity

This record supports `src/claude/articles/faith/in-support-of-the-particular/main.tex`.

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; faith and theology articles.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive philosophical- and dogmatic-theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Series relation:** a standalone article. It shares its leaf ID and subject with the GPT edition at `src/gpt/articles/faith/in-support-of-the-particular/` but is an independent work: its research, structure, argument, and prose were produced without reading that edition's section prose.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, mutable law, or territorial discipline governs the argument. Identified online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-24.
- **Intended reader:** an intellectually serious Christian, or a reader considering the Christian claim, who can follow a metaphysical distinction but is not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** does the concrete particularity of the biblical economy — one chosen people, one incarnate life, instituted material sacraments, personal address — degrade the universality proper to God, as the objection tradition from Celsus through Spinoza and Lessing contends?
- **Thesis:** the objection rests on the false premise that generality is the divine mode. Only particulars exist concretely; abstraction is the finite knower's economy, not the infinite knower's grandeur; God knows and sustains every singular directly and is in no genus; biblical election is expressly gratuitous and ordered to all nations; the Incarnation could not have been general without being nothing, and its one assumed body grounds the Son's union "in some fashion" with every human being; the sacraments prolong the same concrete style; and the economy's end is particularity perfected (the white stone of Apocalypse 2:17), because love — unlike law — is of particulars.
- **Argumentative limit:** the article establishes coherence and retrospective fittingness, not occurrence. It does not prove the Incarnation or Resurrection from metaphysics, adjudicate among claimed revelations, resolve the Quirinius census question, or supply the act of faith.
- **Intended literary movement:** the offense at Nazareth (Matt 13:55–57; John 1:46) opens the pressure; the objection is stated at full strength in three historical voices (Celsus's frogs, Spinoza's dissolution of election, Lessing's ditch); the metaphysical section inverts the abstraction premise; election, Incarnation, and sacraments each answer one objector in turn (Spinoza in the election section, Celsus's "nobler instrument" through Athanasius, Lessing through the Paschal-presence claim); the close names the deepest premise (perfection as generality) and ends at Rev 2:17. The conversational analogies (form letter, doctor at the ditch) are project synthesis and carry no evidential weight.

## Terms fixed for this study

- **Particular** means concrete, singular, determinate, historically located: this person, people, body, act, sign. It is predicated of creatures, divine actions, and Christ's assumed humanity, never of God as a class member.
- **Abstract universal** means a common nature as object of thought; **universal cause** means God as giver of being to every creature. The article's central distinction is that the second does not entail generality as God's mode of relating to creatures.
- **Scandal of particularity** is a received modern theological label for the objection, used as such and not as a scriptural or magisterial category.
- **Flight into abstraction** is the article's editorial name for the preference for a non-landing God; not a canonical classification of any thinker.
- **Election** means gratuitous divine choice conferring vocation and mission; not antecedent merit, not exclusive divine concern.

## Included scope

1. the Nazareth pericope and Nathanael's question as the native form of the objection;
2. Celsus (via Origen), Spinoza (TTP ch. 3), and Lessing (1777) as the objection's strongest historical statements;
3. the Thomistic account of universals, abstraction, divine knowledge of singulars, particular providence, and God outside every genus;
4. election's self-announced gratuity and outward ordering (Deut 7:6–8; Gen 12:1–3; Amos 9:7; Isa 49:6);
5. the Incarnation's datedness (John 1:14; Gal 4:4–5; Luke 2:1–2), Athanasius's reply to the "nobler instrument" objection, Irenaeus on real flesh, Aquinas on the assumption's individual term and on fittingness, and Gaudium et spes 22;
6. the sacramental economy as continued particularity (Leo, Sermo 74.2; CCC 1084–1085; ST III q. 60);
7. the risen Christ's particularity (John 21) and Rev 2:17 as the economy's terminal image.

## Excluded scope

A complete natural theology; proof of the Resurrection; treatises on revelation, Christology, Israel and the Church, theology of religions, sacramental validity or Eucharistic doctrine; the history of the Quirinius census problem; comparative religion; adjudication of individual salvation. The article does not defend nominalism or nationalism, does not treat Israel as a discarded instrument, does not convert universal salvific scope into universalism, and does not present fittingness as demonstration.

- **Ecumenical council:** Gaudium et spes 22 governs the universality-through-particularity claim, with its qualifier ("in some fashion") preserved.

- **Patristic witness:** Origen/Celsus, Irenaeus, Athanasius, Leo; reception witnesses, not mechanical authority counts. Athanasius's cosmic-Word argument is noted as ad hominem to interlocutors granting the premise.

- **Objection sources:** Celsus (as reported), Spinoza (Elwes translation), Lessing (German wording; project working renderings labeled). Their governing premises are identified, not conceded.
- **Project synthesis:** the argumentative ordering, the inversion of the abstraction premise, the doctor/form-letter/breakfast observations, and the reading of Rev 2:17.
