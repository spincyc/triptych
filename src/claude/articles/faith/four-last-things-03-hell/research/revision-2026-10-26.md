# Hell — Register Revision, 26 October 2026

## Result and boundaries

The maintainer asked that the register guidance of 21 October 2026 ("Say what
the texts say, plainly", in `guidance/editorial.md`) and the rule on speaking
from within the tradition be applied to this essay, which was rewritten on
16 October before that guidance existed. The revision changes the essay's own
prose in the eleven body sections and nothing else.

The defects removed were these:

- paragraph-closing maxims and restatements;
- assertions that a text contains or governs everything;
- denials of positions nobody holds, and staged objections;
- emphatic fragments and inflated connectives;
- a few places where the essay's own inference was put in the mouth of a text.

The essay's own argument, its concrete scenes and its endings were kept. Where
a connecting sentence was cut, the plain reason one source bears on the next
was written in its place, so that the quotations do not stand side by side.

Examples, before and after:

- "Everything the Church believes about hell is contained in that sentence,
  and everything she does about it begins there. It is spoken by the one
  who said …" became "The sentence is spoken by the one who said …".
- "… a man comes into it only by joining them. The kingdom was prepared for
  us from the foundation of the world; the fire was not. That is the first
  thing to know about the place, and it is the last thing the damned will be
  able to forget." now ends at "joining them."
- "This is the inequality of punishments that the Second Council of Lyons
  professed, seen from within: one fire, burning each according to what he
  has done." became "Gregory is describing the unequal punishments that the
  Second Council of Lyons would later profess."
- "The Church has defined no number. What she has done is better than a
  number. … The Gospel does not bid us count those who find the gate. It bids
  us strive to enter." became one statement of the degree, followed by one
  sentence on what the Church prays for and where she directs the Fathers'
  fear.

Direct address was kept only where the texts support a concrete response:
Francis de Sales's resolutions, prayer for those who have left the faith after
the Teresa and Fatima accounts, and the Advent close. A relatable list of
people in the reader's life was reduced to the response itself.

## Advisory repairs after cold review

A cold review found no blocking defect and raised two advisories, both of
which were repaired in prose only. Bare "the same" had become a frequent
connective. In "The Loss of God" it no longer appears in consecutive
sentences. In four other places the connective now states the source's own
point: Catherine puts the loss first and the worm second; Job calls hell a
house of darkness; other New Testament texts speak of a punishment without
end; and the Second Letter of Clement teaches that repentance belongs to this
life. The Kempis sentence now simply introduces his words. "The Fire That Is
Not Extinguished" had ended on an added promise, "the same mercy will keep him
tomorrow", which goes beyond the Ignatian colloquy. It now ends with the
mercy that has kept the soul "up to now" and with the soul's need to go on
asking for it. The conservation check and the build were run again after
these repairs, and the figures above are from that run.
