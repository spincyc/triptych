## Governing purpose

Catholic spiritual reading for the close of the liturgical year and the annual preparation for death. The essay sets out the reality of hell soberly for contemplation — Christ's sentence, the Church's confession, the loss of God, the fire, the worm and the darkness, the eternity, the risen body, the martyrs' fear, the narrow gate, the saints who saw, the fear that saves, and the Church's prayer for deliverance — carried by Scripture, the councils, the liturgy, and the Fathers, Doctors and saints, and turns it toward contrition, confession, perseverance and prayer. It closes in received prayer (Alphonsus, *Preparation for Death* XXVI, third point, Affections and Prayers, Duffy 1843).

## Excluded

Death, the particular and general judgment, purgatory and prayer for the dead, and heaven beyond what the subject requires (the sibling essays). The history of the doctrine of eternal punishment, scholastic disputes, the Supplement's provenance, modern controversies, and the hope for universal salvation as a debate. Lactantius (his denial of immediate judgment conflicts with *Benedictus Deus*); Leonard of Port Maurice (weak provenance of the only English reached); Bellarmine, Hippolytus, Athenagoras, Theophilus, Bede's Drythelm, Faustina, Bosco (not needed, or not lawfully quotable). Any judgment on the state of a particular person beyond what Scripture and the liturgy say of Judas.

## Authority boundary

Defined: existence and immediacy at death; eternity; unequal punishments; the risen body; no predestination to hell; attrition from fear as a gift of God. Church teaching: the loss of God as the chief punishment; a pain of sense called fire; no repentance after death; judgment of persons belongs to God. Common teaching of the Fathers and Doctors: the fire a true fire; the worm as remorse; Judas lost (other position: Benedict XVI, 18 October 2006). Theological opinion: the saved fewer than the lost (the common opinion of the Fathers and Doctors; the Church has proposed no number; other position: *Spe salvi* 46); the mode of the darkness; the place of hell. Approved private revelation: Teresa, *Life* 32 (no decree on the vision as such; in Benedict XIV's sense); Fatima (Leiria 1930). The appendix carries the witnesses.
