- **Title:** *Nothing Said of Anyone: On Hell, and the Shape of What Has Been Defined*
- **Profile:** discursive faith/theology article (`guidance/articles.md`); universal standard `guidance/editorial.md`; source library `guidance/sources.md`.
- **Series:** third of four independent essays on the Four Last Things. No text, section, fragment, or apparatus is shared with the other three; they are referred to by subject only.

## Governing question

What has the Church actually defined about hell, by which acts, and with what force — as against what is common theological opinion or devotional imagery?

## Claim reached

The defined core is narrow, severe, and unrevisable: hell exists; it is entered immediately at death by those dying in mortal sin; its punishments are unequal; it does not end; God predestines no one to it. Everything else popularly attached to the doctrine — the mode of the punishment, the topography, the psychology of the damned, the number, and the identity of any individual — is undefined. The emptiness of the human census is not discretion but a consequence of the Church's own analysis of what a mortal sin requires: grave matter is public, full knowledge and deliberate consent are not, and finality is settled only at an instant no one can observe.

## Method

An audit. Take the eight references in CCC 1035 note 615 — the Catechism's own documentary basis for the existence and eternity of hell — read each at its own locus in the language of promulgation so far as reachable, and report the aggregate. Then work outward: the two punishments; the eternity texts with the contrary readings stated at full strength before any reply; the patristic range including positions later excluded; the Thomistic treatment with the Supplement's status established by direct comparison with the *Scriptum*; the census; the salvific will; and what the Church's prayer presupposes.

## Included

- The eight acts of CCC 1035 n. 615, read whole: DS 76 (*Quicumque*), 409 and 411 (canons of 543), 801 (Lateran IV *Firmiter*), 858 (Lyons II), 1002 (*Benedictus Deus*), 1351 (Florence *Cantate Domino*), 1575 (Trent session 6 canon 25), and Paul VI CPG 12; plus DS 397 (Orange II), 433 (Constantinople II canon 11), 926, 1306, 1541, 1567, 1705, 1997.
- *poena damni* / *poena sensus*: Irenaeus AH V.27.2; Chrysostom *Hom. in Matt.* 23.9; Aquinas I-II q. 87 a. 4; Augustine *De civ. Dei* XXI.9; Supplementum q. 97; CCC 1035, 1057; JPII 1999.
- Eternity: Matt 25:31–46 in Douay–Rheims and transliterated Greek; Augustine XXI.23; and seven contrary readings at full strength — the lexical argument from Jude 6–7 (*aidios* vs *aionios*), the *kolasis*/*timoria* argument with Aristotle *Rhet.* I.10.17 at its locus, the annihilationist reading of 2 Thess 1:9 and Matt 10:28, the corpse imagery of Isaias 66:24, the smoke of Apoc 14:11, the text-critical status of Mark 9:44 and 9:46, and the universalist texts (1 Cor 15:28; Rom 5:18, 11:32; Col 1:20; Acts 3:21; 1 Tim 2:4).
- Patristic range: Justin *2 Apol.* 7–9; Irenaeus; Origen *De princ.* I.6 and II.10 with Rufinus's prologue; Gregory of Nyssa *Great Cat.* XXVI; Augustine XXI.11, 12, 17, 23.
- Thomas: the Supplement's derivation from *Scriptum* IV d. 46 q. 1 a. 3, checked article by article; I-II q. 87 aa. 3–4; Suppl. qq. 94, 97, 98, 99; I qq. 19, 23, 64.
- The census: Luke 13:23–24, Matt 7:13–14, Matt 26:24, John 17:12, Acts 1:25; CCC 1857–1861; Trent DS 1541; JPII 1999 n. 4; the angelic disanalogy; Trent DS 1705 on attrition.
- Salvific will: 1 Tim 2:1–6; Ez 18:23, 33:11; 2 Pet 3:9; Wis 11:24–27; ST I q. 19 a. 6 ad 1; I q. 23 a. 3; DS 397, 1567, 1997; CCC 1037; *Spe salvi* 47.
- Hope: CCC 1056–1058; the Roman Canon as quoted at CCC 1037; the argument from what praying for an outcome presupposes.

## Excluded

Death, judgment, and heaven beyond a transition sentence (the other three essays). Purgatory except where a conciliar clause distinguishes it in the same sentence. The state of those dying in original sin only, beyond the conciliar clause and CCC 1261. The theology of the resurrection of the body. Grace, predestination, and perseverance beyond the salvific-will loci. **The entire modern controversy over the hope for universal salvation, whose literature is largely in copyright and was not read**; the essay says so in the body and in the appendix. Private revelations and visionary descriptions of hell, of any period. The descent of Christ to the dead. Eastern and Reformation treatments. Annihilationism as a systematic position, as against its scriptural arguments, which are stated at full strength. Any judgment about the state, culpability, or destiny of any person.

## Authority boundary

Definitive: existence, immediacy at death, inequality of punishments, eternity, and that God predestines no one to hell. Authoritative but not definitive: the Catechism's identification of the pain of loss as the chief punishment; JPII's 1999 catechesis; *Spe salvi*. Common or school opinion, labelled as such: the corporeal fire, hell's location, the psychology of the damned, the joy of the blessed in divine justice, Augustine's proportion. Undetermined by the Church: the mechanism relating the universal salvific will to the loss of any (Paul V forbade either school to censure the other, DS 1997, and no determination has followed).

## Consequential negative results

- No magisterial act read for this essay names a human being as damned, and John Paul II states the limit as doctrine. The claim is stated in the body as a bounded negative claim with its search boundary named.
- Denzinger prints the anti-Origenist canons under the synod of 543 and Vigilius, not under Constantinople II, whose canon 11 names Origen without specifying a proposition. Mansi and the conciliar critical editions were **not** reached; the essay reports Denzinger's arrangement and declines to adjudicate the historical question.
- *Benedictus Deus* uses no word for eternity in its hell clause while using `usque in sempiternum` of the beatific vision four lines earlier. Reported as a textual observation about that act; the doctrine does not depend on it.
- Trent session 6 canon 25 (DS 1575), cited by CCC 1035, presupposes eternal punishment rather than defining it. Reported as evidentially weaker than the rest of the file.
- Two of the seven contrary readings on eternity are judged correct as far as they go (the Isaian corpse imagery; the text-critical status of Mark 9:44, 46). Reported as findings.
