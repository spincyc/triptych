# The Due Return — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; faith and theology articles. Provider: Anthropic Claude.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs. Theology governs the whole argument; no canon-law claim is made and no juridical as-of date applies.
- **Series position:** Part II of *The Creature Before God*, a three-part Claude series. Part I (*Ontological Vertigo*) established received being, present conservation, the one-sided real relation, the fork of recoil or consent, and the analogical judgment of divine love, closing at adoration named. Part II carries the embodied response: what justice requires the creature to render to God — the unpayable debt, the virtue of religion, its acts (devotion, prayer, adoration, sacrifice), the received ecclesial form, and the freedom of the return — ending at the arrow toward charity. Part III is to carry what revelation adds and the end desired in charity (union, the creature desired, Eucharistic and mystical territory).
- **Series relationship:** an independent Claude edition of the leaf `articles/faith/the-due-return`. It shares the leaf title and subject territory with the GPT edition but is an independent work: its research, argument order, examples, section structure, and prose were produced without reading the GPT edition's sections or main file; only that edition's `research/scope.md` was consulted, for topic parity (the embodied response of justice: religion, worship, sacrifice; Psalm 115/116; Romans 1:21; Aquinas on religion as part of justice; Augustine City of God X.5–6; CCC on the virtue of religion; Dignitatis humanae boundary). The Claude edition deliberately excludes the GPT edition's current-law (Sunday obligation) coverage; no canonical obligation is asserted or interpreted here.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. All online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-24.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader able to follow careful distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** In what sense is a return due to God when no equal return is possible, and what form does the rendering take in an embodied creaturely life?
- **Thesis:** the creature's situation before God has the form of debt without possibility of settlement; the impossibility of settlement is the debt's species, not its defect — what is owed is acknowledgment (equality of wills), not equivalence (equality of things); the rendering virtue, religion, is a moral virtue annexed to justice, its acts engaging the body because man signifies through sensibles; the rendering profits only the renderer, God receiving nothing; sacrifice's natural core is acknowledgment in sign, while revelation discloses the incarnate Son's unique saving oblation and creatures' dependent participation in it; the due return must be free because what is owed is consent; and no aggregate of acts closes the account — the debt of gratitude flows from charity, which is Part III's matter.

## Adopted terms and argument

- Part I's coinages (**ontological vertigo**, **recoil**, **consent**) are used in their defined senses. **Religion** is used in the tradition's sense (a virtue of rendering), flagged where introduced. **"Spiritual bookkeeping"** (fulfilled acts construed as installments toward a discharged standing) and **"recoil's liturgical form"** (idolatry as preference for a repayable god) are this essay's project syntheses, labeled in the appendix.
