\section{The Reading That Recoils}

The first option is to experience the asymmetry as an assault and to pull away from it. Call this \emph{recoil}---another term this essay defines for its own use. Recoil is not the initial lurch, the trembling Augustine felt; a shudder is not a position. Recoil is what the shudder becomes when it hardens into a reading: the interpretation of total dependence as a threat to one's own reality, met with the will's withdrawal. Its inner voice is easy to transcribe. \emph{If my existence is on loan at every instant, then I am not real in my own right. If everything in me is received, then nothing in me is mine---not my acts, which are powered from elsewhere; not my achievements, which are gifts wearing my name; not even my refusal, which uses borrowed breath. A dependence that absolute is indistinguishable from annihilation with extra steps. Better to look away; better still to resent it.}

The transcription deserves respect, because it is not stupid. It correctly registers the scale of what the argument showed and refuses the anesthetic of half-measures---the polite theism in which God made us long ago and now we are mostly on our own. Recoil has, at least, done the metaphysics. Its one load-bearing assumption is that God and the creature occupy the same causal floor, so that whatever is attributed to one must be subtracted from the other. On that picture, agency is a pie; if God supplies my existing and my acting, my slice is zero, and ``I'' names a puppet with unusually complete strings.

But the same-floor picture is precisely what the argument ruled out. The sun and the eye do not compete to explain seeing; the eye sees \emph{because} the sun pours in light---remove the light and you do not liberate the eye, you blind it. The singer and the song do not split the credit for the melody; the song's whole reality is the singer's gift, and the gift is precisely \emph{the song's own existing}. What God causes, in causing me, is not a rival version of my acts; it is \emph{me, acting}. The Catechism's sentence had the structure exactly: God upholds creatures in being and thereby ``enables them to act.''\endnote{\emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church} 301, checked 2026-07-24 as above. The underlying scholastic account---God as primary cause working in every secondary cause according to the creature's own mode, so that the whole effect is God's in one order and the creature's in another---is developed in \emph{Summa theologiae} I, q.~105, a.~5, which this essay summarizes without quoting. The compatibility of divine primary causality with genuine created freedom is a vast question treated here only to the extent the recoil argument requires: dependence in being does not subtract agency, because it is not one more agency alongside mine but the reason there is a mine.} Existence-from-another is not existence-minus. It is the only kind of existence a creature could have, and it is entirely, non-metaphorically existence: the air is really bright, the iron really burns. To feel subtracted-from by the discovery that one is sustained is like feeling plagiarized by one's own reflection. Nothing has been taken. There was never a prior, independent self whose holdings were confiscated; there is a self at all because the holding never stops.

The tradition's name for living inside the corrected picture is not servility but two virtues that recoil misreads as one vice. Aquinas: toward any difficult good, two dispositions are needed---one ``to temper and restrain the mind, lest it tend to high things immoderately,'' which is humility, and one ``to strengthen the mind against despair, and urge it on to the pursuit of great things according to right reason,'' which is magnanimity.\endnote{\emph{Summa theologiae} II-II, q.~161, a.~1, corpus (the twofold virtue regarding the difficult good), with a.~1, ad~5 (humility ``regards chiefly the subjection of man to God'') and a.~2, corpus (humility as the restraint by which one is not ``borne towards that which is above him'' against right reason); checked 2026-07-24, English at New Advent, Latin at Corpus Thomisticum.} They respond to two true facts about the same creature. Humility answers the fact that nothing in me is from me---the exact fact the vertigo disclosed. Magnanimity answers the fact that what has been given to me is genuinely great and genuinely operative: ``There is in man something great which he possesses through the gift of God; and something defective,'' and each estimate is owed its own honesty.\endnote{\emph{Summa theologiae} II-II, q.~129, a.~3, ad~4; checked 2026-07-24 as above. Aquinas's point is that magnanimity esteems the received gifts truly (and so tends to great works) while humility esteems the self's non-aseity and defect truly; both ``follow right reason,'' so their coexistence in one person is not paradox but accuracy.} Humility without magnanimity curdles into the self-contempt that calls itself piety and is really a last bid for self-manufacture---\emph{if I cannot be my own source, I will at least be my own accuser}. Magnanimity without humility inflates into the fantasy of aseity that the vertigo just demolished. Together they are simply the truth, held steady: everything received; the received thing real and great.

Recoil, in other words, is not refuted so much as diagnosed. It is right that the asymmetry is total and right that no dignity could survive it \emph{if dependence were rivalry}. It goes wrong at the picture, and the picture, once seen, can be exchanged; the facts cannot. The creature that finishes reading the diagnosis is exactly as suspended as before, lit air in a universe of lit air. The remaining question is whether the other reading can be held with open eyes, and what it finds if it looks up the beam.
