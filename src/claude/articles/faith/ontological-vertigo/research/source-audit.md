## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- No Kalam or temporal-beginning argument is made anywhere; ST I.46.2 is quoted precisely to close that door, and no conclusion depends on a first moment.
- No claim that the First Way alone establishes divine freedom, knowledge, love, or unicity; the "everyone understands to be God" clause is glossed as deferred identification.
- The freedom of creation and its ordering to an end are marked as dogmatic teaching beyond the philosophical thread; the essay states this seam explicitly where Dei Filius and the CCC are quoted.
- Existential inertia is named as a live position and engaged structurally (rival picture exhibited), not refuted exhaustively; this limit is recorded in the appendix.
- "God loves all existing things" is presented as an analogical philosophical judgment about what God wills and causes; no disclosure of the divine inner life and no Trinitarian claim is made from natural reason (ST I.32.1 quoted).
- The one-sided real relation is stated with its guard: it denies creature-caused accidents in God, not the reality of divine knowing, willing, and loving.
- Augustine's scenes are cited as first-person record, not demonstration; no affective experience is made normative.
- Athanasius's sustaining Word and Irenaeus's grace language are flagged as confessional witness, not philosophical premises.
- Pseudo-Dionysius is identified as pseudonymous, not the convert of Acts 17.
- ST I.105.5 is summarized and labeled as summarized; De ente et essentia is identified but not cited; neither carries a quotation.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval or independent review is made.
