## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- No Kalam or temporal-beginning argument is made anywhere; ST I.46.2 is quoted precisely to close that door, the threefold typology of cosmological arguments is reported so that the reader can locate this essay's argument in the first family, and no conclusion depends on a first moment.
- No claim that the First Way alone establishes divine freedom, knowledge, love, or unicity; the "everyone understands to be God" clause is glossed as deferred identification, and the questions where the identification is done are identified by article title only.
- The contemporary debate over essentially ordered series is reported as live and is not adjudicated; the essay's positive case is routed through essence and existence rather than motion, and the body says so.
- The medieval dispute over the real distinction (Giles, Henry, Godfrey) is reported at a named witness and not adjudicated; no position is attributed to later schools whose texts were not checked. The first edition's note mentioning Scotist and Suarezian accounts was replaced by this checked report, since no witness for those attributions had been consulted.
- The modern quantificational account of existence is reported, the collision with the scholastic claim is stated, and the engagement is expressly excluded from scope.
- Existential inertia and brute-fact accounts are named as live positions and engaged structurally (rival picture exhibited), not refuted exhaustively; self-causation is refused as incoherent. These limits are recorded in the appendix.
- The freedom of creation and its ordering to an end are marked as dogmatic teaching beyond the essay's philosophical thread, with the added note that Aquinas himself argues divine freedom at ST I.19.3, which the essay cites without executing or assessing.
- "God loves all existing things" is presented as an analogical philosophical judgment about what God wills and causes; no disclosure of the divine inner life and no Trinitarian claim is made from natural reason (ST I.32.1 quoted).
- The one-sided real relation is stated with four refusals: it does not make God's lordship notional (I.13.7 ad 5), does not make divine knowing, willing, and loving fictional, does not make the creature unreal, and is not a late scholastic invention (Augustine, Conf. VII.11.17; Dei Filius ch. 1).
- Augustine's scenes are cited as first-person record, not demonstration; no affective experience is made normative.
- Athanasius's sustaining Word and Irenaeus's grace language are flagged as confessional witness, not philosophical premises.
- Pseudo-Dionysius is identified as pseudonymous, not the convert of Acts 17; the technical reconciliation of analogy with apophatic negation is outside scope and is said to be.
- Scripture is not independently quoted or exegeted; the two scriptural clauses in the essay appear inside quotations of Aquinas (Wisdom 11:25 in the sed contra of I.20.2) and of the Catechism (Wisdom 11:24–26 in CCC 301), in those texts' own renderings.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval or independent review is made.
