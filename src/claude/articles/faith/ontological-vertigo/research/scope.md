# Ontological Vertigo — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; faith and theology articles. Provider: Anthropic Claude.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive metaphysical and theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs.
- **Series position:** Part I of *The Creature Before God*, a three-part Claude series. Part I establishes received being, present conservation made concrete, the reflexive arrest named ontological vertigo, the one-sided real relation, the fork of recoil or consent, and the analogical judgment that God loves all existing things, ending at the natural-reason boundary. Part II is to carry the lived, embodied form of the response (adoration taken up by a whole creaturely life); Part III is to carry what revelation adds and what grace makes of consenting creatures. Part I names those continuations in one closing paragraph and otherwise stands complete.
- **Series relationship:** an independent Claude edition of the leaf `articles/faith/ontological-vertigo`. It shares the leaf title and subject territory with the GPT edition but is an independent work: its research, argument order, examples, section structure, and prose were produced without reading the GPT edition's sections or main file; only that edition's `research/scope.md` was consulted, for topic parity and boundary parity (no Kalam or temporal-first-instant claims; no Trinity from natural reason).
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. All online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-24.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader able to follow careful metaphysical distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** What does a creature apprehend when the metaphysics of received being is recognized reflexively as the present truth about the knower, and must that apprehension end in recoil, or can it be consented to without self-deception?
- **Thesis:** existence in creatures is received, not possessed; the reception is presently and continuously sustained (conservation); the dependence is asymmetric, the sustaining source receiving nothing in return (the one-sided real relation); recoil misreads the asymmetry through a same-floor rivalry picture that the argument itself excludes; consent reads it as gift and terminates naturally in the analogical judgment that God loves all existing things, where natural reason reaches its boundary.

## Adopted terms and argument

- **Ontological vertigo** is a project coinage, defined in the body: the reflexive apprehension of asymmetric dependence in the order of being — the arrest when the metaphysics stops being about things and is recognized by a particular knower as presently true of himself. An act of intellect, not a required feeling; not doubt; proportional to the argument's success.
- **Recoil** is a project term: not the initial shudder but the hardened interpretation of total dependence as a threat to creaturely reality, met with the will's withdrawal.
- **Consent** is a project term: the will's agreement with the truth of creaturehood; not resignation, quietism, or supernatural faith.
- **Cause of becoming / cause of being** (house/song, lamp/lit air) frames the argument; **existential inertia** names the live rival position, engaged but not exhaustively refuted.
- The body moves in epistemic order, in eight sections after the 2026-07-25 deepening: (1) the becoming/being distinction with the rival options stated at full strength (existential inertia, brute fact, and the incoherence of self-causation) and the observational underdetermination of the question, no theological premises; (2) the Greeks worked at their loci — Aristotle on potency and act (Met. 3. the real distinction of essence and existence argued from De ente et essentia; the medieval rival accounts and the modern denial of existence as a property, both at reported ceilings; beings by participation; the creation of primary matter; "He Who Is" as signifying present existence;

5. conservation, creation as a relation in the creature, and divine innermost presence, philosophically argued and patristically attested;
6. the dogmatic teaching of free creation ex nihilo and CCC 300–301, cited as received teaching;
7. the reflexive turn, Augustine's record, the coinage, and the one-sided real relation with four refused misreadings;
8. recoil at full strength and its diagnosis via primary and secondary causality; humility with magnanimity;
9. consent; divine freedom as argued by Aquinas beside the essay's own seam; the analogical judgment that God loves all things; the Trinity boundary; apophatic restraint; adoration named; a synopsis of the positions engaged.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded are: any Kalam or temporal-first-instant argument (the essay states ST I.46.2, reports the typology so the reader can place its own argument, and builds nothing on a beginning); physical cosmology; exhaustive answers to existential inertia and brute-fact objections; engagement with the quantificational account of existence; adjudication among the medieval and later schools on the real distinction; assessment of the contemporary literature on essentially ordered series; the technical reconciliation of analogy with apophatic negation; divine concurrence and freedom beyond what the recoil diagnosis requires; theodicy; the bodily acts of adoration and the virtue of religion (Part II); grace, mysticism, beatitude, and deification (reserved to Parts II–III); Trinitarian theology; independent exegesis of Scripture; and judgments about particular persons' responses.

The essay does not present revealed claims as philosophical conclusions (free creation and the sustaining Word are labeled received/confessional); does not make any affective experience normative; does not equate recoil with the initial shudder; does not treat consent as quietism; does not claim the analogical judgment discloses the divine inner life.
