## Source classes and rights

- **Public domain:** Douay–Rheims (Challoner); Mackey's *Treatise* translation; Saint-Jure (Périsse, 1877); Colombière (*Œuvres*, 1832; *Réflexions chrétiennes*, 1684); McMahon 1887 and Strickland 1921; the 1913 *Catholic Encyclopedia*; the underlying acts of 1687 and 1699.
- **Official texts quoted briefly with attribution:** the Catechism's official Vatican English.
- **In copyright, handled with ceilings:** the 1961/1980 English translation of *Trustful Surrender to Divine Providence* (bibliographic facts reported; two short phrases of the publisher's note quoted as the object of analysis; no text reproduced) and Salin 2007 (short quotations under ordinary fair-quotation practice).
- **Witness transcriptions:** the patristica.net Denzinger presentation and the papalencyclicals translation are identified as delivery witnesses; the Latin governs, and no original printing was collated.
- **Unauthorised copy:** one open-web posting of the copyrighted English translation was opened once to confirm a structural correspondence. No wording is reproduced anywhere in the article; the use is disclosed in the body, the appendix, and here; it is not treated as an authorised witness, and the correspondence it confirmed is independently visible from the publisher's own contents and the French original.
- **Project content:** the three source identifications and the attribution table; the condemned-propositions table; the inventory of what is and is not surrendered; the rules of speech for consolation; the witnesses-and-editions appendix; all working glosses of French and Latin; the section order and formulations.

## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- No seventeenth-century printing of Saint-Jure's work was consulted; the 1877 re-edition is the witness, and no first-publication date is asserted.
- The 1961 Alba House first English edition, with whatever editorial apparatus it carried, was not obtainable; the identity of the compiler is unknown and is not guessed.
- A nineteenth-century English translation of Saint-Jure (P. O'Shea, 1875) was located, but its second volume covers a different portion of the work and no volume containing book III, ch. VIII was obtainable; the French therefore supplies the article's quotations, with working glosses.
- Gagey (2001) and Olphe-Galliard's editions of the Caussade material were not obtainable; the attribution history is reported at the ceiling of the Salin witness.
- Bossuet's works on the states of prayer were not read; his role is reported at the same ceiling, and no claim is made about the content of his teaching beyond it.
- No original or official printing of either condemnation was consulted, and the two witnesses conflict on the date of *Coelestis Pastor* (20 November versus 2 November 1687). Both are recorded.
- No claim is made that the collapse of any particular attribution affects the doctrinal value of the texts; the textual findings are kept separate from the doctrinal argument throughout.
- No inference from a particular affliction to a particular punishment, and no assignment of meaning to any particular person's suffering, is made or licensed.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval, imprimatur, or independent review is made for this article.
