# The Four Last Things (Claude series): cold review

Review date: 16 October 2026. Subject: `src/claude/articles/faith/four-last-things-0{1-death,2-judgment,3-hell,4-heaven}/`, the installed PDFs (36, 37, 38 and 39 pages) and the web editions. The GPT series (25/25/25/24 pages, recast 13 October) is used only as a comparator. This is a review. Nothing in the repository was changed.

## The standard applied

The maintainer asked for these qualities, and the series is judged against them:

1. **Unapologetically Catholic.** The essays speak from inside the faith to a Catholic reader. They do not argue with a secular skeptic or a "popular" misreading.
2. **Hell set out as a sober reality for contemplation,** carried by what the Fathers and saints have said across the centuries.
3. **Research, synthesis and presentation** with the elegance and pedagogy the project expects.
4. **Fit for the season:** reading for the close of the liturgical year and for the annual preparation for death.

The repository's own gates still apply as well: source fidelity, authority levels kept distinct, and no invented or recalled quotations (`guidance/editorial.md`, `guidance/articles.md`, `guidance/the-shape.md`).

**This standard is already on record.** On 30 September 2026 the maintainer gave the Claude angelology edition this direction (`guidance/theology/angelology.md:105-133`, "Voice and determination"):

- the work is "an authentic Catholic treatise … not a secular or skeptical review";
- "declare each teaching at its degree of determination and move on";
- "do not write sentences about what an act is not or what the Church has not decided: 'not a definition', 'defines nothing' …";
- "let the Fathers and saints speak at length";
- hagiography and devotional tradition are related "as the Church receives them … without debunking".

The direction is currently scoped to angelology. The Four Last Things series predates it (July 2026), but it breaks every clause. There are 28, 13, 18 and 18 "not defined / never taught / says nothing" phrases in Death, Judgment, Hell and Heaven, against 2 in the whole GPT series. Today's request is the third time the maintainer has given this direction (30 Sept for angelology, 13 Oct for GPT, and today). It belongs in a series-wide rule, not one profile (see Next steps).

## Verdict

| Essay | Scholarship | Fit to the brief | One line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Death — *The Debt of Nature* | High; minor slips (D3–D7) | Low–middling | The nearest to devotion: the *ars moriendi* and the *Proficiscere* close are fine. But its frame is a correction of "what everyone believes", and it never turns the reader toward preparing for death. |
| Judgment — *What Death Does Not Finish* | High, but one load-bearing error (D1) | Low | The best-built argument in the series, with a superb last paragraph. It is a disputed question ("why two judgments?"), not a meditation on standing before Christ. |
| Hell — *Nothing Said of Anyone* | High, with material errors | **Fails** | A dossier audit that sets the objections out "at full strength", concedes two of them, and makes its climax that "the census is empty". It calls the saints' tradition of vivid meditation "the lurid habit". |
| Heaven — *Then Face to Face* | High; authority and translation slips (V7, V8) | Low | It takes apart the "postcard" picture of heaven and gives a full section to the case that Palamas might be "the stronger account" against *Benedictus Deus*. The ending ("Heaven is a Person, seen") is beautiful but comes too late. |

The Claude series is a strong set of **doctrinal audits** written for an educated reader who has been misled by popular piety. It is not what the maintainer asked for, and the essays know it. Hell's own "strongest counterargument" box (`03-hell/sections/80-what-may-be-hoped.tex:52-54`) states the maintainer's objection exactly: "An essay that translates all of that into the boundary between the defined and the undefined has said nothing false and produced something no one will ever be afraid of, which is precisely what those words were given for." It then concedes: "This essay is an audit and not a sermon." Heaven's ending says the same about itself: "the reader who came for one has been given something else" (`04-heaven/sections/80-the-end-of-the-four.tex:5`).

This is not a polishing problem. The **register, the governing question and the choice of sources** are all set by the "discursive article" profile and by an audit method. Line edits cannot repair a series whose subtitles are *On Hell, and the Shape of What Has Been Defined* and *On Heaven, and on Telling What Has Been Defined from What Has Been Guessed*.

## Series-level findings

### 1. Register: the posture is wrong

All four essays have the same narrative shape. A "popular" or "received" picture is held by the reader. The essay then audits what has actually been defined, and the result is smaller. Here is the pattern in the essays' own words:

| Where | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Hell 00:5, 00:9 | "Almost everything a reader thinks he knows about hell is not in it"; "a reader who discovers how little of it is defined feels he has been sold something" |
| Hell 20:60 | "Against the lurid habit … the anatomical detail that a certain kind of devotional literature has supplied in enormous quantity … most of them are not even old" |
| Heaven 00:5 | "There are harps, one to a customer." |
| Heaven 00:23 | "the honest answer shrinks the picture. A reader who wants heaven described will get less here than a devotional book would give" |
| Death 00:13 | "the credibility of the whole set depends on whether he can say something true and unsentimental about the part everyone can check" (an argument aimed at "one who believes nothing") |
| Death 50:3 | "Ask a catechism class … and the room goes quiet" |
| Death 80:53 | "a warning about how much of what everyone believes on the subject was never taught" |
| Judgment 00:15 | "Sermons, private revelations, devotional manuals … have furnished the two judgments with courtroom furniture" |

The reader this addresses is educated, mildly embarrassed by Catholic piety, and needs persuading that the doctrine survives inspection. That is the reader the maintainer ruled out. The posture also turns the saints' own tradition of meditating on the last things into the thing to be corrected. That tradition includes the Ignatian application of the senses to hell, Teresa's vision, Alphonsus's considerations, and Gregory the Great's *Dialogues*. The "full strength" device makes the same point structurally: there are 6, 7, 9 and 9 synthesis or "strongest objection" boxes per essay, and the Hell essay gives seven objections to eternal punishment a full subsection (30:19–33) before the Church's reading.

### 2. The witnesses are too few

Augustine and Aquinas carry almost the whole series. They are named roughly 30–40 times each per essay. Origen is named 35 times in Hell's body sections alone, 42 counting its appendices. Across all four essays the body text never names the Dies irae, Ignatius, Teresa, Alphonsus, Bellarmine, Bernard, Catherine of Siena, Francis de Sales, Ambrose, Bede, Newman, John Vianney, Fatima or Faustina. Gregory the Great, John of the Cross and the *Imitation* each appear once, and only at second hand, through Aquinas or the Catechism. In Hell, after Augustine, the patristic witnesses are Justin, Irenaeus, Origen, Gregory of Nyssa and Chrysostom. Two of those five are presented as the saints "on the other side".

The maintainer asked for what the Fathers and saints said "over the years". The series offers two Doctors read with great care and a dossier of conciliar acts.

### 3. The four essays repeat each other

They were written at the same time by independent agents, and it shows:

| Repeated unit | Where |
| --- | --- |
| *Benedictus Deus* (1336) expounded clause by clause | Death 50, Judgment 10, Hell 10, Heaven 10 |
| "The fourfold list is a devotional ordering, not a dogmatic one" | Death 00, Judgment 10, Heaven 80 |
| The Supplement is not the *Summa* (with Leonine evidence) | Judgment 50, Hell 50 |
| No judgment of any person; canonization has no counterpart | Judgment 70, Hell 60 |
| A "what is defined / what is named / what is open" audit | All four (Death 60, Judgment 10, Hell 10, Heaven 10) |

Read in order, the series makes the reader sit through the same audit of the same constitution four times.

### 4. Apparatus outweighs the reading

The figures in words:

| Essay | Body text | Endnotes | Appendices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Death | 15.6k | 7.8k | 4.2k |
| Judgment | 16.4k | 6.3k | 4.6k |
| Hell | 20.0k | 5.5k | 3.6k |
| Heaven | 16.7k | 6.4k | 5.3k |

Apparatus therefore takes 32–44% of each PDF. The notes are largely verification logs: "read 2026-07-25 in this library's tracked transcription … at artifact lines 17321–17392 and registered there as a passage". Death note 1 even carries an internal artifact ID. This reproduces the process ledger that `guidance/repository.md:22` keeps out of `pdf/` and `web/`, and it restates `research/source-audit.md` a second time. That breaks the rule in `guidance/the-shape.md` §2 to derive each fact once. Access dates are legitimate where online evidence matters (`guidance/editorial.md:281`). Artifact line numbers, registration states and "the ceiling of the claim" belong in the audit, not in the reader's notes.

### 5. No pedagogy of conversion and no season

None of the four essays does what the whole tradition of the last things does:

- It never moves the reader from consideration to affection and then to resolution.
- It contains no examination of conscience, no colloquy or prayer, and no practice for the day.
- It is not set in the end of the liturgical year. There is no All Souls, no last Sundays, no *Dies irae* and no Advent. Every occurrence of "Advent" in the sources is the website New Advent.

The only prayers the reader meets are the *Proficiscere* (Death 80) and the *Hanc igitur* (Hell 80). Both are quoted as evidence for a thesis, and both are used well.

### 6. What is genuinely good, and worth keeping in a new form

- **Exactness about authority.** Definitive, authoritative, common opinion and disputed claims are kept distinct throughout. Fathers are read at their loci in public-domain translations rather than through anthologies.
- **Death:** *memorare novissima* found at the end of a list of works of mercy at the deathbed (00). Augustine's "Die, that thou sin not" (40). The *ars moriendi* and the litany's "from a sudden and unforeseen death" (70). The close on the *Proficiscere*: "nothing to offer by way of explanation and every reason not to leave" (80:51).
- **Judgment:** *nihilominus* in Lyons and *Benedictus Deus* (10). "Both parties are surprised" (20:27). Arius and the apostles as acts still working after death (50:23). The final paragraph (70:39), the best prose in the series.
- **Hell:** Irenaeus V.27.2, Chrysostom's "ten thousand hells", and Aquinas I-II q.87 a.4 on the pain of loss as infinite (20). Irreparability in I-II q.87 a.3 (50). Trent DS 1705 vindicating fear of hell as a motion of grace (60:61). The close on the *Hanc igitur* (80:63).
- **Heaven:** 1 John 3:2 read as the reason heaven cannot be described (80:21), and "Heaven is a Person, seen" (80:39).

### Strong reservations (theological judgment, not a citation error)

- **Hell 30:37–39.** The essay concedes as "straightforwardly correct" that in Isaias 66:24 "the undying worm is the permanence of the disgrace, not the permanence of a sufferer". Christ applies the verse to Gehenna (Mark 9:47). Augustine (*De civ.* XXI.9, which the essay itself quotes) refers both the worm and the fire to the damned. Conceding the image to the annihilationist reading is an exegetical choice the tradition did not make.
- **Hell 20:60.** "Most of them are not even old" understates how old the vivid descriptions are. Ignatius's fifth exercise (*Spiritual Exercises* 65–71, approved by Paul III in 1548) prescribes applying the senses to hell. Gregory the Great's *Dialogues* IV and the earliest Christian literature describe its pains concretely.
- **Hell, governing thesis (60, 80).** "The census is empty" is accurate as a statement about **declarations**. Made the climax of a meditation on hell, and set against Matt 7:13–14, Luke 13:23–24, the near-unanimous reading of the Fathers and Doctors (E1), and the Fatima vision of "where the souls of poor sinners go", it turns sober fear into reassurance. The Church holds both that no one is declared damned and that the way to destruction is broad. The essay keeps only the first as its conclusion.
- **Death `50-what-the-soul-is-doing.tex:67`.** It says "that the dead observe the living … not taught at any level". The Church teaches the saints' intercession (Trent session XXV; *Lumen gentium* 49–50). CCC 956 quotes Dominic ("I shall be more useful to you after my death") and Thérèse ("I want to spend my heaven in doing good on earth"). The Heaven essay itself affirms the intercession (`04-heaven/sections/60-no-one-is-blessed-alone.tex:51`). The claim is overstated, and the series contradicts itself.
- **Judgment 00:15 and 70:35.** These stretch Trent's session XXV rule against "difficult and subtle questions" in popular preaching into a rule about the imagery of judgment. That decree concerns purgatory.
- **Heaven 30:51–61.** It offers "four reasons why a serious reader might find [Palamas's account] the stronger account" against a defined papal constitution, and ends its judgment "at low confidence". In an ecumenical study this is defensible. In the closing volume of a Catholic series on heaven it is out of place.

## Per-essay notes

### Death: *The Debt of Nature* (36 pp)

- **Governing question.** Its question is what death *is*: a separation of soul and body, natural and also penal, with an instant that cannot be located. The tradition's question for this volume is how to prepare. The essay reaches that question only in its last two sections (70, 80), and even there it reports the practice as history (the *ars moriendi*, canon law on Viaticum) rather than leading the reader into it.
- **Canon law and the deathbed.** The synthesis box on canon 1005 (20:23–29) is ingenious, but it belongs to a different essay. The canon-law digressions (20, 80) give a meditation the texture of a study of the Code.
- **The devotional inheritance treated as a fault.** The "Platonic drift" charge against the *Craft of Dying* (70:23) treats the devotional inheritance as an error to be corrected. The Church's own prayers ask to "go forth" and speak of "exile". The tension can be stated without diagnosing the genre.
- **Omissions** (lane detail below):
  - Cyprian's *De mortalitate* is used only for the plague objection, never for its consolation: "not lost, but sent before" (20) and "Let us greet the day which assigns each of us to his own home" (26), both read in the repository's ANF V artifact (lines 47085, 47193).
  - Ambrose, *De bono mortis* and *De excessu fratris*.
  - Augustine at Ostia, *Confessions* IX.
  - Bellarmine's *Art of Dying Well*.
  - Alphonsus's considerations on death.
  - The *Imitation* I.23, beyond the CCC citation.
  - Ash Wednesday's *Memento, homo*.
  - The Office of the Dead.
  - Francis's "Sister Death", which appears only through CCC 1014.
- **Inconsistency.** "That the dead observe the living … not taught at any level" (50:67): see the errors section.

### Judgment: *What Death Does Not Finish* (37 pp)

- **The best-built argument,** with one load-bearing error (D1: Florence does not keep the judgment clause). One question, "if the sentence at death is final, what is the general judgment for?", is pursued to an answer: verdict and vindication (50:79–90). The close (70:39) is the finest paragraph in the series.
- **Wrong genre for the brief.** It is a *quaestio disputata*. The reader is never placed before the Judge. Nothing here sets the reader before Christ the Judge in the voice of the liturgy:
  - The *Dies irae*, the Church's own meditation on judgment, sung for centuries at the Requiem, with its *Recordare, Jesu pie*.
  - The Gospel of the Last Sunday after Pentecost (Matt 24).
  - Chrysostom on Matthew 25.
  - Bernard.
  - Alphonsus's consideration on the particular judgment.
  - Any examination of conscience.
- **Digressions.** Two scholarly detours belong in a reference, not in the reading:
  - The Supplement's provenance, with Leonine preface evidence and variant readings (50:25–47).
  - The form-critical argument about Matthew 25:31 (20:5–19).
- **Weight on the defined/named/open boundary.** That boundary (10:47–57) takes more space than the act of judgment itself.

### Hell: *Nothing Said of Anyone* (38 pp)

- **Inversion of the brief.** The brief asks for the reality of hell, carried by Scripture, the Fathers and the saints, for contemplation and conversion. The essay instead:
  - audits the Catechism's footnote (00, 10);
  - ranks the two punishments (20, which is good);
  - argues the eternity texts against seven objections, conceding two (30);
  - surveys patristic dissent, Origen and Nyssa, at length (40);
  - qualifies Aquinas (50);
  - argues that no human being is named damned (60);
  - discusses the will that wants all saved, ending with the schools that may not censure each other (70);
  - concludes on hope (80).
- **What is missing.** In about 20,000 words the reader is never asked to consider what the essay describes: the loss of God felt as loss, the worm of conscience, the fire, the risen body, the darkness, the "for ever". Christ's own words on hell are handled as data for exegesis.
- **Where it errs.** E1–E4 and V1–V6 above. Most of them tilt the same way.
- **What to salvage:**
  - section 20: Irenaeus, Chrysostom, Aquinas on loss as the infinite pain;
  - Trent on fear (60:61);
  - the Justin passage on "big words and bugbears" (40:9–13), as a witness that the doctrine has been preached from the start;
  - the *Hanc igitur* close.
- **The rest.** It belongs, if anywhere, in a doctrinal reference with the weights tables.

### Heaven: *Then Face to Face* (39 pp)

- **Framing.** The opening "postcard" (00:5) is clever and condescending. It mocks the images by which ordinary Catholics hold their hope. Meanwhile it omits:

## How the material should be encapsulated

### Diagnosis

Three jobs are being forced through one genre:

1. **Doctrine and its weights:** what the Church teaches, by which acts, with what force. The Claude series does this very well.
2. **The witness of the tradition:** what the Fathers, Doctors, saints and liturgy have said. "All the Fathers and saints" cannot fit inside a 25–40-page essay. It is a florilegium.
3. **Contemplation and conversion:** what the reader does with it, day by day. This is the classical "considerations" form: Ignatius's First Week, the meditations of Part I of the *Philothea*, and Alphonsus's *Preparation for Death*.

The articles profile (`guidance/articles.md`) is built for the first job. Its priorities are explicit: distinguish authority at every claim, give fair treatment to counterarguments, and consolidate scope in terminal apparatus. That is why both providers' series bend toward argument whenever they try to do the third job. The GPT series bent less, but its 25-page target was then met with general moral theology.

### Recommended architecture: three layers on one source of truth

| Layer | What it is | Governs | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Florilegium** *(provider-neutral; precedent: Julian of Toledo's *Prognosticon*, 688, itself a patristic chain on the last things)* | Passage records in `src/sources/` for each witness: Scripture, the Fathers, the Doctors, the saints, the liturgy (*Dies irae*, Requiem Offertory, *Libera me*, *Proficiscere*, *Subvenite*, *In paradisum*, Holy Thursday collect), the Magisterium, and approved private revelation marked as such. Each is held once, with edition, locus and rights, and arranged by last thing × theme × era. | `guidance/sources.md` (exists) | "What the Fathers and saints have said over the years", verifiably, once |

The florilegium needs one new owned and validated edge, mapping each passage to a closed list of about 40 doctrinal loci (the uncertain hour, the particular judgment, the pain of loss, the worm, eternity, the light of glory, and so on). It would be modelled on the catena's `fragment-loci.yaml`, with catena Rules 1 and 9 carried over. It also needs a declared corpus and completeness rule, so that it never claims to hold "everything the saints said".

**Sequence:** build B first, from texts already held, creating every quotation as a central passage record from the start. A's edge and C can then follow without re-verifying anything.

B and C consume A by reference. They never copy it (`guidance/the-shape.md` §2–3, and AGENTS.md's single-source rule for shared text). The GPT and Claude meditation cycles could then differ in voice while drawing on one verified set of texts.

### Alternatives

**Nearest alternative:** a single book in the considerations form (layer B alone), with its authorities bound as passage records and a graded doctrinal synopsis as an appendix, but no topical edge and no separate reference. It needs one new profile and is the closest to Alphonsus, who wrote "for seculars" and for preachers alike. It gives up the reusable florilegium and the study layer.

**Cheapest alternative, not recommended:** revise the four Claude essays in place, as the GPT series was revised on 13 October. Each would be recast as a contemplative essay in a Catholic voice, with the strong passages kept, the saints and liturgy added, and the audit moved to appendices.

- *For:* it is cheaper, keeps the catalogue's four rows, and could be ready before All Souls.
- *Against:*
  - The articles profile's gate ("serious counterarguments … fair treatment") remains.
  - It still has no unit, prayer or calendar controls.
  - It still cannot carry the breadth of the tradition.
  - The two providers' series would converge on near-duplicates.
  - The essay form keeps pulling back toward argument; GPT's own revision drifted into general moral theology to reach its length.

### Risks and costs of the recommendation

- **Schedule.** All Saints is 16 days away. Twenty-eight verified units through every production gate by then is not realistic. Either release B a week at a time, creating each unit's passage records as it goes, or treat 2026 as the pilot year and build the whole cycle for 2027.
- **Rights.**
  - Several modern witnesses are in copyright: Faustina's *Diary*, Lúcia's memoirs, and many modern translations. These need short quotation or paraphrase under the policy.
  - Most classical witnesses have public-domain English, and the source library already holds:
    - Alphonsus (Duffy 1843);
    - de Sales (Rivingtons 1876);
    - Ignatius (Mullan 1914, which prints no paragraph numbers, so cite by Week and Exercise);
    - Teresa's *Vida* (Lewis);
    - Cyprian, Augustine, Gregory's *Moralia*, Bernard;
    - the Rituale Romanum.
  - Not yet held: Bellarmine (public-domain English: Coffin 1621; Dalton c. 1847, Book I only), the *Imitation*, Louis of Granada, Pinamonti, Thomas More, the *Ars moriendi*. Dionysius the Carthusian and Julian of Toledo have no public-domain English.
  - The editorial standard forbids composed or AI-translated vocal prayer. Every affection and prayer must be a received text, kept in a prayer inventory beside its original.
  - Private revelation (Teresa's vision of hell, Fatima, Faustina) needs status grading of the kind `guidance/theology/mariology.md` already provides.
- **Profile work.** A new profile must be written before multiplying documents, as AGENTS.md requires.

### What the classical forms share and both present series lack

These devices are drawn from Ignatius, de Sales (*Introduction to the Devout Life* I, meditations 5–8: Death, Judgment, Hell, Heaven), Alphonsus (*Preparation for Death*: death I–X, judgment XXIV–XXV, hell XXVI–XXVIII, heaven XXIX), Louis of Granada, Pinamonti, the *Imitation* I.23–24, the *Ars moriendi* and Bellarmine. All were read at source by the forms lane.

- **A unit one sitting long,** repeated on a cycle: daily (de Sales), weekly (Granada, Pinamonti), the retreat (Ignatius, Alphonsus), the deathbed (the *Ars moriendi*, Bellarmine II).
- **A Scripture text heading each unit.**
- **Composition of place and the application of the senses.** Ignatius's petition in the meditation on hell is "an interior sense of the pain which the damned suffer, in order that, if, through my faults, I should forget the love of the Eternal Lord, at least the fear of the pains may help me not to come into sin" (Mullan 1914, held in the source library).
- **Considerations, then affections, then resolution, then colloquy or nosegay.** Every unit ends in prayer or resolve, never in exposition.
- **Received prayers printed for use.**
- **The saints as the voice of the meditation,** not as evidence under audit.
- **A liturgical frame,** and practice for the hour of death.

The forms lane's full table covered sixteen works and recorded which editions are held and which are missing.

## Decisions taken, 16 October 2026

The maintainer made these decisions after reading the review:

1. **Rewrite the four Claude essays in place.** Not the three-layer architecture, and not a single considerations book.
2. **No page target.** Each essay runs as long as its content earns.
3. **Approved private revelation is included and labelled.** Teresa's vision of hell (*Life* 32) and the Fatima vision of 13 July 1917 are narrated as the Church receives them, marked with their status.
4. **Hell states the few/many witness plainly.** Christ's words come first. Then the Fathers and Doctors who read them as few saved, among them Chrysostom, Augustine, Gregory, Aquinas and Alphonsus. One clause records that the Church has defined no number and declares no one damned.
5. **The doctrinal audit becomes a short graded appendix.** Each essay gets a one- to two-page appendix grading each teaching. The body states each teaching at its degree in one clause. The objection boxes and the verification logs are removed.
6. **The voice rule of 30 September 2026 becomes universal.** It moves from `guidance/theology/angelology.md` into the universal editorial standard and binds all faith and theology work.
7. **Publication.** When the rewrite passes review it goes to `main` through an ordinary push, the deploy gates run first, and the deployment is verified, aiming for before All Saints (1 November 2026).

The GPT series stays untouched.
