# The Four Last Things (Claude): rewrite and review record, 16 October 2026

This record covers the in-place rewrite of the four Claude essays under the
maintainer's decisions recorded in `cold-review-2026-10-16.md`. It records
what was produced, how it was reviewed, the corrections those reviews required,
and the artifacts accepted for publication. Each leaf's `research/scope.md`,
`research/source-audit.md` and `research/source-bindings.toml` hold the
claim-level evidence.

All four essays now speak from inside the faith to a Catholic reader. The Fathers, Doctors, saints and liturgy speak at length in identified public-domain texts. Each essay is set in the close of the liturgical year and moves from the truth to its consideration and then to the reader's response.

Each body states every teaching at its degree in one clause. A short graded appendix, "Teaching and Its Grades", carries the grades. A concise "Scope, Method, and Qualifications" appendix and the references follow it.

Removed from the July edition:

- the objection boxes;
- the "full strength" contrary cases;
- the defined/undefined machinery;
- the corrections of popular piety;
- the verification logs in the notes.

Hell sets out the following plainly, through Irenaeus, Chrysostom, Augustine, Gregory, Aquinas, Bernard, Catherine, Ignatius, de Sales, Alphonsus and the liturgy of the dead:

- the loss of God;
- the fire;
- the worm and the darkness;
- eternity;
- the risen body;
- the martyrs' fear.

It also contains:

- **The few/many witness.** Christ's words come first. Then come Chrysostom, Augustine, Gregory, Aquinas (*ST* I q.23 a.7 ad 3) and Alphonsus. One clause follows: the Church has defined no number and declares no one damned (CCC 1861).
- **Private revelation.** Teresa (*Life* 32) and Fatima (13 July 1917) are labelled with their status.

The series shares one frame, `format.tex`. It provides the title page with epigraph, the sibling line, a reading measure of about 75 characters, and the appendix table widths.

## Cold-review errors

Each essay's audit records how the cold review's confirmed errors were resolved: E1–E4, V1–V10 and D1–D8. Each correction was then checked by an independent quotation lane. In summary:

- **Aquinas on the number saved** is quoted from *ST* I q.23 a.7 ad 3.
- **The Holy Thursday collect** on Judas is quoted, in Latin and in Lasance's English.
- **The other New Testament eternity texts** stand beside Matthew 25:46.
- **Augustine's choice in *De civitate Dei* XXI.9** is confined to the worm.
- **The Supplement's fire** is "of the same species".
- **The Douay–Rheims Mark 9 refrains** are verses 43, 45 and 47.
- **Florence and *nihilominus*:** the general-judgment clause is cited only from Lyons II and *Benedictus Deus*.
- **Florence on the degrees of vision** is cited for *pro meritorum diversitate*.
- **The survival of the self** is graded Defined.

## Reviews

Each essay received two independent reviews after drafting: a content review and a verification of every quotation and attributed claim. Both lanes read the essay whole. The content lanes also rendered and viewed sample pages.

The authors applied every correction. Two items were declined in part, because the needed text was not read in this session: Bellarmine on the real fire, and a "Greek reserve" in Judgment. Each essay's audit records its additions after review.

The coordinator made these doctrinal rulings:

- "the saints in heaven pray for us" is Defined, on DS 1821 and 1867;
- the necessity of the light of glory is Defined, on Vienne, DS 895;
- "the saved are fewer than the lost" is Theological opinion, the common opinion of the Fathers and Doctors, with *Spe salvi* 46 named as the other position held;
- "Judas was lost" is common teaching of the Fathers and Doctors, with Benedict XVI's audience of 18 October 2006 named;
- Teresa's vision is labelled with its basis: canonized 1622, Doctor 1970, no decree on the vision itself.

A final production lane viewed all 128 rendered pages. It found:

- one split closing prayer in Death, now kept whole;
- three series inconsistencies, now made uniform: unnumbered subsections in Hell, Appendix B run-in labels, and colophon alignment.

It also checked fonts (all embedded), PDF title and subject, anchors and bookmarks, and the web editions. In the web editions every endnote resolves and no macro leaks.

These reviews are internal editorial review. They do not constitute ecclesiastical approval or specialist collation.
