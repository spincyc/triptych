\section{Scope, Method, and Qualifications}

\paragraph{Reader and purpose.} This is spiritual reading for Catholics at the close of the liturgical year and for the annual preparation for death. It treats death as the separation of soul and body, its natural and penal character, its transformation by Christ, the hidden hour, Christian grief, the art of dying, the last sacraments, the Church's prayers for the dying and the dead, and the practice of preparation. The particular and general judgment, purgatory and prayer for the dead, hell, and heaven are treated in the three companion volumes and are named here only where the subject requires it. Questions of medical ethics, the determination of death, and funeral law are outside its scope.

\paragraph{Sources.} Scripture is quoted from the Douay--Rheims Bible (Challoner revision) in Vulgate numbering, with Latin from the Clementine Vulgate. The Fathers are quoted in the English of the Ante-Nicene and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers series and, for the \emph{City of God}, in Marcus Dods's translation; Aquinas in the translation of the English Dominican Fathers. Later spiritual writers are quoted in public-domain English: the \emph{Imitation} in William Benham's translation, Francis de Sales in the Rivingtons translation of 1876, Ignatius in Elder Mullan's, Alphonsus and Leonard of Port Maurice in the Dublin translation of 1843, Bellarmine in John Dalton's, Th\'er\`ese in the edition of T. N. Taylor, and the \emph{Craft of Dying} in Frances Comper's modernized edition of 1917. The rites are quoted in Latin from the \emph{Rituale Romanum} of 1872 and the Roman Missal, with English from the \emph{Manual of Prayers} prepared by order of the Third Plenary Council of Baltimore (1889) and from Lasance's \emph{New Roman Missal}. Conciliar acts are cited by Denzinger--Sch\"onmetzer number. The Catechism of the Catholic Church, the Code of Canon Law, and other official texts of the Holy See are quoted briefly in their official English; that text remains the property of the Libreria Editrice Vaticana.

\paragraph{Prayers.} Every prayer printed for use is reproduced in an identified received translation. Where only Latin is given, any English beside it is marked as a gloss and is not offered for recitation.

\paragraph{Grades.} The grades in the preceding table follow the usual distinctions of Catholic theology. A grade states the authority of the teaching as the cited witnesses present it; it does not replace the judgment of the Church's magisterium.

\paragraph{Law and currency.} References to the Code of Canon Law are to the 1983 Code of the Latin Church, as of 16 October 2026. Liturgical references name the book from which they are taken. Nothing here is pastoral or canonical advice for a particular case; the dying and those who care for them should call a priest.

\paragraph{Review.} These pages are a study aid prepared within the Triptych library. They carry no ecclesiastical approval, and they have received no external theological review.
