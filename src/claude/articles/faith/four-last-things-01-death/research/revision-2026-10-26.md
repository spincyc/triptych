# Death — Register Revision, 26 October 2026

## Result and boundaries

The maintainer asked that the register guidance of 21 October 2026 ("Say what
the texts say, plainly", in `guidance/editorial.md`) and the rule on speaking
from within the tradition be applied to this essay. The revision changes the
essay's own prose in the ten body sections and nothing else.

Four kinds of sentence were removed or rewritten:

- paragraph-closing maxims, paradoxes and restatements, for example "Death has
  not been pardoned. It has been conscripted. The enemy remains an enemy, and
  serves", "That is the mercy in the concealment", "In her calendar a
  Christian is born when he dies" and "Love at a deathbed sends for the priest
  in time";
- asserted significance, for example "Everything the Church teaches about
  Christian death is in that room" and the Preface "that answers in a single
  sentence everything the Fathers said about death";
- contrasts against positions nobody holds and staged objections, for example
  "The Son of God did not abolish death by forbidding it to touch him. He
  entered it", "The doctrine is not a theory of biology", and "Someone will
  name the good thief", which is now Bellarmine's own question;
- texts given agency they do not have ("The prayer knows the danger",
  "The Church writes the same counsel on the forehead"), stress devices
  ("Here is the first thing the Christian must know about death") and
  paraphrases of quotations the reader has just read.

Where a cut sentence had been doing connecting work, the plain connection
replaces it, so the argument still moves from one source to the next. Three
examples:

- Section 4 had set Ambrose and Cyprian side by side with a maxim after each.
  Cyprian's paragraph now opens on Ambrose's own distinction: "The other
  sorrow, the distrustful sadness that treats the dead as though they were no
  more, is the one Cyprian of Carthage fought."
- Section 3 closed on "The hour is hidden so that every hour may be lived as
  if it were the last. That is the mercy in the concealment." It now ties the
  hidden hour to Bellarmine's rule and to the Catechism's sentence that
  follows: "God keeps the hour hidden so that we may be ready at every hour,
  and Bellarmine's rule says what that readiness is: to live well to the
  end."
- Section 6 closed on "That is what the craft of dying comes to. It is the art
  of making the last act of life an act of love." It now ends on the account
  of Thérèse's death and the *Craft*'s definition, quoted earlier in the
  section: "Her death is the one the *Craft* describes, given up to God gladly
  and with a willing heart, and her last words were an act of love."

Where a link is the essay's own, it is stated in the essay's voice and not
given to a source: the Preface's agreement with Augustine and Cyprian
(section 8), Monica's death read against the spiritual writers (section 5),
and the canon on Viaticum applied to the sick man's family (section 7, where
the essay now says the canon is addressed to pastors). Teaching degrees are
unchanged: the councils still "define", the Catechism "says" or "states", and
Leonard's appeal to Gertrude is still labelled as private revelation.

## Repairs after cold review

A cold review asked for one blocking repair and four advisory ones, and all
five were made. Closers that paraphrased the quotation just read, or summed up
a section, were cut in sections 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. The two section-end
summaries in 4 and 7 were removed outright. Where a bridge was needed, the
step the reader did not yet have was written instead: Monica's request to be
remembered at the altar now leads to the Church's prayer for the dead in the
Mass, and the Preface paragraph now ties its sadness to Augustine and its
promise to Cyprian. The "A Christian
who ..." portrait survives only in the conclusion of section 9, and the "The
Christian ... therefore" openers in sections 2 and 3 are gone. Section 1 no
longer claims that the liturgical year follows the meditation's order; it
says what the calendar keeps in these weeks. Section 3 again judges that it is
right to fear death and right to hope through it, and it now explains
Ignatius's "do not hinder me from living" in the essay's own sentence. In
section 4 the *Imitation* is credited only with naming what gives "great
confidence of a happy death"; calling these habits is the essay's own
comment. The conservation check still passes with no problems. Body words are
now 9,818 against 10,825 before the revision, a ratio of 0.907. The build
exits 0 and produces 23 pages, SHA-256
`90225bc5b271102b677e7eca99cb419859aea6cbc0375838240b46f3327071df`. The log
is as before: no overfull boxes or undefined references, and only the three
underfull boxes the unrevised text also has. The figures under "Extent" and
"Build" above are those of the first pass.

A second cold-review round accepted those repairs and raised one blocking
finding and one advisory, both in sentences the repairs had introduced. In
section 4, "they are formed before the last hour or not at all" went beyond
the *Imitation*, which warns only that few are made better by infirmity, and
it contradicted the good thief later in the section; the sentence now says
only that these are habits formed over the course of a life. In section 8 the
Preface paragraph no longer speaks of what "has already been heard ... in this
essay", and the statement that the life promised is the baptismal life now
stands as the essay's own sentence rather than as the Preface's meaning. A
third round passed with no findings. Body words are now 9,798 against 10,825,
a ratio of 0.905.

## Print layout after visual review

English *Proficiscere* ended with two lines alone at the head of an otherwise

adjustments answer them without changing any words: a column break between
the Latin and English *Proficiscere*, which now stand whole side by side, and
a space reservation that keeps the *Manual* prayer whole in one column. The
web edition is unaffected. The final PDF has 23 pages and SHA-256
