## Governing purpose

Catholic spiritual reading for the close of the liturgical year and the annual
preparation for death. The essay speaks from inside the faith to a Catholic
reader and moves from the truth of death (separation of soul and body; natural
and penal; conquered by Christ) through its consideration (the hidden hour,
Christian grief, Ostia, the *ars moriendi*) to resolution and prayer (the last
sacraments, the Church's rites for the dying, the monthly preparation for
death), closing in the *Proficiscere* in a received English translation.

## Included

- Ecclus 7:37–40 at the deathbed; Ash Wednesday's *Memento, homo*; Gen 3:19.
- Death as separation (CCC 1005; Vienne, Lateran V in one clause each);
  Augustine's "wrench" (*De civ.* XIII.6); natural and penal (CCC 1006; Trent
  V can. 1; Aquinas's pillar and stone; Ambrose's "remedy"); the debt of nature
  (the *Craft*; *Commendo te*).
- Heb 2:14–15; Augustine XIII.4–5 ("Die, that thou sin not"); Ignatius
  *Romans* 6; *Martyrdom of Polycarp* 9, 14, 18; Phil 1:21–23.
- The hidden hour: Matt 24:42–44, 25:13; the Baltimore *Manual*'s prayer;
  Augustine XIII.10; Rule 4.47 (Latin); *Imitation* I.23; de Sales I.13;
  Alphonsus Consid. I; Bellarmine I.1.
- Christian grief and the communion of saints: 1 Thess 4:12–13; Ambrose I.10;
  Cyprian *De mortalitate* 20–26; Trent XXV (DS 1821); CCC 956, 1053.
- Ostia: *Confessions* IX.10–12.
- The *Craft of Dying*; the litany against a sudden death; the Hail Mary;
  St Joseph; Alphonsus Consid. VIII; Thérèse's last letter and last words.
- The last sacraments: CCC 1525, James 5, Trent XIV can. 1, the anointing form,
  *Accipe, frater*, CCC 1517, can. 922.
- The commendation of the soul, the last-agony ejaculations, the *Subvenite*,
  the Preface for the Dead, the Office of the Dead (Job 7:16, 19:25–26).
- The practice: Ignatius's third rule of election, de Sales's resolution,
  Leonard of Port Maurice's monthly retreat, Alphonsus's Protestation of Death
  and daily prayer for a good death; Advent I (Luke 21).

## Excluded (by the division of labour or the maintainer's decisions)

- *Benedictus Deus* expounded (Judgment's home; one clause and cross-reference here).
- Particular and general judgment, purgatory and prayer for the dead
  (Judgment); hell (Hell); the reunion and the vision (Heaven).
- The former canon-law synthesis on can. 1005, the matter/form objection box,
  the "Plato, not Vienne" charge, the John XXII / 1979 CDF / "resurrection in
  death" material, the separated-soul analysis, and all correction of popular
  belief (removed by decision).
- Medical determination of death, bioethics, funeral law.

## Authority boundary

Each teaching is stated at its degree in one clause; the grades are in
Appendix A (Defined / Church teaching / Common teaching of the Fathers and
Doctors / Pious tradition). The essay is a study aid with no ecclesiastical
approval.

## Consequential negative results

- No public-domain English of Rule of Benedict ch. 4 is held: the library's
  Henderson 1910 English omits chapter 4 (checked 2026-10-16). Rule 4.47 is
  given in Latin with a labelled gloss.
- No public-domain English of Trent was read this session (Waterworth is not
  held as local text). Trent's canons are stated in the essay's own words with
  DS numbers; only DS 1821's Latin clause is quoted.
- The Bellarmine witness reachable online is a modern digital transcription of
  Dalton's 1847 translation, not a facsimile of the 1847 printing; the 1622
  English facsimile (archive.org item TheArtOfDyingWell) corroborates the sense
  ("he who liueth wel, doth dye well"). Quotations are limited to I.1.
- The Rituale Romanum's work locus scheme has no units for the titles quoted, so
  the 1872 Rituale is cited but not separately bound.
