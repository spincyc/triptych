# At the End of Every Why — Research Scope and Qualification Record

## Work identity and governing question

- **Provider and collection:** Anthropic Claude; faith and theology articles. Provider: Anthropic Claude.
- **Genre and profile:** discursive theological article; `guidance/articles.md` governs. Theology governs the whole argument; no canon-law claim is made and no juridical as-of date applies.
- **Series position:** Part III, the conclusion, of *The Creature Before God*, a three-part Claude series; with this part the trilogy is complete. Part I (*Ontological Vertigo*) established received being, present conservation, the one-sided real relation, and the fork of recoil or consent. Part II (*The Due Return*) established the unpayable debt, the virtue of religion and its acts, the freedom of the return, and the open account, ending at an arrow from gratitude toward charity. Part III follows the arrow: the end desired in charity — beatitude, the vision of God, friendship with God, hope in the meantime, and the everlasting sabbath — closing the series deliberately.
- **Series relationship:** an independent Claude edition of the leaf `articles/faith/at-the-end-of-every-why`. It shares the leaf title and subject territory with the GPT edition but is an independent work: its research, argument order, examples, section structure, and prose were produced without reading the GPT edition's sections or main file; only that edition's `research/scope.md` was consulted, for topic parity (the end desired in charity once justice has named necessary action; charity as friendship with God; grace making the voluntary return possible; union as participation, never absorption). The Claude edition deliberately does not replicate the GPT edition's coverage of prayer practice, Eucharistic reception, suffering, scrupulosity, or the mystical witnesses; its own spine is the last end (ST I-II qq. 1–5), the vision (ST I q. 12; *Benedictus Deus*), charity as friendship (II-II q. 23–24), hope (II-II q. 17), and the sabbath (De civ. Dei XXII.30), per its assignment.
- **Language, rite, jurisdiction, and currentness:** English; no liturgical rite, civil jurisdiction, canonical jurisdiction, or mutable discipline governs the argument. All online witnesses were checked on 2026-07-25.
- **Intended reader:** a serious general reader able to follow careful distinctions but not presumed to know scholastic terminology.
- **Governing question:** What is the creature finally for — what stands at the end of every why, once Part I has named the gift of being and Part II the actions required in justice?
- **Thesis:** the will of the rational creature is calibrated to the universal good, which no created good is; the only end that would close its desire is the vision of the divine essence, which no created power can attain; this incapacity is the creature's rank, not its defect; that the end is offered is a datum of revelation — God has ordered man to a supernatural end, and the vision is dogmatically guaranteed, no creature mediating; the love by which the creature moves toward the end is charity, friendship with God founded on God's communication of His own beatitude and infused, not acquired; hope is the virtue of the interval, leaning on the divine help for nothing less than God Himself; the consummation (rest, vision, love, praise; the account open forever) perfects rather than repeals dependence, freedom, and the due return; the trilogy closes with the falling-that-was-carrying arriving at the face of the carrier.

## Excluded scope and material limits

Excluded: treatise-scale accounts of grace, justification, merit, predestination, assurance, perseverance; infused-virtue mechanics and the gifts of the Holy Spirit; Christology and soteriology (presupposed, not developed); ecclesiology and sacraments including Eucharistic theology; purgatory, judgment, hell, the resurrection of the body beyond the definition's boundary; eschatological timing beyond Benedictus Deus's stated scope; mystical theology, contemplative prayer, private revelation, religious-experience phenomenology; the Thomist disputes over the natural desire for the supernatural (named, not adjudicated); pastoral counsel on despair or scrupulosity; judgments about persons.

Material limits (fully stated in the appendix): the last-end analysis presupposes Part I's participation framework; ST I-II.3.8 and I.12.1 are used for the conditional, with Aquinas's theological setting flagged; the desire is never treated as an exigency binding God, and the gratuity of the supernatural end is kept dogmatically protected; the light of glory and "deiform"/"partakers" language assert participation, never absorption; friendship does not abolish latria or the asymmetry; the Trinitarian identification of infused charity is received, not argued; presumption/despair mapping is labeled synthesis with no personal judgments; the Dods and Catechism renderings of XXII.30 are identified separately and not conflated.
