## Source classes and rights

- **Public domain:** Douay–Rheims (Challoner); English Dominican Summa translation; Pilkington Confessions (NPNF 1/1); Dods 1871 City of God (tracked 1871 transcription); Dei Filius Latin (1870 conciliar act); Benedictus Deus Latin (1336 papal act; the received Denzinger text was read at a public transcription, with printed Denzinger editions retaining their own editorial rights).
- **Copyrighted, quoted briefly with attribution:** CCC official Vatican English web text. Corpus Thomisticum's site edition retains its stated rights; it is used as a checked Latin witness by link and no bulk text is incorporated. The unattributed papalencyclicals.net English translation of Benedictus Deus was consulted as a finding aid only and is not quoted in the article.
- **Project content:** the why-chain opening and its three endings; the corridor/room, compass, ceiling, open-mouth, scaffolding, and letters-from-the-road images; the presumption/despair mapping; the friendship-circulation reading of the open account; all formulations not attributed above.

## Consequential negative results and boundaries

- The ascent/revelation seam is expressly marked at the close of the ceiling section; no revealed claim (supernatural end, the vision as fact, infused charity, the Trinity) is presented as a conclusion of the philosophical thread.
- The natural-desire argument (I.12.1) is used at its stated level only; the intra-Thomist dispute about its force is named in the appendix and not adjudicated; nothing treats the desire as an exigency binding God, and Dei Filius's gratuity clause is quoted to protect the point.
- Benedictus Deus is used strictly within its own boundary (purified souls before the resurrection); no claim about eschatological timing, purgatory, or the resurrection body is made beyond it.
- The New Advent transcription of City of God Book XXII was attempted as a cross-check of the tracked artifact; the fetched page content was truncated before chapter 30, so no cross-check is claimed and the tracked, hashed 1871 artifact stands as the sole (and stronger) witness. The Catechism's own rendering of the same sentences (CCC 1720) was verified separately and is identified as a distinct rendering in the article.
- The parity-consulted GPT scope record was used for topic parity only; the GPT edition's sections and prose were not read. Its spine (prayer practice, Eucharist, suffering, scrupulosity, mystical witnesses) was deliberately not replicated; this edition's spine is the last end, the vision, friendship, hope, and the sabbath.
- CCC 1720's rendering of De civ. Dei 22, 30 differs verbally from Dods 1871 ("we shall rest and see, we shall see and love…" vs. "There we shall rest and see, see and love…"); both are identified exactly where they appear and neither is altered or conflated.
- No claim of ecclesiastical approval or independent review is made.
