# Judgment — Register Revision, 26 October 2026

## Result and boundaries

The maintainer asked that the register guidance of 21 October 2026
(`guidance/editorial.md`, "Say what the texts say, plainly", with "Plain is
not flat") and the rule "Speaking from within the tradition" be applied to
this essay. The revision touches only sentences in the essay's own voice in
the nine body sections. Quotations, notes, headings and the closing
*Dies irae* are as they were; the closing section's one-line lead was
reworded.

Four kinds of defect were removed.

- Paragraph closers that restated the paragraph as a maxim or chiasmus were
  cut: "The Advocate and the Judge are one"; "The time for repentance is the
  present. The book is still open, and the hand that writes in it is still
  one's own"; "Every suffering accepted in union with Christ is purgatory
  begun on this side of death"; "The only soul whose judgment a Christian is
  commanded to anticipate is his own."
- Asserted significance was replaced by what the text does. "Then comes the
  word on which the whole constitution turns: *mox*" became "The constitution
  then says *mox*"; "The Gospel of that Sunday ends on a sentence that gathers
  the whole year into its hand" became "The Gospel of that Sunday ends"; "The
  definition is worth reading phrase by phrase, because every phrase carries
  weight" was cut.
- Denials of positions nobody holds were stated positively or cut: "is not an
  abstraction of justice, nor the Father hidden in unapproachable light, but
  the Word made flesh" became "is the Son, the Word made flesh"; "These are
  not two stories about the same event, nor a provisional hearing followed by
  the real one" and "The coming of Christ is not the soul's private meeting
  with him at death writ large" were cut, keeping the positive claim in each.
- Stress devices were removed: "Notice what the good have to show", the
  question "Who are the least of his brethren?" answered at once, "Holy fear
  and blessed hope are not two moods between which the Christian must
  choose", and intensifiers such as "with great beauty", "of great
  boldness" and "astonishingly plain".

The argument was kept moving by giving the reason one source bears on the
next. Where "The saint understood that the particular judgment is not a
stranger's verdict ... The whole spiritual meaning of the particular judgment
lies in that word *now*" stood between Alphonsus's prayer and *Spe salvi* 45,
the text now says that the prayer rests on two things, that the Judge is the
Jesus who died for the soul and that the time for asking pardon ends at
death, and that Benedict XVI states the second. Where "The list rewards slow
reading" introduced a paraphrase of Aquinas's five ways just quoted, the text
now goes straight to the third way, which Aquinas himself illustrates from
Arius and the apostles. Inferences that are the essay's own are stated once
as inferences ("It follows that God's first intention for every soul is the
kingdom"; "It follows that the soul does not sleep after death"), and none
was moved onto a Father. The verdict-and-vindication summary that ended
section 3 was folded into the essay's closing paragraph, so that the thesis
is stated once, at the end. The direct response in "Remember Me at the Altar"
is kept and now follows from Benedict XVI's sentence on charity beyond death.

No claim, qualification, attribution, date, locus or distinction was
removed, and none was added. The grading clauses (Catherine's treatise as
the private revelation of a canonized saint; Justus's appearance as pious
tradition; the nature of the fire named once as open) stand as before.

## Repairs after cold review

A cold review of the first pass asked for one correction of fact and five
smaller changes. Each is repaired as follows.

- **The acts that confess the second judgment (section 3).** The first pass
  opened the section with "Both of the acts that define the immediacy of the
  first judgment go on at once to confess the second." That implied that only
  two acts define the immediacy of retribution after death. Florence also
  defines it and has no clause on the day of judgment, and the appendix lists
  *Ne super his* as well. The sentence now names the two acts that do confess
  the general judgment: "The profession of faith received at Lyons and the
  constitution of Benedict XII, having defined the immediacy of the first
  judgment, both go on at once to confess the second." Nothing is said of
  Florence. ("Having defined" was itself corrected on 27 October; see the
  last section.)
- **The order of *Spe salvi* 44 (section 8).** "He goes on" put the "image of
  hope" sentence after the "frightening image" question, which reverses the
  order of the paragraph. The link now reads "In the same paragraph he
  writes", which asserts no sequence.
- **The degree of the claim about purgatory and prayer (section 7).** The
  first pass had softened the published "in great part" to "in part". The
  published degree is restored: the teaching on purgatory "rests in great
  part on that prayer". The Catechism's own word, "also", is still quoted
  exactly in the next sentence.
- **Where the singer's prayer begins (section 1).** Two consecutive
  paragraphs said that the singer prays from the seventh strophe. The second
  statement is cut. The sentence keeps what it adds, that the sequence is sung
  in the Mass for the dead and ends by asking rest for them, and joins it to
  the reason the Church sings it over the dead.
- **Three devotional closers (sections 2, 5 and 7).** Sections 2, 5 and 7
  ended on closers of the same shape. Section 2 now ends on the sentence that
  calls confession a judgment, and the closer that restated it is cut. In
  section 5 the closer is replaced by the connection the published text gave:
  the book contains every confession, and that is why de Sales can say the
  good will be seen in their penitence and in the fruits of grace. Section 7
  now ends on the practices of November. The unsourced statement that the
  souls who are helped "will not forget it" is cut, together with the rest of
  that closer.
- **The reason there are two judgments (closing paragraph).** The first pass
  joined three ideas with "and" and lost the published reason. The paragraph
  now says in its own sentence that, because a man's case closes at death
  while what he did goes on working until the end of history, God has
  ordered two judgments, and the Church confesses both.

No quotation, note, heading or source was added or changed, and the series
conservation check reports no problem. Three repair sentences say something
the published text had not said in so many words: that the sequence ends by
asking rest for the dead, that God ordered two judgments for the reason
given, and that both acts in section 3 had "defined" the immediacy of the
first judgment. The first two are supported by the sequence's last strophe
and by the *Summa* (III q.59 a.5). The third overstated the degree of the
Lyons profession and was corrected on 27 October.

## Correction, recheck and repair, 27 October 2026

The series record (`four-last-things-shared/register-revision-2026-10-26.md`,
"Correction of 27 October 2026") explains why this work was repeated. In this
essay kimi-k3 had given the round-1 verdict on the repairs above, made both
visual reviews and the space reservation, and written the earlier form of the
section above and the contribution line, which credited the work to the
Claude model. None of that work is relied on here.

A fresh reviewer rechecked the six repairs against the sources: Denzinger in
the bound edition (DS 851–861, 1000–1002 and 1304–1306), *Spe salvi* 44 and
47 and Catechism 1032 at vatican.va, and the retained texts of de Sales and of
the *Summa* (III q.59 a.5). The five advisory repairs hold. The repair of the
blocking finding no longer implies a closed set of acts or anything of
Florence, but "having defined" called the Lyons text of 1274 a definition.
That text is the emperor's profession of faith ("tenet et praedicat",
"firmiter credit et firmiter asseverat") and has no defining formula. The
essay's next sentence says it "teaches", and the scope record names
*Benedictus Deus* alone as defining the immediacy. That was a new blocking
finding.

The repair changed two phrases:

- Section 3: "having defined the immediacy of the first judgment" became
  "having taught the immediacy of the first judgment", which is true of both
  acts. The next paragraph still says that *Benedictus Deus* defined it.
- Section 1: "in the acts that define them she joins them with one word" became
  "in her teaching she joins them with one word". The old phrase implied that
  both judgments, and the Lyons profession, are defined; Appendix A grades the
  particular judgment as Church teaching. The degree of each act stays where
  the body and Appendix A state it.
