\section{Scope, Method, and Qualifications}\label{app:scope}

\paragraph{Reader and purpose.} Spiritual reading for Catholics at the close of the liturgical year and in the annual preparation for death: the particular judgment, the general judgment, purgatory, prayer for the dead, and the holy fear and hope with which a Christian awaits Christ the Judge. It is the second of four essays under the title \emph{The Four Last Things}; death itself belongs to \emph{The Debt of Nature}, the pains and eternity of hell to \emph{The Fire That Is Not Extinguished}, and the vision of God to \emph{Then Face to Face}. It is a study aid, not a catechism or an act of the Magisterium.

\paragraph{Teaching and grades.} Each teaching is stated in the body at its degree in a single clause and graded in Appendix~\ref{app:grades}, using six grades: defined; Church teaching; common teaching of the Fathers and Doctors; theological opinion; pious tradition; approved private revelation. Two witnesses rest on private revelation and are labelled where they are introduced: Catherine of Genoa's \emph{Treatise on Purgatory}, which she says God made known to her by special grace (the private revelation of a canonized saint, honoured but not made the Church's doctrine), and the appearance of the monk Justus after thirty Masses, which Gregory reports (pious tradition). No approved private revelation is used. Nothing here judges the eternal state of any person.

\paragraph{Witnesses and translations.} Scripture is quoted from the Douay--Rheims in Bishop Challoner's revision, with Vulgate numbering. Conciliar and papal Latin is quoted from the \emph{Enchiridion symbolorum}; where English is given for these acts outside quotation marks it is a paraphrase, and the Catechism's official English is used where it quotes them. The same holds for the liturgical and monastic Latin: English given outside quotation marks for a line of the \latin{Dies irae}, the \latin{Te Deum}, the Requiem Offertory or the Rule of St Benedict is a paraphrase, not a translation for prayer. Trent is quoted in Waterworth's English (1848). The liturgical Latin is that of the \emph{Missale Romanum} printed at Tournai in 1889; the 1962 and Postconciliar Missals are named for the readings and chants they appoint. The English of the \latin{Dies irae} is W.~J. Irons's, as printed in \emph{Hymns Ancient and Modern}; the verse of the \latin{Te Deum} on the Judge is taken from the Latin printed in J.~H. Blunt's \emph{Annotated Book of Common Prayer} (1866), and the Rule of St Benedict from the Latin text of Wikisource. The Fathers are quoted in the nineteenth-century translations of the \emph{Ante-Nicene} and \emph{Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers} and of Marcus Dods (Augustine, \emph{The City of God}); Gregory's \emph{Dialogues} in the English of 1608 as re-edited in 1911; Aquinas in the translation of the English Dominican Fathers. The Supplement to the \emph{Summa}, quoted from question 88, article 1, was compiled after Aquinas's death from his commentary on the \emph{Sentences}. Alphonsus is quoted in the Dublin translation of 1843, de Sales in the Rivingtons translation of 1876, Ignatius in Elder Mullan's of 1914, the \emph{Imitation} in William Benham's, and Catherine of Genoa in the London translation of 1858.

\paragraph{Rights.} The texts named above are in the public domain in the United States. The \emph{Catechism of the Catholic Church}, Benedict~XVI's \emph{Spe salvi}, and the 1979 letter of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith are official texts of the Holy See, quoted briefly with attribution; they are not relicensed by this publication.
