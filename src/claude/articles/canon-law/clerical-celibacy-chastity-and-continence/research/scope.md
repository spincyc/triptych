- **Governing question:** what does the current law of the Catholic Churches oblige of clerics in the matter of chastity, continence, and celibacy; on what authority; with what effects; and how do the Latin and Eastern disciplines differ?
- **Thesis (argued in epistemic order, not announced):** the three words name three different things — a virtue binding every baptized person by state of life, an abstention from sexual activity, and an unmarried status with three distinct juridical faces — and almost every serious confusion in this field is a collapse of one into another. The Latin Church obliges continence and therefore celibacy (c. 277 §1), attaches the obligation by a public act before the diaconate (c. 1037), enforces it by a diriment impediment (c. 1087) and a penal norm (c. 1394 §1), and relaxes it by two distinct acts of two distinct authorities (cc. 290–292); the Eastern common law obliges chastity of celibate and married clerics alike (CCEO c. 374), honours both states by name (c. 373), refers the admission of married men outward (c. 758 §3), and agrees exactly with the Latin Code that no cleric may marry after ordination (c. 804); the divergence is a Western development rather than an Eastern departure, and CCEO c. 2 requires the Eastern canons to be read from the Eastern tradition rather than against a Latin norm; and the Latin Code has left one question genuinely unsettled — whether c. 277 §1's continence binds the married permanent deacon — which no authentic interpretation resolves.

## Argument order

1. Three obligations in one sentence: c. 277 §1, c. 599, CCEO c. 374.
2. Fixing the terms: chastity (CCC 2337, 2348–2350), continence, celibacy in its three juridical faces; the three-term table.
3. Canon 277 clause by clause: the double object and the *ideoque*; the motive clause against *Presbyterorum ordinis* 16; §2 and §3; what kind of law this is; obligation not vow.
4. How the obligation attaches: validity and liceity (cc. 1024–1025); selection (c. 1029); declaration and public assumption (cc. 1036–1037); age and the wife's consent (cc. 1031 §2, 1050 3°); the simple impediment (c. 1042 1°) and its reserved dispensation (c. 1047 §2 3°).
5. The impediment and the penal backstop: c. 1087; the reserved dispensation (c. 1078 §2 1°) and the danger-of-death exception for deacons (c. 1079 §1); c. 1394 §1; the placement of c. 1398 under offences against the person.
6. Loss of the clerical state and dispensation from celibacy as two distinct acts (cc. 290–293, 976), with the verified canon inventory.
7. The Eastern common law from the *Acta*: cc. 1, 2, 373–375, 758 §3, 762, 804, 795–796, 180 3°, 285 §2, 390, 394–398, 1453; the eight-question comparative table.
8. The Churches *sui iuris*: the count reconstructed honestly; the 2014 dicasterial statement; the twenty-four-row table; the diaspora history and the 2014 dispositions.
9. The permanent diaconate: *Lumen gentium* 29; c. 288 and the two readings; the 1998 Basic Norms and Directory; widowhood, the reserved dispensation, and the 1997 criteria as the 1998 notes report them; what the wife's consent is not.
10. How the Western discipline was built: Elvira, Nicaea and Paphnutius, Siricius, Trullo, Lateran I and II with Pisa 1135, Trent, the 1917 Code; the chronology table.
11. Married priests in the Latin Church: *Sacerdotalis caelibatus* 42; *Anglicanorum coetibus* VI and the Complementary Norms.
12. The modern magisterial file: the Council, *Sacerdotalis caelibatus*, *Pastores dabo vobis*, the Amazon synod and *Querida Amazonia*.
13. The arguments, each at its strongest; three arguments that do not work; what would have to change.
14. Where a real question belongs: the distinctions, the competence table, and the referral.

Bounded to that page and date.
- *Querida Amazonia*'s official English delivery contains no occurrence of "celibacy", "celibate", "viri probati", "married men", "ordain", or "ordination"; the only cognate is "ordained ministers" at n. 93. Literal case-insensitive search over the text extracted from the exact response; bounded to one language of one delivery.
- No official Holy See online source enumerating the Churches *sui iuris* with a current total was located: the Dicastery for the Eastern Churches' pages describe categories only and point to the printed *Oriente cattolico*; the *Annuario Pontificio* is a printed book; the dicastery's S.I.C.O. review online stops at 2013. The article's count is therefore a reconstruction and is marked as such.
- The text of Paul VI's letter of 10 October 1965 to Cardinal Tisserant is not obtainable at any Holy See delivery; it was never published in *AAS* 57 (1965), whose whole volume was searched, and the Holy See's index of his 1965 letters lists only *Laeto animo* of 9 November 1965 to the same addressee. The letter is attested only in the note to *Sacerdotalis caelibatus* n. 2, which also fixes the date at 10 October and the reading in the general congregation at the following day.
- The circular letter of the Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments, Prot. n. 26397 of 6 June 1997, was not located at any Holy See delivery; its content is reported only as the 1998 Basic Norms (note 44) and Directory (note 226) state it.
- The three Eastern diaspora decrees of 1929–1930 were not examined at their own *Acta* pages; they are reported as the 2014 act quotes and cites them.
- Gelasius of Cyzicus was not obtained at any direct witness and is reported only as Hefele reports him. No Greek witness for the Trullan canons was obtained. No public-domain English translation of Elvira c. 33 was verified, and none is offered as one.
- Delivery defects found and recorded, not repaired: the English Code delivery's "aVected"; the Latin Code delivery's "3 ab impedimento" at c. 1047 §2; the Latin *Sacerdotalis caelibatus* delivery's *posset conferr* and *eirum*; the 1998 deacon documents' "OIC"; Percival's "Ballarmin".
