## 1. Introit

**Reference:** Psalm 24:16, 18, with psalm verse 24:1–2 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 25 in most modern Bibles).

Respíce in me, et miserére mei, Dómine: quóniam únicus, et pauper sum ego: vide humilitátem meam, et labórem meum: et dimítte ómnia peccáta mea, Deus meus. Ps. ibid., 1–2 Ad te, Dómine, levávi ánimam meam: Deus meus, in te confído, non erubéscam. ℣. Glória Patri.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 24 is an acrostic lament that asks forgiveness, covenantal guidance, rescue from enemies, and finally the redemption of Israel. The Introit makes its solitary sufferer's appeal and opening act of trust the assembly's entrance voice.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's stopping point after the doxology is retained, with the secondary's explicit repeat incipit documented above.

## 2. Collect

Protéctor in te sperántium, Deus, sine quo nihil est válidum, nihil sanctum: multíplica super nos misericórdiam tuam; ut, te rectóre, te duce, sic transeámus per bona temporália, ut non amittámus ætérna. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical oration confesses that strength and holiness depend on God, asks multiplied mercy, and petitions for divine rule and guidance through temporal goods without loss of eternal goods. As the Collect, it gathers the assembly's prayer before the readings.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 3. Epistle

**Reference:** 1 Peter 5:6–11.

Caríssimi: Humiliámini sub poténti manu Dei, ut vos exáltet in témpore visitatiónis: omnem sollicitúdinem vestram proiciéntes in eum, quóniam ipsi cura est de vobis. Sóbrii estóte, et vigiláte: quia adversárius vester diábolus tamquam leo rúgiens círcuit, quærens quem dévoret: cui resístite fortes in fide: sciéntes eándem passiónem ei, quæ in mundo est, vestræ fraternitáti fíeri. Deus autem omnis grátiæ, qui vocávit nos in ætérnam suam glóriam in Christo Iesu, módicum passos ipse perfíciet, confirmábit solidabítque. Ipsi glória, et impérium in sǽcula sæculórum. Amen.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** First Peter was read in full. The letter grounds a largely Gentile Christian people in baptismal new birth, Christ's suffering and resurrection, holy conduct amid social hostility, and future glory. Chapter 5 addresses elders and the flock before humility, entrusted care, sobriety, resistance, worldwide solidarity, and God's strengthening converge in the appointed conclusion.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 4. Gradual

**Reference:** Psalm 54:23, 17, 19 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering; Psalm 55 in most modern Bibles).

Iacta cogitátum tuum in Dómino: et ipse te enútriet. ℣. Dum clamárem ad Dóminum, exaudívit vocem meam ab his, qui appropínquant mihi.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 54 is a lament over violence in the city and betrayal by a trusted companion. Prayer at evening, morning, and noon leads to confidence that God hears, redeems, and sustains. The Gradual places that entrusted care immediately beside First Peter's command to cast every anxiety on God.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 5. Alleluia

**Reference:** Psalm 7:12 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering).

Allelúia, allelúia. ℣. Deus iudex iustus, fortis et pátiens, numquid iráscitur per síngulos dies? Allelúia.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalm 7 is an individual appeal by a pursued and accused worshipper to the God who searches hearts and judges peoples. Its movement from plea to confidence ends in praise of divine justice. Before the Gospel, the Alleluia holds God's strength and patience together rather than opposing judgment to mercy.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 6. Gospel

**Reference:** Luke 15:1–10.

In illo témpore: Erant appropinquántes ad Iesum publicáni et peccatóres, ut audírent illum. Et murmurábant pharisǽi et scribæ, dicéntes: Quia hic peccatóres récipit, et mandúcat cum illis. Et ait ad illos parábolam istam, dicens: Quis ex vobis homo, qui habet centum oves: et si perdíderit unam ex illis, nonne dimíttit nonagintanóvem in desérto, et vadit ad illam, quæ períerat, donec invéniat eam? Et cum invénerit eam, impónit in húmeros suos gaudens: et véniens domum, cónvocat amícos et vicínos, dicens illis: Congratulámini mihi, quia invéni ovem meam, quæ períerat? Dico vobis, quod ita gáudium erit in cælo super uno peccatóre pæniténtiam agénte, quam super nonagintanóvem iustis, qui non índigent pæniténtia. Aut quæ múlier habens drachmas decem, si perdíderit drachmam unam, nonne accéndit lucérnam et evérrit domum, et quærit diligénter, donec invéniat? Et cum invénerit, cónvocat amícas et vicínas, dicens: Congratulámini mihi, quia invéni drachmam, quam perdíderam? Ita dico vobis: gáudium erit coram Angelis Dei super uno peccatóre pæniténtiam agénte.

**Rubric:** Credo.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Luke 14–17 and the Gospel's Jerusalem-journey frame were read in full. Jesus' table teaching and demands of discipleship precede the tax collectors' approach and the religious leaders' complaint; the two appointed parables and the following lost son defend his saving welcome by joining divine initiative, repentance, and shared joy. The Gospel proclaims that controversy and Christ's answer as the Sunday's central narrative.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 7. Offertory

**Reference:** Psalm 9:11–13 (Vulgate/Septuagint numbering).

Sperent in te omnes, qui novérunt nomen tuum, Dómine: quóniam non derelínquis quæréntes te: psállite Dómino, qui hábitat in Sion: quóniam non est oblítus oratiónem páuperum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** Psalms 9–10 form one acrostic in the Greek and Latin tradition. Thanksgiving for God's worldwide justice and rule turns toward the oppressed, Zion, and the afflicted person's cry. At the Offertory, those sought by mercy answer with trust, praise, and remembrance of the poor.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 8. Secret

Respíce, Dómine, múnera supplicántis Ecclésiæ: et salúti credéntium perpétua sanctificatióne suménda concéde. Per Dóminum nostrum Iesum Christum, Fílium tuum: Qui tecum vivit et regnat in unitáte.

**Rubric:** Preface of the Most Holy Trinity.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this non-biblical prayer asks God to look favorably on the supplicating Church's gifts and grant that they be received with enduring sanctification for believers' salvation. The Secret turns the Introit's personal request for God's gaze into an ecclesial petition over the offered gifts.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; the baseline's extended conclusion is retained and the secondary's abbreviated conclusion is documented above.

## 9. Communion

**Reference:** Luke 15:10.

Dico vobis: gáudium est Angelis Dei super uno peccatóre pæniténtiam agénte.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** this verse closes the lost-coin parable and repeats the lost-sheep parable's heavenly interpretation. The Communion antiphon gives the Eucharistic assembly the angels' joy over repentance without separating welcome from conversion.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.

## 10. Postcommunion

Sancta tua nos, Dómine, sumpta vivíficent: et misericórdiæ sempitérnæ prǽparent expiátos. Per Dóminum.

**Immediate context and liturgical function:** after reception, the Church asks that the holy things received give life and prepare the cleansed faithful for everlasting mercy. The Postcommunion therefore directs present sacramental reception toward continuing purification and the eschatological end.

**Verification status:** facsimile-collated; no unresolved wording issue.
