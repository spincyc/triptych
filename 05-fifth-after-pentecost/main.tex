\weektitle{Fifth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Quinta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: reconciliation before sacrifice}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Exaudi, Domine, vocem meam} (Ps. 26)}{Seeking the saving God}{St. Augustine on Psalm 26; St. Anselm on desire for God}{The entrance cry is personal and urgent: prayer seeks the face that makes peace possible.}
\properrow{Collect}{Love God in all things and above all things}{Ordered charity}{St. Augustine, De doctrina christiana I; St. Bernard, On Loving God}{The collect gives the key: all reconciliation depends on loving God above every injury.}
\properrow{Epistle}{1 Pet. 3:8-15}{Unity, compassion, blessing, peace}{St. Cyprian on unity; St. John Climacus on meekness}{Peter turns holiness into a social form: bless, seek peace, sanctify Christ in the heart.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 5:20-24}{Leave the gift; be reconciled}{St. John Chrysostom on Matthew; St. Jerome on the Sermon on the Mount}{Sacrifice cannot bypass charity. The altar itself sends the worshipper toward the offended brother.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Benedicam Dominum... providebam Deum} (Ps. 15)}{God before the eyes}{St. Ignatius of Antioch on unity around the altar}{Offering becomes true when the heart stands before God without cherished resentment.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Unam petii a Domino} (Ps. 26)}{Dwelling in the house of the Lord}{St. Augustine on the one desire}{The one thing sought purifies many divided passions.}
\end{properstable}

\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Matthew 5 commands reconciliation before offering the gift at the altar; 1 Peter commands unity, compassion, blessing, and the pursuit of peace.\\
Allegorical & The altar points to Christ's sacrifice and the Church's Eucharistic offering, where fractured charity contradicts the sacrament of unity.\\
Moral & The worshipper must leave resentment, contempt, and evasive delay, then seek peace in the concrete form appropriate to the offense.\\
Anagogical & Reconciliation before the altar prepares the soul for the peace of the heavenly temple, where no hatred can enter the vision of God.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Symbolic Through-Line Extraction}
\textbf{Symbolic field.} The signs are voice, face, altar, gift, brother, anger, peace, house, and one desire. The Introit's cry to be heard is tested by the Gospel's demand that worship become reconciled truth.

\textbf{Proper-to-proper movement.} The Introit asks God not to abandon; the Collect orders love above all things; the Epistle commands blessing, unity, and peace; the Gospel interrupts sacrifice for reconciliation; the Offertory fixes God before the eyes; the Communion gathers desire into dwelling in the Lord's house.

\textbf{Sacramental arc.} The altar is not a symbol of escape from the brother but the place where false division is exposed. Eucharistic unity requires the worshipper to seek peace according to truth before offering the gift.

\textbf{Homiletic synthesis.} The through line is purified offering. The Mass takes the worshipper from a cry for divine hearing to the hard work of being hearable: anger, contempt, and unreconciled memory must pass through charity before sacrifice is whole.

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Matthew 5 moves from the external act of murder to anger, contempt, and unreconciled memory.
\item Christ says, ``if thy brother hath anything against thee.'' The initiative belongs to the worshipper even when pride prefers delay.
\item The altar image gives urgency: even a sacred act pauses before the demand of charity.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. John Chrysostom}{Be reconciled so the work may not remain imperfect.}{Use this to show that unreconciled worship is unfinished worship.}
\witnessrow{St. Gregory the Great}{Strife throws away what should aid remission.}{This gives a sharp warning without weakening the mercy of confession.}
\witnessrow{St. Jerome}{The text says the brother has something against you.}{Preach initiative: do not wait for the other person to make peace easy.}
\witnessrow{St. Cyprian}{Unity belongs to the sacrifice of the Church.}{Connect private resentment to ecclesial communion.}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Altar and brother & This Sunday makes reconciliation liturgical. The movement is not from worship to ethics as an afterthought; ethics is demanded by the altar.\\
Interior and communal peace & The Epistle's humility and the Gospel's reconciliation reveal that peace starts in the sanctified heart and becomes ecclesial communion.\\
Justice and mercy & Christ does not abolish justice; he deepens it from external nonviolence to purified memory, speech, and intention.\\
One desire & The Communion's \latin{Unam petii} gathers the scattered soul. The person who wants one thing can surrender the many claims of resentment.\\
Prayer focus & Before offering prayer, name one relationship needing repair and take the first truthful step toward peace.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Distinguish reconciliation from pretending no wrong occurred; truth and charity must meet.
\item Ask hearers to examine contemptuous speech, not only obvious anger.
\item Recommend a concrete step: apology, restitution, intercessory prayer, or counsel where direct contact would be imprudent.
\end{itemize}
