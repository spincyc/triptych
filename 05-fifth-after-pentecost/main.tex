\weektitle{Fifth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Quinta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: reconciliation before sacrifice}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Exaudi, Domine, vocem meam} (Ps. 26)}{Seeking the saving God}{St. Augustine on Psalm 26; St. Anselm on desire for God}{The entrance cry is personal and urgent: prayer seeks the face that makes peace possible.}
\properrow{Collect}{Love God in all things and above all things}{Ordered charity}{St. Augustine, De doctrina christiana I; St. Bernard, On Loving God}{The collect gives the key: all reconciliation depends on loving God above every injury.}
\properrow{Epistle}{1 Pet. 3:8-15}{Unity, compassion, blessing, peace}{St. Cyprian on unity; St. John Climacus on meekness}{Peter turns holiness into a social form: bless, seek peace, sanctify Christ in the heart.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 5:20-24}{Leave the gift; be reconciled}{St. John Chrysostom on Matthew; St. Jerome on the Sermon on the Mount}{Sacrifice cannot bypass charity. The altar itself sends the worshipper toward the offended brother.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Benedicam Dominum... providebam Deum} (Ps. 15)}{God before the eyes}{St. Ignatius of Antioch on unity around the altar}{Offering becomes true when the heart stands before God without cherished resentment.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Unam petii a Domino} (Ps. 26)}{Dwelling in the house of the Lord}{St. Augustine on the one desire}{The one thing sought purifies many divided passions.}
\end{properstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Altar and brother & This Sunday makes reconciliation liturgical. The movement is not from worship to ethics as an afterthought; ethics is demanded by the altar.\\
Interior and communal peace & The Epistle's humility and the Gospel's reconciliation reveal that peace starts in the sanctified heart and becomes ecclesial communion.\\
Prayer focus & Before offering prayer, name one relationship needing repair and take the first truthful step toward peace.
\end{connectionstable}
