\weektitle{Fifth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Quinta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: ordered love makes sacrifice truthful and fruitful for all}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Exaudi, Domine, vocem meam} (Ps. 26:7, 9)}{Urgent prayer to the saving God}{The worshipper begins by seeking the divine face and refusing abandonment, not by rehearsing the grievance against a brother.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Deus, qui diligentibus te bona invisibilia praeparasti}}{Infused and ordered love}{God is asked to pour his love into the heart so he may be loved in all things and above all things.}
\properrow{Epistle}{1 Pet. 3:8--15}{Unity, compassion, blessing, peace}{Peter gives charity a social form: reject retaliation, govern speech, seek peace, and sanctify Christ in the heart.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Protector noster aspice, Deus} (Ps. 83:10, 9)}{God's searching, protecting gaze}{Before Christ searches anger and contempt, the Church places herself under God's searching and protecting gaze.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{Domine, in virtute tua laetabitur rex} (Ps. 20:1)}{Strength, salvation, joy}{Peace is sought from received strength rather than the power gained by winning a quarrel.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Matt. 5:20--24}{Anger, contempt, altar, reconciliation}{The altar requires truthful charity: the worshipper who has injured a brother must take the initiative toward reconciliation.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Benedicam Dominum} (Ps. 15:7--8)}{God kept before the eyes}{Offering begins by displacing the self-justifying gaze and placing the Lord at the worshipper's right hand.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Unam petii a Domino} (Ps. 26:4)}{One desire, one house}{Desire for God's house gathers the divided heart toward the communion that resentment contradicts.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Quos caelesti, Domine, dono satiasti}}{Hidden cleansing and protection}{Those fed by the heavenly gift ask cleansing from hidden faults and freedom from the enemy's snares.}
\end{properstable}
\begin{sensestable}
Literal & Matthew moves from murder to anger and contempt, then commands the worshipper whose brother has something against him to seek reconciliation before offering; Peter commands concord, compassion, blessing, disciplined speech, and pursuit of peace.\\
Allegorical & The altar finds fulfillment in Christ's sacrifice and the Church's Eucharistic offering. Communion in Christ's one body exposes the contradiction of cherishing contempt while receiving the sacrament of unity.\\
Moral & The hearer must govern speech, refuse retaliation, acknowledge harm done, and take the first truthful and prudent step toward peace; reconciliation does not require denial of wrong or unsafe contact.\\
Anagogical & Ordered love and reconciled worship prepare the soul for the one house and final vision of God, where contempt cannot enter and communion is complete.\\
\end{sensestable}

\section*{A Moral Anatomy of Sacrifice}
The propers address reconciliation through the faculties by which communion is either fractured or restored. Read down the body: grace does not bypass speech, sight, movement, or material responsibility; it orders the whole person toward one ecclesial offering.

\begin{fourstable}{Faculty or relation}{Proper}{Disorder exposed}{Formation given}
Voice and face & Introit & Affliction can become an inward rehearsal of accusation or abandonment. & The voice cries to God and seeks his face before judging the brother.\\
Heart & Collect; Epistle & Civility without charity cannot reach anger's root. & God infuses love; Christ is sanctified within as the heart's governing allegiance.\\
Eyes & Gradual; Offertory & Self-justification sees only the other's fault. & The reader first stands under God's gaze, keeps the Lord in sight, and receives counsel.\\
Tongue & Epistle; Gospel & Guile, insult, \latin{raca}, and \latin{fatue} make inward contempt socially destructive. & Evil speech is restrained; blessing replaces retaliatory naming.\\
Feet & Epistle; Gospel & Delay protects resentment and lets ritual substitute for repair. & Seek peace and go first toward the brother when the grievance is one you caused.\\
Hands and gift & Gospel; Secret & A private offering can conceal a private refusal of communion. & What each offers to God's name is ordered to the salvation of all.\\
Whole person and house & Communion & Many grievances divide desire into competing loyalties. & The one desire is to dwell in the Lord's one house and behold him.\\
Hidden interior & Postcommunion & Reception can be mistaken for proof of innocence. & The heavenly gift deepens dependence by cleansing secret faults and guarding against snares.\\
\end{fourstable}

\section*{Anger's Escalation and the Liturgy's Countermovement}

\begin{studybox}{Two opposed sequences}
\centering
\small
\textbf{Disintegration:}\quad injury $\rightarrow$ anger $\rightarrow$ contempt $\rightarrow$ insult $\rightarrow$ retaliation $\rightarrow$ fractured offering

\medskip
\textbf{Liturgical formation:}\quad God hears $\rightarrow$ love is infused $\rightarrow$ Christ is sanctified in the heart $\rightarrow$ blessing replaces retaliation

$\downarrow$

repair is initiated $\rightarrow$ each gift serves all $\rightarrow$ one desire enters one house $\rightarrow$ hidden faults are cleansed
\end{studybox}

The countermovement is not a technique for winning a dispute. Its decisive verbs belong first to God: he hears, infuses, strengthens, receives, feeds, cleanses, and protects. Human initiative toward repair becomes possible inside that prior action.

\section*{Who Owes What? A Responsibility Map}
Matthew 5 and 1 Peter 3 address different directions of injury. Keeping them distinct prevents both evasion and coercion.

\begin{fourstable}{Situation}{Controlling text}{Basic responsibility}{Necessary qualification}
I caused a justified grievance & Matt. 5:23--24; Augustine & Remember, go, acknowledge the injury, and make truthful repair or restitution where possible. & The command begins with responsibility for harm caused, not with a demand that the injured person minimize it.\\
I suffered injury & 1 Pet. 3:8--12 & Refuse retaliation, restrain evil speech, bless, and seek peace. & Forgiveness does not call evil good or automatically restore trust.\\
Responsibility is mutual or unclear & Both readings & Examine one's own acts without inventing false equivalence; seek competent counsel when facts or duties are disputed. & Peace is not served by a confession of wrong that is untrue or manipulated.\\
Contact would be coercive or unsafe & Prudential application & Maintain necessary boundaries and pursue justice, prayer, counsel, or indirect repair as circumstances permit. & Direct contact is not automatically required where abuse, danger, or coercive control is present.\\
\end{fourstable}

\begin{studybox}{Five related acts that are not identical}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.4in,labelwidth=1.25in,itemsep=0.2em]
\item[Forgiveness] relinquishes personal vengeance and entrusts judgment to God.
\item[Reconciliation] restores a relationship and ordinarily requires truth and participation from both sides.
\item[Restitution] repairs, as far as possible, damage one has caused.
\item[Restored trust] grows from truth, repentance, and tested reliability; it cannot simply be declared.
\item[Boundaries] can protect persons and goods while forgiveness and justice are still being pursued.
\end{description}
\end{studybox}

\section*{Each for All: The Topology of the Secret}
The Secret widens the Gospel beyond private conflict management. It asks that what each person offers to God's name may profit everyone unto salvation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=10mm and 12mm]
\node[guide node,text width=0.20\linewidth] (many) {many persons\\many histories\\many gifts};
\node[guide emphasis,text width=0.22\linewidth,right=of many] (altar) {one altar\\one offering in Christ};
\node[guide node,text width=0.24\linewidth,right=of altar] (all) {the salvation of all\\one desired house};
\draw[guide arrow] (many) -- node[above,font=\scriptsize]{what each offers} (altar);
\draw[guide arrow] (altar) -- node[above,font=\scriptsize]{received and ordered} (all);
\node[above=7mm of altar,align=center,font=\small] (collect) {Collect: love God \emph{in all} and \emph{above all}};
\node[below=7mm of altar,align=center,font=\small] (communion) {Communion: \emph{one thing} desired in \emph{one house}};
\draw[guide arrow] (collect) -- (altar);
\draw[guide arrow] (altar) -- (communion);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

This convergence explains why resentment is not merely a private imperfection carried alongside worship. The offering is ecclesial by its stated purpose: personal gifts are taken into Christ's sacrifice for a common saving good.

\sectionguard
\section*{Two Patristic Guardrails}

\begin{threestable}{Witness}{Evidence}{Interpretive boundary}
St. John Chrysostom & \emph{Homily 16 on Matthew}: ``He receives not the gift before the reconciliation, indicating great displeasure.'' & The delay of the gift reveals both the gravity of rupture and the holiness of sacrifice. Christ does not dismiss the altar; he refuses to let offering become a refuge from charity.\\
St. Augustine & \emph{On the Sermon on the Mount} I.10.26: ``We are therefore to proceed to reconciliation, when it has occurred to our mind that we have perhaps injured our brother in something.'' & The Gospel directs attention first to injury the worshipper has caused. This controls the examination without pretending that every conflict is symmetrical.\\
\end{threestable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Before Mass: Examination Without Presumption}
\begin{massbox}{Questions to carry through the proper sequence}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item Where have I caused a real grievance, and what truthful repair belongs to me?
\item Where has anger become contempt, retaliatory speech, or a settled way of seeing another person?
\item What would be a prudent and safe first movement toward peace?
\item When the Secret is prayed, can I hear my individual gift being ordered to everyone's salvation?
\item What hidden fault am I asking the heavenly gift to cleanse rather than conceal?
\end{enumerate}
\end{massbox}
