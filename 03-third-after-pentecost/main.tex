\weektitle{Third Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Tertia Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: humility, vigilance, and the shepherd's joy}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Respice in me, et miserere mei} (Ps. 24)}{Poverty before God}{St. Augustine, Enarrationes in Psalmos; St. Francis of Assisi}{The soul begins as solitary and poor, but poverty becomes the place where mercy is seen.}
\properrow{Collect}{God as protector; temporal goods passed through toward eternal goods}{Pilgrimage and right use}{St. Gregory of Nyssa on ascent; St. Augustine, City of God XIX}{Temporal goods are not despised but traversed under God's rule.}
\properrow{Epistle}{1 Pet. 5:6-11}{Humility under God's hand; vigilance against the devil}{St. Leo the Great on humility; St. Anthony and desert vigilance}{The Christian resists not by self-trust but by sober humility and faith.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Luke 15:1-10}{Lost sheep and lost drachma}{St. Ambrose on Luke; St. Gregory the Great on pastoral care}{The murmuring of the righteous is answered by heaven's joy over repentance.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Sperent in te omnes} (Ps. 9)}{Trust of the poor}{St. Basil on care for the poor}{The poor are not forgotten; the offering becomes their prayer in the Church.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Gaudium est Angelis Dei} (Luke 15:10)}{Angelic joy over one sinner}{St. Bernard on conversion; St. Therese of Lisieux on confidence}{Communion is not a prize for the never-lost but the feast of being found.}
\end{properstable}

\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Luke 15 recounts Christ receiving sinners and answering murmurers with the lost sheep and lost drachma; 1 Peter exhorts humility, vigilance, and trust.\\
Allegorical & The shepherd is Christ bearing fallen humanity home on his shoulders, and the woman searching the house images the Church seeking the lost by the light of grace.\\
Moral & The sinner must let himself be found through humility and repentance, while the devout must renounce murmuring and rejoice at another's return.\\
Anagogical & The Communion antiphon points to heavenly joy: one repentant sinner on earth becomes the occasion of angelic gladness in heaven.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Symbolic Through-Line Extraction}
\textbf{Symbolic field.} The controlling signs are poverty, lostness, search, shoulders, house, lamp, vigilance, and joy. The Introit's solitary poor man becomes the Gospel's sheep and coin: lost, noticed, sought, and restored.

\textbf{Proper-to-proper movement.} The Introit asks to be seen in humiliation; the Collect asks to pass through temporal goods without losing eternal ones; the Epistle teaches humility and vigilance; the Gospel reveals divine initiative toward the lost; the Offertory trusts the Lord not to forget the poor; the Communion names angelic joy.

\textbf{Sacramental arc.} Confession and Communion are symbolically present as finding and rejoicing. The sinner is not self-assembled into worthiness; he is carried home, restored to the house, and made a cause of heavenly praise.

\textbf{Homiletic synthesis.} The through line is mercy against isolation. Pride isolates in murmuring; despair isolates in shame. Christ breaks both by searching, carrying, and teaching the Church to rejoice where heaven rejoices.

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Luke 15 begins with publicans and sinners drawing near. The first movement belongs to grace: sinners are attracted to Christ.
\item The lost sheep is not lectured back; it is carried. This supports a homily on confession, absolution, and pastoral patience.
\item The Epistle's devil imagery is not theatrical. Pride isolates; isolation makes the soul easier to devour.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. Gregory the Great}{The shepherd places the sheep on his shoulders.}{Preach Christ's Passion as the shoulders on which the sinner is borne home.}
\witnessrow{St. Ambrose}{The sheep is wearied, not despised.}{Use this to distinguish contrition from self-hatred.}
\witnessrow{St. Peter}{``Casting all your care upon him.'' 1 Pet. 5:7}{Let the scriptural phrase become the invitation to confession and trust.}
\witnessrow{St. Therese of Lisieux}{Confidence and love are her way to God.}{Connect childlike confidence with being carried by the Shepherd.}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Humility and mercy & Peter's command to humble ourselves prepares us to be found. The sheep is carried because it cannot restore itself.\\
Vigilance and joy & The devil hunts by isolation and despair; the Gospel answers with a shepherd who searches and a heaven that rejoices.\\
Murmuring and mission & The Pharisee asks why Christ receives sinners; the Church must answer by becoming the place where repentant sinners are actually received.\\
Poverty and praise & The Introit's poor man becomes the Communion's cause of angelic joy. Poverty under mercy turns into doxology.\\
Prayer focus & Replace anxious self-repair with a concrete act of humble confession and renewed trust in the Shepherd.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Speak directly to shame: the Gospel does not minimize sin, but it puts the initiative of mercy first.
\item Warn the devout against joyless correctness when another sinner returns.
\item Invite confession as the practical enactment of being found and carried.
\end{itemize}
