# Discursive Articles

This profile governs essays and expositions that are not guides to a particular liturgical formulary or Order of Mass and are not repeatable works governed by a dedicated theological-reference profile. The Rosary and Marian-apparition judgment studies follow `guidance/theology/mariology.md`, and comprehensive historical heresy references follow `guidance/theology/heresies.md`, not this article profile. Articles belong under `src/gpt/articles/faith/<document>/` or `src/gpt/articles/canon-law/<document>/` according to their governing question; another provider uses the same taxonomy beneath its own provider directory. They do **not** inherit the 1962 weekly guide's proper inventory, section titles, page architecture, liturgical numbering, or requirement to force every argument through a formulary.

Each article should open with its question or thesis and then follow the argument. The intended reader, coverage and source boundaries, included and excluded material, date range, jurisdiction, method, terminology qualifications, and material limitations belong in a terminal `Scope, Method, and Qualifications` appendix. References and AI-generation metadata remain required, and substantial research notes should be stored beside the source. Keep the structured provenance in the article leaf's `generation-metadata.tex`, import it exactly once as the terminal `Generation Metadata` section, and use the shared renderer rather than handwritten fields.

Do not turn the appendix into a second introduction. Order its fields by their effect on interpretation, state each control once, and move any substantive exposition back into the argument. A short non-authority or urgent reliance warning may remain on the title page when deferral would be unsafe. A disputed attribution, jurisdictional exception, historical uncertainty, or other qualification that changes a particular claim remains beside that claim.

## Faith and theology

A faith or theology article distinguishes the authority and function of its sources. Its first substantive section should present the governing theological or historical problem, not a taxonomy of its sources. Classify important claims as appropriate:

- Scripture and the article's exegetical judgment about it;
- dogma or another definitively held teaching;
- authoritative but non-definitive magisterial teaching;
- liturgical, patristic, conciliar, or saintly witness;
- common theological teaching or a named school;
- disputed opinion, historical judgment, or prudential application; and
- the project's own synthesis or speculation.

Do not describe every recurring or well-supported theological position as dogma, flatten sources of unequal authority into a consensus, or use `the Church teaches` when the cited source establishes only one theologian's judgment. Cite magisterial texts by exact document and paragraph or stable section, attend to the document's genre and addressee, and preserve relevant development or qualification. Patristic breadth is evidence of reception, not by itself a mechanical authority count.

A theological article may be devotional, historical, systematic, biblical, moral, or comparative. Let its title and opening argument identify its subject; record the formal genre and claim boundary in the terminal appendix. Clearly bound exploratory AI synthesis and never attribute it to cited authorities.

## Canon law

A canon-law article keeps a `Legal Scope and Currentness` subsection in its terminal appendix that records:

- the law or code and edition being applied, such as the 1983 Latin Code or the 1990 Eastern Code;
- the authoritative language text and the identified translation, if any;
- the jurisdiction and persons in scope, including Latin or Eastern Church, territory, office, institute, or other relevant status;
- the as-of date through which amendments and official acts were checked;
- whether the question concerns universal law, particular law, proper law, or a combination; and
- the material facts assumed and any facts whose change could alter the result.

Because a legal conclusion is unintelligible without its governing law, a compact identity line may remain below the title naming the controlling code or body of law, jurisdiction, and as-of date. It points to the appendix and does not reproduce the assumptions, amendment history, source hierarchy, or limitations. A jurisdictional or factual qualification that changes a conclusion remains with that conclusion.

Verify current legal claims against official primary sources. Check promulgation, effective date, amendments, authentic interpretations, complementary norms, particular law, and special or proper law that can govern the case. Keep canons from different codes and historical periods separate. A commentary, tribunal practice note, diocesan FAQ, or unofficial translation may illuminate the law but does not replace the controlling text.

Distinguish:

- the text of law from interpretation;
- validity from liceity;
- obligation from permission or recommendation;
- legislative law from administrative act or judicial decision;
- the ordinary rule from indult, privilege, dispensation, derogation, or exception; and
- a general educational analysis from advice on an actual case.

State uncertainty and competing interpretations where they matter. Do not infer a universally binding rule from one territory's policy, apply Latin law to an Eastern Catholic without establishing applicability, or present an article as a canonical opinion from competent counsel. Questions involving rights, penalties, marriage status, sacramental access, office, litigation, or imminent deadlines require a prominent reminder that the reader should consult the competent authority or qualified canonist with the complete facts.

## Mixed articles

An article may join liturgy, theology, history, and law, but it must identify its primary profile and preserve each field's source and authority rules. A theological reason does not itself establish a juridic obligation; a legal permission does not settle a theological or pastoral judgment; a historical practice does not prove present law.

Put a historical timeline, corpus or census rule, evidence key, comparative edition manifest, and current-law matrix in appendices unless the item itself carries the article's argument. Integrate a correction to a popular claim into the relevant argument rather than interrupting the opening with a free-standing claim-audit chapter.

## Completion gate

An article is ready to publish only when:

- its question, scope, audience, thesis, and limitations are explicit;
- its question or thesis begins the substantive body, while its work-wide scope, dates, legal frame, method, and limitations are consolidated in the terminal appendix;
- material claims have exact, fit-for-purpose sources;
- levels of theological or legal authority are not blurred;
- canon-law work identifies its code, jurisdiction, controlling text, and as-of date;
- counterarguments or material uncertainties receive fair treatment;
- original synthesis is distinguished from received authority; and
- references, research records, copyright review, terminal generation metadata, and PDF checks are complete.
