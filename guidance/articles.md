# Discursive Articles

This profile governs essays under `src/<provider>/articles/faith/` and
`src/<provider>/articles/canon-law/`. Use a dedicated profile instead when the work is a
repeatable liturgical, historical, devotional, biographical, or theological
reference. A mixed article names its primary profile and applies each additional
profile only to the claims in its field.

## Governing priorities

1. Make the governing question or claim identifiable from the argument and scope
   record, without requiring an opening thesis statement, and identify whether
   faith, theology, canon law, or a deliberate combination governs the argument.
2. Distinguish received authority, historical evidence, interpretation, and
   project synthesis at every consequential claim.
3. Begin the body with substantive argumentative pressure in the work's natural
   epistemic order; do not inherit another genre's inventory or section template.
4. Verify mutable law and discipline against the competent current sources.
5. Consolidate work-wide scope, dates, jurisdiction, method, and qualifications
   in terminal apparatus without moving claim-local limits away from their claims.

Each leaf keeps `research/scope.md` and `research/source-audit.md`; add a focused
matrix or corpus record when the argument depends on a legal, historical, or
comparative inventory that prose cannot audit reliably.

In mixed work, theological reasons do not create juridic obligations, legal
permission does not settle theology or prudence, and historical practice does
not establish present law.

## Voice and argumentative movement

An article may let its governing claim emerge progressively when stating it at
the outset would preempt the inquiry. Preserve epistemic order: do not name a
cause, category, or conclusion before the relevant argument or received source
has warranted it, and do not present a revealed claim as though it were the
result of a philosophical ascent.

Openings and section seams should derive their force from a real tension in the
subject, evidence, or argument. Do not manufacture suspense with doctrinally
foreclosed questions, assign a reaction or simplistic objection to the reader,
announce what the next passage will prove, or substitute generic uplift for
intellectual stakes. Retain a question when it is genuinely live at that point;
otherwise state the pressure directly and let the next claim answer it.

A conversational article addresses the reader at equal altitude. Concrete
examples, images, and changes of pace should perform analytical work rather than
simulate intimacy or reduce a serious position to a teaching prompt. Each
section should begin and end with enough argumentative consequence to sustain
the reader's attention without repeating the title or previewing the next
heading.

## Faith and theology

Classify important claims as Scripture and exegesis; dogma or other definitive
teaching; authoritative non-definitive teaching; liturgical, patristic,
conciliar, or saintly witness; common or school theology; disputed opinion or
prudential judgment; and source-grounded or exploratory project synthesis.
Exact magisterial document and locus, genre, addressee, development, and material
qualification control. Patristic breadth can show reception but is not a
mechanical authority count. Do not turn one theologian's view into “the Church
teaches,” or attribute exploratory synthesis to a source.

## Canon law

The controlling text is the official law, not a commentary, FAQ, practice note,
or unofficial translation. Verify promulgation, effective date, amendments,
authentic interpretations, complementary norms, and material particular,
special, or proper law. Keep codes, jurisdictions, and historical periods
separate.

Distinguish text from interpretation; validity from liceity; obligation from
permission or recommendation; legislation from administrative or judicial acts;
the ordinary rule from privilege, indult, dispensation, derogation, or exception;
and general study from advice on a case. Do not generalize one territory's policy
or apply Latin law to an Eastern Catholic without establishing applicability.
Rights, penalties, marriage status, sacramental access, office, litigation, and
imminent deadlines require a prominent referral to the competent authority or a
qualified canonist with the complete facts.

## Reader order and terminal apparatus

After title and contents, begin the argument. Put timelines, evidence keys,
edition manifests, corpus rules, and current-law matrices in appendices unless
one carries the argument itself. Integrate corrections to popular claims where
the relevant evidence is discussed.

End the argument with a `Scope, Method, and Qualifications` appendix, then
references and terminal generation metadata. The appendix records reader,
question and claim boundary, included and excluded material, terminology,
period, geography, source hierarchy, method, limitations, and review state. For
canon-law work it contains `Legal Scope and Currentness`: governing code or body
of law and edition, authoritative language and identified translation,
jurisdiction and persons, universal/particular/proper-law boundary, material
facts assumed, amendments checked, and as-of date. A compact title identity line
may name the controlling law, jurisdiction, and as-of date when omission would
make the conclusions unsafe; a local exception or uncertainty stays with the
conclusion it changes.

## Profile gate

The thesis must be answered from exact, fit-for-purpose sources; serious
counterarguments and uncertainties must receive fair treatment; theological and
legal authority levels must remain distinct; and every legal conclusion must be
reproducible from the identified law, jurisdiction, facts, and date.
