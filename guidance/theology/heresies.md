# Historical Heresy Reference Works

This profile governs source-first histories of condemned propositions, persons,
schools, movements, and ecclesial responses beneath `src/gpt/theology/heresies/`.
They are not juridical determinations of a living person's culpability.

## Governing priorities

1. Define and reconcile a controlling corpus before claiming completeness.
2. Preserve the exact doctrinal object, authority, act, censure, jurisdiction,
   evidence, and personal-status limit of each case.
3. Search for expected acts, target texts, fragments, critical editions, and
   early witnesses when a dossier rests on weak or late evidence.
4. Begin with the chronological dossiers and positive doctrine they clarify.
5. Put global corpus, period, terminology, evidence grades, currentness, and
   qualifications in terminal apparatus while keeping dossier-specific limits in
   each dossier.

## Controlling census and terms

`research/corpus-inventory.md` controls every claim of “all,” “complete,” or
“comprehensive.” Each baseline item is a full dossier; alias/subgroup/proposition
assigned to one; compact source-limited notice; contextual controversy not
formally labeled heresy; or reasoned exclusion. Record stable key, normalized
name and aliases, dates/places, object type, baseline witnesses, formal response,
treatment class, evidence grade, location, and unresolved issue. Do not imply a
universal Church registry of culpable persons.

An all-history census reconciles principal Greek and Latin heresiologies;
doctrinal objects in ecumenical councils received by the Catholic Church;
consequential papal, Roman, regional-synodal, and medieval canonical acts;
Reformation propositions addressed by papal acts and Trent; and principal modern
Roman doctrinal censures through the stated date. Compendia and critical editions
are indexes or witnesses, not promulgating authorities.

Canon 751 supplies a present conceptual boundary for heresy, apostasy, and
schism, not a retroactive penal finding. Keep distinct:

1. **historical object** — proposition, person, school, movement, communion,
   schism, discipline, or historiographical allegation;
2. **doctrinal relation** — incompatible or formally condemned proposition,
   counter-definition, unsettled dispute, or disputed/polemical label;
3. **recorded censure** — the act's own grade, including mixed censures rather
   than rewriting every proposition as heresy;
4. **personal status** — text, authorship/acceptance, obstinacy evidence,
   recantation/reconciliation, and penalty, without judging internal forum or
   descendants; and
5. **mechanism and jurisdiction** — exact authority, act, date, locus, territory,
   confirmation, and force.

Anathema, deposition, excommunication, prohibition, or civil penalty does not by
itself make every proposition a solemnly defined heresy; a positive definition
does not provide a complete sociology of its opponents.

## Required records and evidence grades

Each leaf keeps:

- `research/scope.md`: question, reader, thesis, corpus rule, period/geography,
  authority classes, inclusions/exclusions, currentness, uncertainties, rights,
  and review state;
- `research/source-audit.md`: exact acts, target texts, editions, loci, source
  classes, translation status, searches, negative results, and discrepancies;
- `research/corpus-inventory.md`: normalized census, aliases, classifications,
  and exclusion ledger; and
- `research/timeline-audit.md` when a timeline is published.

Prefer the extant formal act; extant target writing or identified fragment;
critical edition, official translation, or documentary collection; contemporary
reports and refutations; then later heresiologies, handbooks, summaries, and
modern reconstruction. Grade the controlling access:

- **A:** extant act and target text;
- **B:** extant act but target known through extracts, judicial propositions, or
  summary;
- **C:** near-contemporary report without extant formal act; or
- **D:** late, hostile, legendary, or substantially disputed reconstruction.

Grade measures access, not gravity. State whether wording comes from the target,
refuter, judicial extract, or recantation. Every grade C/D baseline item triggers
a targeted search of the reasonably accessible act, target text or fragment,
critical edition, manuscript/archive description, near-contemporary witness, and
professional reconstruction; record the boundary and consequential negative
result. Identify every edition and translation; never present a modern
translation as the original act or critical text.

## Dossier and historical discipline

Each full dossier identifies:

- `Object and setting`: aliases, dates, places, proponents/communities, type, and
  evidence limit;
- `Propositions at issue`: checked wording or close paraphrase and its source;
- `Ecclesial response`: authority, act, date, locus, jurisdiction, exact censure,
  positive doctrine, and later confirmation; and
- `Aftermath and qualification`: recantation, survival, diffusion, coercion,
  clarification, agreement, and present terminology.

Report civil exile, confiscation, imprisonment, torture, crusade, or execution
separately from doctrinal acts. Do not infer historical descent from doctrinal
similarity or hide dependence on hostile witnesses.

Preserve the different functions of ancient catalogues, medieval testimony,
mixed Reformation censures, and modern notifications. Where controversy led to
separation, distinguish the condemned historical proposition from present
communions and their members. Include material common declarations,
reconciliations, and clarifications in the aftermath; inherited labels such as
“Nestorian,” “Monophysite,” “Lutheran,” “Calvinist,” or “Anglican” are not present
adjudications by themselves. Put non-Christian religions, ordinary sins, abuse,
political programs, permitted schools, doctrinally undefined schisms, and
unrecoverable accusations in context or the exclusion ledger.

## Reader order and terminal apparatus

After title and contents, enter the earliest boundary and proceed
chronologically, keeping related doctrinal complexes together and stating the
positive Catholic doctrine clarified by major responses. Conclude with recurrent
fault lines without inventing genealogies.

Then give a response-event timeline; alias/context/exclusion accounting; terminal
`Scope, Terms, and Qualifications`; references keyed to acts and source families;
and generation metadata. The scope appendix owns reader warning, corpus and
completeness rule, period/geography, global definitions, current-law boundary,
authority/evidence keys, source hierarchy, method, checked-through date,
uncertainties, rights, and review. No global apparatus precedes the first dossier.
The title itself must not overclaim. Mixed censure, attribution, hostile-witness,
personal-status, and ecumenical limits remain in their dossiers.

The timeline uses one row per datable response and separates ecclesial judgment
from civil enforcement while preserving approximate dates, sessions,
confirmations, mixed censures, and provenance limits.

## Currentness and profile gate

Before installation, recheck current canon law, material DDF acts, and official
common declarations; date only mutable law and present ecclesial claims rather
than giving stable history artificial currentness. Publication, census, dossiers,
and timeline must agree exactly. Source audit is not independent historical,
theological, canonical, ecumenical, or ecclesiastical review, and a broad survey
cannot establish personal culpability.
