# Expositions of the Ordinary and Order of Mass

This profile governs studies of stable Mass texts and ritual sequence in an
identified 1962 or postconciliar Roman Missal. It does not inherit a proper-guide
template. Edition-specific works belong under that edition's `ordinary/` path;
cross-edition studies belong under `liturgy/roman-rite/comparative/`.

An edition-specific reading preface may use `O00` only as a technical assembly
key before variable propers. It is not a title, temporal identity, or public
navigation rule.

## Governing priorities

1. Define the received liturgical object and controlling edition precisely.
2. Keep received text and rite, authoritative interpretation, and editorial
   synthesis visibly distinct.
3. Let each movement's ritual and textual form determine its exposition rather
   than imposing a repeated dossier or equal length.
4. Begin with the received movement, governing theological question, or usable
   comparison.
5. Put global object boundaries, editions, history, numbering, law, and
   qualifications in terminal apparatus while retaining local rubrical and
   historical limits beside the affected movement.

## Object, editions, and sources

Record whether `Ordinary` means the sung Ordinary, broader stable texts, complete
Order of Mass, or one unit. Stable does not mean invariant: distinguish seasonal
omissions, options, roles, ritual-Mass variations, local choices, and proper
slots.

Identify Missal, typical edition, language, territory, Order of Mass, applicable
rubrics or General Instruction, and companion books actually used. A comparison
keeps a separate source trail for each witness. Never construct a generic
“traditional Mass” or “Novus Ordo” from several editions or transfer vocabulary,
roles, rubrics, translations, customs, or options across them. Current
authorization to use an edition is a separate, dated canonical question.

Maintain three layers:

1. received wording, sequence, speaker, action, and condition;
2. authoritative interpretation from Scripture, liturgical books, magisterium,
   and identified historical or theological witnesses; and
3. source-grounded project comparison or synthesis.

A proper used for context remains a proper. Frequency does not make an option or
custom universally stable.

## Required records and analytical contract

Each leaf keeps `research/scope.md`, `research/edition-manifest.md`,
`research/sequence-inventory.md`, and `research/source-audit.md`. Together they
record the exact boundary and incipit of every movement; stable locator; speaker,
minister, addressee, action and mode where prescribed; fixed, omitted, optional,
or local status; principal questions; relevant textual/rubrical differences;
and exact sources used.

An editorial numbering scheme requires a reproducible map to the edition and a
non-official status; historical or devotional antecedents are claimed only when
checked. Do not make a reception pattern an official taxonomy or original
compositional intention.

A sequential study gives each movement analysis proportionate to its
significance: literal ritual function; relevant scriptural, doctrinal, and
patristic illumination; historically supportable development; and relation to
the whole action. These are coverage controls, not mandatory visible headings or
quotas. Read psalm, creed, oration, dialogue, gesture, procession, Canon, and
Communion passage according to their distinct grammar. Use received names,
standard designations, or incipits as primary headings; keep interpretation
subordinate.

The research dossier identifies each received text boundary, principal biblical
locus and type of liturgical use, earliest secure witness or calibrated
terminus, and source-grounded attribution. Do not assign an author when none is
secure. Prayer or ritual wording in English must be an exact identified human
witness; otherwise write third-person analysis that cannot function as a
translation or recitation substitute.

Material historically attached before or after Mass remains outside the Order
unless the controlling book places it there. Research any prescription,
exceptions, effective dates, and suppression from competent sources, and do not
turn devotional proximity or a popular origin story into liturgical identity or
history.

## Reader order and terminal apparatus

After title and contents, begin the exposition. A compact sequence map may lead
only when it is itself a usable guide. Do not front-load definitions, source
taxonomy, edition manifests, evidence keys, numbering history, global ritual
history, current-law surveys, or review limits.

After the synthesis, give terminal `Scope, Edition, and Qualifications`, then any
global development timeline, sequence concordance, numbering audit, or adjacent-
prayer history; references; and generation metadata. The appendix owns the
received boundary, edition, language, territory, option range, inclusions and
exclusions, terminology, source classes, method, historical range, currentness,
rights, limitations, and review state. Exact rubric dependency, cross-edition
difference, disputed attribution, or authority qualification remains beside its
movement.

## Profile gate

Every text and rubric must map to the named edition, language, and territory;
ordinary, proper, option, custom, and exceptional material must remain distinct;
and comparisons must be direct. The sequence inventory must show proportional
literal, theological, historical, and whole-order coverage without forcing
unlike movements into a uniform published template. Editorial numbering and
adjacent devotions require the special controls above.
