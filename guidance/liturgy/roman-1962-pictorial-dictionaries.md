# Pictorial Dictionaries of the Roman Sanctuary at the 1962 Horizon

This profile governs the comprehensive adult-reference pictorial dictionary
of the Roman sanctuary at the 1962 horizon and its use-derived editions. It
also governs their canonical object inventory, shared artwork, plates, and
research records. Universal editorial, source, repository, rights, metadata,
review, and publication rules remain in force.

The family belongs under
`src/<provider>/liturgy/roman-rite/1962/reference/roman-sanctuary-dictionary/`.
The owner root is non-publishable. Its independently printable leaves mirror
under `build/<provider>/` and `doc/<provider>/`.

## Governing priorities

1. Identify an object correctly before explaining, depicting, or classifying
   it.
2. Distinguish universal Roman provision, conditional or privileged use,
   documented regional or religious-community variation, historical use,
   practical furnishing, and unresolved evidence.
3. Let accurate monochrome drawings and comparisons do most of the teaching.
4. Give every reusable fact and artwork asset one canonical owner, and derive
   each audience edition from that owner.
5. Never infer a 1962 norm, historical origin, or postconciliar disposition
   from visual resemblance or current custom.

## Corpus and completeness boundary

The operative corpus covers the material objects provided for or presupposed
by the universal Roman books at the 1962 horizon. It includes ordinary Low
Mass, Missa Cantata, Solemn Mass, pontifical Mass, Requiem, Nuptial and other
ritual Masses, and Holy Week.

Asperges, processions, Exposition, Benediction, Communion outside Mass, and
other ceremonies directly surrounding Mass form a separately labelled
related-ceremonies section. Regional, institutional, and religious-community
variants enter only when materially distinct, historically important, likely
to cause confusion, and supported by an identified source.

Post-1962 additions and changed uses are excluded. They belong to a parallel
edition-specific postconciliar family governed initially by
`postconciliar-illustrated-dictionary-handoff.md`. Objects discontinued before
1962 belong in a historical section ordered chronologically and then by type.

Do not claim completeness until the declared official and material-culture
source corpus has been closed, every inventory category audited, and the
completeness matrix has no undisclosed gap. A scaffold or incomplete
inventory remains a held prototype and says so.

## Publication family and ownership

The family contains one comprehensive adult-reference volume and use-derived
altar-server, sacristan, master-of-ceremonies and trainer, general-reader, and
pontifical-ceremonies editions.

All leaves consume the same canonical object records and artwork. Smaller
editions are curated audience renderings, not editable copies. Their guide
maps declare selection rules, plate order, audience fields, and omissions.

## Source and data contract

Edition-identified official liturgical and ceremonial books control object
presence, status, function, and ritual relationships. Official acts and
particular law control their actual scope. Checked contemporary ceremonials
may establish practical detail not specified by higher sources. Dated,
provenanced objects, inventories, manufacturer records, museum records, and
specialist studies may establish morphology and material history; they do not
establish ritual use by themselves.

Every entry identifies one of these states: universal Roman, conditional or
privileged, authorized or regional, religious-community use, practical or
local furnishing, historical, or unresolved.

Each canonical record includes:

- stable object ID;
- preferred English and Latin names and material aliases;
- category, period, edition horizon, status, ceremonies, and branches;
- identifying appearance and substantive variants;
- dimensions or relative scale only where sourced;
- placement, function, ordinary handler, and disposition;
- whether a server touches, carries, presents, prepares, or only recognizes
  it;
- related and easily confused objects;
- brief sourced symbolism where appropriate;
- source loci, evidence state, rights status, artwork IDs, audience
  selections, and validation state.

Decorative variation alone does not create a new substantive form. Do not
prescribe material, fold, cross placement, ornament, dimension, or color
without evidence appropriate to that claim.

## Artwork and plates

The publications are US Letter portrait, monochrome, image-dominant
illustrated field guides. Design pages and facing-page sequences for visual
discovery rather than rendering the object inventory as uniform cards. Use a
deliberate rhythm of hero drawings, grouped scenes, isolated studies,
comparisons, cutaways, and detail insets; vary scale and placement, and reduce
or move prose before shrinking diagnostic artwork into thumbnails. Dense or
heterodox compositions are encouraged when labels and object identity remain
immediate.

Ordinary plates devote approximately 65--75 percent of their area to artwork
and normally show six to ten distinctive objects, four to six confusable
objects, two or three multi-view vestments, or one major composition with
detail insets. These are density targets, not a repeated page template.

Every publication-facing illustration is a restrained graphite or pencil
drawing on white. Generated artwork contains no words, letters, arrows,
numbers, borders, or semantic marks. TeX owns headwords, labels, status marks,
scale notices, movement paths, and callouts.

Project-owned AI generation and revision are permitted for these
illustrations. Generation is a production method, not evidence: the depicted
identity, morphology, scale, arrangement, ceremonial use, and historical state
must remain controlled by publicly reachable checked sources and the canonical
object record. Do not prompt a model to fill an unresolved factual field or
treat visual plausibility as identification. Generated output supplies no
source evidence; the checked object record controls the depicted facts.

An ambiguous object receives an isolated view and a compact folded,
unfolded, worn, placed, or in-use inset. Use common scale where diagnostic.
Otherwise provide sourced dimensions, a scale bar, or a clear `not to common
scale` notice.

Give an object its own isolated drawing when it can be clearly understood as a
distinct object. When objects are customarily encountered together, also show
and label the combined formation in the relevant plate and text. Keep a
subordinate functional set together when separation would not improve
recognition or use; the incense boat and spoon are one such set. Audit the
whole artwork corpus for accidental duplication, especially images that
purport to isolate one object while depicting its companion without
identifying the combination.

Each asset has a stable artwork ID and a manifest record naming its brief,
generator or creator, date, dimensions, mode, hash, references, corrections,
consumers, rights, and validation state. Audit invented or missing parts,
impossible folds or wearing, false ornament, incorrect scale, duplicate
figures, impossible placement, and ambiguous monochrome identification.

## Reader order

The comprehensive volume begins with a compact title, a short plate and symbol
key, and an actual sanctuary-composition plate. It then proceeds through
church and sanctuary; altar appointments; vessels; linens and textiles;
books; service objects; incense; priestly vesture; deacon and subdeacon;
pontifical and prelatial vesture and insignia; servers, ministers, and choir;
Requiem; Nuptial and ritual Masses; Holy Week; pontifical compositions; and
related Roman ceremonies.

The historical section follows, ordered chronologically and then by the same
object types within each period. Terminal apparatus includes only finished,
populated reader aids supported by the rendered edition. A selective
pronunciation appendix, English--Latin or Latin--English index, visual index,
variant or terminology notes, and numbered source notes may be included when
they contain useful entries; omit an inapplicable or unpopulated aid rather
than printing a placeholder or fabricating entries. Retain concise scope and
qualifications and references actually used, followed by the compact
generation summary and rights colophon in the universal order. Full scope and
source-audit evidence remains in the required tracked records.

The tracked generation metadata remains complete under the universal standard,
but dictionary PDFs must not print per-agent/runtime entries, contribution
histories, or other internal production ledgers. Render each distinct
model/configuration identity once in the compact terminal summary.

Main entries give English and Latin headwords, one-line identity and use,
compact handling status, and at most one brief sourced symbolic association.
Pronunciation belongs in a compact appendix for commonly spoken words and,
where helpful, selectively at first use in the young-server edition.
Citations remain in keyed endnotes unless disputed status requires an
on-page qualification.

## Use-derived editions

The altar-server edition covers everything that may be present around a
server, including objects the server must not handle, and emphasizes
recognition, location, handler, handoff, confusion, and safety. The sacristan
edition emphasizes preparation, assembly, placement, folding, storage, and
ceremonial changes. The MC and trainer edition emphasizes roles, giver,
receiver, destination, branches, checks, and recovery. The general-reader
edition emphasizes identity, purpose, brief symbolism, and history. The
pontifical edition emphasizes vesture, insignia, furnishings, ministers, and
relationships.

## Alpha publication

The family uses the repository-wide alpha model. A dictionary may publish the
checked portion of its inventory when source integrity, rights and lawful
distribution, safety, reproducibility and artifact identity, mechanical
validity, and basic visual usability pass. It does not wait for or record
specialist, priestly, intended-reader, physical-use, or ecclesiastical review.

The first page states `Alpha` and identifies the actual inventory and edition
horizon represented. It also gives one short reliance boundary: the work is a
Triptych study reference, not an official liturgical book or local direction.
Do not repeat that status in running footers, catalog labels, questionnaires,
or terminal pending-review lists.

Every rendered record must have a stable identity, governing rite and edition
horizon, exact source support for printed factual claims, distributable text
and artwork, generated-content provenance, and no known contradictory or
misleading view. A source-bounded unresolved morphology, variant, or practical
detail may be identified locally; plausible placeholder prose may not replace
it. No image may teach unsafe handling of a sacred, hot, sharp, fragile, or
lighted object.

Basic visual usability requires legible images and labels at rendered size,
unambiguous object identity, no clipping or overlap, mechanically consistent
scale notices and consumers, and no impossible construction, folds, wearing,
placement, or simultaneous composition. Publication does not require a human
print trial, photocopy trial, intended-reader test, or external artwork review.

The production manifest records `source_audit_status`,
`rights_status`, `safety_status`, `production_status`,
`alpha_status`, and the exact installed artifact identity. `published` records
deployment of that verified alpha artifact and does not imply a higher
editorial or external-approval state.
