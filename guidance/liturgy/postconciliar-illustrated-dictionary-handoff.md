# Postconciliar Illustrated Dictionary: Production Handoff

## Status and purpose

This is a production handoff, not a publication, catalog entry, publication
commitment, source profile, or claim that the proposed inventory is complete.
It records the decisions needed for a future agent to research and produce a
postconciliar Roman Rite companion to the planned 1962 illustrated dictionary
without treating 1962 terminology, objects, ministers, arrangements, options,
or ceremonial practice as postconciliar norms.

Before creating publication leaves, the future agent must read the universal
[editorial](../editorial.md), [repository](../repository.md), reusable
[source](../sources.md), and [web-edition](../web-editions.md) guidance. It
must then add or revise a genre profile for illustrated liturgical dictionaries
if no governing profile yet exists. The dictionary is not a proper guide and
must not inherit the template in
[postconciliar-propers.md](postconciliar-propers.md). An edition-specific
explanation of the Order of Mass may also require
[ordinary-expositions.md](ordinary-expositions.md), but that profile does not
by itself govern the visual dictionary.

Nothing in this handoff authorizes research acquisition, tracked third-party
images, source migration, content drafting, image generation, a PDF install,
catalog changes, commits, integration, a push, or deployment. Each remains a
separate production action governed by repository instructions and the user's
authorization.

## Fixed editorial decisions

Preserve these decisions unless the user deliberately revises them:

- Produce one comprehensive postconciliar Roman Rite visual dictionary and
  smaller editions selected by reader use rather than by subject.
- Treat the comprehensive volume as the authoritative reader-facing
  dictionary. Derive smaller editions from one canonical inventory and shared
  artwork rather than maintaining independent copies.
- Use US Letter portrait pages.
- Use beautiful monochrome pencil or graphite artwork. Do not use color as the
  only carrier of information; identify liturgical colors in typeset labels or
  a monochrome pattern key.
- Favor image-dense plates, normally several objects per page, with concise
  captions. When an isolated object is not self-explanatory, add a small
  context, placement, worn, unfolded, sectional, front-and-back, or in-use
  inset rather than a long paragraph.
- Show substantive variations where appearance, construction, authorized use,
  or handling differs materially. Do not multiply decorative examples that
  teach no distinction.
- Give preferred English and Latin names, together with material alternate or
  historical names. Keep pronunciation out of ordinary entries; place
  pronunciation for commonly spoken Latin terms in a terminal appendix.
- Include brief, appropriately sourced symbolism without allowing it to crowd
  out identification and practical use. Label later devotional associations
  as such; do not invent symbolism for every material, fold, ornament, or
  color.
- Keep citations off ordinary visual plates. Use stable entry or figure keys
  to connect claims to numbered terminal notes, except when an on-page source
  qualification is required to prevent a disputed identification from
  misleading the reader.
- Write the comprehensive volume for a broad adult reference audience. The
  server extraction may use younger-reader wording.
- Open with actual visual content and a minimal key. Put scope, qualifications,
  source method, checklists, production notes, and similar apparatus at the
  end. A local warning or qualification that changes identification, handling,
  or immediate safety remains beside the affected object.
- Include everything that a server may encounter around the server, not only
  what the server may touch.
- Distinguish objects that a server handles, carries, presents, prepares, or
  only recognizes, and objects reserved to clergy or another minister.
- Give historically superseded postconciliar forms a separate historical
  treatment organized chronologically and then by object type. Do not present
  them as current merely because they are post-1962.
- Bound completeness by an identified corpus of universal Roman liturgical
  books. Include territorial, diocesan, institutional, and
  religious-community variants selectively when authoritative evidence shows
  that they are materially distinct, important, or likely to cause confusion.
  Do not attempt to catalog every parish custom.

## Edition, language, territory, and date must precede claims

There is no source-valid generic “Novus Ordo dictionary.” Postconciliar books
have successive typical editions, translations, adaptations, territorial
implementations, later decrees, and local choices. The production must
therefore separate:

1. universal Roman provisions in an identified Latin typical edition;
2. provisions that changed between typical editions or through later universal
   acts;
3. approved translations and adaptations in an identified language and
   territory;
4. choices assigned to the diocesan bishop, conference of bishops, celebrant,
   or another competent authority;
5. legitimate local or institutional arrangements;
6. well-documented custom that is neither represented as universal law nor
   silently normalized; and
7. historical material no longer governing the selected edition and as-of
   date.

The first publication should declare one exact primary production instance
before drafting. A reasonable pilot is the currently governing English
translation and approved adaptation for one named territory, collated against
the corresponding Latin typical edition and universal books. That suggestion
is not an assertion about which edition is current when production begins.
Verify the edition, promulgating and approving authorities, effective dates,
territory, material amendments, later decrees, and as-of date from official
sources.

Do not let the pilot's territorial selections become the universal inventory.
Store universal identity separately from edition-locale dispositions so later
territories can add, qualify, replace, or mark an entry inapplicable without
rewriting the base object. An object absent from the pilot territory is not
therefore absent from the Roman Rite.

## Proposed publication family

### Comprehensive volume

A working title is:

> *The Illustrated Dictionary of the Postconciliar Roman Sanctuary: Objects,
> Vestments, Linens, Books, Furnishings, and Insignia of the Roman Rite*

The title and subtitle must name the controlling edition, territory, or
declared universal/territorial relationship as the final scope requires.

Begin with a compact plate-reading key and an actual sanctuary composition.
Then organize the main body functionally. The final category list must follow
the completed source inventory, but the working structure is:

1. church, sanctuary, and ministers' places;
2. altar, ambo, chair, credence, tabernacle, font, and their appointments;
3. sacred vessels and vessels used for Communion;
4. altar linens, vessel linens, towels, veils, and other textile articles;
5. Missal, Lectionary, Book of the Gospels, ritual books, chant books, covers,
   stands, and markers;
6. cruets, lavabo equipment, bells, candles, processional objects, and other
   service equipment;
7. incense equipment;
8. vesture of bishop, priest, deacon, instituted and deputed ministers,
   servers, choir, and other persons expressly provided for by the selected
   books;
9. episcopal and pontifical insignia and furnishings;
10. Communion under the forms and modes admitted by the selected books,
    including their distinct vessels and service arrangements;
11. Ritual Masses and Masses for Various Needs where objects materially differ;
12. funerals and Masses for the Dead;
13. Palm Sunday and the Paschal Triduum;
14. initiation, reception, marriage, ordination, dedication, blessing, and
    other rites celebrated within or directly joined to Mass;
15. related Roman ceremonies and celebrations likely to share the sanctuary
    or its equipment, clearly distinguished from Mass; and
16. complete sanctuary and ceremonial compositions that show how otherwise
    isolated objects relate in use.

This is a discovery scaffold, not a claim that every listed subject produces a
separate chapter or that every adjacent rite falls within the eventual
publication boundary. The governing books and completed inventory decide.

### Use-based derived editions

All extractions use the same stable entries, evidence, and artwork:

- **Altar server visual dictionary.** Everything the server may encounter,
  including items the server does not handle. Emphasize recognition, ordinary
  location, immediate use, handling boundary, handoff, confusion risks, and
  safety.
- **Sacristan visual dictionary.** Emphasize authorized preparation, assembly,
  placement, care, storage, seasonal changes, and ceremony-specific checks.
  Do not present unverified conservation advice.
- **Master of ceremonies and trainer visual dictionary.** Emphasize ministers,
  giver and receiver, destination, options, branch-dependent equipment,
  recovery points, and common handoff errors.
- **General reader visual dictionary.** A shorter survey emphasizing identity,
  purpose, brief symbolism, and historical relationship.
- **Episcopal and major-ceremony visual reference.** A focused extraction of
  episcopal vesture, insignia, furnishings, ministers, and object
  relationships where the source inventory supports it.

Do not create a second authoritative description merely to simplify an entry.
Use audience-specific render fields or controlled summaries traceable to the
canonical record.

## Canonical inventory

Create the inventory before multiplying publication prose or artwork. Its
exact tracked location and schema belong in the future illustrated-dictionary
profile, but each canonical object or composition should be capable of
recording:

- stable, provider-neutral object or composition ID;
- preferred English and Latin names;
- material alternate, historical, and territorial names;
- object family and functional category;
- definition and identifying features;
- physical construction only to the extent verified and useful;
- dimensions or relative scale when supported;
- substantive variants and the feature that distinguishes each;
- universal, territorial, diocesan, institutional,
  religious-community, customary, or historical status;
- governing edition, language, territory, jurisdiction, and as-of date;
- exact liturgical books, loci, decrees, and other sources;
- celebrations and ritual moments in which it may appear;
- normal location, preparation state, movement, and destination;
- ministers who prepare, carry, present, use, receive, or merely recognize it;
- explicit server handling boundary and immediate safety issue, if any;
- related and easily confused objects;
- brief sourced symbolism and its evidence class;
- object history, without implying continuous use;
- 1962 counterpart relationship, if any;
- artwork and figure IDs;
- publication and audience selections;
- rights and provenance state for every visual reference;
- research, artwork, liturgical, copy, and page-validation status; and
- unresolved questions that prevent a completeness or currentness claim.

Use controlled, edition-scoped dispositions rather than a single timeless
`used` flag. At minimum, the model must distinguish `required`, `provided`,
`permitted`, `optional`, `territorial adaptation`, `local selection`,
`customary`, `superseded`, `not applicable`, and `unresolved`, refining the
terms only when the governing profile defines their evidence.

An exact one-to-one correspondence with the 1962 inventory must not be assumed.
Record relationships such as `continued`, `renamed`, `materially changed`,
`changed use`, `optional`, `superseded`, `newly provided`, or `no established
counterpart` only after direct comparison of the identified editions.

## Artwork system and reuse

Reuse 1962-volume artwork only when the depicted physical object remains
accurate for the postconciliar entry or when the picture is explicitly
presented as a historical comparison. Reusing pixels does not establish
continuity of name, status, handling, minister, location, or ritual use.

Give every approved asset a stable figure ID and record:

- the objects and variants depicted;
- view, angle, scale treatment, and context;
- whether it is an isolated specimen, comparison, construction detail, worn
  view, placement view, or ritual composition;
- factual visual references and their rights;
- prompt or creation provenance required by repository guidance;
- edition-specific claims encoded by placement or dress;
- permitted consumers and crops;
- accuracy-validation state; and
- the exact source and PDF versions in which the asset was validated.

Keep generated lettering, labels, arrows, color names, minister names, and
movement paths out of raster artwork. Add them as typeset overlays so they
remain exact, searchable where feasible, and correctable. Monochrome output
must remain intelligible when printed without color.

Ordinary pages should favor dense comparison plates. Use several images or
views where distinction requires them:

- folded and unfolded;
- front, back, and side;
- isolated and in context;
- empty and assembled;
- worn by the correct minister;
- same-scale where size distinguishes objects; or
- an explicit `not to common scale` notice where compact layout prevents a
  true comparison.

Visually audit generated artwork for invented parts, impossible folds,
incorrect fastenings, extra handles, stems, feet or vessels, false embroidery,
wrong vestment construction, mistaken minister or insignia, misleading scale,
duplicate or missing equipment, and impossible liturgical placement. A
plausible-looking image is not verified evidence.

## Source plan

Begin with primary and official sources. The exact corpus must be registered
and dated, but it will ordinarily require the relevant:

- Latin typical edition of the Roman Missal and its General Instruction;
- approved Missal and General Instruction for the selected language and
  territory;
- Lectionary introductions and Book of the Gospels provisions;
- Ceremonial of Bishops;
- rites of Christian initiation and reception;
- rites for Holy Week and the Paschal Triduum as contained in the controlling
  books;
- rites of ordination, marriage, funerals, dedication, institution, blessing,
  and other rites within the declared boundary;
- norms for Holy Communion and Eucharistic worship outside Mass where included;
- universal instructions, decrees, authentic interpretations, and later
  amendments materially affecting objects, vesture, ministers, or sanctuary
  arrangement;
- approved territorial adaptations and conference norms;
- diocesan law or directions only for expressly local examples; and
- museum, conservation, vestment, architectural, or specialist references only
  for physical identification and history that the liturgical books do not
  establish.

The future agent must verify current official sources at production time.
Mutable official webpages require retrieval dates and appropriate snapshot
records. Copyrighted translations and artwork remain subject to the rights
boundary: do not reproduce protected rubrics, book text, photography, designs,
or illustrations merely because they were useful for research. Before adding
or reusing a repository-wide source, follow [sources.md](../sources.md), create
truthful work/edition/artifact identity, record rights per artifact, and bind
each publication to what it actually used.

OCR, retailer listings, auction descriptions, image search, parish guides,
blogs, and generated images may supply leads but cannot establish normative
identity, current discipline, or exact wording. Physical examples must not be
used to infer that an ornamental feature is prescribed.

## Historical treatment

The historical part should explain changes inside the postconciliar period and
must not duplicate the 1962 volume's earlier Roman history without a deliberate
cross-reference. Organize first by verified reform or edition period, then by
object type. Potential period boundaries include promulgated editions,
territorial implementation dates, and later universal changes, but do not
invent a neat periodization before sources establish it.

For every historical entry state:

- the exact edition, territory, and date range supported;
- whether the object itself disappeared, its name changed, its prescribed or
  optional status changed, or only its use and placement changed;
- whether it remains permitted, survives by local provision, or is merely
  documented historically; and
- the source that controls the transition.

Avoid progress narratives, claims of universal abandonment from a single
territory, and assertions that a preconciliar object was “abolished” when the
evidence shows only omission, changed use, or a different local norm.

## Reader-facing plate contract

A normal entry block should remain compact:

1. preferred English name;
2. Latin name and only material alternate names;
3. one-line identification or use;
4. a small status/handling key where relevant;
5. brief symbolism only when sourced; and
6. a stable note key.

Use four to ten distinct objects on a page when they remain legible, fewer
where multiple views or close comparison are needed, and a spread for a major
sanctuary or ceremonial composition. Text may not compensate for an
unrecognizable drawing. If a white linen, book, garment, or vessel cannot be
distinguished in isolation, add the smallest adequate context inset.

Terminal apparatus should include:

- pronunciation of commonly spoken Latin terms;
- English-to-Latin and Latin-to-English indexes;
- visual/category index;
- variant and terminology notes;
- chronological postconciliar change table;
- numbered entry and figure source notes;
- edition, territory, jurisdiction, and as-of statement;
- completeness boundary and unresolved inventory gaps;
- rights and visual-provenance statement;
- alpha status; and
- generation metadata.

## Postconciliar-specific safeguards

The future production must explicitly prevent these errors:

- transferring 1962 servers' roles, altar orientation, side names, sanctuary
  levels, handoffs, gestures, or handling rules into postconciliar practice;
- treating one celebration style, architectural arrangement, or parish custom
  as the Roman norm;
- depicting an optional arrangement without labelling its status;
- confusing liturgical law with a manufacturer's customary object name;
- assuming an object with the same name has the same use across editions;
- collapsing instituted acolytes, instituted lectors, deputed ministers,
  extraordinary ministers, altar servers, and ordained ministers;
- confusing the universal books with territorial adaptations or diocesan
  directions;
- treating Mass, Communion outside Mass, Eucharistic exposition, and devotional
  practice as one ceremony;
- representing historical survivals as current requirements;
- using color alone to distinguish rank, season, vestment, or status;
- deriving currentness from a later publication date without checking
  amendments and effective law; or
- describing protected English liturgical wording so closely that the
  dictionary becomes a substitute for the approved book.

## Production phases and gates

### Phase 1: profile and scope

1. Confirm the governing illustrated-dictionary profile exists; add it before
   multiplying leaves if it does not.
2. Select and record the primary typical edition, approved edition-locale,
   territory, jurisdiction, and as-of date.
3. Define Mass, rites-within-Mass, adjacent-ceremony, episcopal, local-variant,
   and historical boundaries.
4. List the exact official source corpus and unresolved access or rights
   limitations.

Gate: no claim of completeness and no publication leaf until the edition and
source boundaries are reviewable.

### Phase 2: canonical inventory

1. Import only stable factual and artwork relationships from the 1962
   inventory.
2. Collate each postconciliar entry directly against the selected books.
3. Create edition-locale dispositions and object relationships.
4. Record unresolved objects and material negative results instead of
   guessing.

Gate: every proposed plate object has a stable ID, source locus, status,
handling evidence where claimed, and rights-safe visual plan.

### Phase 3: artwork prototypes

1. Establish the monochrome graphite style with representative hard cases:
   white linens, black garments, reflective vessels, a worn vestment, a dense
   comparison plate, and a sanctuary composition.
2. Test legibility at final printed size and in monochrome.
3. Review physical construction, minister, placement, and edition-specific
   assumptions.

Gate: approve the artwork grammar before producing the full asset set.

### Phase 4: comprehensive volume

1. Draft plates from the canonical inventory.
2. Keep ordinary captions concise and move global apparatus to the end.
3. Add indexed endnotes and cross-references.
4. Build enough passes to settle references and indexes.

Gate: content, source, rights, image-accuracy, log, and every-page visual review
must pass before installing a PDF.

### Phase 5: alpha validation

1. Verify source integrity and edition-locale dispositions for every rendered
   claim and object.
2. Verify rights and lawful distribution for text and artwork.
3. Reject unsafe handling instruction, misleading identity, impossible
   construction, and unresolved factual placeholder prose.
4. Run reproducibility, artifact-identity, mechanical-validity, and basic
   visual-usability checks.
5. Put the actual scope and one short work-wide reliance boundary in the
   terminal scope-and-qualifications appendix. Keep internal release state out
   of reader-facing pages and prose. Keep immediate safety notices beside the
   affected object or action, then record the exact installed artifact
   identity.

Gate: alpha eligibility requires those six concerns and no external-review,
intended-reader, or physical-human-review process.

### Phase 6: use-based editions

1. Derive selections and audience wording from canonical records.
2. Preserve every handling and status qualification needed by that audience.
3. Reuse approved figures or declared crops without silently changing meaning.
4. Rebuild and review each extraction as a separate publication.

Gate: compare every derived statement and plate against the comprehensive
record; no independently drifting text or artwork.

### Phase 7: web and release preparation

1. Declare web eligibility honestly. Image-dense plates and overlays may
   require a conditional or ineligible classification until accessible
   equivalents are designed.
2. Where a web edition is eligible, provide meaningful object-level alt text,
   reading order, captions, and textual equivalents without creating a second
   authoritative edition.
3. Run source, metadata, build, log, link, catalog, and profile checks.
4. Use repository review tooling, inspect every rendered page for basic visual
   usability, and install only exact validated artifacts.

Gate: published records deployment of the verified alpha artifact. It does not
erase unresolved completeness, rights, edition-locale, or accessibility
limitations.

## Mapping to the alpha workflow

The future postconciliar profile should adopt the alpha concerns and
no-misrepresentation controls defined by the 1962 pictorial-dictionary
profile, adapted only where postconciliar edition and jurisdiction evidence
requires more specificity:

- `alpha` identifies a publication that passes source, rights, safety,
  reproducibility, mechanical, and basic visual checks.
- The terminal scope-and-qualifications appendix states edition-locale scope
  and any work-wide reliance boundary once. Internal release state does not
  appear on the first page, in running matter, in catalogs, or in explanatory
  prose.
- Immediate safety warnings and legally necessary notices remain at the point
  of reliance rather than being deferred to terminal apparatus.
- The production record identifies every edition-locale disposition rendered
  and keeps universal, territorial, diocesan, institutional, and customary
  examples distinct.
- Keep `source_audit_status`, `rights_status`, `safety_status`,
  `production_status`, `alpha_status`, and deployment state distinct.
- `published` records deployment only; it does not promote the work to a higher
  editorial or external-approval state.

When the postconciliar profile is written, restate this workflow there rather
than making a publication leaf depend normatively on this handoff.

## Definition of a production-ready handoff

Before a future agent calls this project ready to publish, the repository
should contain:

- the governing illustrated-dictionary profile;
- a declared edition-locale and as-of scope;
- a source-corpus inventory and publication-local bindings;
- a canonical object/composition inventory with edition dispositions;
- an artwork manifest with rights and accuracy review;
- the comprehensive source leaf and any authorized derived leaves;
- terminal indexes, notes, qualifications, rights notice, and metadata;
- explicit build dependencies for shared records and artwork;
- validated installed PDFs and, where eligible, validated web editions;
- catalog entries only for publications actually installed and approved; and
- a remaining-gaps record that prevents “complete” from outrunning the checked
  corpus.

Completion means comprehensive within the declared Roman, edition,
territorial, ceremonial, source, and as-of boundary. It never means every
object ever found in a Catholic church, every local custom, or every future
revision of the Roman books.
