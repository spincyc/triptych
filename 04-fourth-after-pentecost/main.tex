\weektitle{Fourth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Quarta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: groaning creation and apostolic obedience}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Dominus illuminatio mea} (Ps. 26)}{Light, salvation, courage}{St. Augustine on Psalm 26; St. John of the Cross on dark faith}{The soul faces enemies by receiving light, not by denying conflict.}
\properrow{Collect}{Peaceful ordering of the world; tranquil devotion of the Church}{Providence and peace}{St. Thomas Aquinas on providence; St. Benedict on ordered peace}{The Church asks for external order so inner devotion can be steady and fruitful.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 8:18-23}{Creation groans for glory}{St. Irenaeus on recapitulation; Maximus the Confessor on cosmic restoration}{Christian hope is bodily and cosmic: redemption reaches creation, not only private conscience.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Luke 5:1-11}{Duc in altum; miraculous catch}{St. Ambrose on Luke; St. John Chrysostom on Peter's humility}{Apostolic fruit follows obedience after apparent failure: \latin{in verbo autem tuo}.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Illumina oculos meos} (Ps. 12)}{Light against death}{St. Ephrem on divine illumination}{The offering asks that the eyes be kept awake for hope.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Dominus firmamentum meum} (Ps. 17)}{God as refuge and deliverer}{St. Teresa of Avila on confidence}{Communion strengthens the apostle sent back onto deep water.}
\end{properstable}

\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Luke 5 narrates Christ teaching from Simon's boat, commanding a new cast of the nets, granting a miraculous catch, and calling Peter to catch men.\\
Allegorical & The boat is the Church, the sea is the restless world, the net is apostolic preaching, and the abundant catch is the gathering of peoples into Christ.\\
Moral & The disciple must obey Christ's word after failure: \latin{in verbo autem tuo laxabo rete}. Weariness is not permission to stop trusting.\\
Anagogical & Romans 8 widens the catch into cosmic hope: creation groans toward the revelation of the children of God and the redemption of the body.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Symbolic Through-Line Extraction}
\textbf{Symbolic field.} The repeated signs are light, fearlessness, groaning, water, boat, net, depth, catch, and vocation. The Introit's light does not remove the sea; it enables the Church to enter it.

\textbf{Proper-to-proper movement.} The Introit gives courage before enemies; the Collect asks peace for the Church's ordered devotion; the Epistle reveals creation's groaning; the Gospel shows Peter's boat entering the deep; the Offertory asks illumined eyes; the Communion rests in God as firmament and refuge.

\textbf{Sacramental arc.} The boat is an image of the Church bearing Christ's teaching, and the net is apostolic preaching. Eucharistic communion strengthens those sent back into the deep, where creation waits for redeemed sons to be revealed.

\textbf{Homiletic synthesis.} The through line is obedient depth. The Mass moves from illumination to mission: the soul sees by God's light, hears Christ's word after failure, and casts the net where human prudence would stop.

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Luke places teaching from the boat before the catch. The Church's apostolic work begins with Christ's doctrine.
\item Peter's confession, \latin{homo peccator sum}, follows abundance. Grace reveals unworthiness without crushing vocation.
\item Romans 8 gives the cosmic frame: the apostolic net is cast into a groaning world destined for renewal.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. Ambrose}{Peter's boat signifies the Church.}{The pulpit can move from one fishing boat to the visible Church carrying Christ's voice.}
\witnessrow{St. Augustine}{The sea often figures the unstable world.}{Use this image to preach mission amid confusion, not flight from it.}
\witnessrow{St. John Chrysostom}{Peter's humility follows the miracle.}{Abundance should deepen repentance and vocation together.}
\witnessrow{St. John Paul II}{``Duc in altum.''}{Though later than the Fathers, the phrase gives a memorable pastoral refrain.}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Cosmos and mission & Romans shows creation waiting; Luke shows the apostolic net cast into the world. The mission of Peter is part of creation's groaning toward glory.\\
Failure and obedience & A night of empty labor becomes abundance when Peter acts on Christ's word. The Fathers regularly read this as the Church's missionary pattern.\\
Light and depth & The Introit's light does not keep Peter on shore. Divine illumination sends him into depth.\\
Sin and vocation & Peter's unworthiness is real, but Christ answers it with mission: from now on he will catch men.\\
Prayer focus & Bring an area of fruitless labor to Christ and make one obedient act without demanding visible success first.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Encourage those exhausted by fruitless fidelity: the command may come after the empty night.
\item Correct activism: the catch follows Christ's word, not human cleverness alone.
\item Connect ecological or bodily anxieties to Romans 8 without reducing the text to politics; creation waits for sanctified sons.
\end{itemize}
