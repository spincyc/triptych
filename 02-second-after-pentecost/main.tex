\weektitle{Second Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Secunda Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: charity embodied in the banquet of God}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Factus est Dominus protector meus} (Ps. 17)}{Rescue into breadth}{St. Ambrose on the Psalms; Cassiodorus on Psalm 17}{The Lord does not merely defend; he brings the soul into the wide place of divine charity.}
\properrow{Collect}{Fear and love of the holy Name}{Holy fear and stable love}{St. Bernard on the Name of Jesus; St. Catherine of Siena on holy fear}{The prayer joins reverence and intimacy: fear keeps love from presumption, love keeps fear from servility.}
\properrow{Epistle}{1 John 3:13-18}{Passing from death to life by loving the brethren}{St. Augustine, Homilies on 1 John; St. John Chrysostom on almsgiving}{Charity must leave the tongue and enter work and truth.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Luke 14:16-24}{The great supper}{St. Gregory the Great, Gospel homilies; St. Bede on Luke}{Excuses reveal disordered loves; the poor and broken reveal the gratuitous wideness of the invitation.}

\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Cantabo Domino, qui bona tribuit mihi} (Ps. 12)}{Thanksgiving for benefits}{St. Ignatius of Loyola, gratitude as root of service}{The communicant sings because the supper is already gift before it is task.}

\section*{Homily Sketch}
\textbf{Proposition.} The great supper is charity offered before it is charity commanded. The soul that has been invited into God's abundance must stop treating mercy as an interruption.

\textbf{Opening movement.} The Introit begins with deliverance into breadth: \latin{eduxit me in latitudinem}. The Gospel shows that breadth as a banquet large enough for the poor, weak, blind, and lame. The narrowness is not in God but in our excuses.

\textbf{Doctrinal center.} The Collect asks for fear and love together. Holy fear hears the summons; love rises and comes. Without fear, invitation becomes cheap; without love, fear never reaches the table.

\textbf{Moral turn.} The Epistle refuses sentimental religion: ``not in word, nor in tongue; but in deed, and in truth.'' The invited man becomes an inviting man, especially toward the brother in need.

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Luke 14 is spoken at table. Christ turns an ordinary meal into an image of the Kingdom and of Eucharistic readiness.
\item The excuses are not gross sins. Land, oxen, and marriage are legitimate goods made ultimate.
\item The servant's mission widens in stages: invited guests, city streets, then roads and hedges. Grace presses outward.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. Augustine}{``Love, and do what thou wilt.'' Homily on 1 John}{Explain carefully: Augustine means rightly ordered charity fulfills action, not that impulse replaces obedience.}
\witnessrow{St. Gregory the Great}{The poor brought to supper show those whom the proud overlook.}{Use Gregory to expose respectable refusals and to honor the hidden guests of God.}
\witnessrow{St. John Chrysostom}{Almsgiving is a concrete test of love for Christ.}{Tie the Epistle's ``deed and truth'' to parish works of mercy.}
\witnessrow{St. Benedict}{Prefer nothing to the love of Christ.}{The good things named in the excuses must remain below the supreme good.}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Banquet and almsgiving & The Epistle tests the Gospel. One cannot accept the invitation to the great supper while closing the heart to a needy brother.\\
Excuses and attachments & Field, oxen, and marriage are goods; they become refusals when treated as ultimate. This is a classic patristic reading of ordered love.\\
Fear and love & The Collect prevents two errors: servile terror that avoids the table, and casual presumption that ignores the summons.\\
Church and mission & The servant who announces supper is an image of apostolic preaching: the Church compels by truth, beauty, mercy, and urgency.\\
Prayer focus & Identify the respectable excuse that keeps charity theoretical, then answer the invitation through one concrete act of mercy.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Name modern excuses without contempt: busyness, family logistics, work, property, reputation, and curated comfort.
\item Press the Eucharistic question: do I come to the Lord's table while refusing the brother Christ has seated near me?
\item Offer one concrete practice: before Sunday Mass, choose one act of hidden charity for someone who cannot repay it.
\end{itemize}
