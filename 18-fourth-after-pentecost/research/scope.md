# Fourth Sunday after Pentecost — Research Scope

**Last updated:** 2026-07-10

This record preserves the source roles, searches, and claim boundaries compressed in the reader-facing guide. Complete appointed texts, checksums, image locations, witness differences, and line-by-line collation results remain in [the verified propers](../propers/verified.md).

## Scriptural and Liturgical Scope

- Appointed and immediate contexts studied: Psalms 26, 78, 9, 12, and 17 in full; Romans 8:1–30; Luke 4:42–5:16; and John 21:1–14.
- Closely related doctrinal texts actually used: Romans 8:11; Philippians 2:12–13; Ezekiel 18:31–32 and 36:22–27 as treated by Augustine.
- The Collect, Secret, and Postcommunion were analyzed in their complete verified Latin forms. The PDF reproduces only the clauses needed for its argument because it is designed for use beside a hand missal.
- The authoritative architecture follows four textual stages: light/order/travail; mercy/judgment/Christ’s command; illuminated eyes/resistant wills; refuge/purification/protection. This is an editorial map of the appointed formulary, not a historical-source claim.

## Direct Reception Retained

- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 26* §§1–3, on divine light, salvation, protection, and courage amid enemies. The English page is headed [Psalm 27](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801027.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 78* §§12–13, on God as healer, divine help and human action, forgiveness for God’s name, and the nations’ question. The English page is headed [Psalm 79](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801079.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 9* §§6, 9–10, on equitable divine judgment, knowledge of hearts, poverty, refuge, and tribulation: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801009.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 12* §§3–6, on the eyes of the heart, the enemy as devil or entrenched habit, sin’s eclipse, and perseverance as mercy. The English page is headed [Psalm 13](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801013.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Exposition on Psalm 17* §§2–4, on *totus Christus*, received strength, refuge following deliverance, prior divine help, and non-reliance on self. The English page is headed [Psalm 18](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1801018.htm).
- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 14 on Romans*, directly on Romans 8:18–23: creation’s personified expectation, subjection in hope, travail toward incorruption, firstfruits, and complete bodily liberation: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/210214.htm).
- St. Cyril of Alexandria, *Commentary on Luke* at 5:2–8, on teaching and sign, Christ’s power, apostolic preaching, partners, and Christ’s continuing gift of the catch: [translation](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/cyril_on_luke_02_sermons_12_25.htm).
- St. Ambrose, *Exposition of Luke* IV.68–79, on Peter’s boat, Christological depth at *Duc in altum*, apostolic discourse as nets, humility after fruitless labor, alternative readings of the boats, confession, and vocation: [Latin text](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Expositio_evangelii_secundum_Lucam/IV).

These witnesses expound appointed biblical passages, not the later assembled Sunday formulary.

## Related Illumination Retained

- St. Augustine, *City of God* XIX.13, on the peace of all things as the tranquility of order and on temporal versus final peace: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120119.htm). This illuminates the Collect but is not commentary on it.
- St. Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.7.1–2, on Romans 8:11 and the mortal body vivified by the Spirit: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103507.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Tractate 122 on John* §7, on Luke’s present mixed catch and torn nets contrasted with John’s final unbroken net and shore: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701122.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Confessions* VIII.5 and 8–12, on entrenched habit, incomplete willing, self-resistance, and conversion: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/110108.htm).
- St. Augustine, *Grace and Free Will* §§31–33, on commanded conversion, God’s gift of effective willing, and operating and cooperating grace: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1510.htm).
- St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 8 on Philippians*, on 2:12–13, keeping earnest action beside God’s work of willing and doing: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/230208.htm).
- St. Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.2.2–3, on Eucharistic nourishment of the flesh and that flesh’s future incorruption: [text](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103502.htm).

These sources provide close canonical, doctrinal, or sacramental illumination. They are not presented as direct commentary on the Collect, Secret, Communion antiphon, or Postcommunion.

## Controlling Distinctions

- The Latin *compelle* is rendered “compel,” not softened into “draw.” The adverbial/predicative *propitius* requires God’s action to be understood as favorable or merciful, but grammar alone does not supply a complete theology of grace.
- *Rebelles voluntates* names the worshippers’ rebellious wills. It does not grammatically assert competing wills within each person; Augustine’s divided will is related illumination.
- The received oblations and rebellious wills belong to coordinated petitions. The prayer does not identify the wills as the oblations.
- Luke does not call Simon rebellious. Using his truthful objection, consent, action, confession, and following to imagine merciful compulsion belongs to the speculative section.
- Ambrose offers alternative interpretations of the two boats; none is presented as the single settled patristic identification.
- Augustine’s contrast between Luke and John is documented canonical reception, not Luke’s literal sense or a claimed universal consensus.
- The Alleluia does not place God’s throne above the lake or identify Christ’s boat-seat as the throne. That juxtaposition is kept speculative.
- Visible abundance is not treated as proof of sanctity, and present strain is not treated as proof that Christ’s word failed.

## Cultural Reception

Wording from Romans 8 and Luke 5 was tested across public-domain literature, poetry, biblical cantatas, hymn indexes, digitized books, quotation searches, and ecclesial reception. Three compact examples were retained:

- William Lyon Phelps, *Reading the Bible* (1919), pp. 8–9: a humorous childhood illustration of “the whole creation groaneth” as universal indigestion, verified in the [Cornell scan](https://archive.org/details/cu31924029280489/page/n20/mode/2up).
- J. S. Bach, *Siehe, ich will viel Fischer aussenden*, BWV 88 (1726), movement 4: Luke 5:10 assigned to tenor narration and bass *vox Christi*, verified against the [German libretto and translation](https://www.emmanuelmusic.org/bach-translations/bwv-88).
- Mary Baker Eddy, *Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures*, 1906 edition, chapter IX: Romans 8:22–23 used as an epigraph for “Creation,” verified in the [edition transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Science_and_Health_with_Key_to_the_Scriptures_(1906)/09_Creation).

Leads that were devotional restatements, weak phrase coincidences, uncertain attributions, or insufficiently located were discarded. Notable unused but verified leads included S. Weir Mitchell’s poem “The Whole Creation Groaneth,” John Paul II’s programmatic reuse of *Duc in altum*, and older hymns and sermons based on Luke 5.

## Survey and Material Limits

- Surveyed accessible NPNF/ANF and New Advent collections, the Ambrose Latin text, Cyril’s Payne Smith translation, public facsimiles and OCR, digitized books, cantata/libretto indexes, hymn indexes, and targeted Latin and English web searches.
- No secure direct patristic commentary was located for the Collect, Secret, or Postcommunion. The orations are therefore handled through verified grammar, close doctrinal illumination, and explicit source-role labels.
- The survey does not exhaust manuscripts, untranslated works, subscription databases, or modern critical scholarship. Older public-domain translations are working texts.
- Sources were retained because they establish or materially qualify claims made in the guide; no numerical or source-category quota was applied.
- All links were accessed 2026-07-10.
