## Scope and method

Each assigned PNG produced by the repository `pdf-review` run was opened as its own full-size image (1700x2200 source raster) with the Read tool before any observation was recorded; no contact sheet, thumbnail, new rasterization, source acquisition, word-level collation, text correction or date reconstruction was performed. One JSON observation per page was appended to the lane ledger after viewing.

Each page was checked for the displayed title, responsible issuer, Latin label and omission of the unresolved act date against the frozen metadata; headings, two-column flow and gutter, margins, glyphs, clipping/overlap; natural source closing; modern digitizer signatures; raw placeholders; and, specifically for this derivative, (a) whether every printed marginal summary is emitted as its own short TAB-led block and never fused inside a body sentence or hyphen-split word, (b) natural two-column reading order, (c) running heads, folios and signature marks left inline, (d) misplaced but separate marginal blocks, and (e) fragment presentation.

Mechanical support (read-only, lane-local scripts): the source selection of every document was split into paragraphs from the reading-order derivative `hybrid.txt` (artifact `reading-order-497a692a65d1`) at the manifest line ranges. It contains **223 TAB-led marginal paragraphs**, and every one of them was matched to a standalone `\par`-delimited paragraph of the generated body TeX (223/223). No source paragraph mixes TAB-led and body lines. No `Missing character` entry occurs in any lane PDF log; no `Google`/`Digitized` string or bare `None` occurs in any lane body TeX. This mechanical check supports, but does not replace, the visual review.

## Limitations

These are literal, uncorrected, uncollated historical OCR reading editions mechanically typeset in two columns from a coordinate-reconstructed reading-order derivative. This review is a layout and presentation review only: it does not collate words against the printing or a facsimile, verify that every native OCR word appears exactly once, authenticate historical wording or attribution, normalize or repair dates, or adjudicate rights. Marginal-block placement was judged against the rendered order and the derivative text, not against page images of the 1859 printing.
