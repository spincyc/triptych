\weektitle{Sixth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Sexta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: the baptized are buried, fed, steadied, and fortified}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Dominus fortitudo plebis suae} (Ps. 27:8--9)}{Strength for God's people}{God enters as strength and saving protector of a people whom he blesses and rules.}
\properrow{Collect}{\latin{Deus virtutum}: increase religion; nourish and guard the good}{Growth in grace}{The new life requires God both to implant love of his name and preserve what grace begins.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:3--11}{Buried with Christ; newness of life}{Baptism gives participation in Christ's death and resurrection, not advice for renovating the old man.}
\properrow{Gradual}{\latin{Convertere, Domine}; \latin{Domine, refugium factus es nobis} (Ps. 89:13, 1)}{The Lord's return and refuge}{The newly alive answer the Epistle by asking the Lord to return and by confessing him as their refuge.}
\properrow{Alleluia}{\latin{In te, Domine, speravi} (Ps. 30:2--3)}{Trust and deliverance}{Trust in God's inclined ear prepares the assembly to hear why Christ will not send the hungry away fasting.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Mark 8:1--9}{Christ feeds the four thousand}{Christ's compassion supplies a people who cannot sustain themselves on the way.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Perfice gressus meos} (Ps. 16:5--7)}{Steady steps in God's paths}{The baptized life becomes an offered walk whose steps still require divine keeping.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Circumibo et immolabo} (Ps. 26:6)}{Sacrifice of jubilation}{At the altar, received mercy returns to God as praise and jubilant sacrifice.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Repleti sumus, Domine, muneribus tuis}}{Cleansing and aid}{God's gifts are asked to cleanse by their effect and fortify by their help.}
\end{properstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Romans 6 says that the baptized have been buried with Christ and must reckon themselves dead to sin; Mark 8 recounts Christ's compassionate feeding of a hungry multitude.\\
Allegorical & Baptism joins the Church to Christ's paschal death and life. Augustine reads the loaves as scriptural and spiritual nourishment; in the wider canonical and liturgical tradition, the multiplication also prefigures the superabundance of the Eucharist.\\
Moral & The Christian must refuse the old servitude, walk in the grace already given, confess dependence, and receive rather than pretend to be self-sustaining.\\
Anagogical & Christ keeps his pilgrim people from failing on the road and directs them toward the final feast, where resurrection life is no longer threatened by sin, hunger, or death.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\sectionguard
\section*{From Font to Road to Altar}

The propers form a three-stage mystagogical itinerary. Each stage pairs what God does with the posture his action creates in the worshipping people. Baptismal newness is therefore neither a merely past event nor an autonomous achievement.

\begin{threestable}{Stage and texts}{God's action}{Posture formed in the people}
Font --- Introit, Collect, Epistle & God strengthens and rules his inheritance, plants and preserves the good, and joins the baptized to Christ's burial and resurrection. & A people receives its identity as gift: buried with Christ, alive to God, and dependent upon grace for growth.\\
Road --- Gradual, Alleluia, Gospel & The Lord returns, hears, shelters, has compassion, blesses, and feeds those who cannot sustain themselves. & The newly alive remain trusting pilgrims: hungry, refuge-seeking, and willing to receive provision.\\
Altar --- Offertory through Postcommunion & God steadies actual steps, makes vows fruitful, fills his people, and lets the received gifts cleanse and fortify. & An unfinished walk is offered; gift becomes jubilant thanksgiving and strength for the continuing journey.\\
\end{threestable}

\begin{massbox}{Why the Secret is the hinge}
The Offertory places still-unsteady steps on God's paths. The Secret asks God to receive the offering and make faithful desire effective. Communion and Postcommunion then show the result: gift becomes praise, cleansing, and strength. New life is real, but its continuation is also grace.
\end{massbox}

\sectionguard
\section*{The Grammar of New Life}

\begin{fourstable}{Textual phrase}{Grammatical force}{What it establishes}{Liturgical echo}
Romans 6:4 & ``We are buried together with him'' states a sacramental fact before Paul issues a command. & Baptism incorporates the Christian into Christ's death; the old life is not merely refurbished. & Cyril calls the font both grave and mother because dying and birth coincide there.\\
Romans 6:4, 11 & ``Let us walk in newness of life'' and ``reckon yourselves'' draw action from what God has already done. & The imperative does not manufacture baptismal identity; it requires conduct truthful to that identity. & The Offertory's \latin{Perfice gressus meos} asks God to make actual steps firm.\\
Collect & The petitions ask God to give love of his name, increase religion, nourish the good, and preserve what he has nourished. & Divine action initiates, matures, and guards the baptized life. & The Gospel crowd likewise receives what it cannot supply for itself.\\
Gradual--Alleluia & \latin{Convertere, Domine}, ``Turn, O Lord,'' and \latin{In te, Domine, speravi}, ``In thee, O Lord, have I hoped,'' are prayer and confession. & The newly alive do not leave dependence behind. & Refuge and trust prepare the hearer for Christ's compassion toward exhausted pilgrims.\\
Secret & God is asked to receive the offerings, prevent fruitless vows, and make faithful petition effective. & Resolve and religious desire do not complete themselves. & The altar carries the offered walk from intention into received gift.\\
Postcommunion & Having been filled, the people still ask to be cleansed by the gifts' operation and fortified by their aid. & Reception begins an effect that must continue beyond the rite. & Eucharistic fullness becomes strength for the road rather than self-congratulation.\\
\end{fourstable}

\begin{studybox}{Indicative, imperative, petition}
Paul's order is pedagogically decisive: \textbf{indicative}---God has joined the baptized to Christ; \textbf{imperative}---therefore live according to that gift; \textbf{petition}---the Mass asks God to make such living possible and enduring. Removing any one of the three produces either passivity, moralism, or a merely notional account of baptism.
\end{studybox}

\section*{How to Read the Seven Loaves}

The feeding should not be reduced to one symbol. Its senses are related, but they are not interchangeable.

\begin{fourstable}{Layer}{Textual data}{Legitimate reading}{Interpretive boundary}
Literal event & Three days with Christ; hunger in a desert place; seven loaves; four thousand people; seven baskets remaining. & Christ's compassion answers bodily need and prevents the crowd from failing on the way home. & The material feeding and its narrative sequence remain the basis of every further sense.\\
Patristic doctrine & Augustine treats scriptural exposition as bread broken for hearers and relates the seven loaves to the Spirit's sevenfold work. & Christ nourishes the Church through intelligible divine teaching, not only through an unexplained image of abundance. & This is Augustine's documented reading in \emph{Sermon 45}; it should not silently be relabeled as his Eucharistic exegesis.\\
Canonical sacrament & The multiplication's blessing, breaking, distribution, and superabundance stand within Scripture's wider bread pattern. & The Church recognizes the miracle as a prefiguration of the superabundance of the Eucharist (\emph{CCC} 1335). & This claim has its own canonical source; it is compatible with Augustine's reading but distinct from it.\\
Liturgical and final horizon & The Gospel is followed by offered steps and vows, jubilant sacrifice at Communion, and a prayer for cleansing and aid. & Word and sacrament provision a baptized people traveling toward resurrection and the final feast. & This is the sheet's synthesis of the appointed sequence, not a quotation attributed to a Father.\\
\end{fourstable}

\begin{studybox}{Number and sign laboratory}
\textbf{Three days} gives the crowd's exhaustion a paschal resonance; comparison with Cyril's threefold baptismal descent is an editorial analogy. \textbf{Seven loaves} supports Augustine's association with the Spirit's fullness. \textbf{Four thousand} widens attention from individual hunger to an ecclesial multitude. \textbf{Seven baskets} makes the ending one of ordered superabundance: Christ's provision exceeds immediate consumption without becoming waste.
\end{studybox}

\sectionguard
\section*{Two Mystagogical Source Cards}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\begin{studybox}{St. Cyril of Jerusalem: the font}
\emph{Mystagogical Catechesis II}, 4--7, interprets descent and ascent in the font as sacramental participation by likeness in Christ's burial and resurrection. The likeness communicates a real saving gift:

``And at the self-same moment you were both dying and being born; and that Water of salvation was at once your grave and your mother.''
\end{studybox}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\begin{studybox}{St. Augustine: bread understood}
In \emph{Sermon 45 on the New Testament}, 1--2, Augustine interprets the bread doctrinally: the seven loaves signify the Spirit's sevenfold work, and exposition distributes intelligible nourishment to the Church:

``In expounding to you the Holy Scriptures, I as it were break bread for you.''
\end{studybox}
\end{minipage}

\begin{massbox}{What to notice during this Mass}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Entrance and Collect:} ``people,'' ``inheritance,'' and the verbs give, nourish, preserve.
\item \textbf{Epistle:} baptismal facts before baptismal commands.
\item \textbf{Gradual through Gospel:} dependence, hope, compassion, and provision.
\item \textbf{Offertory:} Paul's ``walk'' becoming the psalmist's ``steps.''
\item \textbf{Secret:} God, not resolve alone, makes faithful desire effective.
\item \textbf{Communion and Postcommunion:} reception becoming praise, cleansing, and strength.
\end{itemize}
\end{multicols}
\end{massbox}
