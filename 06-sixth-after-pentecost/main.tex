\weektitle{Sixth Sunday After Pentecost}{Dominica Sexta Post Pentecosten}{TLM propers: baptismal death and eucharistic feeding}

\begin{properstable}
\properrow{Introit}{\latin{Dominus fortitudo plebis suae} (Ps. 27)}{Strength and shepherding}{St. Augustine on Psalm 27; St. Leo the Great on Christ the shepherd}{God strengthens by saving, blessing, ruling, and feeding his inheritance.}
\properrow{Collect}{Increase of religion; nourish and guard the good}{Growth in grace}{St. Gregory the Great on pastoral nurture; St. Francis de Sales on devotion}{Grace implants love, feeds what is good, and guards what has begun to live.}
\properrow{Epistle}{Rom. 6:3-11}{Buried with Christ; walk in newness of life}{St. Cyril of Jerusalem, Mystagogical Catecheses; St. Ambrose, On the Mysteries}{Baptism is not a symbol only; it is death with Christ ordered to a new life.}
\properrow{Gospel}{Mark 8:1-9}{Feeding of the four thousand}{St. Bede on Mark; St. Augustine on the loaves}{The baptized are fed in the wilderness, sustained by Christ before they faint on the way.}
\properrow{Offertory}{\latin{Perfice gressus meos} (Ps. 16)}{Steady steps in God's paths}{St. Benedict on perseverance}{The offering asks that baptismal life not wobble into old servitude.}
\properrow{Communion}{\latin{Circumibo et immolabo} (Ps. 26)}{Sacrifice of jubilation}{St. Thomas Aquinas on Eucharistic sacrifice}{The fed people become a praising people; Eucharist turns survival into jubilation.}
\properrow{Postcommunion}{\latin{Repleti sumus, Domine, muneribus tuis}}{Cleansing and aid}{St. Ambrose on sacramental nourishment; St. Augustine on grace's defense}{Filled with God's gifts, the Church asks to be cleansed by their effect and fortified by their help.}

\section*{Four Senses of Scripture}
\begin{connectionstable}
Literal & Romans 6 teaches that the baptized are buried with Christ and must walk in newness of life; Mark 8 recounts Christ feeding the hungry multitude.\\
Allegorical & Baptism is participation in Christ's death and resurrection, while the multiplied loaves signify the Eucharist nourishing the pilgrim Church in the wilderness.\\
Moral & The Christian must reckon himself dead to sin, refuse old servitude, and receive Christ's food for perseverance rather than feeding disordered appetites.\\
Anagogical & The wilderness meal points toward the final rest where the baptized, fully freed from death, are satisfied by God without hunger or fatigue.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Core Scriptural Symbols}
\begin{connectionstable}
Burial & Romans' burial with Christ makes baptism a real passage through death. The Fathers, especially in mystagogical catechesis, read the font as tomb and womb: descent with Christ, rising into newness. The symbol forbids superficial moralism. The Christian life begins not with advice but with a sacramental death that breaks the old dominion.\\
New Walk & Paul moves from burial to walking. The symbol of walking makes resurrection visible in time: steps, habits, perseverance, direction. The Offertory's plea for perfected steps belongs here. The Church teaches that baptismal life must become a road, not merely an event remembered; grace must take form in repeated movement away from old servitude.\\
Wilderness & Mark's multitude is hungry in a deserted place after remaining with Christ. The wilderness is testing, dependence, and divine feeding, recalling Exodus manna and prophetic pilgrimage. Patristic readers see the Church journeying through this age. The symbol dignifies need: hunger is not proof of abandonment when it becomes the place where Christ's compassion feeds.\\
Loaves & The loaves signify Christ's nourishment through doctrine and Eucharist. Bread is ordinary, but in Christ's hands it becomes superabundant. The symbol joins teaching and sacrament: the faithful are fed by truth and by the altar, not by emotion alone. The Church shows that the baptized new man must be sustained by Christ, not by the energies of the old man.\\
Fragments and Seven Baskets & The gathered fragments reveal excess, order, and preservation. Seven suggests fullness; nothing of Christ's gift is wasted. Patristic imagination sees abundance for the whole Church's pilgrimage. The symbol opposes anxious scarcity: grace is not barely enough, and the Church gathers what remains so future hunger may also be met.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Patristic Exegesis of the Grouped Propers}
The Church places baptismal death beside the feeding of the multitude to teach the full economy of Christian life: the old man is buried, the new man is fed. Patristic exegesis reads Romans 6 as sacramental participation in Christ's death and Mark's loaves as Christ nourishing the Church in the wilderness. The faithful are not merely told to improve; they are shown death, passage, hunger, compassion, bread, and abundance.

\begin{connectionstable}
Introit: God's people and inheritance & Patristic psalm reading hears the Church asking to be saved, ruled, and lifted up. This creates an ecclesial frame for Romans: baptism makes a people, not isolated moral reformers.\\
Collect: nourished and guarded good & The Collect asks God to increase what he has planted and preserve what he has nourished. It anticipates the Gospel's feeding and the ongoing care required after baptism.\\
Epistle: Romans 6 & Chrysostom and Augustine read burial with Christ as a real sacramental break with sin's dominion. Baptism is not decoration of the old life, but death and newness of walk.\\
Gospel: Mark 8 & Bede and the Fathers read Christ's compassion for the hungry multitude as the mercy that feeds the Church in pilgrimage. The loaves signify doctrine, Eucharistic nourishment, and the abundance of Christ.\\
Offertory: steady steps & The Offertory asks God to perfect the steps of the baptized. The new life of Romans becomes a walked road, not a one-time memory.\\
Communion and Postcommunion & Communion completes the pattern, and the Postcommunion asks that God's gifts cleanse and fortify the fed people. Those buried and raised with Christ are sustained by Christ and return praise as the sacrifice of the living.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Death and bread & Romans gives the negative passage from sin; Mark gives the positive sustenance of new life. The Church teaches that baptismal renunciation must be fed by Eucharistic mercy.\\
Wilderness and Church & The multitude in the wilderness is the pilgrim Church. Patristic exegesis sees need not as failure, but as the condition in which Christ's compassion is manifested.\\
Compassion and doctrine & Christ feeds because he has compassion, and the Fathers often link bread with teaching. True doctrine is not cold information; it is nourishment from mercy.\\
Fragments and hope & The surplus fragments oppose despair. No crowd, no hunger, and no poverty exhausts Christ's gift.\\
Church's instruction & The faithful are being told: live as baptized men who have died to sin, confess your hunger without shame, and receive the Eucharistic bread that sustains the new walk.\\
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Exegetical Notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Paul speaks in the indicative before the imperative: you have died with Christ; therefore reckon yourselves dead to sin.
\item Mark notes the crowd has been with Christ three days. The Fathers often hear paschal resonance in this detail.
\item Seven loaves and seven baskets invite a reading of fullness: Christ gives enough for the whole journey of the Church.
\end{itemize}

\begin{witnessstable}
\witnessrow{St. Cyril of Jerusalem}{Baptism is a sharing in Christ's burial.}{Use this in a mystagogical homily: the font is not decoration but death and birth.}
\witnessrow{St. Ambrose}{Water does not work alone; the Spirit works there.}{Clarify sacramental realism without magic: God acts through signs.}
\witnessrow{St. Bede}{The hungry crowd images those fed by Christ's doctrine and bread.}{Join teaching and Eucharist: Christ feeds mind and body.}
\witnessrow{St. Thomas Aquinas}{The Eucharist is food for the wayfarer.}{The Gospel's wilderness feeding points naturally to viaticum and perseverance.}
\end{witnessstable}

\begin{connectionstable}
Baptism and Eucharist & Romans names the death and resurrection given in baptism; Mark shows Christ feeding those who follow him in the desert; the Postcommunion asks that the gifts cleanse and defend. Initiation, sustenance, and preservation belong together.\\
Wilderness and Church & The crowd has remained three days with Christ. The Fathers hear paschal overtones: the people who share his death receive bread for the journey.\\
Death and appetite & The old man is crucified, but the appetites of Egypt still call. The Eucharistic Lord retrains desire.\\
Sevenfold fullness & Seven loaves and seven baskets can be preached as a sign of sufficiency: grace is not meager.\\
Prayer focus & Renew baptismal renunciation by refusing one old servitude and receiving today's duties as food for new life.
\end{connectionstable}

\section*{Preaching Applications}
\begin{itemize}
\item Invite renewal of baptismal promises in concrete terms: what sin must be treated as dead today?
\item Present Sunday Mass as food for perseverance, not a reward for already feeling strong.
\item Encourage families to connect holy water, confession, and communion as one sacramental pattern of death and life.
\end{itemize}
